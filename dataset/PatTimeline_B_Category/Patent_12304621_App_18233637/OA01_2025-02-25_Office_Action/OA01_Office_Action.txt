REASONS FOR ALLOWANCE
Claims 1-15 are allowed.
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
The prior art of record fails to disclose or make obvious the combined limitations of Applicant’s claimed invention. Specifically, with regard to claim 1, Tian (CN 113911322 A) teaches a rotorcraft (Abstract) with a fuselage (machine body) comprising a lower shell (Examiner notes that the lower shell is simply part of the helicopter’s fuselage), an inner floor (fixed struts and longitudinal beam web plates, Fig. 1, 2, 4, & 6), and at least one lower fuselage compartment arranged between the inner floor and the lower shell (undercarriage), wherein the at least one lower fuselage compartment is equipped with at least two lateral fittings (Fig. 4 & 5), and wherein the at least one lower fuselage compartment is at least accessible via an opening formed in the lower shell (Examiner notes that although not explicitly disclosed, undercarriages are typically accessible via openings in the shell, e.g., a wheel well), the rotorcraft comprising: at least one non-retractable wheel-type landing gear rigidly attached in the at least one lower fuselage compartment (Claim 2 “non-retractable front undercarriage”, Fig. 1-6) and extending at least partly through the opening formed in the lower shell (Examiner notes that the landing gear must extend through the opening in order to be functional), comprising: at least one wheel (Fig. 1); at least two mounting arms extending laterally from the tube toward the at least two lateral fittings in the at least one lower fuselage compartment (connection points B and C, Fig. 1-3), wherein the at least two mounting arms are mounted to the at least two lateral fittings (Fig. 4 & 5).
Furthermore, Mairou (US 20110260000 A1) teaches a shock absorber connected to the at least one wheel (shock absorber (11)), comprising: a shock absorber tube (Fig. 1), and a shock absorber rod telescopically mounted to the shock absorber tube (Para. [0065] “the shock absorber is an oleopneumatic shock absorber”, Fig. 1).
However, the prior art does not teach the combined limitations of the claimed invention, specifically a mounting pin extending from the shock absorber tube toward the floor fitting provided at the inner floor, wherein the mounting pin is connected to the floor fitting. Further, it would not have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art to arrive at the claimed arrangement, since the mounting element of Tian is specifically connected to a fixed strut via a lug in the transverse direction, and modifying the mounting element to be a pin would change the load transmission and transfer generated by the connection points A, B, and C.

US 20060237584 A1 – “Dual Brace-determinate Landing Gear” teaches a landing gear with two oblique mounting arms that attaches to appropriate parts of the aircraft, as well as a mounting element extending from a shock strut to a cantilever support
US 20050279882 A1 – “Landing Assist Probe Retention Strap” teaches a probe attached to a telescoping shock absorber, the shock absorbing assembly attached to an underside of an aircraft via a vertical mounting element and two angled links
US 4345727 A – “Body-braced Main Airplane Landing Gear” teaches landing gear arranged in a wheel bay of an aircraft fuselage, the landing gear including a shock strut, a mounting pin connected to a trunnion shaft, and two arms extended at an angle away from the shock tube, each connected to the body frame of the aircraft
