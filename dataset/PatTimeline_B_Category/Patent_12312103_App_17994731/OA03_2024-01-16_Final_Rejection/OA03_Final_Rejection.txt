Response to Arguments
In regards to the drawing objection, in light of the applicant’s arguments on pages 1-2 of the remarks, the drawing objection concerning the limitations of claims 11-13 and 15 has been withdrawn.
Applicant's arguments filed 1 and 16 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
The applicant’s argument on page 3-4 of the remarks that “…Song article does not mention anything about antenna array systems and beam steering involving the antenna arrays of a plurality of satellites in a formation. Further, Song does not disclose a formation of satellites with antennas where observation data collected by at least one of the antennas in the formation may be used for improved beam steering operations and dynamically changing the formation based on the collected observation data.”, the examiner respectfully disagrees. The Song article implicitly have antenna arrays formed by antennas (i.e., device to transmit/receive signals) of individual satellites, Fig. 4, 7-8). The beam steering of the antenna is the result of the movement of the individual satellites in the satellite formation. Therefore, the satellite of the satellite formation inherently involve beam steering. Song does inherently disclose a formation of satellites with antennas where observation data collected by at least one of the antennas in the formation  ( (i.e., temporal and spatial data, Fig. 5) may be used for improved beam steering operations and dynamically changing the formation based on the collected observation data (Fig. 4, functional). Here, the applicant seem to rely on functional limitation, rather than structural differences, to differentiate the claimed invention from the prior art, a recitation of the intended use of the claimed invention must result in a structural difference between the claimed invention and the prior art in order to patentably distinguish the claimed invention from the prior art.  If the prior art structure is capable of performing the intended use, then it meets the claim.
The applicant’s argument on page 5 of the remarks that “…Song is silent to a system and method adapted for changing the relative angle directions of the radiation lobes of the observation antenna arrays of two separate satellites based on observation data collected by at least one of the observation antenna arrays and where the change in the relative angle directions of the radiation lobes is used for improved beam steering operations for tracking an object whose observation data is currently collected”, the examiner respectfully disagrees.
Song discloses a control system configured to dynamically change the configuration and shape of said formation by controlling the propulsion system of at least one of the satellites in the set by  (as shown in Fig. 4 by controlling the thrust module, Fig. 7 and Fig. 13) sending commands to the propulsion system of at least one of the satellites in the set at least partly based on observation data  (i.e. temporal and spatial data, Fig. 5) collected by at least one of the observation antenna arrays of the at least one of the satellites in the set (inherent) to thereby change at least the relative angle directions of the radiation lobes of the observation antenna arrays of two separate satellites in the formation (i.e., the change in formation or at least a position of at least one satellite changes the relative angle direction of the radiation lobe of the observation antenna arrays of two separate satellites).
The limitations “…for improved beam steering operations for tracking an object whose observation data is currently collected” of claim 1 and 16 are a ‘functional limitation’ and the combination of Song and Stuart references are capable of performing the identified advantage functional limitation since the observation antenna arrays of the satellites in formation can be adapt to the movement pattern of the object/satellite as desired via the control system.
In order to overcome, the above rejection, the applicant is advised to amend the claims to structurally distinguish the claimed inventions from the applied prior arts.
