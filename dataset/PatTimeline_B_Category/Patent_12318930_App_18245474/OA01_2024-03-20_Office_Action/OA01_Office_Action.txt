Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claims 1, 2, 4-7, 9 and 10 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by Jansen et al (US 2011/0196509)
Jansen discloses and shows a module robot comprising:
a first link 120;
a second link 84 movably linked to the first link; and
a fluid pressure cylinder 42/46 configured to move the first link and the second link relative to each other, wherein
the first link has a cylinder block in which a cylinder chamber of the fluid pressure cylinder is formed (see Figs. 2-3).
Cl. 2 – the module robot is configured by coupling a plurality of modules (Fig. 6), the modules each has the first link 224, the second link 228, and the fluid pressure cylinder 262/236, 260,264 and two of the modules are coupled by connecting the first link or the second link of a first module with the first link or the second link of a second module, the first module being one of the two modules, and the second module being other of the two modules.
Cl. 4 – the module robot is configured of a plurality of modules, the modules being formed so as to be partially integrated.
Cl. 5 – the fluid pressure cylinder is a liquid pressure cylinder, and a pump port (at 128/132) connectable to a liquid pressure source and a tank port (at 76/80) connectable to a tank are formed in at least two surfaces among a plurality of outer surfaces of the cylinder block.
Cl. 6 – the cylinder block is formed with a passage (128/132) configured to connect the pump port and the tank port with the cylinder chamber.
Cl. 7 – the fluid pressure cylinder is a liquid pressure cylinder, a pump port (at 128/132) connectable to a liquid pressure source and a tank port (at 76/80) connectable to a tank are formed in at least two surfaces among a plurality of outer surfaces of the cylinder block, and the module further has (para 0057): a state-quantity detector configured to detect a state quantity of the module; a control valve configured to control communication between the cylinder chamber and the pump port and the tank port; and a controller configured to control motion of the module by controlling operation of the control valve based on a detection result from the state-quantity detector.
Cl. 9 – the module robot is configured by coupling at least two of the modules, the modules being identical (Figs. 6, 9).
Cl. 10 – the cylinder block of the first module (Fig. 10, 354) is formed with a joint hole (pin holes at four-bar linkages) into which a joint tool (dowel/pivot pins) is inserted, the joint tool being configured to connect the cylinder block with the first link or the second link of the second module (358).
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim 3 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Jansen as applied to claim 2.
Jansen discloses and shows the invention of claim 2 as described elsewhere above.  The cylinder block of the first module, however, is not connected with the first or second link of the second module.  Yet, one of ordinary skill, knowledge and sense (enough to design, manufacture and use robotics) would have easily recognized that it would be desirous to make the cylinder block of the first module connectable with the first or second link of the second module to provide simultaneous actuation of the first and second module, for example.
Claim 8 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Jansen as applied to claim 1 in view of Nishino (US 2016/0152302).
Jansen discloses and shows the invention of claim 1 as described elsewhere above.  Jansen also includes a liquid pressure cylinder, a supply-discharge port (at 128/132) selectively connectable to a liquid pressure source and tank, and a cylinder block 120 is formed with a passage 128/132 configured to connect the supply-discharge port with the cylinder chamber.  Jansen does not include a tank formed in an outer surface of the cylinder block.
Nishino teaches a liquid pressure cylinder connected with a tank 46 formed in an outer surface of the cylinder block 28c, the arrangement minimizes space of the assembly.  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to modify the cylinder block to include a tank formed in the outer surface to save space of the modular robot assembly.
