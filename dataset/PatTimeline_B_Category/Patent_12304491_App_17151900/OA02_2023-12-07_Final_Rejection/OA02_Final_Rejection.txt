Claim Objections
Claims 1-3, 5-7, and 10 are objected to because of the following informalities: in each of these claims the term “autonomous driving vehicle” is used synonymously with the term “target vehicle”.  While Applicant may be their own lexicographer, for clarity, each element of the claims should only be addressed with a single name.  In independent Claims 1 and 5-7, the Examiner is thus interpreting (a) “a first stop position for stopping the autonomous driving vehicle as a target vehicle based on position information transmitted from the target vehicle that indicates the autonomous driving vehicle to terminate the autonomous driving and map information previously prepared” to instead read “a first stop position for stopping the autonomous driving vehicle autonomous driving vehicle”, (c) “acquire a type of a passenger of the target vehicle” to instead read “acquire a type of a passenger of the autonomous driving vehicle”, (d) “set a second stop position for stopping the autonomous driving vehicle as the target vehicle” to instead read “set a second stop position for stopping the autonomous driving vehicleautonomous driving vehicle to the center device and acquire the first stop position”, (f) “cause the target vehicle to head to the second stop position” to instead read “cause the autonomous driving vehicle to head to the second stop position”, (g) “decided by the vehicle control device placed on the targeautonomous driving vehicle”, (h) “cause the target vehicle to head to the first stop position in a case where the first stop position is acquired from the center device” to instead read “cause the autonomous driving vehicle to head to the first stop position in a case where the first stop position is acquired from the center device”, and (i) “the type of the passenger of the target vehicle includes types of a passenger classified based on…” to instead read “the type of the passenger of the autonomous driving vehicle includes types of a passenger classified based on…”.  In Claim 2, the Examiner is thus interpreting “a position intermediate between a present position of the relief vehicle and a present position of the target vehicle” to instead read “a position intermediate between a present position of the relief vehicle and a present position of the autonomous driving vehicle”.  In Claim 3, the Examiner is thus interpreting “a position temporally intermediate between a present position of the relief vehicle and a present position of the target vehicle” to instead read “a position temporally intermediate between a present position of the relief vehicle and a present position of the autonomous driving vehicle”.  And in Claim 10, the Examiner is thus interpreting “the center device decides the first stop position depending on the type of the passenger of the target vehicle based on the position information transmitted from the target vehicle and the map information” to instead read “the center device decides the first stop position depending on the type of the passenger of the autonomous driving vehicle based on the position information transmitted from the autonomous driving vehicle and the map information”.  Appropriate corrections are required.
Claims 1 and 5-7 are objected to because of the following informalities: it is unclear as to what is meant by “receive inappropriate information indicating that the autonomous driving is inappropriate for the autonomous driving vehicle”.  This limitation found in these independent claims is unclear as “inappropriate” is subjective terminology, as what may constitute inappropriate information and/or inappropriate driving to one person may not be to another person.  As merely an example, a cuss/curse word may be inappropriate information to a mother that has young children within ear shot, but may not be to a sailor on a navy ship only surrounded by other sailors.  Appropriate corrections are required.
Claims 1 and 6 are objected to because of the following informalities: it is unclear as to what is meant by “set a relief method for the passenger according to the type of the passenger”.  There are no prior limitations describing that any given passenger is actually in need of relief, nor what said relief may constitute for any particular complaint/ailment/injury/etc., or how it could be measured objectively, so it is unclear as what is meant by “setting a relief method” within the context of these claim limitations.  Furthermore, “relief” is subjective language, as what may constitute as relief (or a relief method) to one passenger may not constitute relief (or a relief method) to another passenger.  As merely an example, death may be considered “relief” to one person that is old and in non-stop agonizing pain from a diagnosed terminal illness, but may be considered the opposite of “relief” to another who is young and otherwise healthy and wants to get home to their young children.  Appropriate corrections are required.
Claims 4-5 and 7 are objected to because of the following informalities: it is unclear as to what is meant by “a person requiring care” and “a predetermined position having space necessary for care”.  If a person is sad and in their own mind needs a hug, does that person require care according to anyone other than themselves?  If a person is critically hurt but in a triage situation is not taken back to the operating room first because someone else required the attention more urgently, did that person that was not taken back immediately actually require care according to the person who selected the more urgent needs patient instead?  If someone will die without attention but they’re 100 years old and don’t want any further intervention, do they require care?  If we are all going to die someday then is any intervention meant to prolong our lives a sign that we actually don’t require care?  It seems as though these limitations could only be clear if the care itself was defined (e.g. surgery) and there was a defined outcome that results from the care (e.g. so that the passenger doesn’t die within X number of minutes), particularly because care could simply mean giving a complement to someone over the phone, and that doesn’t take up any space at all.  As such, these limitations are unclear.  It should be further noted that the phrase ““a predetermined position having space necessary for care” appears as though it should instead read “a predetermined position having space necessary for the required care”.  Appropriate corrections are required.

Response to Amendments/Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed 20 Nov 23 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
Firstly, regarding Applicant’s remarks pertaining to a request for an interview prior to the issuance of another Office action, the Examiner notes that there was already an interview held between them and the Applicant’s representative on 10 Oct 23 prior to the current Examiner having even done a prior art search on the subject case, since this case was transferred from another Examiner to the current Examiner following the non-final rejection Office action.  It is not a reasonable request to have yet another interview prior to the current Examiner having done a proper examination on the case.  If Applicant would like to have another interview following their review of the instant Office action, the Examiner suggests Applicant propose at least one non-broadening amendment to at least one independent claim along with an After-Final Consideration Program (AFCP) 2.0 request, wherein said request, if proper and granted, will result in an after-final interview being scheduled.
Secondly, regarding Applicant’s remarks pertaining to subjective language, such as “relief”, the Examiner is not persuaded by the Applicant’s reference to the specification’s page 8 lines 16-19 and page 13 lines 15-23.  Examiner believes there are several subjective terms used within the claims and directs Applicant to review the current Office action’s claim objections (pertaining to subjective terminology found in the original claim language) and claim rejections under 35 USC 112(b) (pertaining to subjective terminology found in the amended claim language).  These objections and rejections include detailed explanation as to why these terms are indeed subjective even in view of the instant specification.
Thirdly, regarding Applicant’s remarks pertaining to the previously identified 35 USC 112(f) terms uses within the originally filed claims (and pertaining to the associated 35 USC 112(a)/(b) rejections relating to the lack of written description support and indefiniteness of these 112(f) terms’ structures) the Examiner has removed all claim interpretations of these terms, and the associated 35 USC 112(a)/(b) rejections, now that structure (i.e. at least one processor and a memory coupled to the at least one processor) has been added to the claim limitations in conjunction with these terms’ functionalities.  As such, the Applicant’s remarks pertaining to these terms are moot.
Fourthly, regarding Applicant’s remarks pertaining to the previously made 35 USC 103 prior art rejections, the Examiner is not persuaded.  Applicant’s first point was “First, Poeppel et al. describes “[t]he vehicle rendezvous coordination method starts in step 300 of FIG. 3A.  It is implemented by a computer system within the autonomous automotive vehicle which includes wireless communications capabilities.”  Therefore, Step 308 is performed within a vehicle”.  Applicant argues that their amended independent claims require acquisition of a type of passenger be performed by a center device, which is located outside of the vehicle.  In rebuttal to this argument, firstly, the citation provided is from Wilson, the secondary reference, and not Poeppel, as alleged in the remarks.  Poeppel’s dislosure involves determining the first stop destination at the center device, outside the vehicle, and sending control signals to the vehicle to go to said first stop destination.  As such, the Examiner holds that the control method of Poeppel, in contrast to the control method of Wilson, is in fact done outside the vehicle.  Applicant’s second point was that the type of passenger of the target vehicle is unrelated to a medical emergency situation.  In rebuttal, Examiner states that nothing in the claim language excludes the type of passenger from being classified as either requiring medical attention versus not requiring medical attention, as receiving medical attention when it is required is certainly a type of relief.  As such, the Examiner holds that the cited references in combination certainly render obvious the amended claims.
It should be also noted that Applicant’s arguments allege that the claims have been amended “so as to more clearly recite that the first stop position is given preference over the second stop position”.  The Examiner disagrees with this statement, as nothing in the claims actually discuss preferences.  To better clarify the above prior art rejections, the Examiner notes that one of ordinary skill in the art when considering the combination of Poeppel with Wilson would likely determine that this combination acts to the contrary of this statement (i.e. that the second stop position is actually a priority stop position over the first stop position) without violating the claim limitations.  The Examiner states that the combination provided and explained above generally discloses, teaches, suggests, and/or renders obvious the following: Upon an incident causing a vehicle damaging/compromising condition that results in the termination of a pre-incident autonomous driving plan, the vehicle need not consider any relief for any passengers if (a) they are not present in the vehicle at the time of the incident, or (b) if there are passengers but there are no indications that any of the passengers actually require help of some sort.  In the case of (a) or (b), there is no need for the vehicle to coordinate with an external EMS network in finding a rendezvous location for the vehicle to move to in order to most expeditiously meet up with a nearby/available EMS provider that could bring passenger relief (i.e. second stop position), and instead, the incident would only trigger the vehicle to receive commands from a center device (remote computer such as a fleet management system) to move the vehicle to a location out of the flow of traffic.  That is a lower priority first stop position, that is only received in the case of (a) or (b).  But in the case of (c), where there are one or more passengers present and there is at least an indication that at least one of them does require help of some sort following the incident, then instead of receiving commands from the center device to tell the vehicle where the lower priority first stop position is, the vehicle calculates a higher priority second stop position by coordinating with an external EMS network in finding a rendezvous location for the vehicle to move to in order to most expeditiously meet up with a nearby/available EMS provider that could bring passenger relief.  Therefore, this combination places priority on the second stop position (an EMS rendezvous location determined by the vehicle when there is at least one passenger in need of help) over the first stop position (a center device determined location that may simply be a location to get the vehicle out of the flow of traffic), because there is no need to consider or even receive the lower-priority first stop position when the conditions are there for determining the higher-priority second stop position, since the second stop position actually involves the life, health, and/or safety of one or more passengers, whereas the first stop position does not.  As such, the Examiner is not persuaded by the Applicant’s remarks.
