Claim Interpretation
As previously stated, Claims 4-5 and 7-8 each utilize the phrases “a person requiring care” and/or “a predetermined position having space necessary for the required care”.  “Care” can include emotional support like words of encouragement, and no space is necessary at all to offer said emotional support, and further, it’s subjective at best as to whether or not care, emotional and/or physical, is actually “required” in any given situation (versus what might be merely “helpful” to someone), as is how much space is truly “necessary” for the required care (versus what might be ideal for the required care to be carried out properly/well but in a pinch it could actually be a much smaller space if the required care were carried out improperly/poorly).  Therefore, even though “care” certainly includes emotional support under broadest reasonable interpretation (BRI), in order to eliminate the potential issues relating to these specific limitations due to that broad interpretation for the word “care” in combination with the subjective nature of “required care” and “space necessary for the required care”, the Examiner is instead interpreting “care” to be only physical assistance that involves at least some additional physical space to the exterior of the vehicle that said person is in (and wherein “space necessary” is a bare minimum for providing at least some physical assistance), and is interpreting “requiring” to essentially be “at least appears to require”, such as what would align with dependent Claim 9 when it says that “the person not requiring care is a person who is able to transfer from the autonomous driving vehicle to another autonomous driving vehicle by himself/herself”.  In other words, a person requiring care could be a person that at least appears to require physical mobility assistance to at least some degree (for example, the person may appear to be old enough to be considered elderly, the person may appear to be too young to walk on their own, the person may appear to be unconscious, hurt, in pain, frozen in fear, etc.), and the physical space necessary for the required care is greater than zero because someone or something must be able to get into the vehicle of a person appearing to require this care in order to provide said physical mobility assistance to them.

Applicant's arguments filed 24 Jun 24 have been fully considered but they are not fully persuasive.
Firstly, Applicant appears to be again informally requesting an interview with the Examiner prior to the issuance of another (this instant) Office action if it is not a notice of allowance (which this is not).  The Examiner states that they will be happy to set up an after-final interview with the Applicant if they submit an AFCP 2.0 request along with at least one non-broadening amendment to at least one independent claim.
Secondly, regarding Applicant’s remarks pertaining to the previously made Claim Objection to Claim 8, the Examiner is persuaded by the corrective amendment to the claim and thus this objection has been withdrawn.
Thirdly, regarding Applicant’s remarks pertaining to the previously made 35 USC 103 prior art rejections, the Examiner is not persuaded for several reasons outlined in the rebuttal below.  Applicant’s first point alleges insufficiency with the combination of Poeppel and Wilson in that the combination allegedly fails to disclose, teach, or render obvious “recognize, based on information indicating fault of a device used for autonomous driving or information indicating temporarily unusable state of the device which are transmitted from the vehicle control device, that the autonomous driving vehicle terminates autonomous driving”.  Applicant’s second point trying to allege insufficiency with the combination of Poeppel and Wilson in that the combination allegedly fails to disclose, teach, or render obvious “[the vehicle control device...configured to] set a second stop position for stopping the autonomous driving vehicle, the second stop position being a nearest position to a present position of the autonomous driving vehicle, which is selected from predetermined positions; and [the vehicle control device...configured to] initiate travel of the autonomous driving vehicle toward the second stop position without waiting for receiving of the first stop position transmitted from the center device, upon setting the second stop position.”
Firstly, Poeppel discloses that the autonomous driving vehicle’s vehicle control device recognizes that the autonomous driving vehicle has terminated autonomous driving based on transmitted information indicating fault of a device used for autonomous driving or information indicating temporarily unusable state of the device at least one device (i.e. the vehicle’s original autonomous driving plan is terminated if for example the vehicle is hit during an accident; “Remote interface system 282 may initiate other control actions, such as sending a message to vehicle control system 112…to engage an emergency brake after the autonomous vehicle has stopped”, Paragraph 87, “The recovery plan data 272 can include data indicative of one or more recovery plan(s) associated with the vehicle 104. As an example, the recovery plan data 272 can include data indicative of one or more actions to be taken by the vehicle computing system 102. The one or more actions can include, for example, stopping a motion of the vehicle 10”, Paragraphs 90-91, “At 806, the data is analyzed to determine the condition of one or more vehicle systems of the autonomous vehicle. For example, sensor data may be analyzed to determine whether vehicle systems such as propulsion systems, control systems, and the like are functioning properly”, Paragraph 123).  Based on at least these citations, while information relating to a device fault/unusable state may come from third party data or third party analysis such as from a user interface, it may additionally/alternatively come from the vehicle itself such as from its sensor data.
Secondly, based on the amendments to the claims, the previous citations associated with the first stop position and the second stop position have effectively swapped from how they were previously interpreted.  The first stop position is now the remote recovery location of Poeppel (following modification with Wilson), which requires the autonomous driving vehicle being able to continue driving and having at least one passenger onboard that based on monitoring appears to be having a medical emergency, and thus the autonomous driving vehicle can benefit from coordinating with an external EMS network to determine an effective rendezvous point to meet up with emergency medical personnel.  This first stop position is not the default stop location because it is still possible for the autonomous driving vehicle to be unsafe for continued driving depending on what occurred to it, and/or there may not be any passengers present onboard or if there are they might not actually be in need for emergency medical attention.  Therefore, while making that determination (as to whether or not the autonomous driving vehicle is both safe for continued driving and has at least one passenger onboard that is in actual need of emergency medical attention), the autonomous driving vehicle certainly benefits from having a second stop position to default to.  The second stop position is now the on-site recovery location of Poeppel, which instead only requires the autonomous driving vehicle to pull off the road to the nearest known safe location like an emergency shoulder or parking lot that at least gets the autonomous driving vehicle out of the way of continued traffic.  This second stop position is a logical default because it provides the benefit of the vehicle/passengers being able to avoid the risk of potential secondary accidents if for example the vehicle was already in an accident, and if further analysis/monitoring then identifies that the autonomous driving vehicle is both safe for continued driving and has at least one passenger onboard that is in actual need of emergency medical attention, only then will there be a benefit to determining the delayed first stop position to go to instead of the default second stop position.
