Allowable Subject Matter
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance: The prior art of record fails to disclose, teach, suggest, nor sufficiently render obvious a combination of features as claimed and arranged by applicant when read in light of the specification.
Regarding Claim 1:
Ha discloses:
A robot motion control method, applied to a robot, the method comprising.  Paragraph [0037] describes a robot in which the trajectories of the robot are made using a center of mass of the robot and its feet.
obtaining a center-of-mass reference trajectory for guiding the robot to execute a target motion; the center-of-mass reference trajectory being a center-of-mass motion trajectory of the robot configured to achieve the target motion.  Paragraph [0037] describes a robot in which the trajectories of the robot are made using a center of mass of the robot and its feet.  These trajectories are computing using trajectory optimization.
generating joint control information according to the center-of-mass control information and a structure matrix of the robot; and controlling the robot to execute the target motion based on the joint control information.  Paragraph [0093] describes a Covariance Matrix Adaptation-Evolution Strategy.  Paragraph [0094] describes joint positions being sequentially solved in this matrix to find the joint positions including global position, orientation and joint angles at time t to match the desired trajectory of the motion optimizer.
Ha does not disclose the desired center-of-mass motion data being a desired value of motion data associated with a center of mass of the robot and calculating the center-of-mass control information enabling the value of the objective function to satisfy an optimization stop condition.
Rinke, in an analogous field of endeavor, teaches:
determining desired center-of-mass motion data of the robot based on the center-of-mass reference trajectory, the desired center-of-mass motion data being a desired value of motion data associated with a center of mass of the robot.  Paragraph [0089] describes a Center-of-Mass (COM) deviation in graphs 925, 935 and 945 which describes COM motion data.
calculating center-of-mass control information enabling the value of the objective function to satisfy an optimization stop condition.  Paragraph [0076] describes a differential-algebraic equations (DAE) to retarget motion profiles of the physical robot to minimize the dynamic equilibrium constant to minimize deviations.
Therefore, it would have been prima facie obvious to one of the ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have modified Ha to incorporate the teachings of Rinke to show the desired center-of-mass motion data being a desired value of motion data associated with a center of mass of the robot and calculating the center-of-mass control information enabling the value of the objective function to satisfy an optimization stop condition.  One would have been motivated to do so in order to yield fast and accurate vibration suppression of robot movement (Abstract of Rinke).
Ha and Rinke do not disclose or teach constructing an object function based on center-of-mass rotation data and matrix data constructed based on a position relationship between the center of mass of the robot and contact points and a value of the objective function representing a degree of deviation of the robot from the center-of-mass reference trajectory.
Huang, in an analogous field of endeavor, teaches:
constructing an objective function based on the desired center-of-mass motion data and matrix data constructed based on a position relationship between the center of mass of the robot and contact points, the contact points being contact points between the robot and an environment, a value of the objective function is representing a degree of deviation of the robot from the center-of-mass reference trajectory.  Paragraph [0055] to [0060] and figure 2 describe controlling a stable walking of a biped robot.  In summary, step S104 describes constructing a center of mass state prediction model.  S106 describes simplifying the optimization equations of the center of mass prediction model.  Paragraph [0067] describes these equations having a center of mass, contact points of each supporting leg with the ground and a derivative of the rotation matrix.  Paragraph [0085] describes a weight vector of the center of mass state error at each moment.
Therefore, it would have been prima facie obvious to one of the ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date, with a reasonable expectation for success of the claimed invention, to have modified Ha and Rinke to incorporate the teachings of Huang to show constructing an object function based on center-of-mass rotation data and matrix data constructed based on a position relationship between the center of mass of the robot and contact points and a value of the objective function representing a degree of deviation of the robot from the center-of-mass reference trajectory.  One would have been motivated to do so to obtain control parameters for steady walking by solving the system of quadratic programming equations, i.e. the same role that it plays in the present application.
