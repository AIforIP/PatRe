Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
Claims 2, 3, 11, 12, and 20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor or the applicant regards as the invention.
Regarding claims 2, 11, and 20 the phrase slow enough renders each claim indefinite because it is a subjective limitation.  The specification does not provide any standard for determining the scope of “slow enough”.  Some objective standard must be provided in order to allow one of ordinary skill in the art to determine the scope of the claim.  A claim that requires the exercise of subjective judgments without restriction renders the claim indefinite. In re Musgrave, 431 F.2d 882, 893 (CCPA 1970)
Regarding claims 3, 12, and 20 the phrase faster than renders each claim indefinite because it is a subjective limitation.  The specification does not provide any standard for determining the scope of “faster than”.  Some objective standard must be provided in order to allow one of ordinary skill in the art to determine the scope of the claim.  A claim that requires the exercise of subjective judgments without restriction renders the claim indefinite. In re Musgrave, 431 F.2d 882, 893 (CCPA 1970).
