Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1-3 and 5-10 are allowed.
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
Regarding claim 1, the closest prior art of record Boecker (US 2020/0180605 A1) discloses a method for operating a transverse stabilization system of a vehicle (Starting in paragraph [0074] for example Boecker discloses a method for correcting the trajectory of a vehicle), the method comprising the following steps:
reading in a steering direction of the vehicle (In paragraph [0077], Boecker discloses that an EMU 116 can receive current sensor data from the sensor array(s) 134 and other sensors to measure deviations from an expected trajectory [steering direction of the vehicle]) and a setpoint direction of the vehicle (In paragraphs [0074-0079], Boecker discloses that precise trajectories may be calculated at a given frequency to maneuver the vehicle 108 safely through an environment corresponding to a series of vehicle poses (i.e. position and orientation), linear and angular velocities, as well as linear and angular accelerations that should put the vehicle 108 on the desired trajectory [setpoint direction of the vehicle] unless deviations from the desired trajectory indicate that there is some loss of control);
determining, based on a comparison of the steering and setpoint directions to each other, that there is satisfaction of a condition that deviation between the steering and setpoint directions is greater than a threshold deviation, indicating that a steering being performed is towards a first target point that is different than a second target point to which the setpoint direction is to lead (In paragraphs [0078-0081], Boecker discloses that the EMU 116 can compare the sensor data [steering direction of the vehicle] to expected data [setpoint direction of the vehicle] to determine if the vehicle 108 is “on course” or if there is some significant deviation, wherein the EMU 116 may take corrective action “when the deviations reach a predetermined threshold”);
responsive to the satisfaction of the condition, performing the following:
setting a transverse stabilization direction (In paragraphs [0078-0081], Boecker discloses that if there is a significant deviation (i.e., more than a minor correction is required), the EMU can use steering angle as an output to effect correction back to the desired path, for example, if the vehicle 108 is oversteering, the EMU 116 can send a signal to the CECU 114 for the outer front wheel to apply the brakes, counter steer (i.e., reduce the commanded steering angle), and/or increase the power to the inner rear wheel), wherein the setting of the transverse stabilization direction is performed such that, a degree, relative to the steering direction, to which the setpoint direction factors into the setting of the transverse stabilization is dynamically adjusted depending on an extent of the deviation between the setpoint direction and the steering direction (In paragraphs [0078-0081], Boecker discloses that the EMU 116 can compare the sensor data [steering direction of the vehicle] to expected data [setpoint direction of the vehicle] to determine if the vehicle 108 is “on course” or if there is some significant deviation, wherein the EMU 116 may take corrective action “when the deviations reach a predetermined threshold”); and
performing a transverse stabilization automatically steering the vehicle in the transverse stabilization direction (In paragraphs [0078-0081], Boecker discloses that if there is a significant deviation (i.e., more than a minor correction is required), the EMU can use steering angle as an output to effect correction back to the desired path, for example, if the vehicle 108 is oversteering, the EMU 116 can send a signal to the CECU 114 for the outer front wheel to apply the brakes, counter steer (i.e., reduce the commanded steering angle), and/or increase the power to the inner rear wheel).
Although prior art of record Raste (US 2018/0178769 A1) teaches setting a transverse stabilization direction that is different than the steering direction and the setpoint direction, the transverse stabilization direction being towards a third target point that is different than each of the first and second target points (In paragraphs [0013-0014], Raste teaches that a driver steering demand [transverse stabilization target] can be determined on the basis of the set steering wheel angle [the steering direction], and in an embodiment both the set steering wheel angle [the steering direction] and a direction demand [setpoint direction] determined from the surroundings sensor arrangement are taken into consideration, for example are proportionately combined), the Examiner understands that such a teaching cannot be implemented while the combination of Boecker and Raste continues to achieve the claim limitation reciting “wherein the setting of the transverse stabilization direction is performed such that, a degree, relative to the steering direction, to which the setpoint direction factors into the setting of the transverse stabilization is dynamically adjusted depending on an extent of the deviation between the setpoint direction and the steering direction” as disclosed by Boecker. Therefore, independent claim 1 is allowed.
Similar reasoning is applied to independent claims 9-10. Therefore, independent claims 1 and 9-10, and corresponding dependent claims 2-3 and 5-8 are allowed.

Conclusion
The prior art made of record and not relied upon is considered pertinent to applicant's disclosure.
Obermuller (US 2019/0152520 A1) teaches a method and steering system for steering a vehicle including wherein, through an automatically adjustable steering ratio for the wheels of at least one of the axles, a ratio of the steering wheel angle to the steering angle for the wheels is set, wherein the vehicle travels into a curve on a circular path with a radius at a speed with a first value, wherein a steering wheel angle is adjusted by the driver.
Kalabic (US 2019/0054955 A1) teaches systems and methods for intention-based steering of vehicle including determining a motor command based on the solution to a differential equation to reduce an error between the current heading of the vehicle and the desired heading of the vehicle, such that an amount of actuation by the motor command rotates a direction of the wheels of the vehicle steering system.
