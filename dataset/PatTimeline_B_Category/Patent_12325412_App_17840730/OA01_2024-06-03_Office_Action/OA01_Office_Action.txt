Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Claims 1-10 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because the claimed invention is directed to an abstract idea without significantly more.
Step 1: Regarding independent claims 1 and 9-10, the claims fall under a statutory category as they recite a method including at least one step, a control unit, and a non-transitory machine-readable storage medium respectively.
Step 2a) Prong One: Claims 1 and 9-10 recite a judicial exception. Claim 1 recites: “A method for operating a transverse stabilization system of a vehicle, the method comprising the following steps: reading in a steering direction of the vehicle and a setpoint direction of the vehicle; and determining a transverse stabilization target for the transverse stabilization system using the steering direction and the setpoint direction.”
Claim 9 recites: “read[ing] in a steering direction of the vehicle and a setpoint direction of the vehicle; and determin[ing] a transverse stabilization target for the transverse stabilization system using the steering direction and the setpoint direction.”
Claim 10 recites: “reading in a steering direction of the vehicle and a setpoint direction of the vehicle; and determining a transverse stabilization target for the transverse stabilization system using the steering direction and the setpoint direction.”
These limitations, as drafted, are simple processes that, under their broadest reasonable interpretation, cover performance of the limitations in the mind.
Step 2a) Prong Two: Claims 1 and 9-10 do not integrate the abstract idea into a practical application. Claim 9 recites an element of “A control unit for a transverse stabilization system” and claim 10 recites an element of “A non-transitory machine-readable storage medium on which is stored a computer program for operating a transverse stabilization system of a vehicle, the computer program, when executed by a processor, causing the processor to perform the following steps”. Other than reciting a control unit to perform the abstract steps or a non-transitory machine-readable storage medium storing a program executed by a processor to perform the abstract steps, nothing in the claim elements preclude the step from being performed in the mind. For example, but for the generic computer component language, the claims encompass making a determination based on obtained information, which is understood to be a simple judgment as the determining is recited at a high level of generality. The mere recitation of generic computer components does not take the claim limitations out of the mental process grouping. Accordingly, this additional element does not integrate the abstract idea into a practical application because it does not impose any meaningful limits on practicing the abstract idea. The generic computer components are recited at a high-level of generality (i.e., as a generic processor performing a generic computer function of processing data) such that it amounts no more than mere instructions to apply the exception using a generic computer component – MPEP 2106.05(f).
Step 2b: Claims 1 and 9-10 are ineligible. As discussed with respect to Step 2A Prong Two, the additional elements in the claims amount to no more than mere instructions to apply the exception using a computer. The same analysis applies here in 2B, i.e., mere instructions to apply an exception on a computer cannot integrate a judicial exception into a practical application at Step 2A or provide an inventive concept in Step 2B. Therefore, the claimed elements do not amount to significantly more than the abstract idea.
The dependent claims 2-8 do not add anything significantly more to the abstract idea. Claims 2-8 merely recite additional abstract steps and additional insignificant extra-solution activity. Therefore, claims 1-10 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101.

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
In the event the determination of the status of the application as subject to AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 (or as subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103) is incorrect, any correction of the statutory basis (i.e., changing from AIA  to pre-AIA ) for the rejection will not be considered a new ground of rejection if the prior art relied upon, and the rationale supporting the rejection, would be the same under either status.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
The factual inquiries for establishing a background for determining obviousness under 35 U.S.C. 103 are summarized as follows:
1. Determining the scope and contents of the prior art.
2. Ascertaining the differences between the prior art and the claims at issue.
3. Resolving the level of ordinary skill in the pertinent art.
4. Considering objective evidence present in the application indicating obviousness or nonobviousness.
Claim 2 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Boecker (US 2020/0180605 A1), in view of Raste (US 2018/0178769 A1).
Boecker does not explicitly disclose wherein the transverse stabilization target is determined as lying between the setpoint direction and the steering direction.
However, Raste teaches wherein the transverse stabilization target is determined as lying between the setpoint direction and the steering direction (In paragraphs [0013-0014], Raste teaches that a driver steering demand [transverse stabilization target] can be determined on the basis of the set steering wheel angle [the steering direction], and in an embodiment both the set steering wheel angle [the steering direction] and a direction demand [setpoint direction] determined from the surroundings sensor arrangement are taken into consideration, for example are proportionately combined; the Examiner understands that by proportionately combining, the transverse stabilization target must inherently lie between the setpoint direction and the steering direction).
Raste is considered to be analogous to the claimed invention in that they both pertain to stabilizing control of a vehicle by setting a target by combining a setpoint and steering direction. It would be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to implement the teachings of Raste with the method as disclosed by Boecker where combining the setpoint direction and steering direction may advantageously allow a greater breadth of control options for the vehicle, for example, allowing the vehicle to be more accurately controlled according to a greater variety of information and react appropriately in a wider variety of circumstances.
Claim 5 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Boecker (US 2020/0180605 A1), in view of Utsui (WO 2020/157798 A1).
Boecker does not explicitly disclose wherein the setpoint direction is used to determine the transverse stabilization target when a confidence value of the setpoint direction is greater than a predetermined threshold value.
However, Utsui teaches wherein the setpoint direction is used to determine the transverse stabilization target when a confidence value of the setpoint direction is greater than a predetermined threshold value (In paragraphs 7-8 of page 4 and paragraph 5 of page 8 of the provided translation for example, Utsui teaches determining the availability of the lane keeping support for the road on which the vehicle 100 is running using the characteristic line accuracy [confidence value of the setpoint direction] stored in the map DB 33 in the past by comparing the characteristic line accuracy acquired from the map DB 33 to a predetermined threshold value, and also where a support availability determination unit 11a compares a calculated route feature line accuracy [confidence value of the setpoint direction] with a predetermined threshold value to determine whether or not lane keeping support is available for the route; see also the last two paragraphs of page 3 of the provided translation where Utsui teaches that the characteristic line 110 is for example a boundary line 105 of the road used to perform a lane keeping assist function [transverse stabilization], wherein if the detection of the characteristic line 110 becomes unstable, the assist operation itself becomes impossible).
Utsui is considered to be analogous to the claimed invention in that they both pertain to performing steering stabilization control based on comparing an accuracy of a set route and traveled road to some threshold. It would be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to implement the teachings of Utsui with the method as disclosed by Boecker where assuring that an accuracy meets some threshold value advantageously increase the accuracy of the set information to be utilized for control of the vehicle, increasing the safety and navigational accuracy of the navigation utilizing the information in turn, for example.
Claims 6-7 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Boecker (US 2020/0180605 A1), in view of Ost (US 2022/0281525 A1).
Regarding claim 6, although in paragraph [0095] Boecker discloses that a trajectory calculation 1048 may be performed based on local pose data 1034 and object of interest data 1046, Boecker does not explicitly disclose wherein the setpoint direction is derived from a recognized line of the road.
However, Ost teaches wherein the setpoint direction is derived from a recognized line of the road (In paragraph [0041], Ost teaches providing steering assistance based on a trajectory to follow T for the vehicle 1, wherein a control unit 10 is configured to calculate the trajectory to follow T based on the direction, curvature and/or change in curvature of the road lane RL; the Examiner understands a road lane to be at least an example of a “recognized line of the road” under its broadest reasonable interpretation; see also paragraphs [0043] and [0046] where Ost teaches that the lateral reference position used for calculating the trajectory to follow T may for example be a lateral center position of the road lane RL, i.e. in the middle of the actual road, in-between the lane markings LM, or as another example it may be one of the road lane markings LM, and wherein the road lane RL is determined based on information from the external perception sensor system 20 and from the vehicle state sensor system 21).
Ost is considered to be analogous to the claimed invention in that they both pertain to providing steering assistance based on a trajectory of a vehicle derived from a line of the road traveled by the vehicle. It would be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to implement the teachings of Ost with the method as disclosed by Boecker where utilizing a recognized line of the road to derive a setpoint direction of the vehicle may be advantageous in that doing so “will support the driver in keeping the vehicle on track in the road lane” as suggested by Ost in paragraph [0013], increasing the driving accuracy of the vehicle and preventing the vehicle from exiting the lane or road unintentionally for example.
Regarding claim 7, although in paragraph [0095] Boecker discloses that a trajectory calculation 1048 may be performed based on local pose data 1034 and object of interest data 1046, Boecker does not explicitly disclose wherein the setpoint direction is determined using an environment capture system of the vehicle.
However, Ost teaches wherein the setpoint direction is determined using an environment capture system of the vehicle (In paragraphs [0041] and [0046], Ost teaches providing steering assistance based on a trajectory to follow T for the vehicle 1, wherein a control unit 10 is configured to calculate the trajectory to follow T based on the direction, curvature and/or change in curvature of the road lane RL, wherein the road lane RL is determined based on information from the external perception sensor system 20 and from the vehicle state sensor system 21).
Ost is considered to be analogous to the claimed invention in that they both pertain to providing steering assistance based on a trajectory of a vehicle determined using an environment capture system of the vehicle. It would be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to implement the teachings of Ost with the method as disclosed by Boecker where utilizing a line of the road determined using an environment capture system of the vehicle to derive a setpoint direction of the vehicle may be advantageous in that doing so “will support the driver in keeping the vehicle on track in the road lane” as suggested by Ost in paragraph [0013], increasing the driving accuracy of the vehicle and preventing the vehicle from exiting the lane or road unintentionally for example.
