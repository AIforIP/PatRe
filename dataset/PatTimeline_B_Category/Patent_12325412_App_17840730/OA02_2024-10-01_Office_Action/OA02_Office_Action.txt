Response to Arguments
Applicant’s arguments, see the section titled “III. Applicant's arguments, see the sections titled “IV. Rejection of Claims 1, 3, and 8-10 Under 35 U.S.C. 102”, “V. Rejection of Claim 2 Under 35 U.S.C. 103”, “VI. Rejection of Claim 5 Under 35 U.S.C. 103”, and “VII. Rejection of Claims 6 and 7 Under 35 U.S.C. 103” from pages 5-7 of the reply filed 08/15/2024, with respect to the rejection of claims 1-2 and 5-10 under 35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
For example, Applicant argues that “Neither Boecker nor Raste suggests that where it is determined that a vehicle controller, e.g., a driver, is steering the vehicle in a manner indicating that the controller is intending to guide the vehicle in a direction different than a setpoint direction, then stabilization is performed to guide the vehicle in an altogether different direction than both of those directions.” However, the Examiner disagrees. It is the Examiner’s opinion that the combination of Boecker and Raste discloses the newly amended limitations of the independent claim.
Regarding claim 1, Boecker discloses determining, based on a comparison of the steering and setpoint directions to each other, that there is satisfaction of a condition that there is greater than a threshold deviation between steering and setpoint directions, indicating that a steering being performed is towards a first target point that is different than a second target point to which the setpoint direction is to lead (In paragraphs [0078-0081], Boecker discloses that the EMU 116 can compare the sensor data [steering direction of the vehicle] to expected data [setpoint direction of the vehicle] to determine if the vehicle 108 is “on course” or if there is some significant deviation, wherein the EMU 116 may take corrective action “when the deviations reach a predetermined threshold”);
responsive to the satisfaction of the condition, performing the following:
setting a transverse stabilization direction (In paragraphs [0078-0081], Boecker discloses that if there is a significant deviation (i.e., more than a minor correction is required), the EMU can use steering angle as an output to effect correction back to the desired path, for example, if the vehicle 108 is oversteering, the EMU 116 can send a signal to the CECU 114 for the outer front wheel to apply the brakes, counter steer (i.e., reduce the commanded steering angle), and/or increase the power to the inner rear wheel); and
performing a transverse stabilization automatically steering the vehicle in the transverse stabilization direction (In paragraphs [0078-0081], Boecker discloses that if there is a significant deviation (i.e., more than a minor correction is required), the EMU can use steering angle as an output to effect correction back to the desired path, for example, if the vehicle 108 is oversteering, the EMU 116 can send a signal to the CECU 114 for the outer front wheel to apply the brakes, counter steer (i.e., reduce the commanded steering angle), and/or increase the power to the inner rear wheel).
Raste teaches setting a transverse stabilization direction that is different than the steering direction and the setpoint direction, the transverse stabilization direction being towards a third target point that is different than each of the first and second target points (In paragraphs [0013-0014], Raste teaches that a driver steering demand [transverse stabilization target] can be determined on the basis of the set steering wheel angle [the steering direction], and in an embodiment both the set steering wheel angle [the steering direction] and a direction demand [setpoint direction] determined from the surroundings sensor arrangement are taken into consideration, for example are proportionately combined).
Furthermore, it is the Examiner’s opinion that Raste is considered to be analogous to the claimed invention in that they both pertain to stabilizing control of a vehicle by setting a target that is different than both a setpoint and steering direction. It would be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to implement the teachings of Raste with the method as disclosed by Boecker where combining the setpoint direction and steering direction may advantageously allow a greater breadth of control options for the vehicle, for example, allowing the vehicle to be more accurately controlled according to a greater variety of information and react appropriately in a wider variety of circumstances.
Similar reasoning is applied to independent claims 9-10 which contain corresponding subject matter. Therefore, claims 1-2 and 5-10 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103. See the rejections below.
The Examiner notes that the newly amended subject matter of claim 3 has overcome the rejection under 35 U.S.C. 102, and the rejection has been withdrawn. See the section regarding allowable subject matter below.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claim 5 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Claim 5 recites the limitation the transverse stabilization target; there is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim. The Examiner notes that the antecedent basis which was previously provided for this limitation within the disclosure of claim 1 has been removed by amendment. Without proper antecedent basis, the Examiner understands that it would not be clear to a person having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention what properties or qualities a “the transverse stabilization target” would need to possess in order to constitute “the transverse stabilization target” as claimed. Therefore, claim 5 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b).

The combination of Boecker and Raste does not explicitly disclose wherein the setpoint direction is used for the setting of the transverse stabilization target conditional upon that a confidence value of the setpoint direction is greater than a predetermined threshold value.
However, Utsui teaches wherein the setpoint direction is used for the setting of the transverse stabilization target conditional upon that a confidence value of the setpoint direction is greater than a predetermined threshold value (In paragraphs 7-8 of page 4 and paragraph 5 of page 8 of the provided translation for example, Utsui teaches determining the availability of the lane keeping support for the road on which the vehicle 100 is running using the characteristic line accuracy [confidence value of the setpoint direction] stored in the map DB 33 in the past by comparing the characteristic line accuracy acquired from the map DB 33 to a predetermined threshold value, and also where a support availability determination unit 11a compares a calculated route feature line accuracy [confidence value of the setpoint direction] with a predetermined threshold value to determine whether or not lane keeping support is available for the route).
Utsui is considered to be analogous to the claimed invention in that they both pertain to performing steering stabilization control based on comparing an accuracy of a set route and traveled road to some threshold. It would be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to implement the teachings of Utsui with the method as disclosed by the combination of Boecker and Raste where assuring that an accuracy meets some threshold value advantageously increase the accuracy of the set information to be utilized for control of the vehicle, increasing the safety and navigational accuracy of the navigation utilizing the information in turn, for example.
Claims 6-7 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Boecker (US 2020/0180605 A1) and Raste (US 2018/0178769 A1), in view of Ost (US 2022/0281525 A1).
Regarding claim 6, although in paragraph [0095] Boecker discloses that a trajectory calculation 1048 may be performed based on local pose data 1034 and object of interest data 1046, the combination of Boecker and Raste does not explicitly disclose wherein the setpoint direction is derived from a recognized line of the road.
However, Ost teaches wherein the setpoint direction is derived from a recognized line of the road (In paragraph [0041], Ost teaches providing steering assistance based on a trajectory to follow T for the vehicle 1, wherein a control unit 10 is configured to calculate the trajectory to follow T based on the direction, curvature and/or change in curvature of the road lane RL; the Examiner understands a road lane to be at least an example of a “recognized line of the road” under its broadest reasonable interpretation; see also paragraphs [0043] and [0046] where Ost teaches that the lateral reference position used for calculating the trajectory to follow T may for example be a lateral center position of the road lane RL, i.e. in the middle of the actual road, in-between the lane markings LM, or as another example it may be one of the road lane markings LM, and wherein the road lane RL is determined based on information from the external perception sensor system 20 and from the vehicle state sensor system 21).
Ost is considered to be analogous to the claimed invention in that they both pertain to providing steering assistance based on a trajectory of a vehicle derived from a line of the road traveled by the vehicle. It would be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to implement the teachings of Ost with the method as disclosed by the combination of Boecker and Raste where utilizing a recognized line of the road to derive a setpoint direction of the vehicle may be advantageous in that doing so “will support the driver in keeping the vehicle on track in the road lane” as suggested by Ost in paragraph [0013], increasing the driving accuracy of the vehicle and preventing the vehicle from exiting the lane or road unintentionally for example.

Ost is considered to be analogous to the claimed invention in that they both pertain to providing steering assistance based on a trajectory of a vehicle determined using an environment capture system of the vehicle. It would be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to implement the teachings of Ost with the method as disclosed by the combination of Boecker and Raste where utilizing a line of the road determined using an environment capture system of the vehicle to derive a setpoint direction of the vehicle may be advantageous in that doing so “will support the driver in keeping the vehicle on track in the road lane” as suggested by Ost in paragraph [0013], increasing the driving accuracy of the vehicle and preventing the vehicle from exiting the lane or road unintentionally for example.
