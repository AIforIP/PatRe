Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
This application currently names joint inventors. In considering patentability of the claims the examiner presumes that the subject matter of the various claims was commonly owned as of the effective filing date of the claimed invention(s) absent any evidence to the contrary.  Applicant is advised of the obligation under 37 CFR 1.56 to point out the inventor and effective filing dates of each claim that was not commonly owned as of the effective filing date of the later invention in order for the examiner to consider the applicability of 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(2)(C) for any potential 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2) prior art against the later invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim(s) 15 and 17 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Leger (US 3,670,999) in view of Keich et al. (US 3,781,055).
Regarding claims 15 and 17: Leger teaches the extraction assembly according to claim 13. Leger does not specifically teach an over center locking device configured to lock the extraction parachute release device in the closed configuration and to unlock so that the extraction parachute release device can move to the open configuration.
Keich teaches an extraction parachute release device (c. 1, ℓ. 7-15) having a lever assembly comprising a pair of pivotally mounted jaws (figs. 3-4: hook 33 and latch member 34 are pivotally mounted to each other) configured to engage with and retain or release a coupling portion (c. 3, ℓ. 55-68; c. 5, ℓ. 40-50), and an over centre locking device (toggle linkage 37) configured to lock the extraction parachute release device in the closed configuration (c. 4, ℓ. 30-50), and to unlock so that the extraction parachute release device can move to an open configuration (c. 4, ℓ. 47-59; c. 5, ℓ. 40-49).
It would have been obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art at the time the invention was filed to have configured the extraction assembly of Leger with an extraction parachute release device having pivotally mounted jaws and an over center locking device, such as taught by Keich, for the purpose of securely coupling the extraction parachute to support high tension loads while being able to release with little force (Keich c. 5, ℓ. 30-40) and to dissipate the energy generated by the pull of the extraction parachute without any sudden impacting of parts of the latch assembly (Keich c. 5, ℓ. 53-62).
Claim(s) 20 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Leger (US 3,670,999) in view of Jones (EP 3 293 116 A1).
Regarding claim 20: Leger teaches the method according to claim 19, but does not specifically teach that the extraction parachute is uncoupled from the platform when the platform has moved under the action of the drag force from the first position to a second position relative to the aircraft.
Jones teaches an extraction parachute which is uncoupled from the platform when the platform has moved under the action of the drag force from the first position to a second position relative to the aircraft (¶ [0030]: “allowing the extractor parachute to pull out the platform relative to the aircraft, and releasing the ejectable connector from the main body, thus releasing the extractor parachute from the platform”).
It would have been obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art at the time the invention was filed to have configured the extraction assembly of Leger to uncouple the extraction parachute from the platform when the platform has moved under the action of the drag force from the first position to a second position relative to the aircraft, such as taught by Jones, for the purpose of waiting to release the extraction parachute at the correct time, when the platform leaves the aircraft (Jones ¶ [0062]-[0063]).
