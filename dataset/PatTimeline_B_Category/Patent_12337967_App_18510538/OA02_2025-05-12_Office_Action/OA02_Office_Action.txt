Reasons for Allowance
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
Aerial cargo delivery systems using an extraction parachute to deploy a main parachute are known where the extraction parachute may be jettisoned in the event of an emergency. Generally, for example in US 5,697,581 to Conroy et al., the extraction parachute is initially coupled to the main parachute and only decoupled if necessary. However, some prior art systems are known, such as US 3,670,999 to Leger and US 3,865,333 to Fielding et al., in which the extraction parachute is initially uncoupled from the main parachute, the extraction and main parachutes being coupled during extraction of the cargo while the cargo is still inside the aircraft.
Conroy teaches an aerial cargo delivery system where the extraction parachute may be released while connected to the main parachute (as shown in figures 3 and 8-10) or the extraction parachute may be jettisoned by itself in an emergency (as shown in figures 2 and 11-13), however in Conroy the extraction and main parachutes are interconnected to begin with and only decoupled in the event of an emergency.
In both Leger and Fielding, link members connected to the extraction parachute to the main parachute are interconnected and released simultaneously; Leger when a crank arm is tripped by a fixture on the floor at the aircraft exit and Fielding when a static line attached to the cargo is pulled taut. Since the same event performs both the interconnection and the release of the link members, neither Leger nor Fielding can be readily modified to perform the two operations at different predetermined positions or at different times during extraction.
