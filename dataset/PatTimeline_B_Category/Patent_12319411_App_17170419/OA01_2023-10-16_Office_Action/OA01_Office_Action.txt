Notice of Pre-AIA  or AIA  Status
The present application, filed on or after March 16, 2013, is being examined under the first inventor to file provisions of the AIA .
Examiner’s Comments
The claims are constructed such that they read on multiple groups.  For example, claim 1 claims one or more particular drones that deliver and assembles a particular product.  Claim 1 also claims a particular set of modular heliostat units.  It is unclear if applicant is claiming (1) a drone delivery system using particular drones to deliver a product to a particular location or if (2) they are claiming a particular set of modular heliostat units with a particular weight/size/configuration capable of being delivered by a drone (assumed).  Further, the specification has multiple other groups, for completeness and in an effort to maintain compact prosecution the examiner has noted these groups, as set forth below.  If applicant has any questions they are invited to call the examiner, as detailed below.
Election/Restrictions
Restriction to one of the following inventions is required under 35 U.S.C. 121:
Group I, drawn to a drone delivery system/method, classified in B64U2101/60
Group II, drawn to various heliostat systems generating electricity, classified in H02S20/30
Group III, drawn to various thermal heliostat systems, classified in F24S30/45
Group IV, drawn to the assembly of heliostats, classified in F24S2023/878
Group V, drawn to a method of cleaning heliostat systems generating electricity, classified in H02S40/10
Group VI, drawn to a method of cleaning thermal heliostat systems, classified in F24S40/20
Group VII, drawn to a plant growing system, classified in A01G9/243.
The inventions are independent or distinct, each from the other because:
Inventions Groups I-VII are distinct. The related inventions are distinct if: (1) the inventions as claimed are either not capable of use together or can have a materially different design, mode of operation, function, or effect; (2) the inventions do not overlap in scope, i.e., are mutually exclusive; and (3) the inventions as claimed are not obvious variants.  See MPEP § 806.05(j).  The inventions are distinct if it can be shown that either: (1) the process as claimed can be practiced by another and materially different apparatus or by hand, or (2) the apparatus as claimed can be used to practice another and materially different process.  (MPEP § 806.05(e)).  The inventions can be shown to be distinct if either or both of the following can be shown: (1) the process for using the product as claimed can be practiced with another materially different product or (2) the product as claimed can be used in a materially different process of using that product. See MPEP § 806.05(h).  In the instant case, the inventions as claimed a drone may deliver a different heliostat (e.g. a heliostat with a vertical home position) than claimed and/or the heliostat may be delivered by a different drone (e.g. a drone with a travel range greater than 20 km).  A photovoltaic system is distinct from a solar thermal system since they have materially different design, mode of operation and function.  A cleaning system/method can be used with other heliostats (e.g. a heliostat with a vertical home position) and/or the heliostats can be cleaned by other systems/methods (e.g. vacuuming or blowing air).  The assembly of a heliostat system could be accomplished by other means and a heliostat system could be a decentralized system.  Plant growing apparatus/system/method is distinct from all other groups.  Furthermore, the inventions as claimed do not encompass overlapping subject matter and there is nothing of record to show them to be obvious variants.
Restriction for examination purposes as indicated is proper because all the inventions listed in this action are independent or distinct for the reasons given above and there would be a serious search and/or examination burden if restriction were not required because one or more of the following reasons apply:
the inventions have acquired a separate status in the art in view of their different classification;

The examiner has required restriction between product or apparatus claims and process claims. Where applicant elects claims directed to the product/apparatus, and all product/apparatus claims are subsequently found allowable, withdrawn process claims that include all the limitations of the allowable product/apparatus claims should be considered for rejoinder. All claims directed to a nonelected process invention must include all the limitations of an allowable product/apparatus claim for that process invention to be rejoined.
In the event of rejoinder, the requirement for restriction between the product/apparatus claims and the rejoined process claims will be withdrawn, and the rejoined process claims will be fully examined for patentability in accordance with 37 CFR 1.104. Thus, to be allowable, the rejoined claims must meet all criteria for patentability including the requirements of 35 U.S.C. 101, 102, 103 and 112. Until all claims to the elected product/apparatus are found allowable, an otherwise proper restriction requirement between product/apparatus claims and process claims may be maintained. Withdrawn process claims that are not commensurate in scope with an allowable product/apparatus claim will not be rejoined. See MPEP § 821.04. Additionally, in order for rejoinder to occur, applicant is advised that the process claims should be amended during prosecution to require the limitations of the product/apparatus claims. Failure to do so may result in no rejoinder. Further, note that the prohibition against double patenting rejections of 35 U.S.C. 121 does not apply where the restriction requirement is withdrawn by the examiner before the patent issues. See MPEP § 804.01.
