Claim Objections
Due to the amendments filed 7/12/2024, the objection of claim 7 has been withdrawn.
Rejections under 35 U.S.C. 103
On page 6 of the Remarks filed 7/12/2024, the Applicant contends that von Reventlow does not teach the amended features that further include elements involving a “fleet of robots,” such that a “control model of a second robot” is further updated based on updates of the control model of the first robot.
The Examiner respectfully disagrees. von Reventlow further teaches additional embodiments in which a plurality of robots may access the same database 17 (see ¶0091) and the processing unit 14 may be included in the robot 1 (see ¶0044), such that updates made to database 17 inherently update the “control models” of each of the plurality of robots. The application of prior art may be overcome by providing amendments that further limit “ancillary data” to be specifically sensor data acquired from sensors onboard the “first robot,” in light of the embodiments described on page 26 of the specification filed 4/12/2022, as well as amendments defining the “control model of the first robot” to be different from the “control model of the second robot,” such that the control models of each robot are not synchronized with a remote database, as taught by von Reventlow, but updated via peer-to-peer communication, as described in the Applicant’s disclosure.

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
In the event the determination of the status of the application as subject to AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 (or as subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103) is incorrect, any correction of the statutory basis (i.e., changing from AIA  to pre-AIA ) for the rejection will not be considered a new ground of rejection if the prior art relied upon, and the rationale supporting the rejection, would be the same under either status.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
The factual inquiries for establishing a background for determining obviousness under 35 U.S.C. 103 are summarized as follows:
1. Determining the scope and contents of the prior art.
2. Ascertaining the differences between the prior art and the claims at issue.
3. Resolving the level of ordinary skill in the pertinent art.
4. Considering objective evidence present in the application indicating obviousness or nonobviousness.
Claims 1-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over von Reventlow et al. (US 2023/0168670 A1), hereinafter von Reventlow, in view of Ferguson et al. (US 2020/0150674 A1), hereinafter Ferguson.
Claim 1
von Reventlow discloses the claimed method of operation of a fleet of robots (see Figure 3, depicting the method carried out by the system in Figure 2, where processing unit 14 may be arranged in robot 1, as described in ¶0044; ¶0091, regarding the application to a plurality of robots using the same “intelligence”). von Reventlow is applied, in light of the embodiment described in ¶0044, where the robot 1 includes the processing unit 14, such that a plurality of robots (i.e. “fleet of robots”) may access the same database 17, as described in ¶0091.
von Reventlow further discloses that the claimed method comprises identifying, by a first robot in the fleet of robots, a set of candidate actions (i.e. action definition candidates) that may be performed by a first robot (see ¶0067, with respect to steps S4 and S6 of Figure 3, regarding that processing unit 14 retrieves potential action definitions as candidates in step S5 from database 17 based on the segmented task in step S4), and collecting, by the first robot and for each candidate action of the set of candidate actions, a respective set of ancillary data (see ¶0068, with respect to step S6 of Figure 3, regarding that processing unit 14 calculates a success score for each action definition candidate). “Ancilliary data” is interpreted in light of the Applicant’s specification, defined as including scores associated with a candidate action (see third paragraph on page 26 of the specification filed 4/12/2022).
von Reventlow further discloses that the claimed method comprises transmitting, by the first robot, a request for instructions to a tele-operation system, the tele-operation system communicatively coupled to the fleet of robots (see ¶0074, with respect to steps S7 and S11 of Figure 3, regarding that the service robot system sends a request to the operation center when the candidate’s score does not exceed a given threshold, where processing unit 14 sends the request via communication interface 21 to the remote operation center for control by a human operator, as defined in ¶0054), the request for the instructions which includes each candidate action of the set of candidate actions (see ¶0022, regarding that the request includes a plurality of action definition candidates).
von Reventlow does not further disclose that the request for the instructions which includes…each respective set of ancillary data. However, von Reventlow further teaches that the request for the instructions includes action definition candidates defined by each respective set of ancillary data (see ¶0022, regarding that the request includes the plurality of action definition candidates with particular success scores for operator selection). Given that the “ancillary data” is a tool used for ranking/selection (see ¶0068-0069), it would be reasonable to further provide the success scores to the operator as part of the request in step S11 of Figure 3, in light of Ferguson.
Specifically, Ferguson teaches a similar method in which a remote human operator (similar to the tele-operation system taught by von Reventlow) is requested (similar to the request taught by von Reventlow) when an autonomous vehicle (similar to the first robot taught by von Reventlow) encounters particularly difficult situations (see ¶0155), such that the request communicated to the remote human operator includes a risk rating (similar to the ancillary data or success scores taught by von Reventlow) (see ¶0159, with respect to step 508 of Figure 16), defined as based on an estimated probability of success in executing a particular procedure (see ¶0157).
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have modified the request for the instructions of von Reventlow to further include each respective set of ancillary data, in light of Ferguson, with the predictable result of increasing the remote operator’s awareness of the particular situation (¶0156 of Ferguson).
von Reventlow further discloses that the claimed method comprises receiving, by the first robot, the instructions from the tele-operation system (see ¶0074, ¶0078, with respect to step S12 of Figure 3, regarding receiving operator input, where the input can be used for immediate execution by the robot 1 or added to the knowledge base of the robot 1, as described in ¶0080-0081), executing, by the first robot, the instructions (see ¶0087, regarding that the additional information added by the operator may include a sequence of executable actions that need to be performed by the robot), and updating a control model (i.e. “knowledge base” or database 17) of the robot, based at least in part on each candidate action of the set of candidate actions, each respective set of ancillary data, and the instructions, to increase a level of autonomy of the first robot (see ¶0079, with respect to step S14 of Figure 3, regarding that the operator adds information to database 17 to make improved evaluation of action definition candidates, reconsider the segmentation of the task and/or selection of action definition candidates, where the updated database 17 improves the robot’s capability of autonomously executing a task, as described in ¶0083-0085; Figure 3, depicting the dependency of step S14 on each candidate and respective success score in step S7).
Given the application of von Reventlow, where the robot 1 includes the processing unit 14 (see ¶0044), and a plurality of robots (i.e. “fleet of robots”) access the same database 17 (see ¶0091), von Reventlow further discloses updating, by a second robot in the fleet of robots, a control model of the second robot, based at least in part based on the update made to the control model of the first robot, to increase a level of autonomy of the second robot (see ¶0079, with respect to step S14 of Figure 3, regarding that the operator adds information to database 17 to make improved evaluation of action definition candidates, reconsider the segmentation of the task and/or selection of action definition candidates, where the updated database 17 improves the robot’s capability of autonomously executing a task, as described in ¶0083-0085). Specifically, when the operator adds information to database 17 for improved execution of a task performed by a particular (“first”) robot, all other (“second”) robots that access the same database inherently update their respective “control models” based on the information added by the operator.
