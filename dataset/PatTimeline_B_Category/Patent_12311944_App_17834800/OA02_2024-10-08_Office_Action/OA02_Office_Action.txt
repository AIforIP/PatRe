Response to Arguments
Applicant’s arguments with respect to 35 U.S.C. 103 have been considered but are not persuasive.
Applicant argues that, regarding the limitations added by amendment, “None of the cited references teach or suggest these features. Takeya discloses a method involving a drive unit (driving force control unit) and a braking unit (braking force control unit) for controlling the movement of a vehicle (110020, 0046). Takeya describes balancing the driving and braking forces to maintain constant speed (10046). However, Takeya does not teach or suggest braking by reducing the drive torque. Instead, it discusses controlling the driving force while keeping the braking force constant (10052-53). Additionally, Takeya does not teach or suggest braking by reducing the drive torque. Instead, it discusses controlling the driving force while keeping the braking force constant (10052-53).
Das does not solve the deficiencies of Takeya, as Das discloses braking a vehicle by reducing the drive torque, specifically reducing creep torque until the vehicle comes to a standstill (paragraph 10022). Neither Das nor Takeya teach or suggest wherein the braking torque is reduced with a smaller torque reduction gradient than the restraining drive torque during the braking of the vehicle. This specific control over the reduction gradients is a distinguishing feature that provides a novel and non-obvious improvement over the prior art. As now recited in the amended claims, the specific control of torque reduction gradients during braking (wherein the braking torque is reduced with a smaller torque reduction gradient than the restraining drive torque) provides an improved technical advantage, namely more refined and smooth braking (see 0027 of the present Specification), which is not suggested or taught by the combination of Takeya and Das.”
Examiner respectfully disagrees. In particular, although Takeya and Das do not teach explicitly teach the limitations, Inoue does teach the limitations, as detailed below.
In particular, Inoue teaches reducing the restraining torque by reducing the braking torque and the restraining drive torque, wherein the braking torque is reduced with a smaller torque reduction gradient than the restraining drive torque during the braking of the vehicle. See paragraphs [0188] – [0196] and Fig. 13B, where the driving force and braking force are both reduced starting at time t1, but the braking force is reduced more gradually in comparison to the acceleration force. Paragraph [0192] summarizes: “In addition, the vehicle braking force is reduced gradually toward the vehicle driving force corresponding to the amount of braking operation performed by the driver at a braking force reduction rate which is always lower than the driving force reduction rate. In other words, the vehicle braking force is reduced gently in such a manner that the amount of decrease in the vehicle braking force is always smaller than the amount of decrease in the vehicle driving force.”

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(d):
(d) REFERENCE IN DEPENDENT FORMS.—Subject to subsection (e), a claim in dependent form shall contain a reference to a claim previously set forth and then specify a further limitation of the subject matter claimed. A claim in dependent form shall be construed to incorporate by reference all the limitations of the claim to which it refers.
The following is a quotation of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, fourth paragraph:
Subject to the following paragraph [i.e., the fifth paragraph of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112], a claim in dependent form shall contain a reference to a claim previously set forth and then specify a further limitation of the subject matter claimed. A claim in dependent form shall be construed to incorporate by reference all the limitations of the claim to which it refers.
Claims 8 and 18 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(d) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, 4th paragraph, as being of improper dependent form for failing to further limit the subject matter of the claim upon which it depends, or for failing to include all the limitations of the claim upon which it depends.  Applicant may cancel the claim(s), amend the claim(s) to place the claim(s) in proper dependent form, rewrite the claim(s) in independent form, or present a sufficient showing that the dependent claim(s) complies with the statutory requirements.
Claim 1 recites “reducing the restraining torque by reducing the braking torque and the restraining drive torque, wherein the braking torque is reduced with a smaller torque reduction gradient than the restraining drive torque during the braking of the vehicle.” Independent claim 11, from which claim 18 (ultimately) depends, recites a similar limitation.
Claim 8 recites “wherein reducing the restraining torque comprises reducing the braking torque with a lesser torque reduction gradient than the restraining drive torque.” The limitation here is almost identical to that recited in claim 1 above, and therefore claim 8 does not further limit the subject matter of claim 1.
Claim 18 recites “wherein the braking unit is configured to reduce the restraining torque by one of: reducing the braking torque with a lesser torque reduction gradient than the restraining drive torque, or reducing the braking torque after reducing the restraining drive torque.” The claim is structured such that a limitation functionally identical to the independent claim (“reducing the braking torque with a lesser torque reduction gradient than the restraining drive torque”) will read on claim 18, and therefore does not further limit the subject matter of claim 11.

Claims 10 and 19 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Takeya in view of Inoue as applied to claims 1 and 11 above, and further in view of US 20170043778 by Kelly (hereinafter “Inoue”).
Regarding claim 10, neither Takeya nor Inoue explicitly teach wherein generating the restraining torque comprises generating the braking torque before generating the restraining drive torque.
However, Kelly teaches wherein generating the restraining torque comprises generating the braking torque before generating the restraining drive torque. (See again paragraph [0059], where “upon receipt of the vehicle occupant-initiated brake command signal…the positive drive torque may be ramped out, i.e., it may be reduced gradually over time”, eventually to the “residual drive torque” described in paragraph [0060]. Then in paragraphs [0061] – [0063], the brakes are released in “at least an amount sufficient to indicate a desire to resume movement or progress of the vehicle” (paragraph [0061]) and the system subsequently generates a drive torque (reading on generating the braking torque before generating the restraining drive torque), where the drive torque is dependent upon factors including “the amount or magnitude of the retarding torque being applied to one or more wheels of the vehicle that must be overcome or counteracted to propel the vehicle” (paragraph [0063]), indicating that the drive and retarding torque are still working in opposition and therefore reading on generating the restraining torque.)
It would have been prima facie obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have modified the speed maintaining system of Takeya, modified by the gradual stopping system of Inoue, with the automatic speed control system of Kelly with a reasonable expectation of success. Doing so allows the system to control the system carefully in accordance with a set speed, which is useful in a low-speed situation.
Claim 19 has similar limitations to claim 10 above, and is therefore rejected using a similar rationale.
Claims 6 and 16 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Takeya in view of Inoue as applied to claims 1 and 11 above, and further in view of US 20170297575 by Das (hereinafter “Das”).
Regarding claim 6, Takeya teaches a method further comprising accelerating the vehicle using an acceleration drive torque for a driving maneuver to position the vehicle in a predefined holding position (See paragraph [0002] where Takeya intends to solve the problem of automatically and precisely parking a vehicle. Other paragraphs, such as [0024] and [0057], also discuss parking the vehicle or automatically parking the vehicle, reading on a driving maneuver to position the vehicle in a predefined holding position. Further, as shown in at least Fig. 3, the vehicle can accelerate using increasing driving force (e.g. times t10 – t13), reading on comprising accelerating the vehicle using an acceleration drive torque.)
Takeya does not explicitly teach maneuvering the vehicle at a speed lower than 10 km/h. Although paragraphs [0002], [0024], [0057], etc. indicate Takeya intends to solve controlling the vehicle to park automatically, presumably at a low speed, Takeya does not explicitly describe the speeds involved.
However, Das teaches a method further comprising accelerating the vehicle using an acceleration drive torque for a driving maneuver to position the vehicle in a predefined holding position at a speed lower than 10 km/h. (See for example paragraph [0014] where a “stopping operation of the motor vehicle is underway” and “the longitudinal speed of the vehicle is below approximately 1 km/h”. Further, see paragraph [0032], where “the stopping operation is determined only when the absolute value of a longitudinal speed of the motor vehicle lies below a speed threshold value. Preferably, this threshold value lies in a range of approximately 0 km/h to approximately 2 km/h, for example, 1 km/h.” Either the 0, 1, or 2 km/h stopping operation can read on at a speed lower than 10 km/h.)
It would have been prima facie obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have modified the speed maintaining system of Takeya, modified by the drive force lowering system of Inoue, with the gradual stopping system of Das with a reasonable expectation of success. Doing so allows the system to more gradually come to a stop, allowing a finer control of the speed and jolt of the vehicle, as discussed by Das in paragraph [0005] as motivation for the invention: “It is difficult to entirely avoid the jolt upon stopping of a motor vehicle as a result of an automated intervention in a brake system of the motor vehicle whereby the jolt is no longer perceived by the occupants of the vehicle. This is due primarily to the fact that the brake pressure applied by the brake system must be extremely precisely metered. If the brake pressure is slightly too high at the stopping instant, i.e., during the transition from kinetic friction to static friction at the vehicle brakes, the jolt upon stopping is still perceptible. If the braking torque generated by the brake pressure is slightly lower, than a creep torque provided by the motor vehicle—for example a vehicle equipped with an automatic transmission, the motor vehicle does not stop.”
Claim 16 has similar limitations to claim 6 above, and is therefore rejected using a similar rationale.
