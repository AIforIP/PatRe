Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1 & 5 - 13 are allowed.
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance: With respect to Independent Claim 1, the claim recites the following limitations:
determine a desired torque for operating the vehicle at a current vehicle operating condition;
determine an offset wheel speed parameter; obtain a signal indicative of a wheel speed for the vehicle;
determine a wheel slip limit for the at least one wheel of the vehicle based on the offset wheel speed parameter when the wheel speed is less than a threshold vehicle speed limit;
wherein the wheel speed limit comprises an upper wheel speed limit and a lower wheel speed limit; transmit a control signal indicative of the desired torque to the motion support system for use in controlling the at least one actuator to apply the torque to the at least one wheel of the vehicle;
transmit only the upper wheel speed limit to the motion support system when the desired torque is greater than zero; transmit only the lower wheel speed limit to the motion support system when the desired torque is less than zero;
No single prior art reference has been found to anticipate the above limitations, nor any combination of references to render them obvious, when read in view of the remaining limitations of the claim. Specifically, while references have been found to anticipate the determination and transmission of wheel speed limits based on vehicle operational characteristics, and the exclusive transmission of such upper and lower wheel speed limits based on the sign of the desired torque as recited by the present claimed invention, no reference has been found to anticipate the incorporation of an offset wheel speed parameter as part of the control command when the vehicle is specifically operating below a threshold vehicle speed limit.
In particular, references Ford (US 2021/0370779 A1) and Gauthier (US 9,463,697 B1) are the closest prior art found, with Ford reciting the control of a vehicle based on determined torque demand request values and maximum/minimum speed limits for the respective wheels of the vehicle, and Gauthier reciting the determination of operating torque and actuator rotational speed limits based on the vehicle driveline state and other inputs, as well as exclusive transmission of wheel speed limits based on the sign of the vehicle tractive force command.
More specifically, Ford recites a traction control system for a vehicle, including the determination of torque demand request values and maximum/minimum wheel speed limit values, which are then transmitted to specific wheel control devices, which then implement control of the vehicle wheel(s) based on the transmitted values by operating vehicle wheel actuators. Ford however appears to be silent regarding wherein the control signal includes an offset wheel speed parameter in the specific condition where the wheel speed of the vehicle is less than a threshold vehicle speed limit as recited by the present claimed invention, much less a determination of a driveline state for a vehicle or the determination of an operating torque and rotational speed limit of vehicle wheel(s) based on the driveline state as further recited by Independent Claim 10 of the present claimed invention.
Gauthier recites a traction control system for a vehicle, including the transmission of maximum or minimum wheel speeds to the traction controllers of a vehicle based on the motor torque demand being positive or negative. As depicted in Figure 8, below, the transmission of the maximum or minimum wheel speed is an exclusive transmission based on the torque demand being positive or negative, as the “switches” of the figure only allow the relevant limit to pass depending on the true or false nature of the check on if the tractive force command is greater than zero. Gauthier further recites wherein maximum and minimum motor speeds are computed based on a plurality of factors, including the current vehicle transmission ratio [i.e. driveline state] as additionally recited by Independent Claim 10. Gauthier however appears to be silent regarding wherein the control signal includes an offset wheel speed parameter in the specific condition where the wheel speed of the vehicle is less than a threshold vehicle speed limit as recited by the present claimed invention.

While Inaguma (JP 2651937 B2) was relied upon in the previous office action(s) to reject [now cancelled] Dependent Claim 4, which recites similar limitations to “determine a wheel slip limit for the at least one wheel of the vehicle based on the offset wheel speed parameter when the wheel speed is less than a threshold vehicle speed limit” as recited by Independent Claim 1, applicant arguments filed 8/21/24 are persuasive. Particularly, Applicant argues in Pages 13 & 14 of the aforementioned 8/21/2024 remarks that while Inaguma recites the control of the slip speed when at low vehicle speed values using a preset reference value, which was construed by the Examiner to be a recitation of an “offset wheel speed parameter,” this cannot be construed as such, as in the present claimed invention the offset wheel speed is a correction coefficient [an exemplary embodiment of such a calculation being described in Page 4 of the specification as originally filed as being based on a mapping between desired torque and a tire model] applied to the control signal when the wheel speed for the vehicle is less than a threshold vehicle speed limit. No prior art reference has been found to anticipate such limitations, either alone or in view of the remaining limitations of the claim.
Thus, Independent Claim 1 is found to be allowable over prior art. Independent Claims 10 & 13, while not identical, recite substantially similar limitations, and are similarly found to be allowable over prior art. Dependent Claims 5 – 9, 11, & 12 are similarly found to be allowable based on their dependence on an allowable base claim, as well as the limitations recited within.
With respect to the previously presented rejection(s) under 35 USC 101 as set forth in the non-final office action dated 4/22/24, the rejection(s) have been obviated by explicitly reciting the control of at least one actuator of the vehicle to generate the desired torque, which is a practical application of the abstract idea, and thus renders Independent Claim 1 patent-eligible. Independent Claims 10 & 13, while not identical, recite substantially similar limitations, and are similarly found to be patent-eligible. Dependent Claims 5 – 9, 11, & 12 are similarly found to be allowable based on their dependence on an patent-eligible base claim, as well as the limitations recited within.
The filing of a proper terminal disclaimer dated 1/22/2025 has obviated the double patenting rejection previously presented in the non-final office action dated 11/22/2024, and thus the rejection has been withdrawn.

Conclusion
The following prior art made of record but not relied upon is considered pertinent to the Applicant’s disclosure:
Ford (US 2021/0370779 A1): Ford recites a vehicle traction control system for a vehicle, including the determination of maximum wheel velocity based on slip ratio limit and vehicle velocity values acquired. A torque demand for the vehicle is acquired from the input of a user operating the vehicle, and the torque demand is transmitted to a controller for the wheel(s) of a vehicle in order to operate the wheel(s) of the vehicle based on the requested torque, without operating the wheel above the maximum wheel speed determined.
Suhre (US 6,542,806 B1): Suhre recites a slip ratio upper limit for a vehicle being determined as a function of the tractive force command input to the vehicle, which may be received from an accelerator pedal of the vehicle. Based on the determined slip ratio upper limit, the tractive force command may be dynamically changed to be a fractional part of the tractive force request, to ensure excessive slippage does not occur.
Gauthier (US 9,463,697 B1): Gauthier recites a traction control system for a vehicle, including the transmission of maximum or minimum wheel speeds to the traction controllers of a vehicle based on the motor torque demand being positive or negative. As depicted in at least Figure 8, above, the transmission of the maximum or minimum wheel speed is an exclusive transmission based on the torque demand being positive or negative, as the “switches” of the figure only allow the relevant limit to pass depending on the true or false nature of the check on if the tractive force command is greater than zero. Gauthier further recites wherein maximum and minimum motor speeds are computed based on a plurality of factors, including the current vehicle transmission ratio [i.e. driveline state].
Inaguma (JP 2651937 B2): Iagnuma recites a vehicle control system including a determination and control of the slip ratio of the vehicle. When at low vehicle speeds, Iagnuma recites wherein the slip ratio is highly sensitive to small measurement error, reducing the accuracy of the slip ratio obtained. Therefore, when at low speeds, the difference between vehicle wheel speed and vehicle speed is controlled to a constant target.
Okubo (US 8,825,333 B2): Okubo recites a vehicle wheel slip control system, including the use of slip ratio threshold value(s) to effectuate vehicle control. The slip level is limited to fall within a predetermined range of values before braking and driving force interventions are required, limiting the allowable range of wheel slip.
Owen (US 10,029,677 B2): Owen recites the adjustment of vehicle subsystem responses based on an estimated friction level between the vehicle tires and road. In one such example, friction level between vehicle and road is estimated based on a measurement of surface rolling resistance. Different driving modes for the vehicle may be engaged based on the determined level of friction.
Kuras (US 7,192,374 B2): Kuras recites a method of controlling the transmission of a vehicle including setting upper and lower speed limits on the motor output speed that are set based on the direction of the desired torque from the motor. At least Column 9 Lines 42 – 58 recite a fixed lower [and/or upper] speed limit when service brakes are used, requiring only the upper [and/or lower] speed limit to be computed. This computation takes place as in at least Column 6 Line 41 – Column 7 Line 49 wherein upper [and/or] lower speed limits are continuously calculated based on the torque of the motor, and depending on if the motor is operating in the forward (i.e. positive) or reverse (i.e. negative) direction. Taken in combination with the fixed limit while brakes are operable, above, only one limit is computed [and therefore sent] at a time, depending on the operation direction of the motor.
Osinenko (NPL: Optimal slip control for tractors with feedback of drive torque): Osinenko recites a slip control system for a vehicle, including the optimization of slip with respect to a set point value. A plurality of characteristics are measured at the vehicle, including drive torque, wheel speed, loading characteristics, and the like. Limits of the engine output torque are taken into account in the simulation of vehicle control.
Watanabe (US 6,089,681 A): Watanabe recites a system for controlling individual wheel speeds, including computing and setting upper and lower speed limits, the upper being correlated to when the vehicle accelerates and the lower being correlated to when the vehicle decelerates. Such limits are set for antiskid control of a vehicle, and the speed limits are computed based on the vehicle speed.
Jalali (NPL: Development of a Fuzzy Slip Control System for Electric Vehicles with In-wheel Motors): Jalali recites a slip control system for a vehicle, and the adjustment thereof, including the determination of an error between an actual tire slip with a desired slip limit for the vehicle. Jalali further recites wherein the torque output at each wheel of the vehicle is limited by the respective slip controllers of the wheels to a maximum torque limit.
Seiki (JPH 11310144 A): Seiki recites a vehicle control system including a road surface condition judging means. Based on such road surface condition, a limit of tire slip angle is determined as a function of the determined conditions, a maximum tire slip angle for the vehicle, and tire angle limiting coefficient(s).
Damrongrit (NPL: Active Driveline Torque-Management Systems): Damrongrit recites an electronic stability control system for vehicles, including the control of drive torque on individual wheels of the vehicle in order to control the vehicle slip at the tires. The control algorithm for the vehicle is different at low speeds, in order to improve vehicle control specifically in that speed region. The control system takes into account driveline states when converting the desired action to the implementation at vehicle wheels.
Gao (US 9,637,001 B2): Gao recites a method for controlling at least one wheel of a vehicle based on an estimated longitudinal velocity of the vehicle. This includes a determination of the best speed at which to operate the wheels of the vehicle to ensure the vehicle meets specific slip characteristics. Control of the vehicle wheels is achieved by determination of an optimal torque command for each of the vehicle wheels.
Kojima (JP 5768865 B2): Kojima recites a vehicle control system, including a calculation of a vehicle wheel rotational speed limit [either the maximum or minimum limit] to be used to restrict vehicle wheel speed during control. The wheel rotational speed limit may be determined with reference to a difference between left and right rotational wheel speeds at a given point in time.
Bird (US 9,610,954 B2): Bird recites a vehicle control system for preventing excessive wheel slip, including the determination of the speed of the vehicle. The TC [traction control] system is recited as being inactive when below a threshold speed value, said speed value corresponding to a speed at which vehicle or wheel speed may be accurately measured, or a speed value below which the TC function is able to meaningfully intervene.
Kerber (US 10,328,914 B2): Kerber recites a method for setting a slip threshold of a motor vehicle, including setting wheel-specific slip values derived from wheel-specific slip signals. This may include performing the method at low vehicle velocities, where the deviation in slip level between different wheels is at a maximum.
