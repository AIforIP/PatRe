Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed 5/24/23 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive for at least the reasons set forth below.
Applicant alleges that the combination of Ford and Suhre do not teach each and every aspect of the present claimed invention, and further alleges that the present claimed invention as amended is a meaningful improvement over the hypothetical combination. While Examiner agrees that the invention described in the arguments presented would be an improvement over the combination, Examiner respectfully disagrees that the invention as described in applicant’s arguments is in fact claimed for at least the following reasons, responsive to applicant’s arguments below, and as a result asserts that the combination of Ford and Suhre does in fact teach each and every aspect of the invention as claimed. Applicant argues:
As disclosed by the present application, by using the vehicle motion management system, (i.e. the upper layer control module) to determine if an upper of lower speed limit should be transmitted to the motion support system (i.e. the lower layer control module), will substantially reduce the data bandwidth in the data sent between these modules. Thus, sending only the upper limit or only or lower limit, will, contrary to the Examiner's opinion, meaningfully affect the operation of the system, as the response time will be even further reduced (and therefore improved).
While Examiner agrees that the invention described in the arguments presented would present an improvement, Examiner respectfully disagrees with the assertation above in the interpretation of the invention as claimed. The amended claims filed 5/24/2023 recite, in part in Claim 1, used as a representative claim for the purposes of argument:
…transmit the upper wheel speed limit to the motion support system when the desired torque is above zero; and transmit the lower wheel speed limit to the motion support system when the desired torque is below zero.
Applicant asserts in their arguments that the claimed invention is “sending only the upper limit or only or lower limit” The invention as claimed does not recite in the independent claim sending [transmitting] only the upper limit or lower limit in response to the desired torque being above or below zero, only that the limits are sent [transmitted] in response to the desired torque being below or above zero. Sending both an upper and lower speed limit in response to a determination of the desired torque being, for example, above zero therefore still fulfills the criterion of:
…transmit the upper wheel speed limit to the motion support system when the desired torque is above zero
as the upper wheel speed limit is still transmitted in response to the desired torque being above zero, sending a lower speed limit as well does not interfere with or otherwise violate the language of the invention as presently claimed. Similarly, the second condition of:
…transmit the lower wheel speed limit to the motion support system when the desired torque is below zero.
is fulfilled by the lower speed limit being sent as part of the set of speed limits, as the lower speed limit is still sent, and the upper limit also being sent does not interfere with this claim language.
Additionally, by letting the vehicle motion management system discriminate between a torque level above or below zero, and transmitting a specific wheel speed limit based on this determination, will beneficially enable a simplified motion support system to be employed. Conversely, in the system according to Ford (whether or not combined with Suhre), the control devices 400 at the wheels must be programmed to enable positive and negative torque to be distinguished, and the control devices 400 use different wheel speed limits based on this determination. Turning back to the present case, the vehicle motion management system of claim 1 is connectable to a motion support system that uses the wheel speed limit the motion support system is instructed to use, such that the motion support system does not need to make any active decisions.
While Examiner agrees with the assertation above, that discriminating between torque requests and limiting the speed limit sent based on such would result in an improvement over the combination of Ford and Suhre; for at least the reasons as set forth above, the language of the present claimed invention does not appear to accurately reflect the arguments provided in the view of the Examiner, and thus the combination of Ford and Suhre appear to continue to read on the amended limitations of the present claimed invention as set forth below.
Moreover, the control unit 102 according to Ford is not disclosed as being able to transmit different wheel speed limits based on a positive torque (i.e., torque above zero) or negative torque (i.e., torque below zero), according to the final two clauses of amended claim 1.
As set forth above, the claim language as amended does not require exclusivity in the transmission of wheel speed limit based on the desired direction of torque, and the transmission of speed limit as in Ford anticipates the present claimed invention, as the claimed language merely requires that the required limit is sent without being sent exclusively.
Independent claims 10 & 13 have been amended to recite substantially similar limitations as Independent Claim 1, and therefore arguments provided on at least Page 15 of applicant’s response dated 5/24/2023 are not persuasive for at least the reasons presented above.
Further arguments presented involving dependent claims 4 – 9 & 11 – 12 merely allege patentability by virtue of their dependence on an amended base claim, and remain rejected by virtue of at least the arguments set forth above and the rejections of each dependent claim under 35 USC § 103 as set forth below.

Prior art made of record but not relied upon:
Kuras (US 7,192,374 B2): Kuras recites a method of controlling the transmission of a vehicle including setting upper and lower speed limits on the motor output speed that are set based on the direction of the desired torque from the motor. At least Figure 5 recited the process for determining the upper and lower limits, and applying them as necessary. At least Column 7 Lines 8 – 40 disclose … At least Column 9 Lines 42 – 58 recite a fixed lower [and/or upper] speed limit when service brakes are used, requiring only the upper [and/or lower] speed limit to be computed. This computation takes place as in at least Column 6 Line 41 – Column 7 Line 49 wherein upper [and/or] lower speed limits are continuously calculated based on the torque of the motor, and depending on if the motor is operating in the forward (i.e. positive) or reverse (i.e. negative) direction. Taken in combination with the fixed limit while brakes are operable, above, only one limit is computed [and therefore sent] at a time, depending on the operation direction of the motor.
Watanabe (US 6,089,681 A): Watanabe recites a system for controlling individual wheel speeds, including computing and setting upper and lower speed limits, the upper being correlated to when the vehicle accelerates and the lower being correlated to when the vehicle decelerates. Such limits are set for antiskid control of a vehicle, and the speed limits are computed based on the vehicle speed.
