Election/Restrictions
Claims 8-15 allowable. The restriction requirement between Invention Groups II (Claims 8-15) and III (claims 16-20), as set forth in the Office action mailed on 2/16/2024, has been reconsidered in view of the allowability of claims to the elected invention pursuant to MPEP § 821.04(a). The restriction requirement is hereby withdrawn as to any claim that requires all the limitations of an allowable claim. Specifically, the restriction requirement of 2/16/2024 is partially withdrawn.  Claims 16-20, directed to Invention Group III are no longer withdrawn from consideration because the claim(s) requires all the limitations of an allowable claim. However, claims 1-7, directed to Invention Group I remain withdrawn from consideration because they do not all require all the limitations of an allowable claim. As discussed with Applicant’s Representative in the interview on 1/14/2025, Claims 1-7 were noted to only require a shaped fuel cell having a through-void or an edge cavity with intended usage/functional capabilities or adaptations with respect to unclaimed structure(s) (e.g. an aircraft, a fuel cell area, and aircraft structures that translate). Without the combination of elements as recited in claims 8 and 16, any fuel cell comprising a through-void or edge cavity would appear to read upon the claim as they could be arranged to provide the same intended use/functional capability or adaptation. Examiner notes that with respect to fuel cells having a through-hole, US 2943815; FR 926522; and GB 558459 appear to contain such features, while US 2015/0136906, US 2017/0313434, US 4345698, US 2013/0214095, EP 3296214, EP 3366592, and GB 640293 appear to contain a fuel cell with an edge cavity. In view of this discussion, it was agreed to cancel claims 1-7 as noted above.
In view of the above noted withdrawal of the restriction requirement, applicant is advised that if any claim presented in a divisional application is anticipated by, or includes all the limitations of, a claim that is allowable in the present application, such claim may be subject to provisional statutory and/or nonstatutory double patenting rejections over the claims of the instant application.
Once a restriction requirement is withdrawn, the provisions of 35 U.S.C. 121 are no longer applicable. See In re Ziegler, 443 F.2d 1211, 1215, 170 USPQ 129, 131-32 (CCPA 1971). See also MPEP § 804.01.
Reasons for Allowance
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
First, it is noted that the term “fuel cell” in the present application is not being utilized in the modern context as defined by the Cambridge Aerospace Dictionary as a “Device which converts chemical energy directly into electricity; differs from storage battery in that reactants are supplied at rate determined by electrical load.” but is instead being utilized to define a structure configured to hold a liquid or gaseous fuel, which is colloquially referred to as a “fuel cell” in the earlier prior art (- See “Aircraft” section which recites “Bladder tanks, or fuel cells”) and depicted the following figure ():

The aforementioned distinction has been clarified by the present amendment reciting “a fuel cell configured to contain a liquid or gaseous fuel” such that the previous rejection under 35 USC 112(a) for enablement is no longer valid.
The prior art (see references cited above and on the attached PTO-892) contain a plurality of shaped fuel cells configured to contain a liquid or gaseous fuel while additionally comprising a through-void or edge cavity, however, the prior art is completely silent on the combination of such fuel cells with an aircraft having structures which translate into a region of the aircraft housing such fuel cells and into the location of the through-void or edge cavity, during a drop impact of the aircraft. While several of the prior art references (e.g. US 2017/0313434) might allow for the translation of an aircraft strut or rotor mast into the region of an edge cavity/through-void during a drop impact, the potential for an event to occur does not mean that it will or that it was designed in that particular manner. As this critical structural relationship had no teaching, suggestion, or motivation outlined in any of the prior art, it appears as though impermissible hindsight of the present application would be required to modify the prior art references to achieve the claimed invention.
