Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1-4 and 6 are allowed.
Regarding claim 1, Wyman teaches an aircraft sanitization (NOTE: this is a recitation of intended use, and so long as the prior art structure reads on the instant claimed structure, this limitation would be met because the same structure would be capable of the same function) device comprising:
a curved enclosure (Fig. 7: door 732) operatively coupled to a first surface of an enclosed area (Fig. 1: door 102), wherein at a first position the curved enclosure at least partially encloses a handle affixed to the first surface (Fig. 8), and wherein the handle enables access to the enclosed area (Fig. 1: door handle 106), the curved enclosure further comprises:
an inner surface facing the first surface at the first position of the curved enclosure (Fig. 7, 8: inner surface of door 732); and
an outer surface facing away from the first surface at the first position (Fig. 7, 8: outer surface of door 732;
a set of Ultraviolet (UV) Light Emitting Diodes (LEDs), wherein the set of UV LEDs is configured to sanitize the handle (par. 67: As described in more detail herein, in various implementations, the door handle sanitizer mechanism of sanitation system 600 sanitizes the door handle 706 using one or more UV lights. As shown, sanitation system 700 includes a UV light 726 that emits a UVC light toward door handle 706. Sanitation system 700 also includes a second UV light (not shown) that emits a UVC light toward door handle 706);
at least one locking mechanism, wherein the at least one locking mechanism is configured to: engage with the first surface in the closed state; and disengage with the first surface in the open state (par. 43: In various implementations, sanitation system 100 includes a lock mechanism 128 for locking and unlocking door 102); and
a controller (par. 45: In various implementations, sanitizer system 100 includes a controller system 134 for controlling the operations of sanitizer system 100 ) communicatively coupled to each of the set of UV LEDs, the at least one switch, and the at least one locking mechanism (par. 43: In some implementations, sanitation system 100 may lock door 102 when closed and then unlock door 102 after sanitation system 100 sanitizes the hand of the user), wherein the controller is configured
to activate the set of UV LEDs (par. 69: Once doors 730 and 732 close, the UV lights turn on in order to sanitize door handle 706. Because doors 730 and 732 are shut, UV light is unable to escape thereby protecting the eye's of the user. While implementations of sanitation system 700 are described in the context of two doors).
But does not teach wherein the set of UV LEDs are affixed to the inner surface.
However, in the absence of any teaching to the contrary, the location of the UV lights on the device does not affect operation since UV light would still be capable of reaching the door handle. Absent a showing of significance or unexpected results, the claimed locations of the components are prima facie obvious and do not modify the operation of the invention and further, do not add patentable significance. The Manual of Patent Examining Procedures discloses that in In re Japikse, 181 F.2d 1019, 86 USPQ 70(CCPA 1950), a mere rearrangement of parts for a design change has no patentable significance unless a new and unexpected result is produced.
Furthermore, Marshall teaches a curved structure containing a UV light on its inner surface for sterilizing a door handle attached to a first surface (abstract: A sterilization system is configured to emit energy, such as UV radiation, for sterilizing a surface, such as a door handle; Fig. 1: sanitizing light element 14).
It would be obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to modify Wyman to have a set of UV LEDs affixed to inner surface of the curved enclosure, as taught by Marshall, as an alternative means of placing the UV LEDs so that they are in a position to sterilize a handle.
Wyman modified by Marshall does not teach
at least one switch placed on at least one of the first surface and a second surface of the enclosed area, wherein each of the at least one switch is activated in a closed state of the enclosed area and each of the at least one switch is deactivated in an open state of the enclosed area,
wherein the controller is communicatively coupled to the at least one switch, and the at least one locking mechanism and wherein the controller is configured to activate the set of UV LEDs, when each of the at least one switch is activated and the locking mechanism engages the first surface in the closed state
Mackin teaches a UV sterilization system for a foldable tray table that only operates when the tray table is in a folded position (abstract: A system (100) for sanitizing a tray table (102) may include a sanitation assembly (108) operatively coupled to the tray table (102)… The sanitation control unit (114) operates the UV light source (112) to emit the UV light onto the tray table (102) when the tray table (102) is secured in an upright position, and prevents the UV light source (112) from emitting the UV light when the tray table (102) is not secured in the upright position). Mackin teaches a lock mechanism that is paired with a rotation switch so that unlocking the mechanism would deactivate the UV lights (par. 59: Notably, some tray tables may include a rotatable latch. In connection with such trays, the position of the latch may be sensed. Rotation of the latch out of a locked position may automatically deactivate the UV light source). Thus, Mackin implicitly teaches a switch located on the tray table (alternately interpreted as either the first or second surface) since the rotation of the latch is sensed. The state of the switch when detecting a locked position (a closed state of the tray table, since it is enclosing the space between it and the frame) is interpreted be activated, and the state of the switch when detecting an unlocked position (an open state of the tray table) is interpreted to be deactivated. Furthermore, Mackin teaches wherein a controller is configured to detect whether or not the tray table is in a locked state by being connected to one or more sensors, in order to activate the set of UV LEDs only when the tray table is in a locked state (par. 30: In response to the sanitation control unit 114 detecting that the tray table 102 is securely stowed in the upright position, such as through signals detected by the tray table position sensor 116, the sanitation control unit 114 may transition to a standby mode, in which a sanitation cycle may be activated). Under the broadest reasonable interpretation, the latch sensor and thus the lock mechanism would be connected to the controller in order its signal to be detected for determining whether or not to activate the UV LEDs.
Therefore, it would be obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to modify the first or second surface of Wyman modified by Marshall to have a switch, wherein each of the at least one switch is activated in a closed state of the enclosed area and each of the at least one switch is deactivated in an open state of the enclosed area, and wherein the controller is communicatively coupled to the at least one switch, and by extension, the at least one locking mechanism, and wherein the controller is configured to activate the set of UV LEDs, when each of the at least one switch is activated and the locking mechanism engages the first surface in the closed state, in order to automate UV sterilization to happen when the device is not in use.
Wyman modified by Marshall and Mackin teaches comprising a rotating mechanism operatively coupled to the curved enclosure and communicatively coupled to the controller (par. 68: For example, when the user grasps door handle 706, the UV lights are turned off. After the user uses and subsequently releases door handle 706, doors 730 and 732 close; NOTE: since the controller controls the operations of the device, implicitly it must also control the door 732 in order for the closing of the door to be automated) but does not teach wherein the controller is further configured to:
instruct the rotating mechanism to move the curved enclosure to the first position, when each of the at least one switch is activated and the locking mechanism engages the first surface in the closed state; and
instruct the rotating mechanism to move the curved enclosure to one of a second position and  at least one intermediate position, when at least one of the at least one switch is deactivated and the locking mechanism disengages the first surface.
This is because if the second or intermediate position involved the doors further enclosing the door handle compared to an initial position, it would be counter-intuitive to close the curved enclosure right after the first surface is unlocked and opened, since the latter would require a hand to touch the handle, and the hand would be in the way of the curved enclosure at that moment. If the second or intermediate position involved disclosing the door handle compared to an initial position, there would be no need to do so if the user was already able to grasp the door handle in order to unlock it.
The closest prior art is Pasch (US 20120176241 A1), which teaches a similar rotatable enclosure for sterilizing a door handle (Fig. 7a-7b) and teaches a method for sterilizing the door handle wherein the rotation of the enclosure is timed with the locking of the door (Fig. 3). However, Pasch teaches wherein the enclosure is closed when the door is closed, and wherein the enclosure opens after a sterilization cycle while the door is still closed. Thus, Pasch does not teach moving the enclosure mechanism based on unlocking the door.
Claims 2-4 and 6 are allowed as dependent on claim 1.
