Rejection Under 35 U.S.C. 102
With regards to the rejection of Claims 1 and 5-10 under  35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as anticipated by WIPO Publication No. WO 2000/029287 to Syta et al. (“Syta”), Applicant’s remarks have been fully considered, but they are not persuasive.  In particular, Applicant argues that Syta fails to disclose or suggest “an attachment mechanism attached to the upper frame structure and configured to engage a main rotor transmission of the helicopter to suspend therefrom” (emphasis added)(see Applicant’s Response at 9-10).
As an initial matter, it is acknowledged that the disclosed structure of the present application is structurally different from what is disclosed in Syta.  However, the language of current Claim 1 employs broad terms such as “attached” and “configured to engage,” which encompasses direct or indirect attachments, and direct or indirect engagement. Additionally, the preamble of Claim 1 is directed towards “a tank assembly” and does not require the presence of a helicopter or a main rotor transmission of the helicopter.
When the tank of Syta is mounted and attached to a helicopter as intended by Syta, the supports 1 and connecting pieces 2 are attached to the upper panel 3 (which can be interpreted as corresponding to the recited upper fame structure), and the supports 1 and connecting pieces are operable to engage with the rest of the helicopter, including the main rotor transmission of the helicopter.  Furthermore, it is known in the art that helicopters typically includes a vertical main rotor drive shaft (which can be interpreted as a component of the main rotor transmission), and all lifting forces are transmitted through the main rotor drive shaft to support all mass of the helicopter and all the mass of the tank in Syta when attached and in flight.  As such, the tank of Syta is operable to be engaged with the main rotor drive shaft of the helicopter at least during flight such that the main rotor drive shaft supports the mass of the tank of Syta.
With specific regards to Claim 7, Applicant separately argues that “the connecting pieces 2 of Syta are located at forward and rearward end portions of the upper panel 3, and clearly not in the central portion of the upper panel” (emphasis in original)(see Applicant’s Response at 10).  Claim 7 does not define any structural boundaries delimiting where the forward and rearward end portions begin and end.  As such, Claim 7 can broadly be interpreted to require the attachment mechanism to be attached to any center portion of the upper frame. Moreover, In addition to connecting pieces 2, the Office Action in paragraph 15 also identifies supports 1 as being part of the attachment mechanism.  As shown in Annotated FIG. 1 of Syta, both the supports 1 and the connecting pieces 2 can be interpreted as being within a center portion of the upper panel 3, since neither of these components are positioned at the frontmost or rearmost edges of the upper panel 3.

The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
The factual inquiries for establishing a background for determining obviousness under 35 U.S.C. 103 are summarized as follows:
1. Determining the scope and contents of the prior art.
2. Ascertaining the differences between the prior art and the claims at issue.
3. Resolving the level of ordinary skill in the pertinent art.
4. Considering objective evidence present in the application indicating obviousness or non-obviousness.

Regarding Claims 11 and 20, Syta discloses a tank assembly (water tank for a helicopter; see e.g., page 1, lines 3-5 and FIG. 1) comprising:
a retractable tank for storing liquid or other substances, the retractable tank comprising an upper frame structure (the water tank includes an upper panel 3, a lower panel 4, and side walls 5 positioned therebetween, the sidewalls 5 can be placed in a collapsed position, or an expanded position, and the water tank is operable to store a liquid; see e.g., page 2, lines 7-20 and FIGS. 1-3); and
an attachment mechanism attached to the upper frame structure and is configured to be engaged with the main rotor transmission to suspend therefrom (as discussed above in the Response to Arguments section, Claim 1 does not recite or require any direct attachment or direct engagement of any of the recited elements; the upper panel 3 includes supports 1 and connecting pieces 2 that are configured to attach to a helicopter such that the water tank is secured to the helicopter, and the supports 1 and/or connecting pieces 2 would be capable of being effectively coupled to and engaged with a main rotor transmission such that a lifting force generated and transmitted through the main rotor transmission would ultimately lift the helicopter and the water tank toward the sky during operation; see e.g., page 2, lines 14-17 and FIGS. 1-3).
Syta further discloses that the water tank is intended for firefighting applications using a helicopter and that the water tank is fixed rigidly under the fuselage of a helicopter (see e.g., Syta at page 1, lines 3-5 and FIG. 1). However, Syta does not explicitly show the structures associated with the helicopter.
In the same field of endeavor, the Airbus NPL discloses that the Airbus H215 (also known as a Super Puma) is a twin-engine helicopter that is tailored for a full range of missions including firefighting. Thus, it would have been obvious to one skilled in the art at the time of filing to try installing the water tank system of Syta on to the H215 helicopter with a reasonable expectation of success of being able to use the H215 helicopter for firefighting applications.
