Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b) CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1, 3-11 and 13-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Claim 1, line 8 recites, “the distance.” There is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim. For the purposes of examination, the limitation has been interpreted as introducing a distance.
Claim 4, line 2 recites, “the aperture of the gimbal head” (emphasis added). It is noted that Claim 3, line 2 recites, “a gimbal head defining at least one aperture” (emphasis added) and encompasses claim scope where the gimbal head defines two or more apertures. Under a scenario where the “at least one aperture” of Claim 3, line 2 includes two or more apertures, Claim 4 is rendered indefinite since it is unclear which aperture the limitation “the aperture of the gimbal head” in Claim 4 is referring to. For the purposes of examination, “the aperture of the gimbal head” in Claim 4 has been interpreted as referring to all apertures of the at least one aperture of the gimbal head introduced in Claim 3.
Claim 4, lines 2-3 recite, “the aperture of the lower end portion” (emphasis added). It is noted that Claim 3, line 3 recites, “a lower end portion defining at least one aperture” (emphasis added) and encompasses claim scope where the lower end portion defines two or more apertures. Under a scenario where the “at least one aperture” of Claim 3, line 3 includes two or more apertures, Claim 4 is rendered indefinite since it is unclear which aperture the limitation “the aperture of the lower end portion” in Claim 4 is referring to. For the purposes of examination, “the aperture of the lower end portion” in Claim 4 has been interpreted as referring to all apertures of the at least one aperture of the lower end portion introduced in Claim 3.
Claim 11, line 10 recites, “the distance.” There is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim. For the purposes of examination, the limitation has been interpreted as introducing a distance.
Claim 14, line 2 recites, “the aperture of the gimbal head” (emphasis added). It is noted that Claim 13, line 2 recites, “a gimbal head defining at least one aperture” (emphasis added) and encompasses claim scope where the gimbal head defines two or more apertures. Under a scenario where the “at least one aperture” of Claim 13, line 2 includes two or more apertures, Claim 14 is rendered indefinite since it is unclear which aperture the limitation “the aperture of the gimbal head” in Claim 14 is referring to. For the purposes of examination, “the aperture of the gimbal head” in Claim 14 has been interpreted as referring to all apertures of the at least one aperture of the gimbal head introduced in Claim 13.
Claim 14, lines 2-3 recite, “the aperture of the lower end portion” (emphasis added). It is noted that Claim 13, line 3 recites, “a lower end portion defining at least one aperture” (emphasis added) and encompasses claim scope where the lower end portion defines two or more apertures. Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1, 3-11 and 13-20 would be allowable if rewritten or amended to overcome the rejection(s) under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), 2nd paragraph, set forth in this Office action.
Regarding Claims 1 and 3-10, the prior art of record does not reasonably teach or suggest the combination of features as claimed for a tank assembly for a helicopter including a retractable tank comprising an upper frame structure, and an attachment mechanism comprising: a linking assembly having a head connector, a tail connector attached to the upper frame structure, and an adjustment mechanism disposed between the head connector and the tail connector, the adjustment mechanism being rotatably manipulable to adjust the distance between the head connector and the tail connector; and a rod pivotably attached to the head connector of the linking assembly and extending upwardly therefrom to an upper end portion, the upper end portion of the rod being configured to attach to a main rotor transmission of the helicopter to suspend therefrom.
Regarding Claims 11 and 13-20, the prior art of record does not reasonably teach or suggest the combination of features as claimed for a helicopter including a fuselage having an underside; a main rotor transmission; and a tank assembly for a helicopter including a retractable tank comprising an upper frame structure, and an attachment mechanism comprising: a linking assembly having a head connector, a tail connector attached to the upper frame structure, and an adjustment mechanism disposed between the head connector and the tail connector, the adjustment mechanism being rotatably manipulable to adjust the distance between the head connector and the tail connector; and a rod pivotably attached to the head connector of the linking assembly and extending upwardly therefrom to an upper end portion, the upper end portion of the rod being configured to attach to a main rotor transmission of the helicopter to suspend therefrom.
