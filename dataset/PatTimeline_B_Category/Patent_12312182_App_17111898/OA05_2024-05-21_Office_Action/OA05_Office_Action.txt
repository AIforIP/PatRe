Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of the first paragraph of 35 U.S.C. 112(a):
(a) IN GENERAL.—The specification shall contain a written description of the invention, and of the manner and process of making and using it, in such full, clear, concise, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to which it pertains, or with which it is most nearly connected, to make and use the same, and shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor or joint inventor of carrying out the invention.
The following is a quotation of the first paragraph of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112:
The specification shall contain a written description of the invention, and of the manner and process of making and using it, in such full, clear, concise, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to which it pertains, or with which it is most nearly connected, to make and use the same, and shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor of carrying out his invention.
Claims 1-6, 8-16, and 19-30 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(a) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), first paragraph, as failing to comply with the written description requirement. The claim(s) contains subject matter which was not described in the specification in such a way as to reasonably convey to one skilled in the relevant art that the inventor or a joint inventor, or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the inventor(s), at the time the application was filed, had possession of the claimed invention.  Specifically, the limitation “a mast assembly coupled directly to the slew ring” is considered new matter because it was not described in the original written disclosure, or illustrated in the figures.  In Figure 2, It appears the mast is coupled directly to a lower portion of the arm assembly, and not directly to a slew ring.  Referring to Figures 1 and 3, it is not clear if the mast is connected to the lower portion of the robotic arm assembly or to a slew ring.  The original written disclosure does not describe a “slew ring”  or “the mast assembly coupled directly to the slew ring.”  Note, paragraph [0037] of the written description was added on October 2, 2023, which is not part of the original written disclosure, and cannot be used as support for the newly added claim limitation.  In fact, added paragraph [0037] appears to add new matter by introducing a “slew ring” which is not shown or described in the original disclosure.  A slew ring is considered a rotational ring having rolling elements for supporting relatively rotating components, typically positioned along a horizontal plane.  The Figures and the written disclosure do not illustrate or describe a ring with rolling elements for providing rotational movement between robot components.  Any new matter introduced into the written disclosure or claims must be removed.  Since the original disclosure does not disclose the claimed arrangement, the newly added claim limitation has been given limited patentable weight.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim(s) 1-6, 12-16, 19-23, and 27-30 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Morey (USPub 2021/0031361) in view of Edinger et al. (USP 9,346,160).
Morey teaches a robotic arm system comprising: a robotic arm subsystem (206) mounted on a platform (top surface of 202) which is an autonomous mobile base (202); a slew ring (804 at 602) between the robotic arm subsystem and the mobile base for pivoting the robotic arm subsystem with respect to the mobile base; a first machine vision subsystem (214) mounted on a mast assembly (210) coupled (indirectly) to the slew ring, wherein the first machine vision subsystem is configured to pivot with the robotic arm subsystem (section [0088] describes a serial connection such that rotation of midsection 204 causes any structure located above 204 to also rotate) during motion of the robotic arm subsystem and to enable visually monitoring of areas proximate the robotic arm system; and a control subsystem (inherent, such as 118) configured to effectuate movement of the robotic arm assembly using the first machine vision subsystem.
With respect to the newly amended limitation, Morey teaches the robotic arm (206) directly coupled to the slew ring.  More specifically, the robotic arm subsystem includes a shoulder portion (708) and bracket (712) that is directly coupled to the slew ring.  Alternatively, the limitations “slew ring”  or “the mast assembly coupled directly to the slew ring” is considered new matter and has been given limited patentable weight.

Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed April 8, 2024 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
Applicant argues the amendments are supported by Figures 1-2 and paragraph [0037] of the written disclosure.  As described above, Figure 2 appears to illustrate the mast being coupled directly to a lower portion of the arm assembly, and not directly to a slew ring.  With respect to Figures 1 and 3, it is not clear if the mast is connected to the lower portion of the robotic arm assembly or to a slew ring.  The original written disclosure does not describe a “slew ring”  or “the mast assembly coupled directly to the slew ring.”  Note, paragraph [0037] of the written description was added on October 2, 2023, which is not part of the original written disclosure, and cannot be used as support for the newly added claim limitation.  In fact, previously added paragraph [0037] is considered new matter by introducing a “slew ring” which is not shown or described in the original disclosure.  A slew ring is considered a rotational ring having rolling elements for supporting relatively rotating components, typically positioned along a horizontal plane.  The Figures and the written disclosure do not illustrate or describe a ring with rolling elements for providing rotational movement between robot components.  The new matter introduced into the written disclosure and claims must be removed.  If the invention is the mast being directly connected to the slew ring, it is suggested applicant file a Continuation In Part (CIP) to properly add the new matter.
In view of the foregoing, the claims stand rejected as described above.
