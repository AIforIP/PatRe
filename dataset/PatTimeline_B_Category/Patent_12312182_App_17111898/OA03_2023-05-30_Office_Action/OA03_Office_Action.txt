Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim(s) 1-6, 12-16, 19-23, 27-30 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Tian et al. (USP 10,065,314) in view of Edinger et al. (USP 9,346,160).
Tian teaches a robotic arm system comprising: a robotic arm subsystem (330) mounted on a platform (310) which is an autonomous mobile base (340); a first machine vision subsystem (324) mounted on a mast assembly (320), wherein the first machine vision subsystem is configured to enable visually monitoring of areas proximate the robotic arm system; a second machine vision subsystem (334) mounted on the robotic arm subsystem, wherein the second machine vision system is configured to enable visually monitoring of areas proximate the robotic arm subsystem; and a control subsystem (column 13, lines 30+) configured to effectuate movement of the robotic arm assembly using the first machine vision subsystem and the second machine vision subsystem.
Tian does not teach the arm configured as a detachable, self-contained robotic arm system coupled to a mounting subsystem, the mounting subsystem configured to releasably engage the operating platform of the mobile base, a control subsystem  coupled to the mounting subsystem, and a connectivity subsystem configured to detachably couple the detachable, self- contained robotic arm system to an external system.  The prior art to Edinger teaches a detachable, self-contained robotic arm system comprising: a mounting subsystem (110) configured to releasable engage an operating platform (such as cover 310); a robotic arm subsystem (106) coupled to the mounting subsystem; a control subsystem (computer and power supply mounted on plate 204, lines 5-20 of column 4) coupled to the mounting subsystem and configured to effectuate movement of the robotic arm assembly; and a connectivity subsystem (connector) configured to detachably couple the detachable, self-contained robotic arm system to one or more external systems.  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing of the claimed device to configure the robotic arm system of Tian as a self-contained robotic arm system having a mounting subsystem, a control subsystem, and a connectivity subsystem, as taught by Edinger, motivation being to provide a modular reconfigurable robot device that can be adapted to perform a particular operation.
With respect to claims 2, 3, 5, 19, 20, 22, 27, and 29 Edinger teaches a docking module (212) for providing an electrical and mechanical interface for the robot arm (214).  Column 4, lines 27-34.  Further, Edinger teaches the subsystems may include a power supply (220), a control processor (222) for handling real-time control of the robot arm, a task processor (224) for handling non real-time tasks, and a circuit board (226) that provides electrical connections between a dock station (228) and the processors (222,224).  Column 4, lines 34-42.  Accordingly, the connectivity subsystem includes a power connectivity subsystem (212) configured to provide external power to the detachable, self-contained robotic arm system, and wherein the control subsystem includes an electric control subsystem.  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing of the claimed device to configure the robotic arm system of Tian with a connectivity subsystem having a power subsystem to provide external power to the self-contained robotic arm, and a control subsystem with an electric control subsystem, as taught by Edinger, motivation being to provide power and control of the self-contained robotic arm when connected to the base.
With respect to claims 4, 6, 21, 23, 28, and 30, Tian teaches the manipulator arm may include one of more motors, gears, cylinders, or the like to actuate a mechanism such as an arm based on control signals or commands provided by a processing unit, wherein the arm may be hydraulically, electrically, or otherwise actuated.  Column 8, first full paragraph.  Tian does not specifically disclose the arm including a pneumatic control subsystem having pneumatic controls or pneumatic actuators (claims 4,21,28).  However, it was notoriously known in the art to control a robot arm using a pneumatic control arrangement.  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing of the claimed device to configure the robotic arm system of Tian with a pneumatic control arrangement, as was notoriously known in the art, motivation being provide an alternative control arrangement that produces a particular robot output, such as arm movement speed and lifting capacity.
With respect to claim 12, Edinger teaches the mounting subsystem is configured  to releasably engage the operating platform with one or more of: releasable fasteners (606) and releasable clamps (702).  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing of the claimed device to connect the robotic arm system of Tian using either releasable fasteners or releasable clamps, as taught by Edinger, motivation being to provide a simple and secure connection between the releasable components.
With respect to claim 13, Tian illustrates (Fig. 3) the robotic arm subsystem includes one or more of: an arm base assembly; a shoulder joint assembly coupled to the arm base assembly; an upper arm assembly coupled to the should joint assembly; an elbow joint assembly coupled to the upper arm assembly; a lower arm assembly coupled to the elbow joint assembly; a wrist joint assembly coupled to the lower arm assembly; and a gripper assembly coupled to the wrist joint assembly.
With respect to claim 14, Tian illustrates (Fig. 3) the shoulder joint assembly is configured to enable rotation about one or more of the X, Y and Z axis.
With respect to claim 15, Tian illustrates (Fig. 3) the elbow joint assembly is configured to enable rotation about one or more of the X, Y and Z axis.
With respect to claim 16, Tian illustrates (Fig. 3) the wrist joint assembly is configured to enable rotation about one or more of the X, Y and Z axis.
Claim(s) 8-11 and 24-26 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Tian et al. (USP 10,065,314) in view of Edinger et al. (USP 9,346,160), as applied to claim 1, and further in view of Gilbertson et al. (USP 9,827,678).
Tian does not teach the robot arm having an audio system or a conveyor arrangement.
With respect to claim 8, Gilbertson teaches a robot arm having an audio system (ultrasonic) to audibly monitor areas proximate the robotic arm.  For example, see first full paragraph in column 12. Ultrasonic is generally defined as sound frequencies above 20kHz.  The ultrasonic frequency range is typically inaudible to most, however ultrasound does not necessarily mean inaudible for some individuals.  For example, younger people are sometimes capable of hearing sound in ultrasonic range.  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing of the claimed device to configure the robot arm of Tian with ultrasonic range sensors (audible), as taught by Gilbertson, motivation being to provide a robot arm having alternative navigation with environment recognition, which will have improved sensing in particular work environments as compared to other sensing devices.
With respect to claims 9-11 and 24-26, Gilbertson teaches a robot arm having a conveyor system (204,218), the conveyor system is configured to receive objects from and/or provide objects to the robotic arm subsystem, the conveyor system is capable of receiving a pallet.  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing of the claimed device to configure the robot arm of Tian with a conveyor arrangement, as taught by Gilbertson, motivation being to provide a robot arm specifically adapted to perform a predetermined operation.
