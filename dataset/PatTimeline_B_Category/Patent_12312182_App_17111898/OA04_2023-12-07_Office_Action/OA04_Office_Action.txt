Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1-6, 8-16, 19-30 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Claims 1, 19, and 27, the limitation “control subsystem coupled to the mounting subsystem and configured to effectuate movement of the robotic arm assembly using the first machine vision subsystem and the second machine vision subsystem” is not understood because applicant has not provided antecedent basis for “the second vision subsystem.”
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim(s) 1-6, 12-16, 19-23, 27-30 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Morey (USPub 2021/0031361) in view of Edinger et al. (USP 9,346,160).
Morey teaches a robotic arm system comprising: a robotic arm subsystem (206) mounted on a platform (top surface of 202) which is an autonomous mobile base (202); a slew ring (804 at 602) between the robotic arm subsystem and the mobile base for pivoting the robotic arm subsystem with respect to the mobile base; a first machine vision subsystem (214) mounted on a mast assembly (210) coupled (indirectly) to the slew ring, wherein the first machine vision subsystem is configured to pivot with the robotic arm subsystem (section [0088] describes a serial connection such that rotation of midsection 204 causes any structure located above 204 to also rotate) during motion of the robotic arm subsystem and to enable visually monitoring of areas proximate the robotic arm system; and a control subsystem (inherent, such as 118) configured to effectuate movement of the robotic arm assembly using the first machine vision subsystem.
Morey does not teach the arm configured as a detachable, self-contained robotic arm system coupled to a mounting subsystem, the mounting subsystem configured to releasably engage the operating platform of the mobile base, a control subsystem  coupled to the mounting subsystem, and a connectivity subsystem configured to detachably couple the detachable, self- contained robotic arm system to an external system.  The prior art to Edinger teaches a detachable, self-contained robotic arm system comprising: a mounting subsystem (110) configured to releasable engage an operating platform (such as cover 310); a robotic arm subsystem (106) coupled to the mounting subsystem; a control subsystem (computer and power supply mounted on plate 204, lines 5-20 of column 4) coupled to the mounting subsystem and configured to effectuate movement of the robotic arm assembly; and a connectivity subsystem (connector) configured to detachably couple the detachable, self-contained robotic arm system to one or more external systems.  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing of the claimed device to configure the robotic arm system of Morey as a self-contained robotic arm system having a mounting subsystem, a control subsystem, and a connectivity subsystem, as taught by Edinger, motivation being to provide a modular reconfigurable robot device that can be adapted to perform a particular operation.
With respect to claims 2, 3, 5, 19, 20, 22, 27, and 29 Edinger teaches a docking module (212) for providing an electrical and mechanical interface for the robot arm (214).  Column 4, lines 27-34.  Further, Edinger teaches the subsystems may include a power supply (220), a control processor (222) for handling real-time control of the robot arm, a task processor (224) for handling non real-time tasks, and a circuit board (226) that provides electrical connections between a dock station (228) and the processors (222,224).  Column 4, lines 34-42.  Accordingly, the connectivity subsystem includes a power connectivity subsystem (212) configured to provide external power to the detachable, self-contained robotic arm system, and wherein the control subsystem includes an electric control subsystem.  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing of the claimed device to configure the robotic arm system of Morey with a connectivity subsystem having a power subsystem to provide external power to the self-contained robotic arm, and a control subsystem with an electric control subsystem, as taught by Edinger, motivation being to provide power and control of the self-contained robotic arm when connected to the base.
With respect to claims 4, 6, 21, 23, 28, and 30, Morey teaches (Fig. 1 and elsewhere) the robotic system (100) may include mechanical components (110) and electrical components (116), such as a gearing and electric motors.  Morey does not specifically disclose the arm including a pneumatic control subsystem having pneumatic controls/pneumatic actuators (claims 4,21,28) or a hydraulic control subsystem having hydraulic controls/hydraulic actuators (claims 6,23,30).  However, it was notoriously known in the art to control a robot arm using either a pneumatic control arrangement or a hydraulic control arrangement.  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing of the claimed device to configure the robotic arm system of Morey with either a pneumatic control arrangement or a hydraulic control arrangement, as was notoriously known in the art, motivation being provide an alternative control arrangement that produces a particular robot output, such as arm movement speed and lifting capacity.
With respect to claim 12, Edinger teaches the mounting subsystem is configured  to releasably engage the operating platform with one or more of: releasable fasteners (606) and releasable clamps (702).  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing of the claimed device to connect the robotic arm system of Morey using either releasable fasteners or releasable clamps, as taught by Edinger, motivation being to provide a simple and secure connection between the releasable components.
With respect to claim 13, Morey illustrates the robotic arm subsystem includes one or more of: an arm base assembly; a shoulder joint assembly coupled to the arm base assembly; an upper arm assembly coupled to the should joint assembly; an elbow joint assembly coupled to the upper arm assembly; a lower arm assembly coupled to the elbow joint assembly; a wrist joint assembly coupled to the lower arm assembly; and a gripper assembly coupled to the wrist joint assembly.
With respect to claim 14, Morey illustrates the shoulder joint assembly is configured to enable rotation about one or more of the X, Y and Z axis.
With respect to claim 15, Morey illustrates the elbow joint assembly is configured to enable rotation about one or more of the X, Y and Z axis.
With respect to claim 16, Morey illustrates the wrist joint assembly is configured to enable rotation about one or more of the X, Y and Z axis.
Claim(s) 8-11 and 24-26 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Morey (USPub 2021/0031361) in view of Edinger et al. (USP 9,346,160), as applied to claim 1, and further in view of Gilbertson et al. (USP 9,827,678).
Morey does not teach the robot arm having an audio system or a conveyor arrangement.
With respect to claim 8, Gilbertson teaches a robot arm having an audio system (ultrasonic) to audibly monitor areas proximate the robotic arm.  For example, see first full paragraph in column 12. Ultrasonic is generally defined as sound frequencies above 20kHz.  The ultrasonic frequency range is typically inaudible to most, however ultrasound does not necessarily mean inaudible for some individuals.  For example, younger people are sometimes capable of hearing sound in ultrasonic range.  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing of the claimed device to configure the robot arm of Morey with ultrasonic range sensors (audible), as taught by Gilbertson, motivation being to provide a robot arm having alternative navigation with environment recognition, which will have improved sensing in particular work environments as compared to other sensing devices.
With respect to claims 9-11 and 24-26, Gilbertson teaches a robot arm having a conveyor system (204,218), the conveyor system is configured to receive objects from and/or provide objects to the robotic arm subsystem, the conveyor system is capable of receiving a pallet.  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing of the claimed device to configure the robot arm of Morey with a conveyor arrangement, as taught by Gilbertson, motivation being to provide a robot arm specifically adapted to perform a predetermined operation.
