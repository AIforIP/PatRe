Conclusion
The prior art made of record and not relied upon is considered pertinent to applicant's disclosure.
The references Sada-Salinas et al. (Pub No. US 2016/0297520 A1) discloses a system and method for flight control of an electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft, the system comprising: a pilot control connected to an eVTOL aircraft, wherein the pilot control is configured to transmit a pilot instruction of a pilot; a pusher component connected to the eVTOL aircraft; a lift component connected to the eVTOL aircraft; and a flight controller communicatively connected to the pilot control, wherein the flight controller is configured to: estimate a stall speed for the eVTOL aircraft as a function; receive the pilot instruction from the pilot control; initiate operation of the pusher component as a function of the pilot instruction; terminate operation of the lift component as a function of the pilot instruction; wherein the pilot instruction comprises at least information on a pilot's desired transition from substantially vertical flight of the eVTOL aircraft to substantially horizontal flight of the eVTOL aircraft; wherein the pusher component comprises at least a propulsor; wherein the pusher component is configured to generate a generally forward thrust for the eVTOL aircraft; wherein the lift component comprises at least a propulsor; wherein the lift component is configured to generate a generally upward thrust for the eVTOL aircraft; wherein the flight controller comprises a computing device; and wherein the flight controller is further configured to: increase a rotational speed of the pusher component; and decrease a rotational speed of the lift component; but does not teach a warning for stall speed but rather autonomously corrects for stall as a function of pilot instructions and stall speed.
The reference Kroo et al. (Pub No. US 2018/0086448 A1) discloses a system and method for flight control of an electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft, the system comprising: a pilot control connected to an eVTOL aircraft, wherein the pilot control is configured to transmit a pilot instruction of a pilot; and a flight controller communicatively connected to the pilot control, wherein the flight controller is configured to: estimate a stall speed for the eVTOL aircraft as a function; receive the pilot instruction from the pilot control; wherein the pilot instruction comprises at least information on a pilot's desired transition from substantially vertical flight of the eVTOL aircraft to substantially horizontal flight of the eVTOL aircraft; wherein the pilot control comprises at least one of a control switch and a control lever; and wherein the flight controller comprises a computing device; but does not teach a warning for stall speed but rather autonomously corrects for stall as a function of pilot instructions and stall speed.
The reference Winkelstein (Pub No. US 2021/0053694 A1) discloses a system and method for a flight control of an electric vertical takeoff and landing (VTOL) aircraft, the system comprising: a pilot control connected to an eVTOL aircraft, wherein the pilot control is configured to transmit a pilot instruction of a pilot; a pusher component connected to the eVTOL aircraft; a lift component connected to the eVTOL aircraft; and a flight controller communicatively connected to the pilot control, wherein the flight controller is configured to: receive the pilot instruction from the pilot control; initiate operation of the pusher component as a function of the pilot instruction; terminate operation of the lift component as a function of the pilot instruction; and wherein the flight controller comprises a proportional-integral- derivative (PID) controller.
The reference Ullman et al. (US Patent No. 10,926,868 B1) discloses a system and method for an aircraft, a flight controller configured to: estimate a stall speed for the aircraft as a function; receive the pilot instruction from the pilot control; but does not teach a warning for stall speed but rather autonomously corrects for stall as a function of pilot instructions and stall speed.
