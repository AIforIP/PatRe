Notice of Pre-AIA  or AIA  Status
The present application, filed on or after March 16, 2013, is being examined under the first inventor to file provisions of the AIA .
Reasons for Allowance
Based on the amendments made, the claims have overcome the previous claim objections and rejections. The prior art of record teaches a surgical system where an operating room is created in virtual reality to determine movement of robotic arms to avoid collision with objects within the operating area (Johnson, US 20190133689); a surgical system where a user can input areas on the patient that the robot arms should stay away from (Roh, US 20190262084); updating an environment based on contact with the objects in that environment (Watabe, US 20170010620); adjustable arm supports linking the robotic arms to the operating table (Cagle, US 20180078439); pre-modelling the patient using stored geometries to approximate the location of the patient (Hourtash, US 20170296277); a user being able to manually draw a safety zone (Chen, US 20180036884); pre-mapping the three-dimensional space around an operating table (Thienphrapa, US 20210068908); modelling safety zones around a physical model of an object in a surgical environment (Kara, US 20200335207); and generating a 3D free space model to avoid collision of a C-arm structure with other surgical robotic components (Hamersma, US 20140022353).
However, prior art was not found that teaches updating a model of a medical system based on the position of a collision wherein the position of the collision is based on the mounting location of the rigid object attached to a platform and updating a user-input keep-out zone of one or more keep-out zones by expanding the keep-out zone to include a position of a detected collision as claimed by independent claims 1, 19, 25, 29, and 41. Thus, the prior art does not singly or in combination teach all of the claim limitations together.
