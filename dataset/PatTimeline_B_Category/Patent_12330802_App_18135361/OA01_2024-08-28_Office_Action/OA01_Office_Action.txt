Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
(a)(2) the claimed invention was described in a patent issued under section 151, or in an application for patent published or deemed published under section 122(b), in which the patent or application, as the case may be, names another inventor and was effectively filed before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claims 1, 4, 5, 8, 13 are rejected under 35 USC 102(a)(1) and 102(a)(2) as being anticipated by US Patent Application Number 2017/0313434 by Peryea.
Regarding claim 1, Peryea discloses a rotorcraft (rotorcraft 10) comprising:
A fuselage (fuselage 16) arranged within a fuselage outer shell;
At least one main rotor located on top of the fuselage (rotor 12);
At least one engine provided for driving the at least one main rotor (paragraph 21 discloses “one or more engines that drive rotor system 12”); and
At least one energy source storage unit (fuel tank 28) comprising at least one energy source configured to provide energy for powering the at least one engine for driving the at least one main rotor (paragraph 21 discloses “Liquid fuel is contained within fuel tank 28 that is used as an energy source to power one or more engines that drive rotor system 12 and anti-torque system 18”);
Wherein the at least one energy source storage unit is arranged outside the fuselage outer shell (paragraph 22 discloses “Fuel tank receiving assembly 24…may be independent of but secured to fuselage 16 of rotorcraft 10”); and
Wherein the fuselage and the fuselage outer shell form in vicinity of the at least one energy source storage unit (see the fuselage around fuel system 26 and fuel tank 28 in Figure 1A) a crashable structure configured to be crashable in an emergency landing at least for limiting effects of impact on the at least one energy source storage unit (any structure on an aircraft is crashable in an emergency landing, and the fuselage around fuel system 26 and fuel tank 28 would take some of the impact of a crash landing and limit the effects of impact on fuel tank 28).
Regarding claim 4 (dependent on claim 1), Peryea discloses the at least one energy source storage unit is arranged in a subfloor region of the rotorcraft.  Figure 1 shows fuel tank 28 mounted on the bottom of rotorcraft 10, which would be in a subfloor region.
Regarding claim 5 (dependent on claim 4), Peryea discloses the fuselage and the fuselage outer shell forming a central box-like structure in the subfloor region (Figure 1A shows the bottom of fuselage 16 having a portion to receive fuel system 26 and fuel tank 28, and Figure 2 shows fuel tank receiving assembly 24 that is to be located within fuselage 16, the portion to receive fuel tank receiving assembly 24 would comprise a box-like structure in the subfloor region); and wherein the at least one energy source storage unit is arranged laterally at the central box-like structure, at least essentially in parallel to a length direction of the fuselage (see Figure 1A).
Regarding claim 8 (dependent on claim 5), Peryea discloses the central box-like structure forming the crashable structure.  Any portion of the rotorcraft can form a crashable structure depending on which portions are impacted in a crash.
Regarding claim 13 (dependent on claim 4), Peryea disclose the at least one energy source storage unit comprising at least one fuel tank arranged in the subfloor region (fuel tank 28), and wherein the fuel tank forms the crashable structure (any portion of the rotorcraft can form a crashable structure depending on which portions are impacted in a crash).
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claims 2-3 are rejected under 35 USC 103 as being obvious over US Patent Application Number 2017/0313434 by Peryea.
Regarding claim 2 (dependent on claim 1)¸ Peryea discloses the at least one energy source storage unit comprising at least one energy source storage container for accommodating the at least one energy source (fuel tank 28).
Peryea does not explicitly disclose the at least one energy source comprising at least one of a hydrogen storage device or an electrical energy storage device.  However, having disclosed the use of fuel tank 28 to power the rotorcraft, it would be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art to use known types of fuels for rotorcraft such as hydrogen or electrical energy.
Regarding claim 3 (dependent on claim 2), Peryea discloses the at least one energy source storage container is removably attached to the fuselage and/or the fuselage outer shell.  Paragraph 22 discloses “Fuel tank receiving assembly 24…may be independent of but secured to fuselage 16 of rotorcraft 10”.
Claims 6-7, 9, 12 are rejected under 35 USC 103 as being obvious over US Patent Application Number 2017/0313434 by Peryea in view of US Patent Number 4,860,972 to Lannerd.
Regarding claim 6 (dependent on claim 4), Peryea discloses the fuselage and the fuselage outer shell forming a central box-like structure in the subfloor region (Figure 1A shows the bottom of fuselage 16 having a portion to receive fuel system 26 and fuel tank 28, and Figure 2 shows fuel tank receiving assembly 24 that is to be located within fuselage 16, the portion to receive fuel tank receiving assembly 24 would comprise a box-like structure in the subfloor region).
Peryea does not disclose the at leas tone energy source storage unit is arranged laterally at the lateral extensions, at least essentially in parallel to a length direction of the fuselage.  However, this limitation is taught by Lannerd.  Lannerd discloses auxiliary fuel tanks for a helicopter, and Figure 2 shows these lateral tanks 26 being arranged laterally at lateral extensions of the fuselage in parallel to a length direction of the fuselage.  It would be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art to modify Peryea  using the teachings from Lannerd to use different orientations to store fuel or to store additional fuel as needed.
Regarding claim 7 (dependent on claim 6), Lannerd further teaches the central box-like structure and the lateral extensions form a subfloor outer shell forming with the fuselage outer shell an aerodynamically shaped outer skin (Figure 11 shows the subfloor region having a box like structure (the box around access plate 132) with lateral extensions (to the left of 132, the outer shell forming an aerodynamically shaped outer skin (see Figures 1 and 2)), and wherein the at least one energy source storage unit is arranged outside the aerodynamically shaped outer skin (see auxiliary fuel tanks 26 in Figure 2).
Regarding claim 9 (dependent on claim 4), Lannerd further teaches the at leas tone energy source storage unit forming an accessible step.  Column 2, line 64 to column 3, line 1 discloses “The outer wall 30 has a depression 50, FIG. 4, comprising a flat horizontal wall 52, an inner vertical wall 54 which terminates at top edge wall 32 and vertical side walls 56, 58.  The depression 50 functions as a step for passengers entering or exiting the aircraft”.
Regarding claim 12 (dependent on claim 4)¸ Peryea and Lannerd does not disclose the at least one energy source storage unit comprising a storage extension extending form the subfloor region into an upper region of the rotorcraft.  However, it would have been an obvious matter of design choice to make the different portions of the fuel tank of whatever form or shape was desired or expedient to store fuel in different locations on the aircraft as needed. A change in form or shape is generally recognized as being within the level of ordinary skill in the art, absent any showing of unexpected results. In re Dailey et al., 149 USPQ 47.
Claims 10-11 are rejected under 35 USC 103 as being obvious over US Patent Application Number 2017/0313434 by Peryea in view of US Patent Application Number 2015/0136906 by Fink.
Regarding claim 10 (dependent on claim 4), Peryea does not disclose the fuselage and the fuselage outer shell forming lateral box-like structures in the subfloor region, and wherein the at least one energy source storage unit is arranged between the lateral box-like structures in the subfloor region, at least essentially in parallel to a length direction of the fuselage.  However, this limitation is taught by Fink.  Fink discloses a subfloor fuel storage where the fuselage and fuselage outer shell forms lateral box-like structures in the subfloor region (see box-like structures to the left and right of fuel tank bladders 12 in Figure 2), and wherein the at least one energy source storage unit is arranged between the lateral box-like structures in the subfloor region, at least essentially in parallel to a length direction of the fuselage (fuel tank bladders 12 are arranged between the box-like structures, beneath floor panels 13, and runs essentially in parallel to a length direction of the fuselage).  It would be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art to modify Peryea using the teachings from Fink in order to provide more lateral protection to the fuel tanks.
Regarding claim 11 (dependent on claim 10), Fink further teaches the lateral box-like structures form the crashable structure.  Paragraph 8 discloses “the subfloor structure houses electrical and mechanical systems and the fuel tanks and further provides for substantial kinetic energy absorption in case of a crash scenario”.
Claim 14 is rejected under 35 USC 103 as being obvious over US Patent Application Number 2017/0313434 by Peryea in view of US Patent Number 5,451,015 to Cronkhite.
Regarding claim 14 (dependent on claim 1), Peryea does not disclose the at least one energy source storage unit is provided with at least one energy absorption means, and wherein the at least one energy absorption means preferably comprises at least one bumper.  However, this limitation is taught by Cronkhite.  Cronkhite discloses a helicopter fuel tank 35 with polyurethane foam 83 forming a bumper around the fuel tank.  It would be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art to modify Peryea using the teachings from Cronkhite in order to better protect the fuel tanks from damage.
Claim 15 is rejected under 35 USC 103 as being obvious over US Patent Application Number 2017/0313434 by Peryea in view of US Patent Application Number 2018/0170509 by Mores.
Regarding claim 15 (dependent on claim 1), Peryea does not disclose the at least one energy source storage unit comprising at least one air inlet configured to enable air cooling of the at leas tone energy source.  However this limitation is taught by Mores.  Mores discloses a rotorcraft 1, and paragraph 71 discloses “the exchangeable energy source cover structural shell 8a is at least shaped to allow inflow of a cooling air stream 13a into the at leas tone propulsion system carrying section 10 for cooling at least the exchangeable energy source 11”.  It would be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art to modify Peryea using the teachings from Mores in order to keep the fuel system from overheating.
