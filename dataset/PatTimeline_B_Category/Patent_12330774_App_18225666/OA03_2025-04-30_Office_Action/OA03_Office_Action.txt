The following is an examiner's statement of reasons for allowance:
The prior art of record fails to disclose or make obvious the combined limitations of applicants claimed invention. Specifically, Beach (US 2,869,806) teaches a leverage-reducing landing gear system for an aircraft (Fig. 1, portion of aircraft fuselage and landing gear), said leverage-reducing landing gear system comprising:
(a) said aircraft having a front, a rear, a top, a bottom, and a central vertical y-axis perpendicular to a horizontal x-axis and extending from said front to said rear, said aircraft having an airframe and landing gear (Examiner notes in Fig. 1, compartment 1 formed in the lower side of a fuselage 2 by a pair of spaced brackets 3 and 4 rigidly securing the landing gear of the system to the aircraft), said aircraft having a center of gravity (Examiner notes aircraft in Fig. 1 inherently having front, rear, top, bottom, center of gravity and central vertical y-axis perpendicular to an x-axis extending from front to rear);
(b) said landing gear including a first primary landing gear leg and a second primary landing gear leg said first and second primary landing gear legs positioned on opposite sides of said central vertical y-axis (main beam 8 is a primary landing gear leg, Fig. 1; Examiner notes the landing gear is disclosed as a dual tandem wheel arrangement, see col. 2, ln. 64; therefore, the arrangement comprises two primary landing gear legs, a first on the left and a second on the right; naturally positioned on opposite sides of the central vertical y-axis of the aircraft);
(c) at least one first shock absorber functionally connected to said first primary landing gear leg (tension type shock absorber 24, best seen in Fig. 1 functionally connected to the first primary landing gear leg 8), said at least one first shock absorber connected between said first primary landing gear leg and said airframe (col. 3, ln. 5: one end of the shock absorber engages main beam 8 through pin 26 and the opposite end 27 of the shock absorber is swingably secured to bracket 28 in fuselage 2 through pin 29), said at least one first shock absorber angled from said first primary landing gear leg toward said central vertical y-axis (Examiner notes the shock absorber 24 is angled form landing gear leg 8 toward the central vertical y-axis).
(d) at least one second shock absorber functionally connected to said second primary landing gear leg, said at least one second shock absorber connected between said second primary landing gear leg and said airframe, said at least one second shock absorber angled from said second primary landing gear leg toward said central vertical y-axis (Examiner notes Beach teaches bullet (d), just as Beach teaches bullet (c)).
However, the prior art does not teach the combined limitations of the claimed invention, for example, the leverage-reducing landing gear system for an aircraft comprising the landing gear with the two shock absorbers wherein each shock absorber is angled from the respective primary landing gear leg toward the central vertical y-axis specifically upward such that each of the respective shock absorbers and the horizontal x-axis form an angle less than 30 degrees; and specifically wherein the landing gear is a fixed landing gear (i.e., non-retractable). In fact, the shock absorbers of Beach are taught as controlling the swinging movement of main beam 8 about pin 9 between the retracted and extended positions.
Additionally, with regard to claim 19, Tsukimori (US 5,868,352) teaches a landing-gear system for an aircraft (Fig. 1, Examiner notes the landing gear system comprising the low-wing monoplane aircraft, with front primary landing gear 18 and tail-wheel 1) said landing gear system comprising:
(a) said aircraft having a front, a rear, a top, a bottom, and a central vertical y-axis perpendicular to a horizontal x-axis and extending from said front to said rear, said aircraft having an airframe and landing gear, said aircraft having a center of gravity (the aircraft taught inherently has front, rear, top, bottom, airframe, landing gear, and center of gravity central vertical y-axis perpendicular to a horizontal x-axis and extending from said front to said rear);
(b) a primary landing gear system for an aircraft (primary landing gear 18), comprising:
(i) said landing gear including a first primary landing gear leg and a second primary landing gear leg (left and right primary connection rods 16, Figs. 1-2), said first and second primary landing gear legs positioned on opposite sides of said central vertical y-axis (Fig. 1-2); and
(c) a secondary landing gear system for an aircraft (tail-wheel 1, Fig. 1), comprising:
(i) said landing gear including secondary landing gear including a tire (1, Fig. 1)
(d) wherein said landing gear is fixed landing gear (Examine notes the landing gear taught by Tsukimori is fixed), but the prior art alone or in combination does not teach the combined limitations of the claimed invention, specifically wherein the primary landing gear system is specifically a leverage-reducing system comprising: (ii) at least one first shock absorber functionally connected to said first primary landing gear leg, said at least one first shock absorber connected between said first primary landing gear leg and said airframe, said at least one first shock absorber angled upward from said first primary landing gear leg toward said central vertical y-axis such that said at least one first shock absorber and said horizontal x-axis form an angle less than 30 degrees; and (iii) at least one second shock absorber functionally connected to said second primary landing gear leg, said at least one second shock absorber connected between said second primary landing gear leg and said airframe, said at least one second shock absorber angled upward from said second primary landing gear leg toward said central vertical y-axis such that said at least one second shock absorber and said horizontal x-axis form an angle less than 30 degrees; and
wherein the secondary landing gear system is specifically a constant angle suspension, wherein the tire is specifically castered, and positioned to align with said central vertical y-axis; and (ii) at least one secondary shock absorber mounted between said airframe and said castered tire; and (iii) at least one constant angle suspension mechanism mounted between said airframe and said castered tire, said at least one constant angle suspension mechanism being more toward said front of said aircraft than said castered tire.
