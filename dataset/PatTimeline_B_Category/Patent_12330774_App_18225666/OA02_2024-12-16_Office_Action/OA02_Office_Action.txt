Election/Restrictions
Applicant's election with traverse of Species A drawn to Figs. 2-10 in the reply filed on 11/20/2024 is acknowledged.  The traversal is on the following grounds:
Applicant respectfully submits that the Examiner may have applied the wrong test. Specifically, the Examiner states that the species are independent or distinct.... (Emphasis added.) While the Examiner states that the species describe two separate types of landing gear and that they are not obvious variants of each other, applicant respectfully requests clarification of how this makes the two species independent AND how this makes the two species distinct. Applicant respectfully points out that aircraft can have both types of landing gear. As noted in M.P.E.P. §806.04(f), claims to be restricted to different species must recite the mutually exclusive characteristics of such species. (Emphasis added.)
This is not found persuasive. Examiner did not apply the wrong test. Historically the phrase “two or more independent and distinct inventions” has been interpreted to mean two or more independent or distinct inventions, MPEP §802.01, MPEP §803(I).  In the instant application the restriction between inventions is proper as the inventions are related, yet ‘distinct’ as claimed, and there would be a serious search burden on the examiner if the restriction were not required. Species may be independent or related inventions, MPEP 806.04(b).
The mutually exclusive characteristics of each species as originally stated in the restriction requirement is that they are two separate types of landing gear and they are not obvious variants. In response to Applicant’s request for clarification of how this makes the two species independent AND how this makes the two species distinct”, Examiner notes the above remarks clarifying “independent” inventions. Examiner notes the following remarks clarifying “distinct” inventions:  The distinct nature of the two inventions is evidenced by Applicant’s distinct title, distinct figure set and distinct claim set for each respective invention (i.e., Figs. 2-10 representing a leverage reducing system for energy absorption, distinctly claimed in claims 1-10; AND Fig. 13-17 representing a constant angle suspension system distinctly claimed in claims 11-18).
As applicant states on page 1, line 20 of the specification, distinct methods/apparatuses and/or systems for improving aircraft landing gear are disclosed; One improvement generally relates to the technical field of landing gear having a leverage-reducing system for low profile energy absorption. Another improvement generally relates to the technical field of  landing gear having of a constant angle suspension mechanism using constant caster angle energy absorption.
The two inventions are not obvious variants. In other words, they are each patentably distinct inventions. And, they would require searches in separate classifications, and searches for separate elements of the respective system, i.e., separate search queries and hypothetically separate patents. For example, Species A requires (primary OR front OR main) ADJ (gear OR legs) AND (shock ADJ absorber) OR (spring damper strut suspension recoil cushion absorber), while Species B requires a completely different search (e.g., (caster$4 ADJ tire) AND (angle NEAR2 suspension) WITH (four-bar) WITH (tail ADJ wheel ADJ control ADJ arm), etc.  Examiner notes the requirement is still deemed proper and is therefore made FINAL.
Claims 11-18 are withdrawn from further consideration pursuant to 37 CFR 1.142(b), as being drawn to a nonelected Species B, there being no allowable generic or linking claim. Applicant timely traversed the restriction (election) requirement in the reply filed on 11/20/2024.

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of the first paragraph of 35 U.S.C. 112(a):
(a) IN GENERAL.—The specification shall contain a written description of the invention, and of the manner and process of making and using it, in such full, clear, concise, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to which it pertains, or with which it is most nearly connected, to make and use the same, and shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor or joint inventor of carrying out the invention.
Claim 19 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(a) as failing to comply with the written description requirement. The claim(s) contains subject matter which was not described in the specification in such a way as to reasonably convey to one skilled in the relevant art that the inventor or a joint inventor at the time the application was filed, had possession of the claimed invention. The specification, while being enabling for a leverage reducing landing gear system for an aircraft; and for a constant angle suspension landing gear system for an aircraft, does not originally reasonably provide enablement a landing gear system comprising both a leverage reducing landing gear system for an aircraft; and a constant angle suspension landing gear system for an aircraft. Examiner notes each respective system is presented as completely separate in the figures. While Applicant references on the final page of the specification that the respective systems may be used individually or together, the specification does not necessarily teach a landing gear system comprising both systems. A mere mention at a high level of abstraction is NOT sufficient disclosure in light of the absence of any detailed description, drawing or claim. The claim is rejected as the claim aircraft system of claim 19 is new matter.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
Claims 1-10 and 19 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
Claim 1 recites A leverage-reducing landing gear system for an aircraft, said aircraft having a front, a rear, a top, a bottom, and a central vertical longitudinal plane perpendicular to horizontal ground and extending from said front to said rear, said aircraft having an airframe and landing gear, said aircraft having a center of gravity, said leverage-reducing landing gear system comprising:
This is indefinite because it is unclear if an aircraft is being positively claimed or merely recited functionally in relation to the leverage-reducing landing gear system. It appears the system is merely claimed for an aircraft. Therefore, the details of the particular aircraft (i.e. having a front, a rear, a top, a bottom, and a central vertical longitudinal plane perpendicular to horizontal ground and extending from said front to said rear, said aircraft having an airframe and landing gear, said aircraft having a center of gravity) do not hold patentable weight and will not be considered as such during examination.  Claim 19 is likewise rejected. Claims 2-10 fail to cure the deficiency. Correction/clarification is required.
Similarly, claim 1 (and claim 19) recites the following limitations:
in section (a) - said first and second primary landing gear legs positioned on opposite sides of said central vertical longitudinal plane
in section (b) - said at least one first shock absorber angled upward from said first primary landing gear leg toward said central vertical longitudinal plane at an angle less than 30 degrees
in section (c) - said at least one second shock absorber angled upward from said second primary landing gear leg toward said central vertical longitudinal plane at an angle less than 30 degrees
Each renders the claim indefinite as they are attempting to further limit the invention (i.e., the system) in terms of elements of a hypothetical aircraft that has not otherwise been positively claimed. The limitations are each functions of the system on an aircraft. For example, regarding  section (a) specifically, “the first and second primary landing gear legs are configured to be positioned on opposite sides of a central vertical longitudinal plane of an aircraft”. Correction/clarification of each is required. Claims 5-9 do not cure the deficiency. Claims 2-4, 10 and 19 are likewise rejected for the following limitations, as each are functions of the system as they are configured to be applied to an aircraft:
Claim 2: said at least one first shock absorber angled upward from said first primary landing gear leg toward said central vertical longitudinal plane at an angle less than 20 degrees, and said at least one second shock absorber angled upward from said second primary landing gear leg toward said central vertical longitudinal plane at an angle less than 20 degrees
Claim 3: angling said first and second at least one shock absorbers at angles less than 30 degrees allows for compact packaging of said leverage-reducing landing gear system to at least reduce drag caused by externally-mounted landing gear while preserving interior cabin volume
Claim 4: positioned between said center of gravity and said front of said aircraft
Claim 10: (a) - positioned toward said rear of said aircraft and aligned with said central vertical longitudinal plane; (c) - said at least one constant angle suspension mechanism being more toward said front of said aircraft than said steerable tail wheel.
Claim 19 additionally recites the following: (i) positioned to align with said central vertical longitudinal plane; (ii) mounted between said airframe and said castered tire; (iii) mounted between said airframe and said castered tire… being more toward said front of said aircraft than said castered tire. Each define functions of the system as they are configured to be applied to an aircraft. Correction/clarification is required.
Claim 3 recites “wherein angling said first and second at least one shock absorbers at angles less than 30 degrees allows for compact packaging of said leverage-reducing landing gear system to at least reduce drag caused by externally-mounted landing gear while preserving interior cabin volume”. The term “compact packaging ” and “at least reduce” in the claim are relative terms which renders the claim indefinite. The terms are not defined by the claim, the specification does not provide a standard for ascertaining the requisite degree, and one of ordinary skill in the art would not be reasonably apprised of the scope of the invention. For example, how compact must the “packaging be” and how much “drag reduction” is required to infringe on the limitation of the claim? Correction/clarification is required.
Claim 10 recites the following: The leverage-reducing landing gear system of claim 1, further comprising:(a) said landing gear including a steerable tail wheel positioned toward said rear of said aircraft and aligned with said central vertical longitudinal plane;(b) at least one third shock absorber mounted between said airframe and said steerable tail wheel, said at least one third shock absorber above said steerable tail wheel; and(c) at least one constant angle suspension mechanism mounted between said airframe and said steerable tail wheel, said at least one constant angle suspension mechanism being more toward said front of said aircraft than said steerable tail wheel. The limitation is indefinite as the metes and bounds of the claim are unclear. The scope of the claim appears to be leverage-reducing landing gear system and its intended use for an aircraft. The, leverage reducing aircraft does not appear to contain a steerable tail wheel, at least one third shock absorber or at least one constant angle suspension mechanism. Instead, a hypothetical aircraft appears to comprise each element. The metes and bounds of the claim are unclear. Correction/clarification is required.
Additionally, the elements “a steerable tail wheel”, “at least one third shock absorber” and “at least one constant angle suspension mechanism” each appear to be claimed in terms of an aircraft, and are therefore rejected as being incomplete for omitting essential structural cooperative relationships of elements, such omission amounting to a gap between the necessary structural connections.  See MPEP § 2172.01.  For example, how exactly are the elements incorporated/attached/further comprising the claimed invention (i.e., the leverage reducing landing gear system). Correction/clarification is required.
Claims 1-2 and 9 recite the term “said central vertical longitudinal plane”. There is lack of antecedent basis for this terminology in claims. The claims are rejected. Correction/clarification is required. Claims 2-10 do not cure the deficiency. Correction/clarification is required.
Claims 6, 8 and 10 recite the term “said airframe”. There is lack of antecedent basis for this terminology in claims. The claims are rejected. Correction/clarification is required.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
The factual inquiries for establishing a background for determining obviousness under 35 U.S.C. 103 are summarized as follows:
1. Determining the scope and contents of the prior art.
2. Ascertaining the differences between the prior art and the claims at issue.
3. Resolving the level of ordinary skill in the pertinent art.
4. Considering objective evidence present in the application indicating obviousness or nonobviousness.
This application currently names joint inventors. In considering patentability of the claims the examiner presumes that the subject matter of the various claims was commonly owned as of the effective filing date of the claimed invention(s) absent any evidence to the contrary.  Applicant is advised of the obligation under 37 CFR 1.56 to point out the inventor and effective filing dates of each claim that was not commonly owned as of the effective filing date of the later invention in order for the examiner to consider the applicability of 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(2)(C) for any potential 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2) prior art against the later invention.
Claims 1-9 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Jackson (US 2020/0369381 A1).
Regarding claim 1, Jackson discloses a leverage-reducing landing gear system for an aircraft (Fig. 5), said aircraft having a front, a rear, a top, a bottom, and a central vertical longitudinal plane perpendicular to horizontal ground and extending from said front to said rear, said aircraft having an airframe and landing gear, said aircraft having a center of gravity (Examiner notes 112b rejection associated with the claim language), said leverage-reducing landing gear system comprising:
(a) said landing gear including a first primary landing gear leg and a second primary landing gear leg (Examiner notes the first and second landing legs associated with the first and second landing gear assemblies 100, Fig. 1; more specifically the respective elongate landing gear struts 110 depicted in Fig. 5) said first and second primary landing gear legs positioned on opposite sides of said central vertical longitudinal plane (Fig. 1; Examiner notes this is an inherent feature of primary landing gear legs on an aircraft;
(b) at least one first shock absorber functionally connected to said first primary landing gear leg (actuator 250, Fig. 5), and
(c) at least one second shock absorber functionally connected to said second primary landing gear leg (Examiner notes actuator 250, Fig. 5 is identical to the first primary landing gear leg and first shock absorber, pictured generally in Fig. 1),
