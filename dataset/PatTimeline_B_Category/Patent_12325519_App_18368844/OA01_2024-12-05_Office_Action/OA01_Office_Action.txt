Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 USC § 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 USC § 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1-11 are rejected under 35 USC § 112(b) or 35 USC § 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 USC § 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Claim 1 recites the limitation the triggering in line 14.  There is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim.  Because the triggering is introduced in line 14, the claim should be written as triggering of the main actuator (5) to overcome the insufficient antecedent basis rejection.
Claim 1 also recites the limitations “at least one additional actuator (7)” in line 16 and “an additional actuator (7)” in line 21.  These limitations are vague and indefinite.  It is not clear if these two actuators are the same actuator or if these two actuators are different actuators.  If these two actuators are the same actuator, the limitation in line 21 should be written to indicate that it is the same actuator written in line 16.  For example, “the at least one additional actuator (7)” would be an acceptable manner in which to write the claim limitation in line 21 to indicate that it is the same limitation introduced in line 16.  If the actuator recited in line 21 is not the same actuator introduced in line 16, it is not clear how or why both recited actuators would be in connection with the support element (8) and enable the wings (3) to be rotated as set forth in the claim.  For examination purposes, because of lack of clarity of the claim, and as best understood, the limitations “at least one additional actuator (7)” in line 16 and “an additional actuator (7)” in line 21 will be interpreted as the being the same actuator.
Claim 1 furthermore recites the limitations “the main actuator (5) triggering the control surface (4)” in line 26 and “the main actuator (7) triggering the wing (3) or wings (3)” in line 28.  These limitations are vague and indefinite.  The main actuator is referenced by two different reference characters (5) and (7).  And, a main actuator (5) is introduced in line 12 as “a main actuator (5) moving the control surface (4).  So, it is not clear how the main actuator (5) is also being claimed as the main actuator (7) triggering the wing (3) or wings (3).  Moreover, “at least one additional actuator (7)” is introduced in lines 16-17 as “rotating the wings (3).”  Consequently, it is not clear how the main actuator is claimed as triggering the wing (3) or wings (3) in line 28.  For examination purposes, because of lack of clarity of the claim, and as best understood, the limitation “the main actuator (7) triggering the wing (3) or wings (3)” in line 28 will be interpreted as “the at least one additional actuator (7) triggering the wing (3) or wings (3)”.
Additionally, dependent claims 2-13 necessarily inherit the deficiencies of the parent claim.
Claim 2 recites the limitations the mutual control surfaces in lines 2-3, and the mutual wings in line 5.  There is insufficient antecedent basis for these limitations in the claim.
Claim 4 recites the limitation the worm gears in line 1.  There is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim.
Claim 13 recites the limitation the single main actuator in line 2.  There is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim.
Conclusion
The prior art made of record and not relied upon is considered pertinent to applicant's disclosure.  Krastev, U.S. Patent Application Publication 2015/0102155 A1 teaches two front telescopic wings driven either by two hydraulic cylinders or two ball couples screw/nut with worm reducers, these wings have a mechanism for changing the angle of attack.  But Krastev does not teach a coaxial/concentricity of the support element (8) and the shaft (6) that are moved by triggering of the main actuator and an additional actuator enabling the attack angle of the wings as set forth in claim 1 in lines 14-20.
