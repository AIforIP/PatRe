DETAILED ACTION
Allowable Subject Matter
The following is a statement of reasons for the indication of allowable subject matter: The prior art of record fails to neither disclose or sufficiently suggest the combination of features as claimed and arranged by applicant when read in light of the specification.
Regarding Claim 13, Newman, et al., hereinafter Newman (U.S. Patent Application Pub. No. 2017/0291602) teaches: A mobile warning apparatus for issuing a warning in response to observation of a mobile object approaching a vehicle (Newman, Para. 0051 and 0123 – “a visual or acoustical or haptic alarm to let the driver know when a collision is imminent”; where the imminent collision is between the subject vehicle and a second vehicle), the mobile warning apparatus comprising:
a velocity calculator configured to calculate, based on observation information observed by a sensor installed in the vehicle (Newman, Para. 0013 – “sensors, mounted on the subject vehicle” which acquire sensor data of a second vehicle), one or more relative velocities of one or more observed points (Newman, Para. 0013 and 0034-0035 – where processor components, or calculator, determine position, velocity, or acceleration of the second vehicle based on the sensor data; where the processor component can calculate “the relative velocity between the two vehicles”; where the sensor data is observed data);
a position determiner (Newman, Para. 0019 and 0034 – “a processor component” which determines obstacle positions) configured to perform:
a first determination of whether the observed points include a pair of first and second observed points, the first observed point having a first relative velocity in a first direction approaching the vehicle, and the second observed point having a second relative velocity in a second direction moving away from the vehicle;
a second determination of whether a difference between azimuths of the respective first and second observed points of the pair relative to the vehicle observed by the sensor is smaller than or equal to a predetermined azimuth threshold in response to the first determination that the observed points include the pair of the first observed point and the second observed point; and
a third determination that the first and second observed points of the pair are observed points obtained from a fixed rotor in response to the second determination that the difference between the azimuths of the respective first and second observed points of the pair relative to the vehicle observed by the sensor is smaller than or equal to the predetermined azimuth threshold; and
an eliminator configured to eliminate the first and second observed points from a warning target.
Zeng, et al., hereinafter Zeng (U.S. Patent Application Pub. No. 2016/0291149) teaches a first determination of whether the observed points include a pair of first and second observed points, the first observed point having a first relative velocity in a first direction approaching the vehicle, and the second observed point having a second relative velocity in a second direction moving away from the vehicle (Zeng, Para. 0057-0058 and 0062 – “a cluster of points”, such that there are two or more, which includes “some points moving toward the host vehicle 300 and some points moving away from the host vehicle 300”; where the relative velocity of the points is determined for a top and bottom of a remove wheel of another vehicle), and a second determination of whether a difference between azimuths of the respective first and second observed points of the pair relative to the vehicle observed by the sensor is smaller than or equal to a predetermined azimuth threshold in response to the first determination that the observed points include the pair of the first observed point and the second observed point (Zeng, Para. 0059-0062 – where each point in a cluster has an “azimuth angle” and the points are clustered based on “positional proximity (range r, and azimuth angle α)” and the difference between the azimuth angles are used for calculating “a distance between the two points” for clustering based on “calibration values”).
Kakita, et al., hereinafter Kakita (U.S. Patent Application Pub. No. 2017/0116461) teaches an eliminator configured to eliminate the first and second observed points from a warning target (Kakita, Para. 0054-0058, 0120, 0136, and 0149 – where, based on the “feature point groups”, such that there are two or more points, generated by the grouping unit, a “size determining unit” which deletes groups which are “smaller than a predetermined size”, such that all feature points in a “feature point group” associated with a “moving object” or “still object” are deleted; where additionally, feature point groups are removed based on their positions within a mask during a “masking process” which is based on distance).
However, Newman in view of Zeng and Kakita does not teach a third determination that the first and second observed points of the pair are observed points obtained from a fixed rotor in response to the second determination that the difference between the azimuths of the respective first and second observed points of the pair relative to the vehicle observed by the sensor is smaller than or equal to the predetermined azimuth threshold.
Trombley (U.S. Patent Application Pub. No. 2014/0025285) teaches a method for suppressing collision warnings for a vehicle, where the vehicle system receives position information from a remote vehicle and determines that the remote vehicle is in a safe-zone; where the positions of the vehicle and remote vehicle are measured by utilizing the absolute position of the vehicle and the range and azimuth of the remote vehicle, or possible collision object (Trombley, Para. 0005 and 0037). However, Trombley does not teach the missing limitations as listed above that are claimed in the pending claims.
Akamine (U.S. Patent Application Pub. No. 2019/0293778) teaches a method of estimating a distance between an object and a vehicle by utilizing radar-wave transmitting and receiving to calculated a distance and an azimuth of the object around the vehicle (Akamine, Para. 0003). Specifically, the estimating includes determining a difference between the azimuths of observed proximate points and determining whether the difference between the azimuths of the proximate points are less than a predetermined azimuth threshold (Akamine, Claims 4 and 5). However, Akamine does not teach the missing limitations as claimed in the pending claims. Specifically, Akamine does not teach a third determination that the first and second observed points of the pair are observed points obtained from a fixed rotor in response to the second determination that the difference between the azimuths of the respective first and second observed points of the pair relative to the vehicle observed by the sensor is smaller than or equal to the predetermined azimuth threshold.
Claims 13-17 are allowed.
