Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
In the event the determination of the status of the application as subject to AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 (or as subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103) is incorrect, any correction of the statutory basis (i.e., changing from AIA  to pre-AIA ) for the rejection will not be considered a new ground of rejection if the prior art relied upon, and the rationale supporting the rejection, would be the same under either status.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim(s) 1, 3, 9, and 11 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Newman, et al., hereinafter Newman (U.S. Patent Application Pub. No. 2017/0291602) in view of Aoki, et al., hereinafter Aoki (U.S. Patent No. 9,372,261).
Regarding Claim 1, Newman teaches: A mobile warning apparatus for issuing a warning in response to observation of a mobile object approaching a vehicle (Newman, Para. 0051 and 0123 – “a visual or acoustical or haptic alarm to let the driver know when a collision is imminent”; where the imminent collision is between the subject vehicle and a second vehicle), the mobile warning apparatus comprising:
a velocity calculator configured to calculate, based on observation information observed by a sensor installed in the vehicle (Newman, Para. 0013 – “sensors, mounted on the subject vehicle” which acquire sensor data of a second vehicle), one or more relative velocities of one or more observed points (Newman, Para. 0013 and 0034-0035 – where processor components, or calculator, determine position, velocity, or acceleration of the second vehicle based on the sensor data; where the processor component can calculate “the relative velocity between the two vehicles”; where the sensor data is observed data);
a position determiner configured to determine whether the observed points are in a predetermined proximity extent (Newman, Para. 0019 and 0034 – “a processor component” which determines if a second vehicle position will “come within a predetermined distance limit”, or proximity extent, of the subject vehicle; where the position data is from sensor data); and
an eliminator configured to eliminate, in response to determination that the observed points are in the predetermined proximity extent, all the observed points from a warning target.
While Newman teaches determination that the observed points are in a predetermined proximity extent, Newman does not teach an eliminator configured to eliminate, in response to determination that the observed points are in a predetermined proximity extent, all the observed points from a warning target.
However, Aoki teaches an eliminator configured to eliminate, in response to determination that the observed points are in a predetermined proximity extent, all the observed points from a warning target (Aoki, Col. 13 Line 44-Col. 14 Line 3 – where it is determined whether object data is within, for example, “a position below about 1.8 m”, or a predetermined proximity extent, such that it is within a travel lane with the subject vehicle and is considered a “control target”, such that it needs to be monitored).
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have modified the mobile warning apparatus of Newman to include an eliminator configured to eliminate, in response to determination that the observed points are in a predetermined extent, all the observed points from a warning target, as taught by Aoki, in order to remove irrelevant and erroneous data to both save data space as well as prevent the warning apparatus from inaccurately warning the vehicle using false or irrelevant data.
In regards to Claim 3, Newman in view of Aoki teaches the mobile warning apparatus according to Claim 1, and Newman teaches wherein: the position determiner (Newman, Para. 0019 and 0034 – “a processor component” which determines a second vehicle) is configured to:
determine whether differences among positions of the respective observed points observed by the sensor (Newman, Para. 0013 – “sensors, mounted on the subject vehicle” which acquire sensor data of a second vehicle) are smaller than or equal to a predetermined position threshold; and
determine that the observed points are in the predetermined proximity extent (Newman, Para. 0019 and 0034 – “a processor component” which determines if a second vehicle position will “come within a predetermined distance limit”, or proximity extent, of the subject vehicle; where the position data is from sensor data) in response to determination that the differences among the positions of the respective observed points observed by the sensor (Newman, Para. 0013 – “sensors, mounted on the subject vehicle” which acquire sensor data of a second vehicle) are smaller than or equal to the predetermined position threshold.
While Newman teaches, determining that an observed point is in the predetermined proximity extent, where the observed point is observed by a sensor, Newman does not teach determining whether differences among positions of the respective observed points are smaller than or equal to a predetermined position threshold, nor does it teach determining that observed points are in a proximity extent in response to determination that the differences among the positions of the respective observed points are smaller than or equal to the predetermined position threshold.
However, Aoki teaches determining whether differences among positions of the respective observed points are smaller than or equal to a predetermined position threshold (Aoki, Fig. 14-15 and Col. 15 Lines 6-25 and Col. 17 Lines 16-59 – where as shown on Fig. 14, there are two groups of data representing objects, and a third data point group GS, where it is determined that the third data group needs to be removed based on “removal conditions”, including a reference distance, or predetermined position threshold) and determining that observed points are in a proximity extent in response to determination that the differences among the positions of the respective observed points are smaller than or equal to the predetermined position threshold (Aoki, Col. 6 Lines 38-43, Col. 15 Lines 6-25 and Col. 17 Lines 16-59 – where a front vehicle, or obstacle, is maintained at “a constant distance”; where the data collected by radar representing the front vehicle undergoes the position difference determination cited above).
