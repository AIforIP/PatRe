Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 12 and 18 rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
The term “a fixed- rotor radius assumed based on commercially available fixed rotors” in claims 12 and 18 is a relative term which renders the claim indefinite. The term “a fixed- rotor radius assumed based on commercially available fixed rotors” is not defined by the claim, the specification does not provide a standard for ascertaining the requisite degree, and one of ordinary skill in the art would not be reasonably apprised of the scope of the invention. A radius that is assumed based on commercially available fixed rotors does not provide a definite standard of the size of the radius, as it is an assumption. Additionally, “commercially available fixed rotors” is also indefinite, as what is commercially available does not stay constant.

No. 2016/0291149) teaches a first determination of whether the observed points include a pair of first and second observed points, the first observed point having a first relative velocity in a first direction approaching the vehicle, and the second observed point having a second relative velocity in a second direction moving away from the vehicle (Zeng, Para. 0057-0058 and 0062 – “a cluster of points”, such that there are two or more, which includes “some points moving toward the host vehicle 300 and some points moving away from the host vehicle 300”; where the relative velocity of the points is determined for a top and bottom of a remove wheel of another vehicle), and a second determination of whether a difference between azimuths of the respective first and second observed points of the pair relative to the vehicle observed by the sensor is smaller than or equal to a predetermined azimuth threshold in response to the first determination that the observed points include the pair of the first observed point and the second observed point (Zeng, Para. 0059-0062 – where each point in a cluster has an “azimuth angle” and the points are clustered based on “positional proximity (range r, and azimuth angle α)” and the difference between the azimuth angles are used for calculating “a distance between the two points” for clustering based on “calibration values”).
Kakita teaches an eliminator configured to eliminate the first and second observed points from a warning target (Kakita, Para. 0054-0058, 0120, 0136, and 0149 – where, based on the “feature point groups”, such that there are two or more points, generated by the grouping unit, a “size determining unit” which deletes groups which are “smaller than a predetermined size”, such that all feature points in a “feature point group” associated with a “moving object” or “still object” are deleted; where additionally, feature point groups are removed based on their positions within a mask during a “masking process” which is based on distance).
However, Newman in view of Zeng and Kakita does not teach a third determination that the first and second observed points of the pair are observed points obtained from a fixed rotor in response to the second determination that the difference between the azimuths of the respective first and second observed points of the pair relative to the vehicle observed by the sensor is smaller than or equal to the predetermined azimuth threshold.
