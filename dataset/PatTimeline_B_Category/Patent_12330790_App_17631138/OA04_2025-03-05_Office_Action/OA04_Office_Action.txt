Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1, 4-5, 8-16, and 21-23 are allowed.
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
Regarding claim 1, Nicholas (US 10501188 B2) discloses an aircraft passenger seat unit (Nicholas, figures 7a-d, item 709), configured for use adjacent an aisle of an aircraft cabin (Nicholas, figures 1e and 7a-d, item 111, seat units adjacent aircraft aisle), wherein the seat unit comprises:
- a console (Nicholas, figure 7a-d, item 729)
- a seat (Nicholas, figure 7a-d, item 709),
- a footrest (Nicholas, figure 7b, item 726), and
- a movable panel (Nicholas, figure 7a-d, item 725, end wall made of a panel moves),
the console is positioned on an aisle side of the seat unit and is spaced apart from the footrest along a longitudinal axis of the aircraft cabin to define an access path between the console and the footrest, the access path providing passenger access into the seat unit from the aisle (Nicholas, figure 7a-d, item 733, access path between console and footrest);
the seat comprises a seat pan and backrest (Nicholas, figure 7c, items 715a and 715b, backrest and seat pan), the seat having an upright sitting configuration in which the backrest is substantially upright (Nicholas, figure 7a, item 715a, backrest is upright);
the seat is movable in the upright sitting configuration from a first position to a second position;
in the first position of the seat a front edge of the seat pan is spaced apart from the footrest such that the console is positioned between a lateral edge of the seat pan and the aisle and such that the lateral edge of the seat pan is spaced apart from the access path (Nicholas, figure 7a-d, items 715a-c, 726, and 729, seat spaced apart from the footrest in a first position; console between seat pan and aisle),
in the second position of the seat the front edge of the seat pan is arranged contiguously with the footrest such that the footrest and seat pan together form a continuous surface and such that the lateral edge of the seat pan is positioned in the access path, between the console and the footrest (Nicholas, figure 7a-d, items 715a-c and 726, leg rest bridges gap of the access path between the seat pan and the footrest arranged contiguously to form a continuous surface when extended); and
the moveable panel being movable with respect to the footrest between a closed position in which the movable panel at least partially encloses a side of the footrest (Nicholas, figure 7a, item 725, panel in closed configuration enclosing a side of the footrest) and an open position in which the movable panel at least partially exposes the side of the footrest such that, in use, when the panel is in the open position, a passenger can access the continuous surface formed by the footrest and seat pan from the aisle through the partially exposed side of the footrest and across the lateral edge of the seat pan (Nicholas, figure 7c, item 725, panel in open configuration exposing a side of the footrest and thereby allowing passenger access to surface including the footrest from the lateral edge towards the aisle), except:
wherein the seat unit does not include a leg rest.
Wallace (US 20130241247 A1) teaches a seat comprising a seat pan and backrest wherein the seat unit does not include a leg rest extending from the seat (Wallace, figure 2, item 16, seat including seat pan and backrest), the seat having an upright sitting configuration in which the backrest is substantially upright (Wallace, figure 2, item 16, backrest is upright);
the seat is movable in the upright sitting configuration from a first position in which the seat pan is spaced apart from a footrest (Wallace, figure 2, item 18, seat pan spaced apart from footrest) to a second position in which the seat pan arranged contiguously with the footrest such that the footrest and seat pan together form a surface (Wallace, figure 3, items 14 and 18, seat pan is contiguous with the footrest and forms a continuous surface with the footrest).
Nicholas and Wallace are both considered analogous art as they are both in the same field of aircraft seating. It would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the application for one of ordinary skill in the art to modify the seat of Nicholas with the seat forming a contiguous and continuous surface between the seat pan and the footrest of Wallace with a reasonable expectation of success in order to prevent occupant discomfort from having feet becoming wedged in a gap between the seat pan and footrest.
Alternatively, should applicant argue that Nicholas as modified by Wallace fails to teach the contiguous, continuous surface claimed above, O’Neil (US 11312494 B2) teaches a seat with a seat pan and legrest, wherein a front edge of the seat pan is arranged contiguously with the footrest such that the legrest and seat pan together form a continuous surface (O’Neil, claim 11).
Nicholas as modified by Wallace and O’Neil are both considered analogous art as they are both in the same field of aircraft seating. It would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the application for one of ordinary skill in the art to modify the arrangement of the seat pan and footrest of Nicholas with the seat forming a contiguous and continuous surface between the seat pan and a forward surface of Wallace with a reasonable expectation of success in order to prevent occupant discomfort from having feet becoming wedged in a gap between the seat pan and footrest.
Dowty (US 20200385125 A1) also teaches a seat unit wherein the seat unit does not include a leg rest extending from the seat;
wherein the seat comprises a seat pan without the leg rest directly extending form the seat pan;
wherein the seat is movable from a first position to a second position wherein in the second position of the seat the front edge of the seat pan touches the footrest such that the footrest and seat pan together from a surface without the leg rest (Dowty, figures 1 and 4b, item 106 and 114).
However, it would not have been obvious to modify the seat of Nicholas to remove the legrest, because there would be no reason to remove the legrest.
