9. (Previously Presented) A system for changing a driving mode of a vehicle, the system comprising:

receive the driving environment signal from the sensor;
determine a recommended driving mode based on the driving environment signal from the sensor;

REASONS FOR ALLOWANCE
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance.
Regarding independent claims 9-20, closest prior art Cengil et al (US 20170371507 A1), Breed et al. (US 20060284839 A1), and Mizuno (US 20150324006 A1) (hereinafter Cengil, Breed, Mizuno, respectively), taken either individually or in combination with other prior art of record fails to teach the claimed invention as a whole.
Cengil discloses A system for changing a driving mode of a vehicle, the system comprising:
[a steering wheel], a vehicle drive system, [a plurality of driving mode paddle shifters coupled to the steering wheel], a sensor configured to output a driving environment signal indicative of a characteristic of a driving environment of the vehicle, an output device, and an electronic control unit communicatively coupled to [the plurality of driving mode paddle shifters], the vehicle drive system, the sensor, and the output device, wherein the electronic control unit is configured to:
receive the driving environment signal from the sensor;
determine a recommended driving mode based on the driving environment signal from the sensor;
output a menu of driving mode options including the recommended driving mode with the output device in response to an operation of [one of the plurality of paddle shifters], each of the driving mode options referring to a set of parameters that adjust at least one of the vehicle's economy, responsiveness, or traction;
navigate the menu of the driving mode options of the vehicle using [one or more of the plurality of driving mode paddle shifters];
receive a drive mode shifting signal from [one or more of the plurality of driving mode paddle shifters] indicative of a selection of one of the driving mode options in the menu with [one or more of the plurality of driving mode paddle shifters]; and change a parameter of the vehicle drive system in order to change the driving mode of the vehicle in response to the drive mode shifting signal.
Breed teaches a steering wheel, a vehicle drive system, a plurality of driving mode paddle shifters coupled to the steering wheel, a sensor configured to output a driving environment signal indicative of a characteristic of a driving environment of the vehicle, an output device, and an electronic control unit communicatively coupled to the plurality of driving mode paddle shifters, the vehicle drive system, the sensor, and the output device… output a menu of driving mode options… with the output device in response to an operation of [one of ] the plurality of paddle shifters…; navigate the menu of the driving mode options of the vehicle using one or more of the plurality of driving mode paddle shifters; receive a drive mode shifting signal from one or more of the plurality of driving mode paddle shifters indicative of a selection of one of the driving mode options in the menu with one or more of the plurality of driving mode paddle shifters.
Mizuno teaches output device in response to an operation of one of the plurality of paddle shifters.
The combination of Cengil, Breed, and Mizuno fails to teach change one or more of the plurality of driving mode paddle shifters from a first role to a second role in response to the selection of the driving mode, wherein the first role is a driving mode selector role and the second role is different from the first role;
remain in a current driving mode in response to no selection of the driving mode. Specifically, applicant cited ¶[0031]-[0033] in support of this amendment, wherein the function of the paddle shifters fulfills the traditional role of gear shifting when the manual driving mode is selected. Further, the invention as claimed represents a unique combination of features. While the prior art of record teaches parts of the claimed invention, the combination of each limitation taught by the prior art of record does not yield an obvious combination that would result in the specific invention claimed. Thus, the prior art of record does not render the invention, as claimed, obvious.
