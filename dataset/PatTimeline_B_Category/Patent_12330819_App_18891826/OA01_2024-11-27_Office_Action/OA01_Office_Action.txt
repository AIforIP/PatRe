Drawings
The drawings are objected to under 37 CFR 1.83(a). The drawings must show every feature of the invention specified in the claims. In particular, each of independent Claims 1, 13, and 14 recite, “a set of thrusters comprising: i) a first thruster coupled to the body, ii) a second thruster coupled to the body, iii) a third thruster coupled to the body, and iv) a fourth thruster coupled to the body” and “each of the thrusters are configured to provide 6 DOF thruster firing” (emphasis added). The drawings appear to show thrusters with fixed outlets, but the drawings do not show the thrusters themselves as having structure for reorienting a position of the outlets. Instead, the drawings show the thrusters as being coupled to additional structure, such as extension arms, in order for the thrusters to be reoriented relative to the body of the servicer satellite. As such, the original drawings do not show the recited thrusters being “configured to provide 6 DOF thruster firing.” Therefore, the above-cited features must be shown or the features canceled from the claims. No new matter should be entered.
Corrected drawing sheets in compliance with 37 CFR 1.121(d) are required in reply to the Office action to avoid abandonment of the application. Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of the first paragraph of 35 U.S.C. 112(a):
(a) IN GENERAL.—The specification shall contain a written description of the invention, and of the manner and process of making and using it, in such full, clear, concise, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to which it pertains, or with which it is most nearly connected, to make and use the same, and shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor or joint inventor of carrying out the invention.
The following is a quotation of the first paragraph of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112:
The specification shall contain a written description of the invention, and of the manner and process of making and using it, in such full, clear, concise, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to which it pertains, or with which it is most nearly connected, to make and use the same, and shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor of carrying out his invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b) CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Independent Claims 1, 13 and 14 each recite, “each of the thrusters are configured to provide 6 DOF thruster firing.” It is noted that the present specification states that, “6 DOF refers to the six mechanical degrees of freedom of movement of a rigid body in three-dimensional space” (see Specification at 3). The present specification does not describe or define what “6 DOF thruster firing” encompasses or excludes, and the claims are rendered indefinite since “thruster firing” is an action rather than a structure. It is unclear what is required to meet the limitation of providing six degrees of freedom thruster firing. For example, does “6 DOF thruster firing” require that each thruster on its own has structure for reorienting itself with 6 DOF for firing in any orientation? Or does “6 DOF thruster firing” require that each thruster be capable of performing thruster firing that generates a thrust vector to reorient each respective thruster, or the body of the servicer satellite, with 6 DOF? For the purposes of examination, “6 DOF thruster firing” has been interpreted as requiring each thruster to have structure for reorienting itself with 6 DOF to perform thruster firing in any orientation.
Regarding Claim 13, line 10 recites, “the client satellite.” There is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim.
The dependent claims not specifically addressed above are rejected as being dependent upon a rejected base claim.
Claims 1-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(a) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), first paragraph, as failing to comply with the written description requirement. The claim(s) contains subject matter which was not described in the specification in such a way as to reasonably convey to one skilled in the relevant art that the inventor or a joint inventor, or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the inventor(s), at the time the application was filed, had possession of the claimed invention.
In particular, independent Claims 1, 13 and 14 each recite, “each of the thrusters are configured to provide 6 DOF thruster firing.” It is noted that the specification on page 7, line 14, on page 8, line 19, and on page 9, line 1 each state, “each of the thrusters are configured to provide 6 DOF thruster firing.” However, the specification does not describe what structures or actions are required by the limitation “6 DOF thruster firing.” As discussed above in the 35 U.S.C. 112(b) Section, it is unclear what the limitation “6 DOF thruster firing” encompasses and has been interpreted as requiring each thruster to have structure for reorienting itself with 6 DOF to perform thruster firing in any orientation.
The present specification does not describe any configurations or structures of the thrusters that allow each respective thruster to move and reorient itself with six degrees of freedom. Instead, the specification describes each thruster being attached to separate structures, such as extension arms and gimbals, to perform 6 DOF maneuvers (see Specification at pages 2 and 22). In other words, the specification does not reasonably disclose any thrusters that are on their own “configured to provide 6 DOF thruster firing.” Therefore, the original disclosure does not reasonably convey to one skilled in the relevant art that the inventor was in possession of the above claimed subject matter at the time of filing.
The dependent claims not specifically addressed above are rejected as being dependent upon a rejected base claim.
