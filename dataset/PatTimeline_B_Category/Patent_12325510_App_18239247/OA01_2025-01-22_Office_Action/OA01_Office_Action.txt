Claim Objections
Claim 50 is objected to because of the following informalities:
Claim 50 recites “performing ground operations with for aircraft” which should be corrected to “performing ground operations with the aircraft”.  Appropriate correction is required.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claim 50 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by Currey et al. (US 3,826,450).
- Regarding Claim 50. Currey discloses a method for kneeling (“K” illustrated in fig. 1-3) a landing gear assembly (11) of an aircraft (10), comprising:
performing ground operations with the aircraft (10, column 3 line 60 through column 4 line 2) in which the landing gear assembly is in a fully extended condition (“E” illustrated in fig. 1-3) and a retract actuator (26’) of the landing gear assembly (11) is in an extended piston position (illustrated by fig. 2-3);
activating a kneeling feature (27) of the landing gear assembly (11) to establish a kneel stop position (“K” illustrated in fig. 1-3) in the retract actuator (26’) that reduces a piston stroke for the retract actuator (26’) by inhibiting movement of a piston (19) in the retract actuator (26’) beyond the kneel stop position (K) toward a retracted piston position (“S” illustrated in fig. 1-3); and
moving the landing gear assembly (11) from the fully extended condition (“E”) to a kneeling condition (“K”) by moving the retract actuator (26’) from the extended piston position (“E”) to the kneel stop position (“K”; the movement is illustrated by fig. 1-3).
Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1-4, 6, 8, 12-15, 18, 20, 23-28, and 32 are allowed.
The following is an examiner' s statement of reasons for allowance:
Regarding Claim 1, the prior art of record fails to disclose or teach “a retract actuator, comprising: an actuator housing defining a longitudinal axis extending through a distal end and a proximal end, the actuator housing comprising a cylinder bore along the longitudinal axis; and a piston disposed within the cylinder bore and configured to move within the cylinder bore along the longitudinal axis, the cylinder bore defining a piston stroke within the actuator housing that defines limits for movement of the piston, the piston stroke comprising an extended piston position relating to the distal end of the actuator housing and a retracted piston position relating to the proximal end of the actuator housing; and a kneel stop assembly secured to an exterior surface of the actuator housing in relation to the extended piston position within the cylinder bore and the retracted piston position within the cylinder bore, the kneel stop assembly configured to selectively establish a kneel stop position within the cylinder bore to reduce the piston stroke by inhibiting movement of the piston to the retracted piston position” in combination with the rest of the limitations in the claim.  This limitation, in the apparatus as claimed in claim X is neither anticipated nor made obvious by the prior art of record.  Claims 2-4, 8, 12-15, 18, 20, and 23-28 depend from claim 1 and are therefore also found allowable.
Regarding Claim 32, the prior art of record fails to disclose or teach “a retract actuator, comprising: an actuator housing defining a longitudinal axis extending through a distal end and a proximal end, the actuator housing comprising a cylinder bore along the longitudinal axis; and a piston disposed within the cylinder bore and configured to move within the cylinder bore along the longitudinal axis, the cylinder bore defining a piston stroke within the actuator housing that defines limits for movement of the piston, the piston stroke comprising an extended piston position relating to the distal end of the actuator housing and a retracted piston position relating to the proximal end of the actuator housing; a kneel stop assembly secured to an exterior surface of the actuator housing in relation to the extended piston position within the cylinder bore and the retracted piston position within the cylinder bore, the kneel stop assembly configured to selectively establish a kneel stop position within the cylinder bore to reduce the piston stroke by inhibiting movement of the piston to the retracted piston position; a trailing arm comprising an arm proximal end and an arm distal end, the arm distal end configured to pivotally attach to a fixed point on the aircraft, the arm proximal end configured to attach to a surface contact assembly; and a shock absorber mechanically linked to the retract actuator and configured to pivotally attach to the trailing arm” in combination with the rest of the limitations in the claim.  This limitation, in the apparatus as claimed in claim 32 is neither anticipated nor made obvious by the prior art of record.
