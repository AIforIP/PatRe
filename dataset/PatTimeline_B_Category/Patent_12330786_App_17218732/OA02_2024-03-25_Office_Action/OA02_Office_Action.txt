Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
4.	`The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
5.	Claims 1-9, 12-14, 16-17 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Claim 1 first recites the limitation “a fixture to a monument provided for installation in a passenger cabin” as part of the intended use for “a fastening system” in the preamble of claim 1. However, claim 1 goes on to positively recite the “monument” and define “a wall portion” of the monument in line 3 of the claim. Further, claim 16 goes on to positively recite the “fixture” and define that the fixture comprises “a display”. Still further, claim 17 goes on to positively recite the passenger cabin and define it as being “of an aircraft”. It is therefore unclear from the preamble of claim 1 if Applicant is intending to positively recite the monument, the fixture, and the passenger cabin as features of the claimed fastening system, or if they are intended to be functional limitations directed to the fastening system. Examiner recognizes from the instant disclosure that the claimed invention is directed to “A fastening system”, which comprises the “wall portion of the monument” as part of the disclosed fastening system. Further, it is evident within the disclosed fastening system that the fixture is retained between the claimed wall portion and claimed frame of the fastening system. As such, Examiner will interpret that the fixture and monument are intended to be positively recited within the claim and will interpret the preamble of claim 1 reading as: “A fastening system

A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –

Regarding claim 1, Seeley discloses a fastening system (Fig. 1-3) attaching a fixture (205) to a monument (120, monument is back of a seat, see Col. 2 lines 14-16) provided for installation in a passenger cabin (monument is installed within a passenger vehicle, i.e. cabin, see Col. 1 lines 49-51), comprising:
a wall portion (215) of the monument which has at least one first fastening element (240); and
a frame (210) which has at least one second fastening element (225) and which is configured to cover the fixture at least in part in a fastened state (fixture 205 is disposed between, i.e. covered, frame and wall portion; see Col. 2 line 24-29);
wherein the first fastening element (240) and the second fastening element (225) are configured to engage with one another (see Fig. 3; see Col. 2 lines 43-45) and be movable relative to one another in a pre-assembled state (prior to assembly of frame and wall portions, first and second fastening elements can move relative to one another); and to fix within one another in the fastened state (see Col. 2 lines 43-45),
wherein the fastened state can be produced in that, in the pre-assembled state, the frame is moved relative to the wall portion in a fastening direction which extends at least substantially parallel to a main extension plane of the wall portion (frame is capable of being moved upward in a fastening direction relative to the wall portion, which is parallel to a main extension plane of the wall portion, to produce the fastened state such that fastening elements 225, 240 engage).

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
8.	The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
9.	Claims 7-9 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Chavarria et al. (US 11,365,006; hereinafter Chavarria).
Regarding claim 7, Seeley does not explicitly disclose of a locking unit which, in a locked state, is configured to prevent a release of the fastened state, and in a released state, is configured to allow the release of the fastened state.
Chavarria (Fig. 1-7; see embodiment of Fig. 6) teaches of a similar fastening system, wherein a fixture (626) is attached to a monument (104) within a passenger cabin (see Col. 1 lines 4-5) via the fastening system, wherein the fastening system comprises a wall portion (122) and a frame (634), such that the frame is configured to cover and retain the fixture (see Fig. 6), wherein the fastening system comprises locking units each having a first opening (see openings in 634 that are aligned to receive blocking elements 664) within the frame, a blocking element (664), a second opening within the wall portion (638 have openings to receive blocking elements; see Col. 6 line 65 – Col. 7 line 15), wherein the blocking element extends through the first and second openings to produce a locked state and thereby secures the frame to the wall portion and mechanically prevents a movement of the frame relative to the wall portion (as the blocking element secures frame 634 to wall portion 122 as described in Col. 6 line 65 – Col. 7 line 15).
It would be obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art prior to the effective filing date of the claimed invention to modify Seeley with the teachings of Chavarria, to have the fastening system further comprise locking units, such that each locking unit comprises a first opening in the frame, a blocking element, and a second opening within the wall portion, wherein the blocking element extends through the first and second openings to produce a locked state and thereby secure the frame to the wall portion and mechanically prevent movement of the frame relative to the wall portion. In doing so, the locked state would thereby be capable of preventing a release of the frame from the wall portion when in the fastened state. Also, in a released (unlocked) state, the frame would thereby be capable of being released from the wall portion to all for the releasee of the fastened state.
Regarding claim 8, the combination of Seeley and Chavarria further teach wherein the locking unit comprises at least one blocking element which, in the locked state, mechanically prevents a movement of the frame relative to the wall portion (as taught in claim 7).
Regarding claim 9, the combination of Seeley and Chavarria further teach wherein the frame has a first opening (as taught in claim 7),
wherein the wall portion has a second opening which aligns with the first opening in the fastened state (as taught in claim 7), and
wherein the blocking element extends through the first opening and the second opening, at least in part, in the locked state (as taught in claim 7).
