Claim Objections
Claims 1-20 are objected to because of the following informalities: the claims use both “pushrod” and “push rod”, please maintain consistency.  Appropriate correction is required.
Claim 15 objected to because of the following informalities: line 4, recites “an aircraft” which should read as --the aircraft-- so as to maintain proper dependency.  Appropriate correction is required.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1-20 rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Claims 1 and 15 attempt to positively connect parts of the propulsor assembly to the aircraft but the claim is only to the propulsor assembly.  It is unclear if only the propulsor assembly is required by the claim limitations or if the whole aircraft is being claimed.  The Examiner suggest claiming the aircraft comprising a propulsor assembly so as to positively claim the aircraft.  As the claims are unclear, the claims and the dependent claims 2-14 and 16-20 are indefinite.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claim(s) 1, 4-8, 11, and 12 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by Chribkow (EP #1944234).
For Claim 1, the figure of Chribkow ‘234 discloses a propulsion assembly for an aircraft, the propulsion assembly comprising: a propulsor (13) connected to the aircraft and a cyclic control assembly, wherein the cyclic control assembly comprises: an actuator, wherein the actuator is an electric actuator; and a pushrod (23), wherein the push rod is mechanically connected to the actuator and the propulsor; the push rod is configured to increase a blade angle of the propulsor when the push rod is displaced in a first direction; and the push rod is configured to decrease the blade angle of the propulsor when the push rod is displaced in a second direction.
For Claim 4, the figure of Chribkow ‘234 discloses that the cyclic control assembly further comprises a swashplate rocker (25), wherein the swashplate rocker mechanically connects the pushrod and the actuator.
For Claim 5, the figure of Chribkow ‘234 discloses that the swashplate rocker is configured to: translate movement by the actuator in a first actuation direction into movement of the pushrod in the first direction; and translate movement by the actuator in a second actuation direction into movement of the pushrod in the second direction.
For Claim 6, the figure of Chribkow ‘234 discloses that the cyclic control assembly further comprises a propulsor rocker (26), wherein the propulsor rocker is mechanically connected to the pushrod and the propulsor.
For Claim 7, the figure of Chribkow ‘234 discloses that the propulsor rocker (26) is configured to translate movement by the pushrod (23) in the first direction to an increase in blade angle of the propulsor; and translate movement by the pushrod in the second direction to a decreased in blade angle of the propulsor.
For Claim 8, the figure of Chribkow ‘234 discloses a propulsor link (24), wherein: the propulsor rocker comprises a first propulsor rocker end and a second propulsor rocker end; the second propulsor rocker end is attached to the pushrod; and the first propulsor rocker end is attached to the propulsor link.
For Claim 11, the figure of Chribkow ‘234 discloses that the propulsor comprises: a hub (12), wherein the hub is mechanically connected to a shaft; and a plurality of blades, wherein each of the plurality of blades are connected to and extend outward from the hub.
For Claim 12, the figure of Chribkow ‘234 discloses two blades.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim(s) 2-3, 10-11, 14-18, and 20 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Chribkow (EP #1944234).
For Claim 2, the figure of Chribkow ‘234 discloses the propulsor is mechanically connected to the rotor using a shaft (11), wherein the shaft includes a proximal end and a distal end mechanically coupled to the propulsor; the shaft has a central lumen running from the proximal end to the distal end; and the pushrod (23) extends at least partially through the central lumen.  While the figure of Chribkow ‘234 discloses a (7-9) connected to a mechanical motor, it is silent about the motor being an electrical motor.  The Examiner takes Official Notice that it is well known in the art to use electric motors with a stator and rotor, instead of mechanical motors as they create more torque and don’t require a large and heavy fuel system.  Therefore it would have been obvious to someone of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date to modify Chribkow ‘234 with an electric motor.  The motivation to do so would be to provide increase torque and less structural components.
For Claim 3, the figure of Chribkow ‘234 discloses that the pushrod (23) runs parallel to a rotational axis of the rotor of the motor.
For Claim 9, while Chribkow ‘234 teaches the ability to use electric actuators to control the increase of decrease of blade angle, it is silent about a flight controller connected to the actuator to control the blade angle.  However, the Examiner takes Official Notice that it is well known in the art to use a flight controller as they are common in the art to accurately control an aircraft in real time.  Therefore it would have been obvious to someone of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date to modify Chribkow ‘234 with a flight controller.  The motivation to do so would be to accurately control the aircraft in real time.
For Claim 10, while Chribkow ‘234 is silent about having a monolithic propulsor, the Examiner takes Official Notice that it is well known to have a monolithic construction as they are more aerodynamic, stronger, and have less parts.  Therefore it would have been obvious to someone of ordinary skill in the art at the time of the invention to modify Chribkow ‘234 with a monolithic construction.  The motivation to do so would be to reduce parts and create a more aerodynamic propulsor.
For Claims 14 and 16, while Chribkow ‘234 is silent about a flap mechanism, the Examiner takes Official Notice that it is well known in the art to have a flap mechanism as they allow rotors to automatically adjusting rotor lift to maintain a desired lift.  Therefore it would have been obvious to someone of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date to modify Chribkow ‘234 with a flap mechanism.  The motivation to do so would be to provide automatic control the blade pitch.
For Claims 15, 17, and 18, the figure of Chribkow ‘234 discloses the propulsor is mechanically connected to the rotor using a shaft (11), wherein the shaft includes a proximal end and a distal end mechanically coupled to the propulsor; the shaft has a central lumen running from the proximal end to the distal end; and the pushrod (23) extends at least partially through the central lumen.  While the figure of Chribkow ‘234 discloses a (7-9) connected to a mechanical motor, it is silent about the motor being an electrical motor.  The Examiner takes Official Notice that it is well known in the art to use electric motors with a stator and rotor, instead of mechanical motors as they create more torque and don’t require a large and heavy fuel system.  Therefore it would have been obvious to someone of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date to modify Chribkow ‘234 with an electric motor.  The motivation to do so would be to provide increase torque and less structural components.
While Chribkow ‘234 is silent about a flap mechanism, the Examiner takes Official Notice that it is well known in the art to have blade flap mechanisms that have a base, a hinge, and a spring that allows for automatically adjusting rotor lift to maintain a desired lift.  Therefore it would have been obvious to someone of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date to modify Chribkow ‘234 with a flap mechanism.  The motivation to do so would be to provide automatic control the blade pitch.
For Claim 20, while Chribkow ‘234 discloses a plurality of pilot controls connected to control the propulsor assembly, it is silent about the actuator being mechanically disconnect from the flight controls and a flight controller.  However, the Examiner takes Official Notice that it is well known in the art to use a flight controller as they are common in the art to accurately control an aircraft in real time and to have flight controls be mechanically disconnected from the actuator so as to be controlled by the flight controller.  Therefore it would have been obvious to someone of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date to modify Chribkow ‘234 with a flight controller.  The motivation to do so would be to accurately control the aircraft in real time.
