Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Claims 1 – 20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because the claimed invention is directed to an abstract idea a mental process of evaluation without significantly more.
The claims recite a system. The claims are evaluated under the 2019 Subject Matter Guidance. The claims state of the four statutory categories by claiming a system. The claims are evaluated against examples 40 and 42 of the guidance. The independent claim is listed below with the abstract idea components in italics and the post solution activity in bold.
A system, comprising:
an input;
an output;
a first software component for use in configuring operation of a second software component via a proxy and stub interface;
a non-transitory memory component comprising:
a first memory space comprising the first software component, a proxy software component and an activity monitor software component;
and
a second memory space comprising the second software component and a stub software component; and
at least one processor for executing software components stored on the first and second memory spaces, wherein the activity monitor software component is arranged when executed to:
cause the input to monitor a first event associated with the first software component;
cause the input to monitor a second event associated with the proxy software component, wherein the second event includes a command event at the proxy software component;
assess compliance of the first and second events with a schedule of events, wherein assessing compliance includes checking order and/or timing of the first and second events; and
cause the output to generate an error message in response to assessed non-compliance with the schedule of events.
The features in italics do not identify what is monitored in the first or second events. The claim does not identify what constitutes an activity. Therefore, a driver of a vehicle may witness the performance of a vehicle and then mentally determine the performance of the vehicle isn’t commensurate with what the driver intended or expected. The claim and drawings do not define what an error message conveys or to whom it is communicated and how it is communicated. Furthermore, the dependent claims do not identify the purpose of the features. Dependent claim 6 states the vehicle system is a cruise control system and dependent claim 7 states the second software component comprises a drivetrain component. Those claims do not identify what is monitored and what the error message may convey about the cruise control. Thus the claims fail prong 1.
This judicial exception is not integrated into a practical application because the claims fail to identify what is monitored about the vehicle cruise control system and what aspect of the drivetrain is monitored. Therefore, the error message does not have context as to what aspect of the vehicle system is not performing properly. Thus, the claims fail prong 2A.
The claims do not include additional elements that are sufficient to amount to significantly more than the judicial exception because the claims fail to identify what aspects of the cruise control are monitored and how the drivetrains performance is correlated to the operation of the cruise control. Therefore, it isn’t clear what data is input into the monitoring system, what threshold it is measured against, and then how an error message is conveyed and to whom. The operations are devoid of showing how the claimed invention is uses. Therefore, it fails prong 2B.

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
In the event the determination of the status of the application as subject to AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 (or as subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103) is incorrect, any correction of the statutory basis (i.e., changing from AIA  to pre-AIA ) for the rejection will not be considered a new ground of rejection if the prior art relied upon, and the rationale supporting the rejection, would be the same under either status.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
The factual inquiries for establishing a background for determining obviousness under 35 U.S.C. 103 are summarized as follows:
1. Determining the scope and contents of the prior art.
2. Ascertaining the differences between the prior art and the claims at issue.
3. Resolving the level of ordinary skill in the pertinent art.
4. Considering objective evidence present in the application indicating obviousness or nonobviousness.
This application currently names joint inventors. In considering patentability of the claims the examiner presumes that the subject matter of the various claims was commonly owned as of the effective filing date of the claimed invention(s) absent any evidence to the contrary.  Applicant is advised of the obligation under 37 CFR 1.56 to point out the inventor and effective filing dates of each claim that was not commonly owned as of the effective filing date of the later invention in order for the examiner to consider the applicability of 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(2)(C) for any potential 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2) prior art against the later invention.
Claims 10 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Mueller US 2002/0194551 in view of Moellmann US 2014/0343817.
As per claim 10, The system of claim 9, wherein the sensor component is a wheel speed sensor component. (Moellmann paragraph 0021 teaches, “the plausibility block in the second level of the control unit receives, either directly or by way of a digital bus, information such as the rotational wheel speed values or the acceleration determined from the rotational wheel speeds, as input signal(s). The rotational wheel speeds are acquired by way of corresponding sensors in a known manner anyhow, for example, for a slip control.”)
Mueller discloses a method and device for monitoring control units. Mueller does not disclose collecting the wheel speed of a vehicle for identifying the performance of a system. Moellmann teaches of collecting the wheel speed of a vehicle for identifying the performance of a system. Therefore, at the time of filing it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art to incorporate the teachings of Moellmann et.al. into the invention of Mueller. Such incorporation is motivated by the need to ensure accurate identification of the performance of the control system controlling the vehicle components.
Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed 06/24/2024 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
With respect to applicant’s arguments regarding the section 101 rejection. The applicant has not overcome the rejection with the amendments to the claims. The features added to do not identify what is monitored and with the system. The applicant has not identified what the error message reflects and how it is conveyed and to whom.
With respect to the section 112 rejections, both are withdrawn.
With respect to the section 103 rejection. The rejection is withdrawn as the amended claim required a new search to identify the features added that are substantive with respect to structure and function. The new art of record anticipates the claimed invention with respect to claims 1 – 9, 11 – 20. The art of record performs the functions as claimed with the structure as understood based upon the specification and drawings. The proxy and stub components are software components defined in the specification paragraph 0040 that are anticipated by the art of record in performing functionally the same operations through software. With respect to claim 10 a second art of record is applied as the feature is made obvious when combined with the primary art of record.
