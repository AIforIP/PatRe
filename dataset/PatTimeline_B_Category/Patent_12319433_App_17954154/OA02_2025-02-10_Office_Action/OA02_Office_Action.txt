The application has been amended as follows:
Claim 1 (Proposed Amended) A method of determining a steering angle of a landing gear assembly of an aircraft, wherein the landing gear assembly comprises one or more wheels, and a lidar system is mounted inside a landing gear space above the one or more wheels of the landing gear assembly, wherein the landing gear assembly comprises a torque link, the method comprising steps of:
scanning the torque link of the landing gear assembly with the lidar system to generate a set of three-dimensional position data points, wherein each three-dimensional position data point comprises a set of three orthogonal position values;
generating a two-dimensional image from the set of three-dimensional position data points, by converting
identifying a boundary of an area of the two-dimensional image with the same image property values, wherein the area corresponds to a component of the landing gear assembly;
determining the steering angle of the landing gear assembly from a shape and/or orientation of the identified boundary, and
using the determined steering angle of the landing gear assembly to extend or retract the landing gear assembly.
REASONS FOR ALLOWANCE
Claims 1-18 are allowed.
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
In regards to independent claim 1, the claim recites in particular:
a lidar system is mounted inside a landing gear space above the one or more wheels of the landing gear assembly,
scanning the torque link of the landing gear assembly with the lidar system,
determining the steering angle of the landing gear assembly from a shape and/or orientation of the identified boundary.
No single prior art reference has been found to anticipate the claim as a whole, including the above particular limitations, nor has any combination of prior art references been found that would render these limitations obvious, when viewed in the context of the remaining limitations of the claim. As such, independent claim 1 is found to be allowable.
Independent claims 16 and 18 recite similar features and therefore are found to be allowable for the same reasons.
The dependent claims depend upon an allowable base claim and therefore are found to be allowable by virtue of their dependency, as well as the limitations recited within.

Conclusion
The prior art made of record
and not relied upon is considered pertinent to applicant's disclosure:
Kretschmann et al. (DE-19808836-A1) teaches a method for identifying the aircraft type and recognizing the nose wheel steering angle using laser beam.
Wilson et al. (GB-2587416-A) teaches using microwaves for determining the surface and/or subsurface structural condition of non-metallic components and the condition of aircraft landing gear tires.
Gudarzi et al. (US-20220396322-A1) teaches a conversion function using a software module including multiple lookup tables to determine a best fit three-dimensional wheelbase length.
Avitzur (EP-2886465-A1) teaches a method and system which enables to determine the steering-angle between the longitudinal axis of a tractor and the longitudinal axis of an aircraft which is being led by the tractor by using scanners such as pulse laser scanner.
Franjou et al. (US-20190092493-A1) teaches a system for monitoring steering of an aircraft landing gear wheel.
Ferrell et al. (US-20200290750-A1) teaches a monitoring system and method are provided for real time monitoring of flaps, landing gears, or tail skids to determine safe taxi, takeoff, and flight readiness. Monitoring units, including scanning LiDAR devices combined with cameras or sensors, mounted in aircraft exterior locations produce a stream of meshed data that is securely transmitted to a processing system to generate a real time visual display of the flaps, landing gears, or tail skid for communication to aircraft pilots to ensure safe aircraft taxi, takeoff, and flight readiness.
Cherepinsky et al. (US-20170139045-A1) teaches a system and method for determining weight on wheels for an aircraft with at least one landing gear using LiDAR.
Non-patent Literature He et al. “A Novel Way to Organize 3D LiDAR Point Cloud as 2D Depth Map Height Map and Surface Normal Map” teaches creating a 2D perceptual information from 3D LiDAR point cloud.
Non-patent Literature Fang et al. “Automatic registration of airborne LiDAR point cloud data and optical imagery depth map based on line and points features” teaches the mathematical morphology filter to preprocess point cloud data.
