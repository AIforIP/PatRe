Alvarez discloses: A system for touchless control (via sensors 940 most if not all of which operate without being touched and include a touchless motion sensor as stated in the first sentence of paragraph 81) of a lavatory (102) of an aircraft (1000), including a light control system (900) with visible lights and/or control actuators (938, 800, 802) and an actuator (936) for UV light (910):
substantially as claimed but does not disclose that the central controller is further configured to control the at least one actuator or light source to cause a total power draw of the system to remain at or below a power threshold corresponding to a maximum amount of power available to the system. However, Callahan teaches another aircraft (210) also with UV lights (118, 120) in a lavatory (122) having
the total power draw of the system to remain at or below a power threshold corresponding to a maximum amount of power available to the system as taught in paragraphs 48 and 4-6; for the purpose of ensuring the UV lighting system does not affect the operation of the aircraft. Therefore, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art at the time of the effective filing date of the claimed invention to provide the controller of Alvarez with a threshold power draw limit as, for example, taught by Callahan in order to ensure the UV lighting system does not affect the operation of the aircraft.

Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed 05/23/2022 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive. In the arguments Applicant repeats the claim language on page 8 and the first paragraph of page 9 and then contends in the first paragraph of page 9 that Alvarez does not contemplate or disclose making an inference as recited by claim 1. However, there is no specific reasoning presented in the first paragraph of page 9, merely the statement. No specific definition of the term “inference” has been found in the specification which would define over what Alvarez is disclosing. As stated in the rejection, the sensors and sequence of events or actions as claimed and as disclosed in Alvarez appear to anticipate the language of the rejected claims. The first paragraph on page 9 of the remarks merely disagrees with the rejection without a specific reason, page 8 merely repeats the claim language.
Applicant contends that Callahan and Nguyen do not overcome the deficiencies in Alvarez with respect to the inference. However, as noted above, the rejection over Alvarez does not have any deficiencies.
