a plurality of touchless sensors located in the lavatory, wherein each of the plurality of touchless sensors is configured to receive data corresponding to the lavatory (116, 118, 120, 122 and additional sensors 940 as disclosed in paragraph 81);
an actuator located in the lavatory and configured to perform a first action (actuator 936 which actuates or turns on UV light 910 for a or first action as shown in figure 11, or the actuators in the detection control unit 114 which control cleaning of the lavatory as disclosed in paragraphs 43 and 44 and actuation of the lights in figure 10 indicating the occupancy status plus actuation of UV light 910);
a light source located in the lavatory (UV light 910) and configured to perform a second action (killing microbes as disclosed in paragraph 69); and
a central controller (100, 102, 114, 900) coupled to the plurality of touchless sensors, the actuator, and the light source (as shown in figures 1 and 112), the central controller configured to make an inference as to a portion of the lavatory that was used based on the data received from each of the plurality of touchless sensors (sensors 112-122 as shown in figure 1) and to control at least one of the actuator and the light source based on the inference (as disclosed in paragraphs 43, 44 actuators which
control cleaning of the lavatory are operated such as the toilet and sink and also light 910 is turned on or actuated):
substantially as claimed but does not disclose that the central controller is further configured to determine a health status of the system, the health status including a remaining life of the ultraviolet light source, and to control the ultraviolet light source further based on the health status. However, Childress teaches another aircraft lavatory as shown in figures 18, 19, 23, 24, 35 and 39 also a with UV light source (104, 104a, 104b), and a central controller (118, 620) having the controller (118) determine the radiance and power levels of the UV light sources and controls them for specific time periods wherein since the controller of Childress is able to measure both time and power levels of UV sources as taught in paragraphs 165 and 166, Childress has the ability to, or is “configured to as claimed, determine the remaining life health status of the UV lamps for the purpose of more efficiently controlling the power usage of the UV lights. Therefore, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art at the time of the effective filing date of the claimed invention to provide the lavatory controller of Alvarez with the ability to determine the radiance and power levels of the UV light sources and control them for specific time periods, be able to measure both time and power levels of UV sources and have the ability to, or be “configured to as claimed, determine the remaining life health status of the UV lamps as, for example, taught by Childress in order to more efficiently control the power usage of the UV lights.
