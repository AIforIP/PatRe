Reasons for Allowance
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance: The claims are found allowable because the prior art does not teach a method for applying an aerodynamically functional film to a body around which complex flow occurs as claimed (“complex flow” being interpreted, in light of the specification, as referring to a flow profile which cannot be reasonably approximated by a parallel flow profile), and particularly comprising the combination of determining flow lines on a surface of the body on a basis of a computer model of the body using a numerical flow simulation; locating at least one coherent field (interpreted, in light of the specification, as referring to a continuous region without internal gaps) in a developed surface segment of the computer model having local flow directions readable at flow lines in a predetermined angle range around a main flow direction of the respective field; and applying the aerodynamically functional film to the body within the field boundary of the at least one field such that the film is aligned along the main flow direction of the respective field.  While it is generally known to provide aerodynamically functional surfaces, e.g., riblets, to bodies such as aircraft, the prior art generally does not teach adapting these to a particular modeled surface segment or to complex flow.  Leitl teaches choosing riblet spacing based on, e.g., a calculated overall average flow velocity, but does not teach applying this approach to a complex (i.e., non-parallel) flow at a particular (local) surface segment for which local flow directions around a main flow direction have been modeled and aligning the applied film correspondingly.  The prior art elsewhere does not suggest a similar method involving a local computer model and an applied aerodynamically functional film with alignment determined thereby, such that the claims are found to distinguish from the prior art.
