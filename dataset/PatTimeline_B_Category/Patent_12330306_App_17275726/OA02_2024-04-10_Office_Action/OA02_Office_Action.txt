Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed on 12/12/2023 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive. Examiner has augmented the prior art rejections in light of Applicant's amendments and/or arguments, as indicated below.
Regarding claim 1, Applicant argues that the Orita fails to teach or suggest applying the torque to the motor axle resulting in rotation of the motor axle and the output axle, and determining the stiffness of the gear based on at least the angular position of the motor axle, the angular position of the output axle.
Klimchik teaches a method of obtaining stiffness of a gear of a joint with a motor. Klimchik also teaches determining stiffness of the gear based at least on angular position of the motor, angular position of the output and the dynamic model.  However, Klimchik fails to explicitly teach the motor has an axle which rotates the output axle and torque is applied to the motor axle as well as the dynamic model defines relationship between forces acting on the robot and resulting acceleration.
Orita is not being relied upon for the teaching of determining the stiffness of the gear. Orita in the same field of endeavor, is being relied upon the teaching of having a robot device having a motor with an axle which rotates the output axle and torque is applied to the motor axle as well as the dynamic model defines relationship between forces acting on the robot and resulting acceleration. Incorporating the features taught by Orita would provide predictable solution of obtaining stiffness of the gear of the joint of a robot based on at least the angular position of the motor axle, the angular position of the output axle.
Furthermore, Orita teaches applying the torque to the motor axle resulting in rotation of the motor axle and the output axle (Fig 1, para 0077-0080 wherein the shaft 2b can be considered a motor axle connected to the electric motor 2, and shaft 3b can be considered the output axle. The torque generated by the motor 2 results in the rotation of the motor axle 2b and output axle 3b using speed reducer/gear 3; “[0077] An output shaft 2b as a power output unit of the electric motor 2 is connected with an input shaft (an input unit) 3a of the speed reducer 3 so as to be capable of rotating integrally with the output shaft 2b”).
Claim Objections
Claims 35 is objected to because of the following informalities:
Regarding claim 35, the claim recites “fitting, to a mathematical function, the flexibility torque and the deflection of the gear obtained, where the mathematical function comprises a polynomial function obtained based on a recursive least squares estimation or the stiffness of the gear is based on a slope of the flexibility torque relative to the deflection of the gear or the stiffness of the gear is based on a slope of the flexibility torque relative to the deflection of the gear”. It appears to contain redundant language.  Based on the claim language, the claim should read, “fitting, to a mathematical function, the flexibility torque and the deflection of the gear obtained, where the mathematical function comprises a polynomial function obtained based on a recursive least squares estimation or the stiffness of the gear is based on a slope of the flexibility torque relative to the deflection of the gear”.
Appropriate correction is required.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claim 40-41 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards
as the invention.
Regarding claim 40, the claim language recites “The method of claim 1, where the robotic arm comprises joints including the joint comprising the gear, where a combination of the joints are for connecting a base and a tool flange, and wherein the method further comprises: controlling the joint based on the dynamic model and the stiffness of the gear”. This is duplicate claim language that is identical to the language of claim 11.
Regarding claim 41, the claim language recites “The method of claim 42, further comprising: modifying the dynamic model based on the stiffness of the gear”. However, there is no claim 42 present in the application therefore the dependency of the claim is not clear.
Appropriate corrections are required.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claims 1, 3, 11, 13-16, 18, 19 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Klimchik (IFAC 2018, “Robotic manipulators with double encoders: accuracy improvement based on advanced stiffness modeling and intelligent control”) hereinafter Klimchik in view of Orita (US 20120239198 A1) hereinafter Orita.
Regarding claim 1, Klimchik teaches method of obtaining a stiffness of a gear of a joint on a robotic arm, where the joint is connectable to at least one other joint on the robotic arm (Fig 1, page 741, col 1 Experimental setup section wherein robotic arm with joints connected via robotic components is disclosed; “In more details, the system includes the following components:  Robot manipulator with double encoders at each actuated joint”),
where the joint comprises a motor (Fig 1, page 741, col 1 Experimental setup section, page 741 col 2 1st para wherein each joint has a motor with encoders; “In more details, the system includes the following components:  Robot manipulator with double encoders at each actuated joint”; “In contrast, robots with double encoders provide us the additional possibility to carry out the same experiments with joint feedback on the primary (motor-side) and secondary (link-side) encoders”), and where the method comprises:
obtaining an angular position of the motor (Page 740, col 2 end- page 741 col 1, page 743 col 2 1st para wherein the motor side encoder provides angular position of motor “joint position feedback is implemented using the motor-side encoder …”; “a combination of the primary and secondary encoders that provides the difference between the angles on the motor-side and on the link-side”);
obtaining an angular position of the output (page 741 col 1 1st para, page 743 col 2 1st para wherein link side encoders provide angular position of the output link; “a new technique allows using additional information from the secondary encoder mounted on the manipulator link-side”; “a combination of the primary and secondary encoders that provides the difference between the angles on the motor-side and on the link-side”); and
determining the stiffness of the gear based on at least the angular position of the motor, the angular position of the output (page 741 col 1 1st para, page 742 col 2 3rd last para wherein the stiffness model parameters and joint compliance errors caused by gear elasticity are determined using encoder readings; “It is clear that the difference between the motor-side and link-side encoder readings provide additional information allowing essentially improve the robot positioning accuracy. Usually, the double encoder schema is used to compensate the joint compliance errors (caused by the actuator and gear elasticity). In this paper, we address a more general problem: to compensate, using the double encoders, the compliances errors caused by both joint and link elasticities”; “For the robots with double encoders, there are additional data for the identification of the stiffness model parameters: primary and secondary encoders readings”), and the dynamic model (Fig 3-4 page 742 col 2 last para wherein the dynamic model of the robot with various deflections is used for stiffness model; “It should be stressed that if link masses are not negligible (and provoke essential compliance errors) definition of ∆qi should be clarified”; “Fig. 3. Efficiency of embedded and proposed compliance error compensation algorithms for the experimental set (a) and for entire robot workspace (b) (results corresponds to the model of industrial robot”). Examiner notes that  in case the dynamic properties of the robot, i.e. the masses and the inertia properties of its links and the friction of its joints, are considered influential in the calculation of the gear stiffness of the joints, the terms ∆qi, would then be calculated by taking into account said contributions, which would lead to the use of a robot dynamic model as well known in the field of robotics, in which the accelerations of the robot's joints are in a relationship with the forces applied on the robot itself.
Klimchik also teaches having gear with elasticity (Page 741 col 1 1st para wherein “Usually, the double encoder schema is used to compensate the joint compliance errors (caused by the actuator and gear elasticity)”. Furthermore, Klimchik teaches angular deflection vector ∆qi and kinematic Jacobian Ji for the i-th measurement configuration (page 742, col 2 “Stiffness model parameters identification from joint encoders measurements” section, Equation 7, wherein “Ag, is a vector of angular deflections due to the external force F; applied to the end-effector, and J; is kinematic Jacobian for the i-th measurement configuration”). Examiner notes a force applied to the robot in the workspace at the i-th instance of the algorithm Fi is mapped by means of the transpose Jacobian function JT to the joint space; from that equation, which is a static equation, it follows that a torque must be implicitly applied to a motor axle in order to counter-balance the effect of the external forces.
However, Klimchik fails to explicitly teach that the motor has an axle which rotates an output axle through a gear and torque is applied to the motor axle and applying the torque to the motor axle resulting in rotation of the motor axle and the output axle. Klimchik also fails to explicitly teach that the dynamic model defines a relationship between forces acting on the robot and resulting accelerations of the robot.
Orita teaches that the motor has an axle which rotates an output axle through a gear (Fig 1, para 0077 wherein the motor output shaft 2b rotates the output shaft 3b through the gear 3; “[0077] An output shaft 2b as a power output unit of the electric motor 2 is connected with an input shaft (an input unit) 3a of the speed reducer 3 so as to be capable of rotating integrally with the output shaft 2b. An output shaft (an output unit) 3b of the speed reducer 3 is connected with an input side end (an input unit) 4a of the torsion bar 4 so as to be capable of rotating integrally with the output shaft 3b”). and torque is applied to the motor axle (Fig 1, para 0021, 0077 wherein “[0021] The power output by the actuator and the driving force imparted to the driven element may be either of a rotational force (torque) and a translational force”). Orita further teaches applying the torque to the motor axle resulting in rotation of the motor axle and the output axle (Fig 1, para 0077-0080 wherein the shaft 2b can be considered a motor axle connected to the electric motor 2, and shaft 3b can be considered the output axle. The torque generated by the motor 2 results in the rotation of the motor axle 2b and output axle 3b using speed reducer/gear 3; “[0077] An output shaft 2b as a power output unit of the electric motor 2 is connected with an input shaft (an input unit) 3a of the speed reducer 3 so as to be capable of rotating integrally with the output shaft 2b”).
Orita also teaches the dynamic model defines a relationship between forces acting on the robotic arm and resulting accelerations of the robot (Para 0042-0043 wherein the dynamic model defines the relationship between acting forces and resulting acceleration; “[0042] According to the fourth aspect of the invention, the basic estimated value of the secondary side displacement velocity … has a meaning as an approximate estimated value of the secondary side displacement velocity on the basis of the dynamic relationship (model) between the driven element acting force and the operation of the driven element. [0043] That is, because the relationship between a movement acceleration of the driven element (rotational angular velocity or translational acceleration) and the driven element acting force becomes a dynamically proportional relationship”).
Therefore, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art, before the effective filing date of the claimed invention, to have modified Klimchik’s teachings of having a motor side encoder and output side encoder, determining stiffness model parameters using dynamic model to incorporate Orita’s teachings of having a motor axle and output axle and dynamic model defining forces acting and resulting acceleration in order to stably impart driving force and suppress vibration (Orita, para 0010 wherein “[0010] The present invention has been made in view of the above background, and aims to provide a control device of a power device, …  the control device being capable of effectively suppressing vibration of driving power transmitted from the actuator to the driven element, and also imparting target driving force stably to the driven element).
