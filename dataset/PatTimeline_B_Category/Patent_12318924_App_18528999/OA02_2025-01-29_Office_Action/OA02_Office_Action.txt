Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
Claims 5-6 and 8-9 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Claim 5 recites “a longitudinal axis” and “a lateral axis”, and it is unclear if the axes are intended to have antecedent basis to the amended “roll” and “pitch” axes of claim 1.
Claim 8 recites “the end effector” which lacks antecedent basis in the claim.

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
Claims 5 and 8 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Smith et al. (US 4,283,152).
Smith does not disclose the limitations of claims 5 and 8. However, the use of actuators is ubiquitous to the robotic arts and it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art prior to the time of effective filing as a matter of mere common sense to include actuators in a robotic device. Therefore, since the only difference between the structure of the prior art and that of the claimed device is the inclusion of actuators, and since actuators are considered to be ubiquitous to the robotic arts, the claimed device is not patentably distinct from the device of the prior art as combined.
