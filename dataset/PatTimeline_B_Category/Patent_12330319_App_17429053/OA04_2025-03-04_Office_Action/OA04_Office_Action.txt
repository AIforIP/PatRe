The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance: The claims disclose a furniture assembly robot having a base, robotic arm and multi-functional head with a camera. The camera is configured to scan a barcode that is positioned on a part presented to the robot as it rests on a conveyer. The barcode provides instructions comprising co-ordinates of a first position of the part to be attached to the first hardware component. The image of the part presented to the robot is captured. A first applicator applies adhesive to the part while a first gripping mechanism grips the first hardware component and is maneuvered by the robotic arm to a first position to be attached to the part. An attachment mechanism attaches the first hardware component to the part at the first position using fasteners to assemble the piece of furniture. A computing unit in communication with the robotic arm and head receives instructions and the image of the part from the camera. The computing unit processes the instructions to determine the firs hardware component to be attached to the part. The image is processed to determine dimensions and orientation of the part has it rests on the conveyer. The dimensions, orientation and co-ordinates of the first position that were determined by the barcode are used to locate the first position of the part to provide the first hardware component and control the robotic arm and head to place it at the first position with the gripping mechanism. While the gripping mechanism holds the first hardware component at the first position of the part the first hardware component is attached to the part at the first position with the first attachment mechanism. Independent claim 16 is broader and omits the applicator, gripping and attachment mechanisms in addition to describing the configuration of the robot which includes the head, arm etc. The claimed features support variability in mounting locations by using barcodes that define co-ordinates of the part for attachment to the hardware component (see applicant’s specification paragraphs 3-5 and 98).
The cited reference of Chen (U.S. 4,613,942) discloses obtaining robot assembly operation instructions based on captured indica placed on a part using a camera 14 that views the indicia 12 in its field of vision (see col. 2, lines 55-65). Chen fails to teach or suggest reading a barcode that provides attachment instructions that explicitly define co-ordinates of a first position of the part for attaching a first hardware component. Instead, Chen references indicia that use shapes and color to identify components for assembly.
The cited reference of Heping (U.S. Pub 2011/0087360) discloses application of a robot for parts assembly on a workpiece moving in an assembly line and includes a camera mounted on the robot tool itself (see fig. 5, paragraphs 41 and 59).  The camera further capturing image of the presented workpiece from the assembly line and performing image processing to identify patterns of bolts/studs (see paragraph 35). Thereby teaching capture of an image of presented workpiece to the robot using a camera mounted on the robot assembly. Heping fails to teach or suggest reading a barcode that provides attachment instructions that explicitly define co-ordinates of a first position of the part for attaching a first hardware component.
The cited reference of Shinozaki (U.S. 9,649,790) discloses application of a curing adhesive to a region of an article using a robot (see abstract). Shinozaki fails to teach or suggest reading a barcode that provides attachment instructions that explicitly define co-ordinates of a first position of the part for attaching a first hardware component.
The cited reference of Ross (NPL) discloses that applying Autonomous robots for the purpose of furniture parts assembly is well known (see pg. 855, paragraphs 1-4). Ross fails to teach or suggest reading a barcode that provides attachment instructions that explicitly define co-ordinates of a first position of the part for attaching a first hardware component.
The cited reference of Okuyama (U.S. Pub 2002/0105296) discloses transforming the position of a moving object on the conveyer based on its coordinate system to a robot coordinate system. Further in paragraph 8 indicating use of a camera for determining orientation and position of objects on a conveyer is known (abstract & paragraphs 18, 24 and 32). Okuyama fails to teach or suggest reading a barcode that provides attachment instructions that explicitly define co-ordinates of a first position of the part for attaching a first hardware component.
