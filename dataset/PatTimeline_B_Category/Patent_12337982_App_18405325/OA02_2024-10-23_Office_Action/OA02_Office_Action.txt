Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1-4 and 7-9 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Claim 1 recites the limitation the exterior flow being laminar over a given length in 4.  The limitation renders the claim indefinite since the metes and bounds of the limitation are unclear.  The claim does not define to what feature “a given length” refers.  The given length can refer to an axial length, and inlet length, a surface length, or virtually any length associated with the exterior flow.  There is no point of reference given, and the claim does not provide any context for a person having ordinary skill in the art to determine a length over which the flow is laminar.  Furthermore, a flow being laminar depends in the context of the invention on a number of variables such as velocity, temperature, pressure, angle of attack, air speed, surface geometry and/or altitude, just to name a few.  The claim also fails to define these variable which are necessary to determine a laminar/turbulent flow transition.  As such, for examination purposes, as best understood in view of the specification, the limitation will be interpreted as requiring the flow to be laminar over any length at any velocity, temperature, pressure, angle of attack, air speed, surface geometry and/or altitude.
Claims 2-4 and 7-9 are also indefinite by virtue of their dependency on Claim 1.

Claim(s) 1, 4 and 9 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by Wittman et al. (US 20200031487), hereinafter: “Wittman”.
In Regard to Claim 1
Wittman teaches:
An air inlet(32) of a nacelle(28) of an aircraft(P[0001]-P[0004], P[0014]) comprising:
a lip(52,106) having outer(85) and inner faces(83) and a leading edge(84) configured to split an air flow into an interior air flow(F) and an exterior air flow(flow exterior to nacelle 28), the interior air flow entering the air inlet(Fig 1), the exterior air flow being laminar over a given length(the exterior air flow will be laminar at some velocity over a length just beyond the leading edge of the air inlet; further the limitation interpreted as an intended result; see below for more details),
an exterior panel(56) having an outer face(exterior surface of 56; Fig 2) against which the exterior air flow flows in operation(Fig 1-2), an inner face opposite to the outer face(interior surface of 56 facing 73; Fig 2) and a front edge oriented towards the lip(upstream end of 56 at joint 66 that abuts downstream end of 88; Fig 2-3),
a front frame(58), in the form of a ring(Fig 1), which has exterior and interior edges(64,62), and,
an intermediate panel(90, 108), interposed between the lip(portion 52,106; Fig 5; P[0034])  and the exterior panel(108 is positioned between the lip 52 and the exterior panel 56; Fig 2-3, 5), having an outer face(exterior surface of 90, 108) against which the exterior air flow flows in operation(visually apparent from Fig 1-5), an inner face opposite to the outer face(interior surface of 90,108), a first edge(upstream edge of 108) connected to the lip by at least a first connection(110, butt weld; P[0034]) and a second edge(downstream edge of 108) connected to the exterior panel by at least a second connection(at 66; Fig 2),
wherein the first connection connecting the first edge of the intermediate panel and the lip comprises at least a first butt weld bead(110 is a butt weld bead; P[0034]) having an outer face in an extension of the outer faces of the lip and of the intermediate panel(as visually apparent from Fig 5, the butt weld bead 110 has an exterior face that extends the outer faces of the lip and the intermediate panel).
The limitation “the exterior air flow being laminar over a given length” is construed to be functional language not structurally distinguishable over the prior art because the limitation indicates a manner of operating the device or an intended result. Furthermore, claim containing a recitation with respect to the manner in which a claimed apparatus is intended to be employed does not differentiate the claimed apparatus from a prior art apparatus if the prior art apparatus teaches all the structural limitations of the claim. Ex parte Masham, 2 USPQ2d 1647 (Bd. Pat. App. & Inter. 1987).
Additionally, in Wittman, the lip is structurally capable of producing an exterior air flow being laminar over a given length.
