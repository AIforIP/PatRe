DETAILED ACTION
Response to Amendment and Arguments
The amendment filed 11/22/2024 has been entered. Claims 1-9 remain pending in the application. Applicant’s amendments to the Claims have overcome each §112(b) rejection previously set forth in the Non-Final Office Action. Claims no longer invoke interpretation under 35 USC 112(f).
Applicant’s arguments with respect to the rejection(s) under 35 USC 103 have been fully considered and are persuasive.  Therefore, the rejection has been withdrawn.  However, upon further consideration, a new ground(s) of rejection is made in view of US20070061068A1 Okamoto et al (Okamoto).
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The text of those sections of Title 35, U.S. Code not included in this action can be found in a prior Office action.
Claim(s) 1, 5, 7-9 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over US20180345790A1 Mimura et al (Mimura) in view of Okamoto.
As per claim 1 and 8, Mimura teaches the device of claim 1 and method of claim 8:
A display control device that performs display control on a first display and a second display of a vehicle, the second display being different from the first display (Mimura at least the abstract: “vehicle control system…first display unit…second display unit…position different from a position…display first information…display second information”)
the display control device comprising: : a processor; and a memory including a program that, when executed by the processor, causes the processor to perform functions (Mimura at least [0078])
obtaining switching information indicating that switching between driving modes including an autonomous driving mode and a manual driving mode is to be performed in the vehicle; (Mimura at least [0078-0079]: “master control unit…switching control unit…HMI control unit…operation element state determination unit…occupant state monitoring unit…cancel the driving support and switch…to manual driving”)
causing, before completion of first switching, (i) the first display to display first information and third information and (ii) the second display to display second information when the switching information indicates that the first switching is to be performed, the first is from the autonomous driving mode to the manual driving mode, the first information indicates that the first switching is to be performed, the second information being information assists a user of the vehicle in driving in the manual driving mode. (Mimura at least FIG. 31 & [0050]: “third screen and a fourth screen…switching…to manual driving”)
Mimura does not disclose:
third information, which is displayed on the first display, indicates that the second display, which is different from the first display, is displaying the second information that assists the user of the vehicle in driving in the manual mode.
However, Okamoto teaches the aforementioned limitation (Okamoto at least [0076], warning device operative to warn the driver when the indication control by the second indication control device is executed). *Examiner’s note: here Okamoto teaches a situation where a passenger is using a second display to navigate on a map (information for driving in manual mode). The driver’s display will indicate that the second display is being used (third information).
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to combine Mimura with the aforementioned limitations taught by Okamoto with a reasonable expectation of success. One of ordinary skill would have been motivated to combine these references in order to improve visibility and operability of a system while using two display devices (Okamoto abstract).
As per claim 5, Mimura in combination with the other reference teaches the invention as described above. Mimura additionally teaches:
when the processor obtains, before the completion of the first switching, new switching information indicating that second switching is to be performed, the processor causes, before completion of the second switching, the first display to display fourth information indicating that the second switching is to be performed, the second switching being from the manual driving mode to the autonomous driving mode. (Mimura at least FIG. 13 & [0155]: “driving support…is started…hand is separated from the steering wheel”) *Examiner’s note: here Mimura teaches that autonomous driving is available and supported, the switching being imminent and contingent on the driver releasing control of the steering wheel.
As per claim 7, Mimura in combination with the other reference teaches the invention as described above. Mimura additionally teaches:
the first display is a head-up display, and the second display is mounted on a dashboard of the vehicle. (Mimura at least FIG. 6, 460 & 470)
As per claim 9, Mimura in combination with the other reference teaches the invention as described above. Mimura additionally teaches:
A non-transitory computer-readable recording medium having stored thereon a program for causing a computer to execute the display control method according to claim 8. (Mimura [0018])
Claim(s) 2 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Mimura and Okamoto as in claim 1, in further view of US20160098017A1 Park et al (Park).
As per claim 2, Mimura in combination with the other reference teaches the invention as described above. Mimura does not disclose:
the third information indicates a position of an area in the second display, which is displaying the second information.
However, Park teaches the aforementioned limitation (Park at least FIG. 3). Examiner’s note: here Park teaches a notification icon on a second display (smart watch) informing the user of further information (phone call) and the position of the information (phone screen).
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to combine Mimura with the aforementioned limitations taught by Park with a reasonable expectation of success. One of ordinary skill would have been motivated to combine these references in order to provide a notification on a display that is more readily available to attract the attention of the user to direct the user towards a more detailed message provided on another display.
Claim(s) 3 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Mimura and Okamoto as in claim 1, in further view of US20190094856A1 Kawate (Kawate).
As per claim 3, Mimura in combination with the other reference teaches the invention as described above. Mimura does not disclose:
the processor obtains gaze information indicating a gaze of the user, the second information includes a plurality of notices, and when the second display is displaying the plurality of notices, the processor identifies, according to the gaze of the user indicated by the gaze information, a notice displayed in an area at which the gaze of the user is directed and causes the second display to hide the notice identified by the processor.
However, Kawate teaches the aforementioned limitation (Kawate at least the abstract: advance notice of disabling of the driving assistance on the basis of said information…completion display unit…disabling…displays a completion image for giving notice of the completion of the disabling of the driving assistance, [0027]: “action detection unit…line-of-sight detection unit”). *Examiner’s note: here Kawate teaches the hiding of the notice of the switch to manual mode by user gaze acknowledgement of the notice and subsequently displaying a completion notice.
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to combine Mimura with the aforementioned limitations taught by Kawate with a reasonable expectation of success. One of ordinary skill would have been motivated to combine these references in order to give notice of the state of the driving mode to the driver.
Claim(s) 4, 6 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Mimura and Okamoto as in claim 1, in further view of US20160196247A1 Lee et al (Lee).
As per claim 4, Mimura in combination with the other reference teaches the invention as described above. Mimura does not disclose:
in a period until the completion of the first switching, the processor changes a display style of at least one of the first information, the second information, or the third information from a first display style to a second display style.
However, Lee teaches the aforementioned limitation (Lee at least claim 10: “second user modification comprises a changing a color of a graphical element…animation”, [0024]: scaled up or scaled down in size during the move animation). Examiner’s note: here Lee teaches the style (color and animation) of the display information changing when another user is modifying (thus, in charge of) the program in use.  Correlating the teaching of Lee to the present application, a user can be the driver while another user can be the ego vehicle itself in autonomous mode.  While the user (driver) is in charge (manual mode) the style can be of one particular style, and the style changes (color and/or animation of display information) when another user (ego vehicle in autonomous mode) is in charge.
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to combine Mimura with the aforementioned limitations taught by Lee with a reasonable expectation of success. One of ordinary skill would have been motivated to combine these references in order to allow a user to quickly via visual means identify which user is in control of the program being used.
As per claim 6, Mimura in combination with the other reference teaches the invention as described above. Mimura additionally teaches:
when the processor obtains, before the completion of the first switching, new switching information indicating that second switching from the manual driving mode to the autonomous driving mode is to be performed, before completion of the second switching, the display controller (i) causes the first display to display fourth information indicating that the second switching is to be performed  (Mimura at least FIG. 13 & [0155])
Mimura does not disclose:
in a period until the completion of the first switching, the processor changes a display style of at least one of the first information, the second information, or the third information from a first display style to a second display style
changes the display style of at least one of the first information, the second information, or the third information from the second display style to the first display style.
However, Lee teaches the aforementioned limitations (Lee at least claim 10 & [0024], claim 1: “in response to receiving the first information, changing the presentation…on the first user device to reflect the first user”).
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to combine Mimura with the aforementioned limitations taught by Lee with a reasonable expectation of success.
