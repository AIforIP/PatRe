Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claim 3 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention. The term “that is greater than about zero” in claim 3 is a relative term which renders the claim indefinite. The term “about” is not defined by the claim, the specification does not provide a standard for ascertaining the requisite degree, and one of ordinary skill in the art would not be reasonably apprised of the scope of the invention. For example, it is unclear to what degree a speed greater than zero “about” includes.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Claims 1-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101.
Claim 20 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because the claimed invention is directed to a mental process that can be practicably performed in the human mind, and therefore an abstract idea. The claim recites “and the edge reversing determination machine-learning model determines a machine-learning-determined reversing indication for the second vehicle based on the captured acceleration data.” which, under the broadest reasonable interpretation, is a mental process that could reasonably be done in the human mind provided acceleration data. For example, nothing outside of the recitation of a “the edge reversing determination machine-learning model determines” or “a machine-learning-determined” prevents a human from determining a vehicle reversing indication from acceleration data. A human could observe a vehicle accelerating and associate the movement with a reverse maneuver, and then make a determination that a second vehicle is performing a reverse maneuver on the basis of this observation. See Berkheimer v. HP, Inc., 881 F.3d 1360, 125 USPQ2d 1649 (Fed. Cir. 2018) & In re Brown, 645 Fed. App'x 1014, 1016-17 (Fed. Cir. 2016).
Under step 2A, prong 2 claim 1 does not include additional elements that are sufficient enough to amount the abstract idea into a practical application, because the recitations “A telematics system, comprising: a telematics server running a central reversing determination machine-learning model;”, “a network;” or “a first telematics device coupled to a first vehicle and in communication with the telematics server over the network;”, & “and a second telematics device coupled to a second vehicle which does not provide a reverse gear indication over the network;” are directed to generic linking, merely defining an environment of the abstract idea with generically recited elements, provided for linking the abstract idea to a particular technological environment, and lacking an element to incorporate a result of performing the abstract idea with the recited structures in a meaningful way see MPEP, 2106.05(f) & 2106.05(e).
Further under step 2A, prong 2 “wherein: the telematics server receives training acceleration data and a vehicle- provided reversing indication from the first telematics device;”, “the telematics server trains the central reversing determination machine- learning model and generates a reversing determination model update using the training acceleration data and the vehicle-provided reversing indication;”, “the telematics server sends the reversing determination model update to the second telematics device;”, “the second telematics device updates an edge reversing determination machine-learning model using the reversing determination model update;”, & “the second telematics device captures captured acceleration data for the second vehicle;” is directed to insignificant extra solution activity data gathering/outputting, merely reciting steps for gathering and transmitting the data used for performing steps of the mental process with generically recited elements. For example, reciting steps for merely transmitting acceleration data and reversal indications used for updating machine learning model, and lacking any recitation to incorporate the output of the models as a control step, see MPEP 2106.05(g).
Under step 2B, the claim does not include additional elements that are sufficient enough to amount to significantly more than the judicial exception because for example “A telematics system, comprising: a telematics server running a central reversing determination machine-learning model;”, “a network;” or “a first telematics device coupled to a first vehicle and in communication with the telematics server over the network;”, & “and a second telematics device coupled to a second vehicle which does not provide a reverse gear indication over the network;” again is merely applying and generically linking the abstract idea to a particular technological environment, MPEP, 2106.05(f) & 2106.05(e), and does not impose any other meaningful limitation beyond generally linking the use of the judicial exception to a particular technological environment. For example, the recitations of “A telematics system, comprising: a telematics server running a central reversing determination machine-learning model;”, “a network;” or “a first telematics device coupled to a first vehicle and in communication with the telematics server over the network;”, & “and a second telematics device coupled to a second vehicle which does not provide a reverse gear indication over the network;” are only provided to define the generic structures provided to define an environment for applying the steps of the mental process and perform no additional functions.
Further, the recitations “wherein: the telematics server receives training acceleration data and a vehicle- provided reversing indication from the first telematics device;”, “the telematics server trains the central reversing determination machine- learning model and generates a reversing determination model update using the training acceleration data and the vehicle-provided reversing indication;”, “the telematics server sends the reversing determination model update to the second telematics device;”, “the second telematics device updates an edge reversing determination machine-learning model using the reversing determination model update;”, & “the second telematics device captures captured acceleration data for the second vehicle;” would still be directed to insignificant extra solution activity data gathering and outputting under step 2B, as the transmitting of data pertaining acceleration data and a vehicle- provided reversing indication, and the updating and training of machine learning models, requires no more than ordinary skill in the art, and therefore is directed to well understood, routine, and conventional activity in the art, see MPEP 2106.05(d), II,  i. Receiving or transmitting data over a network, e.g., using the Internet to gather data, Symantec, 838 F.3d at 1321, 120 USPQ2d at 1362 (utilizing an intermediary computer to forward information); OIP Techs., Inc., v. Amazon.com, Inc., 788 F.3d 1359, 1363, 115 USPQ2d 1090, 1093 (Fed. Cir. 2015) (sending messages over a network); buySAFE, Inc. v. Google, Inc., 765 F.3d 1350, 1355, 112 USPQ2d 1093, 1096 (Fed. Cir. 2014) (computer receives and sends information over a network); & iv. Storing and retrieving information in memory, Versata Dev. Group, Inc. v. SAP Am., Inc., 793 F.3d 1306, 1334, 115 USPQ2d 1681, 1701 (Fed. Cir. 2015); OIP Techs., 788 F.3d at 1363, 115 USPQ2d at 1092-93. & see MPEP 2106.05(g), Mere Data Gathering v. Consulting and updating an activity log, Ultramercial, 772 F.3d at 715, 112 USPQ2d at 1754;
Accordingly, the claims are not patent eligible.
Claim 1 is rejected under 35 U.S.C 101 under similar rationale as claim 20.
Additionally, claims 2-19 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 by virtue of their dependency on claim 1.
Claims 2-3, 9-10, 13-16, and 18-19 do not include additional elements that are sufficient enough to amount to significantly more than the judicial exception because the claims are directed to insignificant extra solution activity data gathering and outputting steps, for example reciting steps to further define the data which is output as an alert, as recited in claim 2, or defining the data which is gathered for performing the abstract idea, such as a vehicle speed/reverse gear indication/copied sampled accelerometer readings/post-trigger event acceleration data, as recited in claims 3, 9-10, and 13-16, or outputting/storing a machine-learning-determined reversing indication, as recited in claims 18-19, each merely recites data gathering and output steps, the data amounting to nothing more than a determination of if a second vehicle is performing a reversal maneuver, determined as a result of performing the abstract idea. The mere recitation of when/how/what data is gathered/output, however again lacks a specific element reciting how the result of performing the mental process is applied or integrated as a specific control step, and therefore is directed to well understood routine and conventional activity in the art, see MPEP 2106.05(g) iii. Selecting information, based on types of information and availability of information in a power-grid environment, for collection, analysis and display, Electric Power Group, LLC v. Alstom S.A., 830 F.3d 1350, 1354-55, 119 USPQ2d 1739, 1742 (Fed. Cir. 2016); & See 2106.05(a) iii. Gathering and analyzing information using conventional techniques and displaying the result, TLI Communications, 823 F.3d at 612-13, 118 USPQ2d at 1747-48.
Claims 4-8, 11-12, and 17 do not include additional elements that are sufficient enough to amount the abstract idea into a practical application, because claims recite steps to defining additional structure and data in the environment of the abstract idea, and therefore directed to generic linking. See claims 4 & 11 defining what structure is provided to capture the acceleration data, claims 5-7 reciting the frequency for updating the second device with the reversing determination model, claims 8 & 12 defining when to capture acceleration data, and claim 17 defining a trigger event. Defining a technological environment of the mental process, merely links the process to generic steps or structure, lacking an element reciting how an output of performing the process is integrated to impact the operation of the vehicle, see MPEP, 2106.05(f) & 2106.05(e). These additional elements do not integrate the abstract idea into a practical application because they do not impose any meaningful limits on practicing the abstract idea. Accordingly, these claims are not patent eligible.
Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1-20 would be allowable if rewritten or amended to overcome the rejection(s) under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), 2nd paragraph, set forth in this Office action.
The following is a statement of reasons for the indication of allowable subject matter:
Regarding claim 20 and similarly with respect to claim 1.
The closest prior art of record taken either individually or in combination with other prior art of record fails to teach or suggest the subject matter of independent claims 20 and 1.
The recitation “the telematics server trains the central reversing determination machine- learning model and generates a reversing determination model update using the training acceleration data and the vehicle-provided reversing indication; the telematics server sends the reversing determination model update to the second telematics device; the second telematics device updates an edge reversing determination machine-learning model using the reversing determination model update; the second telematics device captures captured acceleration data for the second vehicle; and the edge reversing determination machine-learning model determines a machine-learning-determined reversing indication for the second vehicle based on the captured acceleration data.” overcomes the art of record, rendering the claims in a manner specific enough to overcome the methods disclosed in the closest prior art. The claims specifically overcome the art of record, because of the limitation directed to using a trained model to generate a reversing model update, and sending the trained model to a second device, which captures acceleration data, and uses the update and acceleration data to determine a reverse indication. The closest prior art for example, discloses using a machine learning model for determining a reverse indication of a vehicle, however , no single prior art reference that fully discloses the claimed invention was found, nor was there determined a reasonable combination of prior art references that would have made the claimed invention obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the invention. The subject matter of the claims is therefore allowable.
Claims 2-19, these claims would be allowable on the basis of the dependency of claim 1.
