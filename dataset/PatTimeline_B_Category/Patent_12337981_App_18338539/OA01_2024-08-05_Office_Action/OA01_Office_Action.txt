Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
(a)(2) the claimed invention was described in a patent issued under section 151, or in an application for patent published or deemed published under section 122(b), in which the patent or application, as the case may be, names another inventor and was effectively filed before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claim(s) 1-3, 6-7 and 11 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) and 102(a)(2) as being anticipated by Martino Gonzalez (US 20080099611 A1).
Regarding Claim 1, Martino Gonzalez teaches a variable fairing (Fig. 26 elements 39 and 40) for covering a hydrogen duct system installation on the outside of an aircraft part, wherein the fairing comprises at least one fairing body to be mounted on the aircraft part, an air inlet and an air outlet (“support structure 10 supports the exhaust pipe 7 and the air intake 6” Par. [0133] lines 2-3) and at least one movable closure mechanism configured to open and close a top portion of the fairing (Shown in Fig. 26).
Regarding Claim 2, Martino Gonzalez teaches the limitations set forth in Claim 1 and further discloses the fairing is configured for covering the hydrogen duct system installation mounted outside of a fuselage of an aircraft (Fig. 26 elements 39 and 40 cover APU 3).
Regarding Claim 3, Martino Gonzalez teaches the limitations set forth in Claim 2 and further discloses the fairing is configured for covering the hydrogen duct system installation mounted on a top of the fuselage (Fig. 26 elements 39 and 40 cover APU 3).
Regarding Claim 6, Martino Gonzalez teaches the limitations set forth in Claim 1 and further discloses the air inlet and the air outlet comprise fixed openings in the at least one fairing body (Fig. 26 element 7 shown as an air outlet with an opening in the fairing).
Regarding Claim 7, Martino Gonzalez teaches the limitations set forth in Claim 1 and further discloses a control configured to control the movable closure mechanism depending on at least one or several of the following: a flight condition of an aircraft, an aircraft speed, a ground condition of the aircraft, or a hydrogen accumulation within the fairing (“The electrical control of the actuator 7 is carried out via a control unit 14 located in a lower portion of the engine 1 such as to be easily accessible for the ground handler” Par. [0042 lines 1-2; “Since said actuators are intended for being used when the aeroplane is on the ground and its engines are switched off the electric power supply to the actuators comes from an energy source that is internal or external (power unit) to the aeroplane” Par. [0008] lines 2-6; ground conditions must be met for the fairings to be opened).
Regarding Claim 11, Martino Gonzalez teaches the limitations set forth in Claim 1 and further discloses an operation method for a variable fairing according to claim 1, comprising opening the variable fairing on ground for allowing hydrogen to escape upwards and closing the fairing during flight allowing airflow through the fairing via the air inlet and the air outlet (“the auxiliary power unit and the ancillary elements thereof are securely attached to a fixed element of the tail cone, and a movable fairing moves, opening and closing the tail cone, therefore leaving the auxiliary power unit exposed and thus facilitating the auxiliary power unit maintenance and repair tasks” Par. [0011] lines 1-4).
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
The factual inquiries for establishing a background for determining obviousness under 35 U.S.C. 103 are summarized as follows:
1. Determining the scope and contents of the prior art.
2. Ascertaining the differences between the prior art and the claims at issue.
3. Resolving the level of ordinary skill in the pertinent art.
4. Considering objective evidence present in the application indicating obviousness or nonobviousness.
Claim(s) 4-5 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Martino Gonzalez (US 20080099611 A1) in view of Chavignier et al. (US 20160229546 A1).
Regarding Claim 4, Martin Gonzalez teaches the limitations set forth in Claim 1 and further discloses the closure mechanism comprises at least one or several of the following: a first faring door (Fig. 26 element 39), a second fairing door (Fig. 26 element 40).
Martin Gonzalez fails to explicitly teach an actuating apparatus for driving a movement of the closure mechanism.
However, Chavignier teaches an actuating apparatus for driving a movement of the closure mechanism (Fig. 1 elements 7).
Martin Gonzalez and Chavignier are considered analogous to the claimed invention as they are in the same field of aircraft fairing design. It would have been obvious to someone of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have modified the fairing system of Martin Gonzalez to have actuators as disclosed by Chavignier. Doing so would allow the fairing to open and close automatically in response to an electrical signal.
Regarding Claim 5, Martin Gonzalez and Chavignier teach the limitations set forth in Claim 4.
Martin Gonzalez further discloses the closure mechanism comprises the first fairing door and the second fairing door wherein the first and the second fairing door are at least one of: split along a longitudinal axis of at least one of the fairing or the fuselage, or configured to be hinged to fuselage or to the fairing body (Shown in Fig. 26).
Claim(s) 8-10 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Martino Gonzalez (US 20080099611 A1) in view of Remy et al. (US 8056344 B2).
Regarding Claim 8, Martin Gonzalez teaches the limitations set forth in Claim 1 and further discloses a variable fairing according to claim 1 for covering the hydrogen duct system installation.
Martin Gonzalez fails to explicitly teach a hydrogen duct system arrangement for an aircraft, comprising: a hydrogen duct system installation configured to be mounted on the aircraft outside of an aircraft part.
However, Remy teaches a hydrogen duct system arrangement for an aircraft, comprising: a hydrogen duct system installation configured to be mounted on the aircraft outside of an aircraft part (“The hydrogen ducts 52 are connected to the combustor inlet 40 for injecting pressurized gaseous hydrogen in close proximity to the nozzle 38” Col. 6 lines 3-5).
Martin Gonzalez and Remy are considered analogous to the claimed invention as they are in the same field of aircraft engine structures. It would have been obvious to someone of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have modified the fairing system of Martin Gonzalez to be used to cover a hydrogen duct system as disclosed by Remy. This would maintain the aerodynamics of the system while in flight. Although Martin Gonzalez did not specifically disclose that the fairing system was used to cover a hydrogen duct system, it is known in the art that fairings are used to cover the engine structures.
Regarding Claim 9, Martin Gonzalez and Remy teach the limitations set forth in Claim 8.
Remy further discloses the hydrogen duct system installation comprises at least one or several of the following components: at least one pipe for transporting hydrogen (H2), at least one pipe coupling, at least one component of a H2 feed unit for supplying a hydrogen consumer of the aircraft with hydrogen, at least one valve, especially automatic valve or controllable valve, at least one vent system for releasing H2 to the outside, at least one security valve, at least one component of a H2 refueling system for at least one of fueling a hydrogen tank with H2 or venting a hydrogen tank during fueling, or a H2 system unit combining at least two of the aforementioned components (“The hydrogen-injection arrangement 48 includes a source of gaseous hydrogen, here represented for example by gas containers 50, 51; hydrogen duct 52; and second controllable valves 54 for closing and opening the hydrogen ducts 52” Col. 5 lines 63-67).
Regarding Claim 10, Martin Gonzalez and Remy teach the limitations set forth in Claim 8.
Remy further discloses an aircraft comprising a hydrogen duct system arrangement according to claim 8 (“If the gas turbine engine 10 is used as an aircraft engine, pressurized gaseous hydrogen is available from the aircraft” Col. 8 lines 23-25).

Martin Gonzalez fails to explicitly teach the variable fairing is opened during a lower aircraft moving speed and closed during a higher aircraft moving speed.
However, Mahjoub teaches the variable fairing is opened during a lower aircraft moving speed and closed during a higher aircraft moving speed (“an air exhaust means 54 for the air heat exchanger 40 is connected to an air output means 44 which opens outside the outer surface 46 of the fairing 34, in a part of this fairing located behind said air inlet means 42 depending on the direction of advance of the aircraft in flight” Par. [0026] lines 25-30).
Martin Gonzalez and Mahjoub are considered analogous to the claimed invention as they are in the same field of aircraft structure design. Claim(s) 13 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Martino Gonzalez (US 20080099611 A1) in view of Chavignier et al. (US 20160229546 A1).
Regarding Claim 13, Martino Gonzalez teaches the limitations set forth in Claim 1.
Martin Gonzalez fails to explicitly teach a non-transitory computer-readable medium having stored thereon instructions to execute the steps of an operation method for a variable fairing according to claim 1, comprising opening the variable fairing on ground for allowing hydrogen to escape upwards and closing the fairing during flight allowing airflow through the fairing via the air inlet and the air outlet.
However, Chavignier teaches a non-transitory computer-readable medium having stored thereon instructions to execute the steps of an operation method for a variable fairing according to claim 1, comprising opening the variable fairing on ground for allowing hydrogen to escape upwards and closing the fairing during flight allowing airflow through the fairing via the air inlet and the air outlet (“The control unit 14 comprises interface means which allow the ground handler to control same. Said interface means are two “SPDT” (Single Pole, Double Throw) switches 16a and 16b, wherein the first switch 16a controls the opening of the cowl 4 and the second switch 16b controls the closing of the cowl 4. The control unit 14 supplies the telescopic actuator 7 via an electrical connector 17 with a control signal that is the result of actuating the switch 16” Par. [0044] lines 1-8).
Martin Gonzalez and Chavignier are considered analogous to the claimed invention as they are in the same field of aircraft fairing design. It would have been obvious to someone of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have modified the fairing system to have the control unit as disclosed by Chavignier. Doing so would allow the fairing to open automatically or when an electrical signal is sent to it. It is known in the art to use stored instructions to perform the opening and closing of fairings, so it is not required to be opened manually.
