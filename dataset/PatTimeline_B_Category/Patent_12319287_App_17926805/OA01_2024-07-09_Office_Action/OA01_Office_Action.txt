Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
In the event the determination of the status of the application as subject to AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 (or as subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103) is incorrect, any correction of the statutory basis (i.e., changing from AIA  to pre-AIA ) for the rejection will not be considered a new ground of rejection if the prior art relied upon, and the rationale supporting the rejection, would be the same under either status.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim(s) 2 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Kazuyuki in view of Yoshitomi (US 2017/0154527 A1).
Regarding claim 2, Kazuyuki teaches all of the elements of the current invention as stated above except:
The travel control apparatus according to claim 1,
wherein the determination unit determines that it is the turn opportunity in a case where a turn signal for the first direction turn is detected to be ON and a vehicle speed of the vehicle is less than a predetermined threshold.
Yoshitomi teaches that it is known to provide the travel control apparatus wherein the determination unit determines that it is the turn opportunity in a case where a turn signal for the first direction turn is detected to be ON and a vehicle speed of the vehicle is less than a predetermined threshold. See at least [0039]-[0042] where, at step S130, the CPU may determine that the driver has activated a turn signal of the vehicle (indicating that the driver intends to make a right or left turn) and proceeds to step S140. At step S140, the CPU determines if the vehicle speed is lower than a pre-set threshold velocity. When the vehicle velocity is lower than the threshold velocity, it is judged that the driver intends to make a left or right turn.
It would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art of vehicle controls to have modified Kazuyuki to incorporate the teachings of Yoshitomi and provide the travel control apparatus wherein the determination unit determines that it is the turn opportunity in a case where a turn signal for the first direction turn is detected to be ON and a vehicle speed of the vehicle is less than a predetermined threshold. In doing so, the travel control apparatus is improved by being able to better recognize the driver’s intention to turn and, further, to better identify locations that the vehicle is able to safely make an upcoming turn [0005].
Claim(s) 3 and 5 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Kazuyuki in view of Kameoka (US 2022/0340123 A1).
Regarding claim 3, Kazuyuki teaches
The travel control apparatus according to claim 1 see at least [0030] where a target acceleration for turning at an intersection approaching ahead of the vehicle at an appropriate speed is calculated. and
calculates the target acceleration rate by a second calculation method on a basis of [a steering angle and] the vehicle speed in a case where it is not determined that it is the turn opportunity. See at least [0029] where, during normal follow-up control, the control ECU 10 calculates a tracking acceleration (acceleration required to properly track the preceding vehicle) and sets the calculated tracking acceleration as a target acceleration during normal tracking control. The calculated tracking acceleration is determined based on a distance and relative speed to the preceding vehicle. While the vehicle is executing the normal follow-up control, this is being interpreted by the examiner as a case where it is not determined that it is the turn opportunity since the vehicle is not anticipating to turn, rather, the vehicle is simply following the preceding vehicle.
Kazuyuki teaches all of the elements of the current invention as stated above except wherein the target acceleration setting unit calculates the target acceleration rate by a second calculation method on a basis of a steering angle.
However, Kameoka teaches that it is known to provide the concept wherein the target acceleration setting unit calculates the target acceleration rate by a second calculation method on a basis of a steering angle in a case where it is not determined that it is the turn opportunity. See at least FIG. 10 and [0026] where a control system of a vehicle includes internal sensor 31 configured to recognize a travel state of the vehicle 100 (which includes a steering angle sensor 22, a vehicle speed sensor 20, and other sensors). Further, see at least [0033]-[0036] where, in a situation in which the vehicle 100 is travelling along a path and detects a stopped vehicle present in front of the vehicle 100 (e.g., a situation in which it is not a turn opportunity), the vehicle control device 101 calculates a target steering angle and a target acceleration/deceleration rate based on outputs of the internal sensor 31 (including the steering angle sensor 22 and vehicle speed sensor 20).
It would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to have modified Kazuyuki to incorporate the teachings of Kameoka and provide the travel control apparatus wherein the target acceleration setting unit calculates the target acceleration rate by a second calculation method on a basis of a steering angle (and the vehicle speed) in a case where it is not determined that it is the turn opportunity. In doing so, this provides an improvement in overall safety of the system since the vehicle can calculate an updated target acceleration/deceleration rate of the ego vehicle in situations when obstacles are blocking the ego vehicle’s current travel path. This capability enables the ego vehicle to avoid collision with obstacles that are detected in the ego vehicle’s travel path.
Claim 5 has substantially the same technical features as claim 3 and is therefore rejected using the preceding logic for claim 3.
Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 4 objected to as being dependent upon a rejected base claim, but would be allowable if rewritten in independent form including all of the limitations of the base claim and any intervening claims.
The following is a statement of reasons for the indication of allowable subject matter:   prior arts of record do not teach or fairly suggest each element and limitation of claims 4 and 6-8.
Claim 4.	The travel control apparatus according toclaim 1,
wherein the vehicle comprises a map locator, the determination unit assesses accuracy of the map locator, and, upon assessing the accuracy to be unfavorable, the determination unit determines that it is the turn opportunity in a case where the turn signal for the first direction turn is detected to be ON and a vehicle speed of the vehicle is less than a predetermined threshold.
Rather, the closest prior arts of record and a brief description of what they disclose is provided below.
Kazuyuki (JP2009126433 (A)) discloses a vehicle control device equipped with an automatic vehicle speed control means for automatically controlling a vehicle speed. The system of Kazuyuki comprises detecting an intersection ahead of the vehicle (based on information from the navigation system), determining, based on information from the navigation system and the state of the turn indicators, whether the vehicle is located within a predetermined intersection control implementation range (which corresponds to the range in which speed control is required to pass through the intersection at an appropriate speed), and calculates a target acceleration for turning at the intersection approaching ahead of the vehicle at an appropriate speed.
Yoshitomi (US 2017/0154527 A1) discloses an apparatus and method for driving assistance. The driving assistance system comprises a variety of sensors, a control device, and an assistance execution unit (including a central processing unit (CPU)) mounted on a vehicle. The CPU may determine that the driver has activated a turn signal of the vehicle (indicating that the driver intends to make a right or left turn) and the CPU then proceeds to determine if the vehicle speed is lower than a pre-set threshold velocity. When the vehicle velocity is lower than the threshold velocity, it is judged that the driver intends to make a left or right turn.
Badshah et al. (“Vehicle navigation in GPS denied environment for smart cities using vision sensors”) discloses a system for vehicle navigation based on vision sensors using modified normalized phase correlation. The disclosure of Badshah is concerned with using vision based navigation in order to determine an estimated position of the vehicle while driving in urban environments (such as in crowded and congested areas) and estimating the position accuracy in comparison with GPS calculated data.
Claims 6-8 have substantially the same technical features as claim 4 and are therefore objected to using the preceding logic for claim 4.
