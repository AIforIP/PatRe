Reasons for Allowance
Claims 1-10 and 12-17 are allowed over the prior art of record.
The closest prior art of record is Bongard et al. (US 20200369148 A1) in view of Oh (US 20220036758 A1) in further view of Chan et al. (US 20230059562 A1), hereinafter referred to as Bongard, Oh and Chan, respectively. The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
Regarding claims 1, 12 and 16, Bongard discloses A method of configuring a vehicle to integrate an accessory (See at least Fig. 1 in Bongard: Bongard discloses that the system 10 is associated with a plurality of the accessories 12 installed to the vehicle 14 [See at least Bongard, 0033])  comprising:
generating, for display on a touchscreen, an initial state of an accessory-equipped vehicle, wherein the initial state includes default values of operational parameters of vehicle and accessory components and sub-systems (See at least Fig. 5 in Bongard: Bongard discloses current values of certain operational parameters of the vehicle, which may be regarded as default values [See at least Bongard, 0079]);
generating a graphical user interface, on the touchscreen, allowing a user to specify a test configuration (See at least Fig. 1 in Bongard: Bongard discloses that the UI module 16 is configured to provide interface controls to allow a user 13 to provide input, for example, to affect operation of any of the accessories 12 associated with the vehicle 14 [See at least Bongard, 0036]) comprising:
a user-specified high-level accessory-related rule relating to the operation of the accessory-equipped vehicle (See at least Fig. 1 in Bongard: Bongard discloses that the UI module 16 is configured to provide interface controls to allow a user 13 to provide input, for example, to affect operation of any of the accessories 12 associated with the vehicle 14 [See at least Bongard, 0036]); and
an operational parameter setting a state of the vehicle and accessory components and sub-systems (See at least Fig. 1 in Bongard: Bongard discloses that the UI module 16 output is configured for controlling operation of the one or more accessories 12, this typically involves communicating with the at least one or more accessories via the wireless communication interface 25 to cause at least one of initiating operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12, ceasing operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12, and adjusting operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12 [See at least Bongard, 0044]);
receiving the user-specified high-level accessory-related rule at the accessory-equipped vehicle (See at least Fig. 1 in Bongard: Bongard discloses that the UI module 16 is configured to provide interface controls to allow a user 13 to provide input, for example, to affect operation of any of the accessories 12 associated with the vehicle 14 [See at least Bongard, 0036]);
translating the received user-specified high-level accessory-related rule into a lower- level accessory-related configuration readable by an accessory interface unit having one or more communication ports adapted for communication (See at least Fig. 1 in Bongard: Bongard discloses that the UI module 16 output is configured for controlling operation of the one or more accessories 12, this typically involves communicating with the at least one or more accessories via the wireless communication interface 25 to cause at least one of initiating operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12, ceasing operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12, and adjusting operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12 [See at least Bongard, 0044]. Preparing the rules for transmission to the accessories via communication interface 25 and transmitting them may be regarded as translating them) with sensors or actuators associated with the accessory (See at least Fig. 1 in Bongard: Bongard discloses that the accessories include a pair of driving lights 121, a pair of air springs 122, an air compressor 123, and a locking differential 124 [See at least Bongard, 0033]. Bongard further discloses that at least some of the accessories 12 are associated with sensor apparatus 28 [See at least Bongard, 0033]); and
providing the lower-level accessory-related configuration to the accessory interface unit (See at least Fig. 1 in Bongard: Bongard discloses that the UI module 16 output is configured for controlling operation of the one or more accessories 12, this typically involves communicating with the at least one or more accessories via the wireless communication interface 25 to cause at least one of initiating operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12, ceasing operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12, and adjusting operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12 [See at least Bongard, 0044]);
whereby the accessory interface unit cooperates with the sensors or actuators associated with the accessory to operate the accessory in accordance with the received user- specified high-level accessory-related rule (See at least Fig. 1 in Bongard: Bongard discloses that the UI module 16 output is configured for controlling operation of the one or more accessories 12, this typically involves communicating with the at least one or more accessories via the wireless communication interface 25 to cause at least one of initiating operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12, ceasing operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12, and adjusting operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12 [See at least Bongard, 0044]. Bongard further discloses that the accessories include a pair of driving lights 121, a pair of air springs 122, an air compressor 123, and a locking differential 124 [See at least Bongard, 0033]. Bongard further discloses that at least some of the accessories 12 are associated with sensor apparatus 28 [See at least Bongard, 0033]).
Oh teaches wherein the initial state includes default values of environmental variables (Oh teaches that the test-driving experience service providing server may execute the vehicle test-driving virtual experience mode to provide a virtual driving environment setting interface by which at least one of a virtual driving parameter and a scenario for setting an environment of the virtual driving on the basis of the vehicle description application is set [See at least Oh, 0023]), wherein the test configuration inputted via a touchscreen further comprises a simulated event (Oh teaches that the test-driving experience service providing server may execute the vehicle test-driving virtual experience mode to provide a virtual driving environment setting interface by which at least one of a virtual driving parameter and a scenario for setting an environment of the virtual driving on the basis of the vehicle description application is set [See at least Oh, 0023]. The virtual driving may be regarded as a simulated event); and
an environmental variable describing an environment in which the vehicle is operating (Oh teaches that the test-driving experience service providing server may execute the vehicle test-driving virtual experience mode to provide a virtual driving environment setting interface by which at least one of a virtual driving parameter and a scenario for setting an environment of the virtual driving on the basis of the vehicle description application is set [See at least Oh, 0023]),
wherein the method further comprises simulating a resultant state of the accessory-equipped vehicle when operating under the control of the test configuration (Oh teaches that the test-driving experience service providing server may provide a test-driving virtual experience scenario image which is an augmented reality image providing the virtual driving experience environment on the basis of at least one of the set virtual driving parameter and scenario [See at least Oh, 0024]); and
displaying, on the touchscreen, the resultant state of the accessory-equipped vehicle (when the test-driving virtual experience scenario image is output, the test-driving experience service providing server may include event content related to a second vehicle function in the test-driving virtual experience scenario image and output the test-driving virtual experience scenario image).
However, none of the prior art of record, taken either alone or in combination, teaches or suggests where the vehicle’s resultant state after the simulation as per the user-specified test configuration is displayed before loading the test configuration into the accessory-equipped vehicle.
While Bongard does deal with integration an accessory, as discussed above, the changes made via the user interface do not involve simulating operation of a vehicle in an environment and they also immediately take effect in the vehicle, rather than displaying results of such a simulation first (See at least Fig. 1 in Bongard: Bongard discloses that the UI module 16 output is configured for controlling operation of the one or more accessories 12, this typically involves communicating with the at least one or more accessories via the wireless communication interface 25 to cause at least one of initiating operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12, ceasing operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12, and adjusting operation of at least one of the one or more accessories 12 [See at least Bongard, 0044]. There is no suggestion here that the state of any accessory is displayed to the user before the changes it implements take effect, nor is there any simulation of a vehicle in an environment).
Conversely, Oh does discuss simulation of driving of a vehicle using user-specified parameters as discussed above without loading any test information into an actual vehicle (See at least [Oh, 0023]). Oh further teaches displaying the results of the simulation (See at least [Oh, 0024]). However, Oh is silent as to whether or not the displaying of the results occurs before, during, or after any step of loading the test configuration into the vehicle, since Oh does not teach loading data into a real physical vehicle at all post-simulation.
Chan does not address the deficiencies of Bongard or Oh. Chan does teach a typical vehicle simulation system in the prior art whereby there is communication between the simulation system and the vehicle (See at least [Chan, 0056]). However, Chan is silent as to whether test configuration information is transferred to the vehicle before, during, or after any step of displaying simulation results to the user (See at least [Chan, 0096]). Chan also suggests that the simulation system may even be present onboard the vehicle, which would of course not be conducive to avoiding loading the test configuration information into the vehicle only after displaying it (See at least [Chan, 0056]).
In other words, the specific order of operations of a user entering in an accessory-related test configuration via a touch screen, followed by displaying the resultant state of the simulation using that test configuration to the user before ever loading the test configuration information into the particular accessory-equipped vehicle is not taught by the prior art of record.
For at least the above stated reasons, claims 1, 12 and 16 are allowed over the prior art of record.
Regarding claims 2-10, these claims are allowed at least by virtue of their dependence on claim 1.
Regarding claims 13-15 and 17, these claims are allowed at least by virtue of their dependence on claim 12.
