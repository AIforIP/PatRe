Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
The text of those sections of Title 35, U.S. Code not included in this action can be found in a prior Office action.
The factual inquiries for establishing a background for determining obviousness under 35 U.S.C. 103 are summarized as follows:
1. Determining the scope and contents of the prior art.
2. Ascertaining the differences between the prior art and the claims at issue.
3. Resolving the level of ordinary skill in the pertinent art.
4. Considering objective evidence present in the application indicating obviousness or nonobviousness.
This application currently names joint inventors. In considering patentability of the claims the examiner presumes that the subject matter of the various claims was commonly owned as of the effective filing date of the claimed invention(s) absent any evidence to the contrary.  Applicant is advised of the obligation under 37 CFR 1.56 to point out the inventor and effective filing dates of each claim that was not commonly owned as of the effective filing date of the later invention in order for the examiner to consider the applicability of 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(2)(C) for any potential 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2) prior art against the later invention.

Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 38-40 are allowed.
The following is a statement of reasons for the indication of allowable subject matter:
Regarding claim 38, Dowty discloses an aircraft passenger seat unit comprising:
a seat (Dowty, ¶127, suite includes a seat);
a privacy shell at least partially surrounding the seat (Dowty, items 2510, 2504, 2508, and 2506, and ¶127-128, form a privacy shell surrounding the seat), the privacy shell being made up of a plurality of walls, including two walls extending in the same plane, and having a primary opening between the two walls allow entry to and exit from the seat unit (Dowty, figure 26e, items 2609 and 2602, door at opening for the privacy shell with left and right sides of door defining a first and second edge); and
a door (Dowty, figure 26e, items 2609 and 2502), movable between a first position in which the door at least partially blocks off the primary opening (Dowty, figure 26e, item 2609, solid line shows door in a position where the door partially blocks of the primary opening), and a second position, in which the door provides at least partial access to the primary opening (Dowty, figure 26e, item 2609 and ¶134, door moves between an opened and a closed position wherein it closes to cover the side edge where the storage opening is located), wherein the door is arranged to move between the first and second positions in a direction offset by an angle from the two parallel walls of the privacy shell (Dowty, figure 26e, see callout below, door direction of movement is offset from an angle of a first (left) edge of the opening), and
wherein the door is arranged to slide between the first position and the second position in a direction along a length of the door (Dowty, figure 26e, items 2602 and 2609, door slides between different positions along a length of the door), except:
wherein the two walls are parallel
wherein the door is arranged to move between the first and second positions in a direction offset by an angle from the two parallel walls of the privacy shell;
and wherein the door is angled relative to the two parallel walls of the privacy shell in the secondary position.
Dowty also teaches the two walls being parallel.
The prior art fails to teach the limitations “wherein the door is arranged to move between the first and second positions in a direction offset by an angle from the two parallel walls of the privacy shell; and wherein the door is angled relative to the two parallel walls of the privacy shell in the secondary position.”
Thus, the prior art fails to teach the claimed matter alone and it would not have been obvious to meet the claims either without undue hindsight based on the applicant' s disclosure.
Claims 39-40 are allowed for being dependent on claim 38.
Response to Arguments
Applicant’s arguments with respect to claim(s) 38-40 have been considered and are persuasive. Previous rejections of these claims have been withdrawn
Applicant's arguments filed 10/01/2024 with respect to claims 38-40 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
Applicant argues that:
Kroll fails to teach a sliding door, that one of ordinary skill would find no motivation to modify Kroll with a sliding door, and that Kroll teaches away from such a modification.
Kroll failing to teach a sliding door is moot because an intervening reference is used to teach such a sliding door.
In response to applicant' s argument that there is no teaching, suggestion, or motivation to combine the references, the examiner recognizes that obviousness may be established by combining or modifying the teachings of the prior art to produce the claimed invention where there is some teaching, suggestion, or motivation to do so found either in the references themselves or in the knowledge generally available to one of ordinary skill in the art.  See In re Fine, 837 F.2d 1071, 5 USPQ2d 1596 (Fed. Cir. 1988), In re Jones, 958 F.2d 347, 21 USPQ2d 1941 (Fed. Cir. 1992), and KSR International Co. v. Teleflex, Inc., 550 U.S. 398, 82 USPQ2d 1385 (2007).  In this case, the door of Kroll is modified with the sliding door of Cailleteau with a reasonable expectation of success in order to reduce the clearance space required to open and close the door (a swinging door as described in Kroll requires that the doors arc of travel be clear to open). Furthermore, applicant does not provide any reasoning as to why there would be no motivation to modify the door of Kroll with a sliding door.
Applicant’s arguments regarding teaching away are not persuasive because the cited parts of Kroll teach preferred embodiments and do not state that the door can not be a sliding door.
Dowty fails to teach a passenger compartment accessible from the interior of the passenger seat unit, that it would not be practical nor would there be reason to consider adapting the forward monument, and that Dowty teaches away from including the forward monument as part of the accommodation unit.
Dowty failing to teach a storage compartment which is accessible from the inside of the seat unit is moot because an intervening reference is used to teach such an accessible compartment.  Furthermore, applicant’s arguments bring into doubt the claim scope of this limitation, which is broader and merely requires that “the front side of the storage unit provides access from the interior of the aircraft passenger seat unit” and not that the front side of the storage unit is located on an interior side of the privacy shell.
Applicant’s arguments that there would be no reason to modify the arrangement of Dowty because altering/modifying the arrangement would not be practical are not persuasive because applicant fails to state what particularly makes modifications not practical or otherwise non-obvious apart from a mere assertation that this is the case. In response to applicant' s argument that there is no teaching, suggestion, or motivation to combine the references, the examiner recognizes that obviousness may be established by combining or modifying the teachings of the prior art to produce the claimed invention where there is some teaching, suggestion, or motivation to do so found either in the references themselves or in the knowledge generally available to one of ordinary skill in the art.  See In re Fine, 837 F.2d 1071, 5 USPQ2d 1596 (Fed. Cir. 1988), In re Jones, 958 F.2d 347, 21 USPQ2d 1941 (Fed. Cir. 1992), and KSR International Co. v. Teleflex, Inc., 550 U.S. 398, 82 USPQ2d 1385 (2007).  In this case, the storage unit of Dowty is modified with the holders and the location of the front of the storage unit providing access from inside the seat unit of Kroll with a reasonable expectation of success in order to provide a space to hang garments (Kroll, ¶50) and to allow passengers to access and remove items from the storage unit without blocking the aisle.
The examiner respectfully disagrees. Applicant points to arbitrary deliniations between the forward monument and the passenger suite, but also notes that the forward monument forms a forward part of the passenger suite. Such arbitrary bounderies do not suggest an actual distinction between the forward monument and the suite as relates to claim language. Additionally Dowty does not say that the modifications could not be made which would be the standard for teaching away.
