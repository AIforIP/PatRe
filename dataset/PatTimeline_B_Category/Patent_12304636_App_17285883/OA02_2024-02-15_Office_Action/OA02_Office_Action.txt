The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:

(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
The text of those sections of Title 35, U.S. Code not included in this action can be found in a prior Office action.
The factual inquiries for establishing a background for determining obviousness under 35 U.S.C. 103 are summarized as follows:
1. Determining the scope and contents of the prior art.
2. Ascertaining the differences between the prior art and the claims at issue.
3. Resolving the level of ordinary skill in the pertinent art.
4. Considering objective evidence present in the application indicating obviousness or nonobviousness.
This application currently names joint inventors. In considering patentability of the claims the examiner presumes that the subject matter of the various claims was commonly owned as of the effective filing date of the claimed invention(s) absent any evidence to the contrary.  Applicant is advised of the obligation under 37 CFR 1.56 to point out the inventor and effective filing dates of each claim that was not commonly owned as of the effective filing date of the later invention in order for the examiner to consider the applicability of 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(2)(C) for any potential 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2) prior art against the later invention.

Claim(s) 37 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Dowty (US 20180281963 A1) in view of Kroll (US 20130248655 A1) as applied to claim 24 above, and in the alternative further in view of Riedinger (US 5024398 A).
Regarding claim 37, Dowty as modified by Kroll teaches the aircraft passenger seat unit according to claim 24, wherein the privacy shell extends to a height above a floor of the aircraft passenger seat unit (Dowty, items 2510, 2504, 2508, and 2506, shell has a height above the passenger seat floor), except:
wherein the height is at least 100 cm.
However, Dowty shows a privacy shell having a dimension that is in a similar range to the claimed range and the privacy shell has inherent dimensions.
The applicant has not established criticality for the privacy shell being at least 100 cm. It would have been obvious for one of ordinary skill in the art to modify the privacy shell to be at least 100 cm in order to obscure the view into the privacy shell thereby providing privacy to any occupant.
Alternatively, Riedinger (US 5024398 A) teaches an aircraft passenger seat unit with a privacy shell wherein the privacy shell extends to a height above a floor of the aircraft passenger seat unit (Riedinger, ¶5, privacy shell height is above eye level; eye level is above 100 cm).
Dowty as modified by Kroll and Riedinger are both considered analogous art as they are both in the same field of aircraft passenger accommodations. It would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the application for one of ordinary skill in the art to modify the privacy shell height of Dowty as modified by Kroll with the privacy shell extending to eye level height of Riedinger with a reasonable expectation of success in order to provide privacy to occupants.
Response to Arguments
Applicant’s arguments, see page 6 of applicant’s reply, filed 01/29/2024, with respect to drawing objections and rejection to claim 39 under 35 USC § 112(b) have been fully considered and are persuasive.  These objections/rejections have been withdrawn.
Applicant's arguments filed 01/29/2024  have been fully considered but they are not persuasive. Applicant argues that Dowty as modified by Kroll fails to teach the “front side of the storage unit may provide access to the holders of the storage unit” and that the back side of the storage unit is “located against the interior face of the privacy shell”
In particular applicant argues that item 2504 of Dowty (of which 2506 is a front edge of) is not a storage unit because the storage unit can not be both the privacy shell and the storage unit because the claim requires that the storage unit include “a back side located against an interior face of the privacy shell.”
The examiner respectfully disagrees. One of ordinary skill in the art would interpret the claim limitation at issue as requiring a back side and an interior shell, but not that these need to be separated structures. The specification of the present application event suggests that there does not need to be a gap between the back side of the storage unit and the shell (see page 4, lines 6-12). As such the broadest reasonable interpretation of this claim language would read on figure 26a-e, as mapped above.
Applicant also argues that Dowty does not teach a privacy shell at least partially surrounding the seat, the privacy shell having a primary opening defining a first and second edge to allow entry to and exit from the seat unit and a door arranged to move between the first and second positions in a direction offset by an angle from the first edge of the primary opening of the privacy shell wherein the first edge is adjacent to the door when the door is in the second position and the door is arranged to slide between the first position and the second position in a direction along a length of the door
In particular applicant argues that the door movement is not offset relative to the edge of a primary opening to which the door is adjacent in the second position and the door is not arranged to slide between a first and second position along a length of the door.
The examiner respectfully disagrees. One of ordinary skill in the art would interpret the door of Dowty as moving at an angle offset from the left edge in accordance with the required claim language. Additionally the door is show to slide between a first and second position in figure 26e.
