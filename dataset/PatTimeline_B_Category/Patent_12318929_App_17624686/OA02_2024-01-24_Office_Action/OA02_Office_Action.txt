Drawings
The drawings are objected to under 37 CFR 1.83(a), which states The drawings must show every feature of the invention specified in the claims.
The bevel gears(claim 7) in combination with “a second axis offset from the first axis” (claim 1 from which claim 7 depends) must be shown or the features canceled from the claims.  A detailed illustration is essential for a proper understanding of the invention, since the meaning of the term “offset” is unclear in combination with the “bevel gears”.  It would appear that bevel gears would necessarily have axis that are angled and thus intersecting with one another rather than “offset”.  Accordingly, a mere graphical symbol or labeled representation is not acceptable.1
No new matter should be entered.

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112(a)
The following is a quotation of the first paragraph of 35 U.S.C. 112(a):
(a)  IN GENERAL.—The specification shall contain a written description of the invention, and of the manner and process of making and using it, in such full, clear, concise, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to which it pertains, or with which it is most nearly connected, to make and use the same,  and shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor or joint inventor of carrying out the invention.
Claim 7 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(a) as failing to comply with the written description requirement.
The claims contain subject matter which was not described in the specification in such a way as to reasonably convey to one skilled in the relevant art that the inventor or a joint inventor, at the time the application was filed, had possession of the claimed invention.
The original disclosure supported three alternative species of second state reduction assemblies (4), i.e., belt/pulley, or spur gears, or bevel gears.  There is no original disclosure of a single embodiment combining the independent claim 1 “belt” (newly amended therein from former claim 5) with the dependent claim 7 “spur gears” or “bevel gears”.  Accordingly, claim 7, which depends from claim 1, constitutes new matter.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112(b)
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
Claims 1-4 and 6-10 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The limitation “a second axis offset from the first axis” (claims 1 and 10) is disclosed as being in combination with “bevel gears” (written description and claim 7) such that the meaning of the term “offset” is unclear.  It would appear that bevel gears would necessarily have axis that are intersecting with one another.  For purposes of application of the prior art elsewhere below the term “offset” is taken to broadly include not only offset spacing without intersection (i.e., parallel and spaced by a constant offset) but also offset angling with intersection (intersecting at an offset angle therebetween).
Claim 10 is worded similarly to claim 1 and claims 2-4 and 6-9 depend from claim 1 such that they are similarly rejected.

Response to Arguments
Applicant argues that bevel gears and offset axis were each separately generically known in the art such that the drawings need not show them together in detail.  This is not persuasive.  As detailed in the drawing objections and 35 USC 112 clarity rejections elsewhere above, bevel gears were known to have angled/intersecting axis rather than parallel/offset axis.  Accordingly, the claimed combination of bevel gears and offset axis must be shown in detail in order to understand the scope of the claims.
Applicant’s arguments that the claims have been amended to overcome anticipation by Wang are not persuasive.  Wang continues to anticipate the newly amended claims as is detailed in the reworded rejections above.  Contrary to applicant’s assertion Wang discloses a belt (17, 18).
Applicant’s arguments that the claims have been amended to overcome anticipation by Pei are not persuasive.  Pei continues to anticipate the newly amended claims as is detailed in the reworded rejections above.  Contrary to applicant’s assertion the examiner’s rejection relies on the shaft to comprise both of elements 43 and 44 (there being no claim limitation forbidding multipart shafts), which are shown to be connected together to rotate within bearing 32 in figure 8.  Further, elements 48 and 43 are shown to be connected to rotate together within a common upper bearing (upper bearing shown without reference character in upper portion of figure 8).  Applicant has failed to obtain the prior art structure, test it, and submit evidence proving the prior art structure to be incapable of functioning as claimed as is required by MPEP 2112.01 and 2114.
