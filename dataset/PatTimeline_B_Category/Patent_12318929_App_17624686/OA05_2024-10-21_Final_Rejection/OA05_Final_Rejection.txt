Response to Arguments
Applicant argues that Pei fails to meet the newly amended limitations of claims 1 and 10, albeit based upon phraseology (e.g., “fixed”, “rotary electric machine”, “cycloidal pinion”, etc.) which is not expressly present in the claims, neither to require nor expressly forbid.  This is NOT persuasive.  Limitations not expressly recited in the claims cannot be read into the claims. See In re Van Geuns, 988 F.2d 1181, 26 USPQ2d 1057 (Fed. Cir. 1993).
For instance, applicant argues that Pei discloses that “the driven pulley 25 is fixed to the input shaft 29 and the input shaft 29 of the hollow cycloidal reducer 22 is driven by the servo motor 21 to cause the first cantilever 20 to rotate about the axis of the input shaft 29” (emphasis added).  This is NOT persuasive.  Firstly, that which applicant argues to be present in Pei is not expressly forbidden by the claim.  For instance, the claim does not expressly forbid a driven pulley 25 from being “fixed” to the input shaft 29.  Secondly, it appears that applicant’s own pulley is fixed to applicant’s own hollow shaft which it drives such that the claim could not be amended to match applicant’s argument (to be unfixed) without the addition of new matter. Note that the first axis (both in applicant’s invention and in Pei) is not a structural element but rather an imaginary axis about which the hollow shaft rotates, i.e., an axis of rotation.  If applicant intends to interpret the axis as being a structural element that rotates with the hollow shaft then note that applicant’s own invention would appear to fail to read on such interpretation.  Regardless, the claim does not expressly forbid the first axis of the hollow shaft from being an axis of rotation about which the hollow shaft rotates, nor does the claim otherwise stipulate that the axis is somehow caused to rotate with the hollow shaft.
Lei suggests the amendments actually recited in claims 1 and 10 as is detailed in the reworded rejection above.  Specifically, Lei suggests “the output (25) of the second stage reduction assembly is adapted (via belt 24) to engage with the input (23) of the second stage reduction assembly to cause a rotation of the first arm link (30) about the first axis (axis of rotation about which 29 and 30 rotate) by driving the hollow shaft (29) to rotate about the first axis (axis of rotation about which 29 and 30 rotate)”.
