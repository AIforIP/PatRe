Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1-2, 4-11, 13-20, and 22 are allowed.
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
As discussed in the Response to Arguments above, each of independent claims 1, 11, and 19 include allowable subject matter.
Regarding independent claim 1, the claim includes limitations directed towards “[enabling] an autonomous movement of said vehicle to avoid said predicted path of said moving object while staying within said bounds of said parking space and (b) if said predicted path of said moving object is determined to collide with said vehicle while within said bounds of said parking space, enable said autonomous movement to move said vehicle outside of said bounds of said parking space and within said distances between said vehicle and said stationary objects…” Independent claim 19 similarly includes limitations directed towards “moving said vehicle to avoid said predicted path of said moving object while staying within said bounds of said parking space.” In other words, the vehicle must be enabled to avoid the predicted path of the moving object without leaving the boundary of the vehicle’s parking space. The closest prior art of record, Han, Sabeti, Nagy, and Lord, fail to teach or suggest, alone or in combination, in combination with the other claimed elements, such collision avoidance while constrained to the bounds of a parking space. A search for this limitation proved unfruitful, providing no prior art with which one of ordinary skill in the art would be motivated to combine or modify with the prior art of record in order to provide at least the above-recited limitation in combination with the other claimed elements. Yoon et al. (US 2017/0096167 A1) teaches a parking guidance apparatus and method for a vehicle, including the maintenance of a distance to a parking slot line to avoid collision between an end of a bumper of the host vehicle and an adjacent vehicle (see at least [0124]). However, this collision avoidance technique does not appear to rely upon a predicted path of a moving object.
Regarding independent claim 11, the claim includes limitations directed towards “…and (v) said processor is further configured to (a) provide a first of said plurality of discrete steps, (b) determine whether said moving object performed said first of said plurality of discrete steps using said computer vision operations, (c) provide a second of said plurality of discrete steps in response to determining that said first of said plurality of discrete steps has been performed, and (d) re-analyze said course correction for said moving object in response to determining that said first of said plurality of discrete steps has not been performed.” The closest prior art of record, Han, Sabeti, Nagy, and Lord, fail to teach or suggest, alone or in combination, in combination with the other claimed elements, the above-recited limitations. A search for this limitation proved unfruitful, providing no prior art with which one of ordinary skill in the art would be motivated to combine or modify with the prior art of record in order to provide at least the above-recited limitation in combination with the other claimed elements. Shannon et al. (US 2021/0115889 A1) teaches ([0040]) that the autonomous vehicle 102 determines whether the passenger has enacted an instructed action. However, these actions correspond to activities such as fastening seatbelts rather than collision avoidance; moreover, these instructed actions are not directed towards a driver or passenger of another vehicle (i.e., a mobile object).
Therefore, independent claims 1, 11, and 19 are considered to be allowable.
Claims 2, 4-10, 13-18, 20, and 22 inherit the above-discussed allowable subject matter of their respective independent claims. Accordingly, claims 2, 4-10, 13-18, 20, and 22 are considered to be allowable under similar reasoning as their respective independent claims.
