Response to Arguments
Applicant’s arguments, see Pgs. 11-13, filed 11/30/2023, with respect to the 35 USC 112(f) interpretation have been fully considered but are not persuasive.
Applicant argues that independent claims 1 and 11 have been amended in a manner which obviates the need for interpretation under 35 USC 112(f). The Examiner respectfully disagrees. Amended independent claim 1 recites in part: “An apparatus comprising: an interface configured to receive pixel data…” While the claim does not use the word “means”, “an interface” corresponds to a generic placeholder term, while “configured to receive pixel data…” corresponds to functional language without sufficient structure to perform the recited function. Indeed, the claim does not define “an interface” as having any particular structure whatsoever. In order to receive pixel data, it must be known what the interface is (e.g., hardware/software, etc.) in order to achieve the claimed functions. As such, the Examiner asserts that the limitation necessitates interpretation under 35 USC 112(f) in order to determine the structure of “an interface”.
Applicant further argues that the “capture device” recited in independent claim 19 “is not a generic placeholder that is coupled with functional language… The claimed ‘capture device’ is specifically described as at least one of the element 102a-102n in the specification and claims, as originally filed,” The Examiner notes, however, that no such definition of the “capture device” as being at least one of elements 102a-102n appears in independent claim 19. Independent claim 19 recites “generating pixel data corresponding to an exterior view from a vehicle using a capture device;” While the claim does not use the word “means”, “a capture device” corresponds to a generic placeholder term, while “generating pixel data… using…” corresponds to functional language without sufficient structure to perform the recited function. As such, the Examiner asserts that the claim language necessitates interpretation under 35 USC 112(f) in order to determine the structure of “a capture device”.
Regarding the interpretation of the claimed “capture device” in the previous Office Action, the Examiner likewise disagrees with Applicant’s arguments. Applicant argues that “The capture device 102a described in the specification and drawings, as originally filed, comprises one or more of the sensor 140a, the logic 142a and the buffer 144a… The sensor 140a, the logic 142a and the buffer 144a are the specific structure of the claimed capture device.” However, Applicant’s arguments pertain to only one specific embodiment, while the broadness of the claims and written description allow for other embodiments. With respect to Applicant’s particular interpretation which cites to Pg. 17 lines 25 – Pg. 19 line 5 and FIG. 1, the Examiner notes that this language, too, is merely exemplary and does not sufficiently define the claimed “capture device”. Claim Interpretation
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(f):
(f) Element in Claim for a Combination. – An element in a claim for a combination may be expressed as a means or step for performing a specified function without the recital of structure, material, or acts in support thereof, and such claim shall be construed to cover the corresponding structure, material, or acts described in the specification and equivalents thereof.
The following is a quotation of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph:
An element in a claim for a combination may be expressed as a means or step for performing a specified function without the recital of structure, material, or acts in support thereof, and such claim shall be construed to cover the corresponding structure, material, or acts described in the specification and equivalents thereof.
The claims in this application are given their broadest reasonable interpretation using the plain meaning of the claim language in light of the specification as it would be understood by one of ordinary skill in the art.  The broadest reasonable interpretation of a claim element (also commonly referred to as a claim limitation) is limited by the description in the specification when 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, is invoked.
As explained in MPEP § 2181, subsection I, claim limitations that meet the following three-prong test will be interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph:
(A)	the claim limitation uses the term “means” or “step” or a term used as a substitute for “means” that is a generic placeholder (also called a nonce term or a non-structural term having no specific structural meaning) for performing the claimed function;
(B)	the term “means” or “step” or the generic placeholder is modified by functional language, typically, but not always linked by the transition word “for” (e.g., “means for”) or another linking word or phrase, such as “configured to” or “so that”; and
(C)	the term “means” or “step” or the generic placeholder is not modified by sufficient structure, material, or acts for performing the claimed function.
Use of the word “means” (or “step”) in a claim with functional language creates a rebuttable presumption that the claim limitation is to be treated in accordance with 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph. The presumption that the claim limitation is interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, is rebutted when the claim limitation recites sufficient structure, material, or acts to entirely perform the recited function.
Absence of the word “means” (or “step”) in a claim creates a rebuttable presumption that the claim limitation is not to be treated in accordance with 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph. The presumption that the claim limitation is not interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, is rebutted when the claim limitation recites function without reciting sufficient structure, material or acts to entirely perform the recited function.
Claim limitations in this application that use the word “means” (or “step”) are being interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, except as otherwise indicated in an Office action. Conversely, claim limitations in this application that do not use the word “means” (or “step”) are not being interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, except as otherwise indicated in an Office action.

Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1-2, 4-10, 19, and 21-22 are rejected under 35 USC 112(b), but would be allowable if amended to overcome the 35 USC 112(b) rejection(s) and if the dependent claims are rewritten in independent form including all of the limitations of the base claim and any intervening claims.
The following is a statement of reasons for the indication of allowable subject matter:
Regarding independent claim 1, the claim includes limitations directed towards “[enabling] an autonomous movement of said vehicle to avoid said predicted path of said moving object while staying within said bounds of said parking space and (b) if said predicted path of said moving object is determined to collide with said vehicle while within said bounds of said parking space, enable said autonomous movement to move said vehicle outside of said bounds of said parking space and within said distances between said vehicle and said stationary objects…” Independent claim 19 similarly includes limitations directed towards “moving said vehicle to avoid said predicted path of said moving object while staying within said bounds of said parking space.” In other words, the host vehicle must avoid the predicted path of the moving object and cannot leave the boundary of the vehicle’s parking space. A search for this limitation proved unfruitful, providing no prior art with which one of ordinary skill in the art would be motivated to combine or modify with the prior art of record in order to provide at least the above-recited limitation in combination with the other claimed elements. Yoon et al. (US 2017/0096167 A1) teaches a parking guidance apparatus and method for a vehicle, including the maintenance of a distance to a parking slot line to avoid collision between an end of a bumper of the host vehicle and an adjacent vehicle (see at least [0124]). However, this collision avoidance technique does not appear to rely upon a predicted path.
Claims 2 and 4-10 are dependent upon claim 1 and therefore inherit the allowable subject matter of claim 1.
Regarding claim 21, the claim includes limitations directed towards “(ii) determine whether said moving object performed said first of said plurality of discrete steps using said computer vision operations, (iii) provide a second of said plurality of discrete steps in response to determining that said first of said plurality of discrete steps has been performed and (iv) re-analyze said course correction for said moving object in response to determining that said first of said plurality of discrete steps has not been performed.” A search for this limitation proved unfruitful, providing no prior art with which one of ordinary skill in the art would be motivated to combine or modify with the prior art of record in order to provide at least the above-recited limitation in combination with the other claimed elements. Shannon et al. (US 2021/0115889 A1) teaches ([0040]) that the autonomous vehicle 102 determines whether the passenger has enacted an instructed action. However, these actions correspond to activities such as fastening seatbelts rather than collision avoidance; moreover, these instructed actions are not directed towards a driver or passenger of another vehicle.
