Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
In the event the determination of the status of the application as subject to AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 (or as subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103) is incorrect, any correction of the statutory basis (i.e., changing from AIA  to pre-AIA ) for the rejection will not be considered a new ground of rejection if the prior art relied upon, and the rationale supporting the rejection, would be the same under either status.
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claim(s) 1-3 and 5-12 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by US 7578477 B2 French; Clive et al.
1.	Regarding claim 1, French teaches, an aircraft fuselage door (fig. 1, element 10) comprising a movable internal panel (fig. 3, element 26) to be subjected to pressurization (col. 4, lines 1-5 pressure), the door comprising an aerodynamic external panel (fig. 3, element 28), the external panel being on an external face of the internal panel, covering a surface of the external face and being resiliently connected to the internal panel to form an internal free space between the internal panel and the external panel (fig. 3), the internal panel being movable relative to the external panel (fig. 6, elements 26 and 28).
2.	Regarding claim 2, French teaches, the door of claim 1, comprising a plurality of resilient elements between the external panel and the internal panel (fig. 3, elements 38), the resilient elements being configured to resiliently connect the external panel to the internal panel (fig. 3).
3.	Regarding claim 3, French teaches, the door of claim 2, wherein the external panel and the internal panel each have a substantially rectangular shape (fig. 2) and the door includes at least four resilient elements arranged respectively in four corners of the rectangular shape of the external panel and of the internal panel (col. 4, lines 30-31 “Each panel 18 comprises two plates: one front plate 26 and one rear plate 28”, fig, 3, elements 38 and each side).
4.	Regarding claim 5, French teaches, the door of claim 1, wherein the internal panel is movable relative to the external panel to be moved toward or away from the external panel (fig. 6, elements 26 and 28), the internal panel being configured to: push the external panel when it is moved toward the external panel, coming into contact, at least partially (fig. 8, element 48 allows contact between element 26 and 28), with the external panel; and pull the external panel with it when it is moved away from the external panel (fig. 8).
5.	Regarding claim 6, French teaches, the door of claim 1, wherein the external panel has at least an access hatch for allowing access to the internal panel (fig. 13, element 74).
6.	Regarding claim 7, French teaches, an aircraft fuselage part comprising a door of claim 1 and a door frame receiving the door (fig. 9).
7.	Regarding claim 8, French teaches, the fuselage part of claim 7, wherein the external panel includes at least a centering piece on at least a part of a periphery of the external panel (fig. 6, element 39), the centering piece comprising a contact face configured to interact with a complementary face of the frame, the contact face being configured to come into contact with the complementary face when the door is in a closed position (fig. 6, element 39, col. 4, lines 49-52).
8.	Regarding claim 9, French teaches, the fuselage part of claim 8, wherein the contact face of the centering piece and the complementary face of the frame are configured such that the contact between the contact face and the complementary face corresponds to frustoconical contact (fig. 13 element 38 and 39).
9.	Regarding claim 10, French teaches, the fuselage part of claim 7, wherein the internal panel of the door includes at least a seal on a periphery of the internal panel, the seal being configured to come into contact with an edge of the frame when the door is in a closed position (fig. 3, element 30).
10.	Regarding claim 11, French teaches, the fuselage part of claim 7, wherein the door includes a plurality of latches configured to block or to allow a movement of the internal panel, relative to the frame, when the door is in a closed position (fig. 3. element 34).
11.	Regarding claim 12, French teaches, an aircraft comprising a fuselage part of claim 7 (fig. 1).
Allowable Subject Matter
Claim 4 objected to as being dependent upon a rejected base claim, but would be allowable if rewritten in independent form including all of the limitations of the base claim and any intervening claims. The following is a statement of reasons for the indication of allowable subject matter:  Prior art French teaches an aircraft door with a resilient member, but fails to teach where the resilient member is a leaf spring. It would have not been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art to add the leaf spring between the door panels without the benefit of the present disclosure.
