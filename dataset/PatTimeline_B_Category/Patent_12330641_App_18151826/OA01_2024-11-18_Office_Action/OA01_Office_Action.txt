Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claim 3 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor, or for pre-AIA  the applicant regards as the invention.
Claim 3 recites the limitation “the various points in time” in line 6. There is insufficient antecedent basis for the limitation in the claim. For examination purposes the limitation “various time constants” at claim 3 line 2 has been considered as --various points in time--.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Claims 1-10 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because the claimed invention is directed to mental processes and mathematical concepts without significantly more.
Regarding claim 1,
Step 2A prong 1: The claim recites:
The mental process of “projecting a traffic lane of the roadway to be followed onto the images”.
The mental process of “determining a travel trajectory of the motor vehicle in reference to the images based on a self-motion estimate and projecting the determined travel trajectory onto the images”.
The mental process of “deriving a control variable for lateral guidance of the motor vehicle from a comparison between the traffic lane projected onto the images and the determined travel trajectory projected onto the images”.
Step 2A prong 2: The claim recites:
The additional element of “at least one camera of the motor vehicle, wherein the at least one camera captures images of a roadway”.
These judicial exceptions are not integrated into a practical application because the element is recited at a high level of generality (i.e., as a general means of gathering images for use in the recited processing steps), and amounts to mere data gathering, which is a form of insignificant extra-solution activity.
Step 2B: The claim(s) does/do not include additional elements that are sufficient to amount to significantly more than the judicial exception because using a camera to collect images of a roadway is a well-understood, routine, and conventional activity in the field.
Claims 2-8 recite further mental and/or mathematical processes and no additional elements capable of integrating the recited judicial exceptions into a practical application or that are sufficient to amount to more than the judicial exceptions.
Regarding claim 9,
Step 2A prong 1: The claim recites:
The mental process of “projecting a traffic lane of the roadway to be followed onto the images”.
The mental process of “determining a travel trajectory of the motor vehicle in reference to the images based on a self-motion estimate and projecting the determined travel trajectory onto the images”.
The mental process of “deriving a control variable for lateral guidance of the motor vehicle from a comparison between the traffic lane projected onto the images and the determined travel trajectory projected onto the images”.
Step 2A prong 2: The claim recites:
The additional element of “a non-transitory computer-readable storage medium on which is stored a computer program”.
The additional element of “at least one camera of the motor vehicle, wherein the at least one camera captures images of a roadway”.
These judicial exceptions are not integrated into a practical application because 1) the storage medium amounts to mere instructions to apply the exceptions using a generic computer component which cannot integrate the judicial exceptions into a practical application, and 2) the camera is recited at a high level of generality (i.e., as a general means of gathering images for use in the recited processing steps), and amounts to mere data gathering, which is a form of insignificant extra-solution activity.
Step 2B: The claim(s) does/do not include additional elements that are sufficient to amount to significantly more than the judicial exception because 1) mere instructions to apply an exception using a generic computer component cannot provide an inventive concept, and 2) using a camera to collect images of a roadway is a well-understood, routine, and conventional activity in the field.
Regarding claim 10,
Step 2A prong 1: The claim recites:
The mental process of “projecting a traffic lane of the roadway to be followed onto the images”.
The mental process of “determining a travel trajectory of the motor vehicle in reference to the images based on a self-motion estimate and projecting the determined travel trajectory onto the images”.
The mental process of “deriving a control variable for lateral guidance of the motor vehicle from a comparison between the traffic lane projected onto the images and the determined travel trajectory projected onto the images”.
Step 2A prong 2: The claim recites:
The additional element of “at least one camera which, during operation, captures images of a roadway”.
The additional element of “a control device”.
These judicial exceptions are not integrated into a practical application because 1) the control device amounts to mere instructions to apply the exceptions using a generic computer component which cannot integrate the judicial exceptions into a practical application, and 2) the camera is recited at a high level of generality (i.e., as a general means of gathering images for use in the recited processing steps), and amounts to mere data gathering, which is a form of insignificant extra-solution activity.
Step 2B: The claim(s) does/do not include additional elements that are sufficient to amount to significantly more than the judicial exception because 1) mere instructions to apply an exception using a generic computer component cannot provide an inventive concept, and 2) using a camera to collect images of a roadway is a well-understood, routine, and conventional activity in the field.
These rejections may be overcome by amending claims 1, 9, and 10 to recite a step of “steering a motor vehicle according to the derived control variable” which would integrate the recited judicial exceptions into a practical application at step 2A prong 2. Support is found in Applicant’s original disclosure at pg. 15 lines 7-11.
