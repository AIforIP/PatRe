Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
Claims 3 and 11 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor, or for pre-AIA  the applicant regards as the invention.
Claim 3 line 3 recites the limitation “one or more first guide grooves or first guide holes”. It is unclear if this limitation is referring to the one or more guide grooves or guide holes recited in claim 1 or if this limitation requires additional guide grooves or guide holes.
Claim 11 line 5 recites the limitation “tightly wound spring tube”. The term “tightly” is a relative term which renders the claim indefinite. The term “tightly” is not defined by the claim, the specification does not provide a standard for ascertaining the requisite degree, and one of ordinary skill in the art would not be reasonably apprised of the scope of the invention.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim 1-3, 10-12 and 15-16 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Cho (US 20180345481 A1) in view of Ihrke (US 20110071678 A1).
Regarding claim 1, Cho discloses a tendon transmission system (see Fig. 1, 1000), with tendon constrain elements (210) comprising: tendons (20); tendon fixing elements (100, 300); wherein the tendon constraint elements each comprises one or more guide grooves or guide holes (see Fig. 4, 212), wherein the tendons are configured to transmit an actuator's driving force (see paragraph [0027], wherein when pulled by an actuator (not shown), the second wires 20 cause the fingers to extend)  to a driven robot's joint (see Fig. 4, joint between 100, 200). Cho fails to disclose compound tendon sheaths or single-layer tendon sheaths. However, Ihrke teaches compound tendon sheaths (see Fig. 5, 44, 46) or single-layer tendon sheaths (44). It would have been obvious to one having ordinary skill in the art as of the effective filing date to modify Cho with compound tendon sheaths, as taught by Ihrke, to protect the tendon; to prevent stretch and creep which would adversely affect the performance of the tendon; and to prevent wear due to movement of the tendon relative to other components of the system that the tendon contacts during operation (see paragraph [0026]). As a result of the combination, the following limitations would necessarily result: a tendon transmission system (Cho, 1000) with compound tendon sheaths (Ihrke, 44, 46) or single-layer tendon sheaths (Ihrke, 44) and tendon sheath constraint elements (Cho, 210), comprising: tendons (Cho, 20); the compound tendon sheaths (Ihrke, 44, 46) or the single-layer tendon sheaths (Ihrke, 44); tendon sheath fixing elements (Cho, 100, 300); and the tendon sheath constraint elements (Cho, 210), wherein the compound tendon sheaths (Ihrke, 44, 46) comprise at least two layers of tendon sheaths from inside to outside (Ihrke, 44, 46), wherein the tendon sheath constraint elements (Cho, 210) each comprises one or more guide grooves or guide holes (Cho, 211) to guide one or more of the compound tendon sheaths (Ihrke, 44, 46) or the single-layer tendon sheaths (Ihrke, 44, 46) to slide along their respective axes (Cho, Fig. 4), wherein the tendons (Cho, 20) are configured to transmit an actuator's driving force (Cho, paragraph [0027], wherein when pulled by an actuator (not shown), the second wires 20 cause the fingers to extend) to the a driven robot's joint (Cho, joint between 100, 200), wherein the tendons (Cho, 20) are configured to slide axially along the compound tendon sheaths (Ihrke, 44, 46) in the compound tendon sheaths (Ihrke, Fig. 5).
Regarding claim 2, Cho discloses the tendon sheath constraint elements (210) comprise a type I tendon sheath constraint element (210), wherein the type I tendon sheath constraint element is a flexible element (see paragraph [0022], wherein the finger member 200 is formed of a silicone material).
Regarding claim 3, the combination of claim 1 elsewhere above would necessarily result in the following limitations: the type I tendon sheath constraint element (Cho, 210) comprises one or more first guide grooves or first guide holes (Cho, 212) to guide one to four of the compound tendon sheaths (Ihrke, 44, 46) or single-layer tendon sheaths (Ihrke, 44) to slide along their respective axes, wherein the type I tendon sheath constraint element is arranged to be installed at interphalangeal joints or metacarpophalangeal joints of a dexterous hand (Cho, Fig. 2), or toe joints or metatarsophalangeal joints of a bionic mechanical foot, or other robotic joints that are passed /bypassed by at least one but no more than four of the compound tendon sheaths or the single-layer tendon sheaths.
Regarding claim 10, Cho discloses at least one end of the tendons (20) is fixed with an actuator's output shaft or capstan (see paragraph [0027], wherein when pulled by an actuator (not shown), the second wires 20 cause the fingers to extend), wherein any point of the tendon is fixed with a rotational part (see Fig. 4, 100) of a driven robot's joint (see Fig. 4, joint between 100, 200) to transmit the actuator's driving force to the driven robot's joint (see paragraph [0027], wherein when pulled by an actuator (not shown), the second wires 20 cause the fingers to extend).
Regarding claim 11, the combination of claim 1 elsewhere above would necessarily result in the following limitations: the compound tendon sheath (Ihrke, 44, 46) comprises an inner tendon sheath (Ihrke, 44) and an outer tendon sheath (Ihrke, 46), wherein the inner tendon sheath is configured to enable the tendon to slide along an axial direction therein (Ihrke, Fig. 5). the inner sheath of the tendon adopts tightly wound spring tube (Ihrke, 46), wherein the outer tendon sheath is a flexible tube (Ihrke, 44). Cho in view of Ihrke fail to disclose the outer tendon sheath is sheathed outside the inner tendon sheath. However, it has been held that a rearrangement of parts, which does not modify the operation of the device, is an obvious matter of design choice. In re Japikse, 181 F.2d 1019, 86 USPQ 70 (CCPA 1950). In this instance, placing the sheath 46 of Ihrke inside of the sheath 44, would not modify the operation of the transmission.
Regarding claim 12, Cho fails to disclose a tendon sheath head. However, Ihrke teaches a tendon sheath head (see Fig. 5, 72). It would have been obvious to one having ordinary skill in the art as of the effective filing date to modify Cho with a tendon sheath head, as taught by Ihrke, to guide the tendon into the conduits (see paragraph [0036]); and to provide a limit for the sheaths so that they do not separate during operation. As a result of the combination, the following limitations would necessarily result: each end of the compound tendon sheaths (Ihrke, 44, 46) is provided with a respective tendon sheath head (Ihrke, 72) to bind each layer of the compound tendon sheaths together (Ihrke, Fig. 5).
Regarding claim 15, Cho discloses the type I tendon sheath constraint element (210) is a hollow soft sleeve structure (see Fig. 2 and 4), or a structure with one or more connecting bands (220) and two or more fasten rings (210), wherein the connecting bands are configured to flexibly connect each fasten ring together (see Fig. 2).
Regarding claim 16, Cho discloses a side wall of the type I tendon sheath constraint element (210) comprises one or more reinforcing ribs (220).
