Election/Restriction
Claims 10-15 are withdrawn from further consideration pursuant to 37 CFR 1.142(b), as being drawn to nonelected groups and species, there being no allowable generic or linking claim.
Applicant timely traversed the restriction (election) requirement in the reply filed on October 15, 2023.
The traversal between groups I and II is on the grounds that (1) there is no serious search burden and (2) that Groups I and II are not distinct under the  combination/subcombination test. With respect to (1), different classes and search strings would have to be searched because claim 1 is only directed to the structure of the pick and place system (this structure merely being capable of transferring a contoured composite structure), whereas claim 10 requires using the pick and place system to perform steps on the contoured composite structure (these steps are not required in a rejection to claim 1).
With respect to (II), Applicant is arguing that the Groups are not distinct by using a different test than the proper test used in the Office action mailed on September 18, 2023 (“Restriction”). In the Restriction, the distinctness test for a process and apparatus for its practice was used, which is the correct distinctness test given the claim scope of the groups. Applicant’s argument regarding combination/subcombination is directed to another unrelated test.
In this argument, Applicant argues that the pick and place system of group I cannot be used to move car parts because claim 1 recites the pick and place system for transferring and installing a contoured composite structure in a composite manufacturing system. The examiner will address this argument because it is also related to the test used the Restriction. This limitation of claim 1 is intended use. Thus, the pick and place system merely has to be capable of this intended use (the transferring and installing of a contoured composite structure in a composite manufacturing system does not have to be taught). As such, it is possible for a pick and place system for car parts to be able to anticipate claim 1.
The restriction requirement between Group I and II is still deemed proper and is therefore made FINAL.

Claim Interpretation
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(f):
(f) Element in Claim for a Combination. – An element in a claim for a combination may be expressed as a means or step for performing a specified function without the recital of structure, material, or acts in support thereof, and such claim shall be construed to cover the corresponding structure, material, or acts described in the specification and equivalents thereof.
The following is a quotation of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph:
An element in a claim for a combination may be expressed as a means or step for performing a specified function without the recital of structure, material, or acts in support thereof, and such claim shall be construed to cover the corresponding structure, material, or acts described in the specification and equivalents thereof.
The claims in this application are given their broadest reasonable interpretation using the plain meaning of the claim language in light of the specification as it would be understood by one of ordinary skill in the art.  The broadest reasonable interpretation of a claim element (also commonly referred to as a claim limitation) is limited by the description in the specification when 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, is invoked.
As explained in MPEP § 2181, subsection I, claim limitations that meet the following three-prong test will be interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph:
(A)	the claim limitation uses the term “means” or “step” or a term used as a substitute for “means” that is a generic placeholder (also called a nonce term or a non-structural term having no specific structural meaning) for performing the claimed function;
(B)	the term “means” or “step” or the generic placeholder is modified by functional language, typically, but not always linked by the transition word “for” (e.g., “means for”) or another linking word or phrase, such as “configured to” or “so that”; and
(C)	the term “means” or “step” or the generic placeholder is not modified by sufficient structure, material, or acts for performing the claimed function.
Use of the word “means” (or “step”) in a claim with functional language creates a rebuttable presumption that the claim limitation is to be treated in accordance with 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph. The presumption that the claim limitation is interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, is rebutted when the claim limitation recites sufficient structure, material, or acts to entirely perform the recited function.
Absence of the word “means” (or “step”) in a claim creates a rebuttable presumption that the claim limitation is not to be treated in accordance with 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph. The presumption that the claim limitation is not interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, is rebutted when the claim limitation recites function without reciting sufficient structure, material or acts to entirely perform the recited function.
Claim limitations in this application that use the word “means” (or “step”) are being interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, except as otherwise indicated in an Office action. Conversely, claim limitations in this application that do not use the word “means” (or “step”) are not being interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, except as otherwise indicated in an Office action.
This application includes one or more claim limitations that do not use the word “means,” but are nonetheless being interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, because the claim limitation(s) uses a generic placeholder that is coupled with functional language without reciting sufficient structure to perform the recited function and the generic placeholder is not preceded by a structural modifier.  Such claim limitation(s) are: indexing assemblies in claims 1 and 16, a vacuum system in claims 1 and 16, a load balancer assembly in claims 1 and 16, a control system in claims 1 and 16, a forming apparatus in claims 4 and 16, an x-axis motion system in claims 5 and 17, a y-axis motion system in claims 5 and 17, a stop device in claims 7 and 19, and a drive mechanism in claims 8 and 20.
Because these claim limitations are being interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, they are being interpreted to cover the corresponding structure described in the specification as performing the claimed function, and equivalents thereof. The corresponding structure is provided below.
Vacuum system comprises vacuum tubes/lines through the main beam and the plurality of end effector assemblies, wherein the vacuum tubes/lines are connected to a vacuum source, and a vacuum plate and/or vacuum cups provided on the end effector assemblies.
Load balancer assembly comprises a pair of load balancer devices, each of the devices comprising a pair of pulleys and a pair of cables or belts, wherein each cable or belt has a first end coupled to a respective pulley and a second end coupled an attachment member on the main beam. Wherein the cables or belts of respective load balancer devices each support the main beam and extend at an angle to the vertical direction (the angle not being 0 or 90 degrees).
Control system comprises a controller and a control panel coupled to the pick and place assembly.
Indexing assemblies comprise a linear actuator coupled to the main beam and having a pin, and, a cup on the tray assembly configured to receive the pin.
Forming apparatus comprises a cold pressing machine.
The x-axis and y-axis motion systems each comprise a motor and a drive belt/chain.
Stop device comprises a piston rod lock engaged to the first linear actuators to hold the position of the vacuum plate.
Drive mechanism is interpreted as an electric motor.

Rejections under 35 USC 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112:
(B)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 2-3 and 16-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112 (b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which applicant regards as the invention.
Claims 2 and 3 recite highly contoured. Claim 16 recites a complex curved configuration. It is unclear what the metes and bounds of these subjective limitations are. Highly contoured and complex contour are defined in para. [0085] of Applicant’s originally filed specification. However, this definition is filled with subjective terms like “steep” and “severe” such that it is still unclear what would and would not read on highly contoured or a complex contour.
In addition, claim 3 is grammatically awkward so as to be confusing, and it is unclear in view of Applicant’s disclosure whether the kitting try includes the straight flat kitting tray and the high contour kitting tray, or if the kitting tray comprises only one of the preceding options. For purposes of examination, claim 3 will be interpreted as follows: the tray assembly comprises one of a straight flat kitting tray, a contoured kitting tray, a tooling device, or a tray mandrel.
Claims 17-20 are rejected for depending from claim 16.

Claims 1 and 4-9 are allowed. Claims 2-3 and 16-20 would be allowable if rewritten to overcome the rejections under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), 2nd paragraph, set forth in this Office action.
