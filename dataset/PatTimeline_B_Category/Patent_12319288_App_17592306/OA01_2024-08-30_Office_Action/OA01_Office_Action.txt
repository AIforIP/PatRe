Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
In the event the determination of the status of the application as subject to AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 (or as subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103) is incorrect, any correction of the statutory basis (i.e., changing from AIA  to pre-AIA ) for the rejection will not be considered a new ground of rejection if the prior art relied upon, and the rationale supporting the rejection, would be the same under either status.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim(s) 1-3, 8, 10-13, 18, and 20 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Brahma et al. (US 2021/0383209 A1) in view of Venkatraman (US 11,782,451 B1).
Regarding claim 1, Brahma teaches
An autonomous driving apparatus for generating an intersection path reflecting a driving record, the autonomous driving apparatus comprising:
a path generating device configured to generate information on a first path for driving of an autonomous vehicle [at an intersection]; see at least FIG. 6 and [0082]-[0084] where a reference path 1610 (equivalent to a first path) may be generated by “another neural network model” (equivalent to a path generating device) of the path planning system.
a path comparison operating device configured to generate offset information between the generated information on the first path and information on a second path along which the autonomous vehicle is driving; see at least FIG. 6 and [0084]-[0085] where a data collection module (equivalent to a path comparison operating device) may determine whether the path 1605 (equivalent to a second path along which the autonomous vehicle is driving) deviates from the path 1610 by a threshold.
a comparison operation evaluating device configured to determine whether to store the information on the second path based on the generated offset information; see at least [0085] where, if the path 1605 deviates from the path 1610 by a threshold, a data collection module (equivalent to a comparison operation evaluating device) may determine that sensor data collected by the vehicle (equivalent to information on the second path) should be stored. and
a comparison operation storage configured to store the information on the second path and the offset information based on the determination of whether to store the information on the second path. See at least FIG. 4B  where the data collection module transmits data to the storage resources 130.
Examiner notes that, although Brahma does not explicitly teach that the information on a first path is for driving an autonomous vehicle at an intersection, nothing in Brahma teaches away from generating information on a first path for driving an autonomous vehicle at an intersection. Further, generating information on a first path for driving an autonomous vehicle at an intersection is considered ordinary and well-known.
For instance, Venkatraman teaches that it is known to provide a path generating device configured to generate information on a first path for driving of an autonomous vehicle at an intersection. See at least Col. 21 lines 1-48 where the AV (autonomous vehicle) uses one or more cost functions in order to generate possible future trajectories for the AV to travel through an intersection.
It would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to have modified Brahma to incorporate the teachings of Venkatraman and provide the path generating device configured to generate information on a first path for driving of an autonomous vehicle at an intersection. In doing so the overall safety of the AV is improved. For example, in a scenario that the AV is approaching an intersection having a green light,  the AV can anticipate that the green light might turn red and avoid speeding up the AV (Col. 21 lines 23-35).

Regarding claim 5, Brahma in view of Venkatraman teaches
The autonomous driving apparatus of claim 2, Brahma in view of Venkatraman teaches all of the elements of the current invention as stated above except wherein the comparison operation evaluating device is configured to:
determine to store the information on the second path in response to the distance offset information and the attribute offset information being smaller than a preset threshold, and
determine not to store the information on the second path in response to the distance offset information or the attribute offset information being greater than the preset threshold.
However, Shapira teaches that it is known to provide the driving apparatus
wherein the comparison operation evaluating device is configured to:
determine to store the information on the second path in response to the distance offset information and the attribute offset information being smaller than a preset threshold.
See at least [0218]-[0219] where “additional target trajectories may be stored with respect to a particular road segment. For example, on a multi-lane road one or more target trajectories may be stored representing intended paths of travel for vehicles in one or more lanes associated with the multi-lane road.” In this scenario, the preset threshold is the multi-lane road and the distance offset information is smaller than the preset threshold since the trajectories are located within the multi-lane road.
It would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to have modified Brahma in view of Venkatraman to incorporate the teachings of Shapira and provide the driving apparatus wherein the comparison operation evaluating device is configured to: determine to store the information on the second path in response to the distance offset information and the attribute offset information being smaller than a preset threshold. In doing so, the vehicle may be navigated based mainly upon the stored trajectory that may require much less storage space [0204].
Shin et al. (US 2019/0071068 A1) teaches that it is known to provide the autonomous driving apparatus wherein the comparison operation evaluating device is configured to: determine not to store the information on the second path in response to the distance offset information or the attribute offset information being greater than the preset threshold. See at least [0027]; [0033]-[0035]; and [0153]-[0155] where, when it is determined that the vehicle deviates from the route (based on a measured distance being greater than a threshold), the controller terminates the route learning mode (equivalent to not storing the second path).
It would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to have modified Brahma in view of Venkatraman and Shapira to incorporate the teachings of Shin and provide the autonomous driving apparatus wherein the comparison operation evaluating device is configured to: determine not to store the information on the second path in response to the distance offset information or the attribute offset information being greater than the preset threshold. In doing so, during a route driving mode and a route learning mode, the vehicle is capable of determining whether the vehicle deviates from the route based on a cumulative distance deviation and can perform a general driving mode by releasing the mode, which is currently performed, according to the result of the determination [0012].
