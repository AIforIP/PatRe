The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claim(s) 1 to 22 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by Taylor, et al. (U.S. Pre-Grant Publication 2019/0392641 A1).
As to Claims 1, 11, and 20: Taylor discloses an approach to determine objects in the real world and their properties. inter alia, the material of which the objects are composed is determined, and TAYLOR also teaches to detect the object material based on acoustic properties of tne object [see Para. 48].  In particular, Taylor discloses a method for determining a type of material of an object in a real-world environment, comprising: [See para. 48, “real world objects ... are identified, as well as, if desired, respective materials of which the objects are composed] capturing a sel of audio data from the real-world environment, wherein the real- world environment includes the object having the type of material; [See Para. 48, “a user may be prompted to tap a real-world object and the resulting sound analyzed using, e.g., fast Fourier transforms”, implicitly requires to capture the audio data and applying an artificial intelligence (Al) model to the set of audio data captured from the real-world environment to determine the type of material of the object within the real-world environment. [See Para. 48,  to match it with one or more entries in an audio fingerprint database that are correlated to respective materials and/or objects”, where using previously determined correlations between audio signal and material clearly is a form of “Artificial intelligence” model}.
As to Claims 2, 12, 14, and 21: Taylor already describes multiple objects, and a pre-trained fingerprint model covering multiple materials and corresponding audio properties.
As to Claims 3 and 13: Amplitudes and frequencies are standard design options for audio fingerprinting which the skilled person would consider when starting from Taylor.
As to Claims 4,15, and 22: Describes a standard design option for a classification system based on supervised machine learning, which the skilled person would consider as alternative to the fingerprint database described in Taylor.
As to Claims 5 to 7, 10, and 16 to 18: Taylor already discloses to use different input modalities for detecting objects and their properties (see Para. 48). As for claims 4, 15, 22, merely training a machine learning model with the input features is a standard design choice which would be considered by the skilled person in view of Taylor.
As to Claims 8, 9, and 19: Taylor already discloses to adapt audio characteristics of a virtual abject depending on the type of detected material (see Paras. 52-54).
