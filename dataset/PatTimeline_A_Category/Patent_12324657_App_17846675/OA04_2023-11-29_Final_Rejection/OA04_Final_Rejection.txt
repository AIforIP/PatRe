Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed 9/21/2023 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive. The amendments appear to be restatements of previously claimed features, already rejected by the Examiner, and therefore this action is made final.
On p. 8-9, Applicant alleges that Murphy does indicate a health status of the animal but does not teach a scaled lung score.
However, in the rejection, the slope (Fig. 9B, a derived parameter of the data of Fig. 9A) is a lung score, it is scaled by a slope threshold constant which yields a scaled lung score. This is compared to thresholds (¶[0081] signal slope threshold), which is defined as corresponding to respiratory conditions (adventitious sounds ¶[0081]) indicating the health status of the animal’s respiratory condition (¶[0077] the adventitious sounds are any of crackles, wheeze, or ronchi, see Table 1).
On p. 8, Applicant identifies the scaling factor as applied by Murphy to be chosen for distinguishing adventitious sounds from environmental noise and states therefore, that Murphy cannot be said to teach a scaled lung score.
However, the Examiner’s position is that this scaling factor is applied to the lung score as identified above, therefore resulting in a scaled lung score. Applicant’s purpose in scaling a lung score may be different from Murphy, but these alleged differences are unclaimed. All that is required by the claims is that the scaled lung scores correspond to respiratory conditions indicating a health status of an animal’s respiratory condition.
As previously identified with respect to Murphy, the cited portions are not directed to solely separating undesirable noise from targeted sounds of respiration, but instead, Murphy teaches a lung sound diagnostic system which analyzes selected frequencies of acquired lung sounds, computes values from the analysis (Fig. 5A, Fig. 8), and predicts a probable diagnosis based on the computed values (¶[0117]: “More specifically, the training database 207 includes typical data corresponding to the ratio of inspiration to expiration, the numbers and locations of coarse, medium, and fine crackles, wheezes, and rhonchi, and RMS values commonly associated with these diseases. The probable-diagnosis prediction program 204 compares the results obtained by the adventitious-sound detection program 203 for the given patient with the information in the training database and provides a proposed diagnosis, which represents the particular disease which most closely matches the results obtained for the given patient.”) As can be seen in the cited portions, the computed values include RMS values derived from the identified adventitious sound portions. With respect to the scaled lung score, in ¶[0081] a scaling factor is applied to the data in order to identify the portions that correspond to respiratory condition (adventitious sound detection): “The slope threshold constant is chosen to distinguish adventitious sounds from discrete sound artifacts (such as machine noise, skin noises, etc.) that may be present in the signal trace and may also have rapidly rising slopes. Again, empirical studies have shown that a slope threshold constant of 0.15 is adequate in most cases to distinguish adventitious sound events from sound artifacts. The resulting value is then added to the preliminary threshold trace to form a slope threshold trace.”

(a) A patent may not be obtained though the invention is not identically disclosed or described as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the subject matter sought to be patented and the prior art are such that the subject matter as a whole would have been obvious at the time the invention was made to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which said subject matter pertains.
