Response to Arguments
Applicant's amendments filed 8/21/2024 overcome the rejections under 35 U.S.C. § 112, 2nd paragraph for indefiniteness.
Applicant’s remarks filed 8/21/2024 have been considered but are not fully persuasive.
On p. 8, Applicant alleges that the Caro reference does not teach an algorithm, and even if Caro does teach an algorithm, the algorithm does not meet the algorithm as claimed.
As to the first point, Caro absolutely teaches an algorithm. An algorithm is any process. Caro teaches a computer implemented process; therefore Caro teaches an algorithm.
Applicant distinguishes the algorithm disclosed by Caro from the algorithm in the instant claims by stating that the instant claims use only passive auscultation and the algorithm of Caro requires both passive and active auscultation.
This point is not argued by the Examiner, however, the claims do not in any way disavow the use of active auscultation. Therefore, the algorithm of Caro reads on the algorithm as claimed. Applicant has used the transitional phrase comprising, which means the claims are interpreted as open and the claim does not therefore exclude any features which the claim is silent to.
On p. 9 of the Remarks dated 8/21/2024, Applicant alleges that none of the algorithms disclosed by Caro are “used with digital sound data to plot resulting data as calculated in a frequency domain where amplitudes of frequencies differentiate between categories of sounds corresponding to various levels of respiratory disease,” and that there is no “actual first data set that can be identified in Caro.”
The Examiner’s position is that the derived indicators, as identified by Applicant depicted in Figs. 3A-3D are the first data set, and they are obtained using the claimed algorithm. Applicant’s position appears to be that since Caro uses active auscultation, the first set of data as taught by Caro cannot be said to be equivalent to the first set of data claimed in the instant claims, because Applicant’s invention uses only passive auscultation. As stated by Examiner above, this is not found in the claims, they do not claim only passive auscultation in a way to disavow teachings with active auscultation. Therefore, this is unpersuasive.
On p. 9, Applicant alleges that it would not have been obvious to combine the teachings of Rothman with the invention of Pratt as modified because Rothman is used within the context of improving patient care at a hospital and is therefore irrelevant to the context of a lung condition score for a non-human animal.
This is not found persuasive, because improving patient care and display of health status, even that of a non-human animal, is considered to be reasonably pertinent as to the problem to be solved by the inventor: “non-invasive diagnosis of diseases for animals…provide a user an indication of the health of the animal being examined,” (Applicant’s Specification filed 6/22/2022 p. 1, lines 14-24). The benefit provided by the teachings of Rothman, therefore, is also relevant: because Rothman teaches that continual measurement and display of patient health improves patient care (Rothman ¶[0002], ¶[0009]). The Examiner considers that improving patient care, even in the case of non-human animals, is desirable.
On p. 9 it appears that Applicant is conceding this point: “It is asserted that one seeking a solution for determining respiratory conditions for non-human animals would seek a solution for such in a patient reference that teachings a scoring system for human patients in a hospital setting,” but it is possible that these is merely a typographical error.
