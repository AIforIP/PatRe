Claim Objections
Claims 1-20 are objected to because of the following informalities:
Regarding claim 1, line 15 recites “the measured intensities” which lacks antecedent basis. The examiner assumes “the measured intensities” are the result of the measurements of the spectral components of the interference pattern made by the spectrometer recited on lines 7-8. The examiner suggests amending line 15 of claim 1 to recite “back-projection of of the interference pattern with the measurement model”. Claims 2-14 depend on claim 1 and are therefore also objected to.

Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1-20 are allowed.
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
Regarding claim 1, the prior art of record, taken alone or in combination, neither anticipates nor renders obvious an optical coherence tomography (OCT) system for profilometry measurements of a specimen, comprising: an interferometer configured to split incident light into a reference beam and a test beam, and to interfere the test beam reflected from the specimen with the reference beam reflected from a reference mirror to produce an interference pattern; a spectrometer configured to analyze spectral components of the interference pattern at non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers; a computer-readable memory configured to store a measurement model with elements connecting different depth values with different non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers and weighted with weights derived from a power spectral density (PSD) of the incident light for corresponding wavenumbers; and a processor configured to determine the profilometry measurements of the specimen as a maximum likelihood estimate of the specimen surface depth by back-projection of the measured intensities with the measurement model (emphasis added via bolded words).
Further regarding claim 1, Boppart et al. (US 2009/0185191 A1), hereinafter Boppart, teaches an OCT system for profilometry measurements of a specimen (Fig. 20(a), ¶ 0173 “A full-field OCT system, in accordance with embodiments of the present invention, is now described with reference to FIG. 20(a)”, see also ¶ 0113) which comprises an interferometer (¶ 0173 “system 1100, as shown, is based on a Michelson interferometer”) configured to split incident light into a reference beam and a test beam (Fig. 20(a) beam splitter 1114, light components 1115 and 1117), and to interfere the test beam reflected from the specimen with the reference beam reflected from a reference mirror to produce an interference pattern (Fig. 20(a) component 1115 reflects of reference mirror 1116, component 1117 is reflected off sample 8, both beams return to beam splitter 1114 which creates an interference pattern, see ¶ 0091). While Fig. 20(a) of Boppart does not teach a spectrometer, Fig. 23 teaches interfered light being directed into a spectrometer which analyzes the spectral components of the interference pattern (see ¶ 0214 and Fig. 23 spectrometer 602). While ¶ 0221 of Boppart teaches measuring non-uniform sampling of spectrum from the detector of the spectrometer, Boppart teaches the use of interpolation to account for and correct this non-uniformly sampled spectrum (see ¶ 0221, 0283-0284).
Since Boppart does not utilize the non-uniformly sampled spectrum in a measurement model, Boppart does not teach a measurement model with elements connecting different depth values with different non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers and weighted with weights derived from a power spectral density (PSD) of the incident light for corresponding wavenumbers. Furthermore, Boppart does not teach a processor configured to determine the profilometry measurements of the specimen as a maximum likelihood estimate of the specimen surface depth by back-projection of the measured intensities with the measurement model.
Wang et al. (US 2016/0213253 A1) teaches a method for the analysis of OCT images using a maximum likelihood estimation (see Wang et al. abstract and ¶ 0053), but does not teach a measurement model with elements connecting different depth values with different non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers and weighted with weights derived from a power spectral density (PSD) of the incident light for corresponding wavenumbers.
Suehira (US 2010/0027019 A1) teaches an OCT imaging method and apparatus comprising an interferometer, a spectrometer, computer-readable memory, and a processor (see Suehira Fig. 1, ¶ 0037-0040), but does not teach a measurement model with elements connecting different depth values with different non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers and weighted with weights derived from a power spectral density (PSD) of the incident light for corresponding wavenumbers.
Fercher (US 6,377,349 B1) teaches an OCT apparatus and method for surface profile measurement (see Fercher abstract) comprising an interferometer, spectrometer, memory, and processor (see Fercher Fig. 1, col. 3 line 43-col. 4 line 5). However, Fercher does not teach a measurement model with elements connecting different depth values with different non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers and weighted with weights derived from a power spectral density (PSD) of the incident light for corresponding wavenumbers.
Riza (US 7,978,346 B1) teaches a method and system for realizing 3D imaging using optical coherence tomography (see Riza abstract) which involves using iterative back-projection and maximum likelihood estimation algorithms (see Riza col. 10 lines 55-67), however, Riza does not teach a measurement model with elements connecting different depth values with different non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers and weighted with weights derived from a power spectral density (PSD) of the incident light for corresponding wavenumbers.
Izatt et al. (US 6,002,480 A) teaches an apparatus and method for depth resolved spectroscopic OCT (see Izatt et al. abstract) which involves obtaining an auto-power spectrum for an OCT system which involves using an auto-correction function having the power spectral density of a light source as a weight (see Izatt et al. col. 16 lines 41-57). However, Izatt et al. does not teach a measurement model with elements connecting different depth values with different non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers and weighted with weights derived from a power spectral density (PSD) of the incident light for corresponding wavenumbers.
Chen (WO 2014/193310 A1) teaches an optical imaging device and method using OCT (see Chen abstract and ¶ 0003-0006) comprising an interferometer and a spectrometer (see Chen Fig. 2A, the grating GR and image sensor are equivalent to a spectrometer). However, Chen does not teach a measurement model with elements connecting different depth values with different non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers and weighted with weights derived from a power spectral density (PSD) of the incident light for corresponding wavenumbers.
Huang et al. (“Maximum-likelihood estimation in Optical Coherence Tomography in the context of the tear film dynamics”) teaches the implementation of a maximum likelihood estimator to interpret OCT data based on mathematical models of Fourier-Domain OCT (see Huang et al. abstract) where the OCT system comprises an interferometer and a spectrometer (see Huang Fig. 1 and section 2. Mathematical Model of FD-OCT in the context of ML estimation). However, this model does not have elements connecting different depth values with different non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers and is not weighted with weights derived from a power spectral density (PSD) of the incident light for corresponding wavenumbers. Further, Huang et al. does not teach determine the profilometry measurements of the specimen as a maximum likelihood estimate of the specimen surface depth by back-projection of the measured intensities with the measurement model.
Kemp et al. (US 9,347,765 B2, made of record by applicant) teaches a method of processing OCT images, but does not teach, among other essential elements, a measurement model with elements connecting different depth values with different non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers and weighted with weights derived from a power spectral density (PSD) of the incident light for corresponding wavenumbers.
Further, the prior art references Suliali et al. (“Development of a low-cost 11 µm spectral domain coherence tomography surface profilometry prototype”, of record), Lawman et al. (“High precision dynamic multi-interface profilometry with optical coherence tomography”, of record), and Endo et al. (“Profilometry with line-field Fourier-domain interferometry”, provided by examiner) which are all cited by applicant in the IDS share similar teachings to the prior art references cited above by the examiner. However, these references similarly do not teach a computer-readable memory configured to store a measurement model with elements connecting different depth values with different non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers and weighted with weights derived from a power spectral density (PSD) of the incident light for corresponding wavenumbers; and a processor configured to determine the profilometry measurements of the specimen as a maximum likelihood estimate of the specimen surface depth by back-projection of the measured intensities with the measurement model (emphasis added via bolded words).
Therefore, for the reasons outlined above, claim 1 is allowed. Claims 2-14 depend on claim 1 and are therefore also allowed.
Regarding claim 15, the prior art of record, taken alone or in combination, neither anticipates nor renders obvious a method for profilometry measurements of a specimen in an optical coherence tomography (OCT) system, the method comprising: splitting by an interferometer, incident light into a reference beam and a test beam, and interfering the test beam reflected from the specimen with the reference beam reflected from a reference mirror to produce an interference pattern; analyzing by a spectrometer, spectral components of the interference pattern at non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers; wherein a computer-readable memory of the OCT system stores a measurement model with elements connecting different depth values with different non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers and weighted with weights derived from a power spectral density (PSD) of the incident light for corresponding wavenumbers; and determining the profilometry measurements of the specimen as a maximum likelihood estimate of the specimen surface depth by back-projection of the measured intensities with the measurement model (emphasis added via bolded words).
Claim 15 has similar subject matter to that of claim 1. Therefore, for the same reasons outlined above, claim 15 is allowed. Claims 16-19 depend on claim 15 and are therefore also allowed.
Regarding claim 20, the prior art of record, taken alone or in combination, neither anticipates nor renders obvious a non-transitory computer readable medium having stored thereon computer-executable instructions which when executed by a computer, causes the computer to perform a method for profilometry measurements of a specimen in an optical coherence tomography (OCT) system, the method comprising: splitting by an interferometer, incident light into a reference beam and a test beam, and interfering the test beam reflected from the specimen with the reference beam reflected from a reference mirror to produce an interference pattern; analyzing by a spectrometer, spectral components of the interference pattern at non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers; wherein a computer-readable memory of the OCT system stores a measurement model with elements connecting different depth values with different non-uniformly sampled wavenumbers and weighted with weights derived from a power spectral density (PSD) of the incident light for corresponding wavenumbers; and determining the profilometry measurements of the specimen as a maximum likelihood estimate of the specimen surface depth by back-projection of the measured intensities with the measurement model (emphasis added via bolded words).
Claim 20 has similar subject matter to that of claims 1 and 15. Therefore, for the same reasons outlined above, claim 20 is allowed.
