Claim Interpretation
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(f):
(f) Element in Claim for a Combination. – An element in a claim for a combination may be expressed as a means or step for performing a specified function without the recital of structure, material, or acts in support thereof, and such claim shall be construed to cover the corresponding structure, material, or acts described in the specification and equivalents thereof.
The following is a quotation of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph:
An element in a claim for a combination may be expressed as a means or step for performing a specified function without the recital of structure, material, or acts in support thereof, and such claim shall be construed to cover the corresponding structure, material, or acts described in the specification and equivalents thereof.
Use of the word “means” (or “step for”) in a claim with functional language creates a rebuttable presumption that the claim element is to be treated in accordance with 35 U.S.C. 112(f) (pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph).  The presumption that 35 U.S.C. 112(f) (pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph) is invoked is rebutted when the function is recited with sufficient structure, material, or acts within the claim itself to entirely perform the recited function.
Absence of the word “means” (or “step for”) in a claim creates a rebuttable presumption that the claim element is not to be treated in accordance with 35 U.S.C. 112(f) (pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph).  The presumption that 35 U.S.C. 112(f) (pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph) is not invoked is rebutted when the claim element recites function but fails to recite sufficiently definite structure, material or acts to perform that function.
Claim elements in this application that use the word “means” (or “step for”) are presumed to invoke 35 U.S.C. 112(f) except as otherwise indicated in an Office action.  Similarly, claim elements that do not use the word “means” (or “step for”) are presumed not to invoke 35 U.S.C. 112(f) except as otherwise indicated in an Office action.
No claim limitation has been interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph.

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1-15 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor, or for pre-AIA  the applicant regards as the invention.
Claim 1 recites “the pressure sensors conf for detecting pressure applied to a vertebra or soft tissue by one or more parts of a hand of a user wearing the glove” in lines 3-5, which is so grammatically awkward that its meaning is unclear.
Claim 1 recites “the pressure sensors” in line 3, but it is not clear if this recitation is connoting that there must be a plurality of sensors in the claimed invention since claim 1 only previously mentions “one or more pressure sensors” in line 3 and later mentions “the one or more pressure sensors” in line 7 and “the one or more sensors” in line 8.
Claims 2-15 are rejected by virtue of their dependence from claim 1.
Claim 2 recites “wherein the glove comprises multiple pressure sensors” in lines 1-2, but it is not clear if these sensors are the same as, related to, or different from the “one or more sensors” of claim 1, line 3 and/or “the pressure sensors” of claim 1, line 3.  If they are different or related, the relationship among these recitations should be made clear.  Also, claim 2 connotes that the pressure sensors are part of the glove while claim 1 connotes that the pressure sensors are distinct and separate elements from the glove.  This contradiction renders claim 2 indefinite.
Claims 3-7 are rejected by virtue of their dependence from claim 2.
Claim 3 recites “wherein the glove comprises multiple spaced apart pressure sensors” in line 1-2, but it is not clear if these sensors are the same as, related to, or different from the “one or more sensors” of claim 1, line 3; “the pressure sensors” of claim 1, line 3; and/or “multiple pressure sensors” of claim 2, lines 1-2.  If they are different or related, the relationship among these recitations should be made clear.  Also, claim 3 connotes that the pressure sensors are part of the glove while claim 1 connotes that the pressure sensors are distinct and separate elements from the glove.  This contradiction renders claim 3 indefinite.
Claims 4-7 are rejected by virtue of their dependence from claim 3.
Claim 8 recites “wherein the glove comprises an inner liner and exterior layer” in lines 1-2, but it is not clear if the inner liner and/or the exterior layer is supposed to be the same as, related to, or different from the “at least one layer of material” of claim 1, line 2.  If they are the same, consistent terminology should be used.  If they are different or related, the relationship among these recitations should be made clear.
Claims 9 and 15 are rejected by virtue of their dependence from claim 8.
Claim 14 recites “A glove system comprising a microprocessor electrically connect and receiving a signal input from the microcontroller of the glove according to claim 1” in lines 1-2, but it is not clear if the microcontroller and/or glove is part of the claimed invention since “receiving a signal input from the microcontroller of the glove according to claim 1” seems to connote an action rather than a structural limitation.  Also, the recitation “receiving a signal input from the microcontroller of the glove according to claim 1” is an action step. A single claim which claims both an apparatus and the method steps of using the apparatus is indefinite under 35 U.S.C. 112(b)  or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, second paragraph, because it creates confusion as to when direct infringement occurs. (MPEP 2173.05(p) citing In re Katz Interactive Call Processing Patent Litigation, 639 F.3d 1303, 97 USPQ2d 1737 (Fed. Cir. 2011)).

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 103(a) which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
(a) A patent may not be obtained though the invention is not identically disclosed or described as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the subject matter sought to be patented and the prior art are such that the subject matter as a whole would have been obvious at the time the invention was made to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which said subject matter pertains. Patentability shall not be negatived by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim 9 is rejected under pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 103(a) as being unpatentable over Wang in view of Montgomery.
Wang discloses the control panel 114 being outside of the glove.  Montgomery discloses that such components may be placed between layers of the glove (pages 4 and 7 of Montgomery).  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art at the time of invention to mount the control panel 114 of Wang between adjacent layers of Montgomery since (1) it can protect the control panel 114 and/or (2) it is a simple substitution of one known element for another to obtain predictable results.
With respect to claim 9, the combination teaches or suggests that the one or more pressure sensors and microcontroller secured between the exterior layer and inner liner of the glove (the above 103 analysis; paragraph 0137 of Wang; pages 4 and 7 of Montgomery).
