Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1 - 4 and 7 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Regarding claim 1, the language “provided to” in line 9 implies a method step; however, it appears the Applicant intends to claim the elliptical ring member and its corresponding structure. Examiner suggests updating the language to either “including” as previously stated in line 5 or “attached” as stated in specification paragraph 0048.
In line 11, the language “provided to” should be updated for the same reasoning above. Examiner suggests replacing “provided to” with either “including” or “attached” as stated in specification paragraph 0003.
In line 15, the language “provided to” should be updated for the same reasoning above. Examiner suggests “occupying” as used in claim 2, line 6 or “includes” in claim 1, line 17.
Claims 2-4 are also rejected as being dependent on claim 1.
Regarding claim 2, the language “provided to” in line 4 should be updated for the same reasoning above. Examiner suggests “occupying” as used in claim 2, line 7 or “includes” in claim 1, line 17.
Regarding claim 7, the language “provided to” in lines 9 and 11 should be updated for the same reasoning above. Examiner suggests updating the language to either “including” or “attached”.
In line 15, the language “provided to” should be updated for the same reasoning above. Examiner suggests “occupying” or “includes”.
Allowable Subject Matter
The following is a statement of reasons for the indication of allowable subject matter:
Regarding claims 1 and 7, the closest prior art of Yokoyama (JP 2013034791 - cited by Applicant) discloses a sphygmomanometer cuff comprised of an inner and outer cloth with an expansion bag, a ring-shaped member, a retaining tube and fasteners. The prior art of record does not disclose an end opening nor a retaining member with the specific structure of a flexible projection part with flat, plate-like support parts that is welded to the back surface of the outer fabric in the end opening.
Additionally, Vivenzio (US 8652057) discloses a blood pressure cuff with an opening of the first sheet. However, the socket inserted into the opening is not similar in structure to the claimed retaining member. Therefore, the prior art of record does not read on the claims nor does it provide sufficient obviousness rationale to modify the teachings together to read on the claims.
Claims 1 - 4 and 7 would be allowable if rewritten or amended to overcome the rejection(s) under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), 2nd paragraph, set forth in this Office action.
