Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claim(s) 20, 26, and 31 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor, or for pre-AIA  the applicant regards as the invention.
Claim 20 recites the limitation “the property of the tubing system” in Ln. 6.  There is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim.  For the purposes of limitation be interpreted as reading “the manufacturing detail of the tubing system”.
Claim 26 recites the limitation “enhancing or suggesting an enhancement … of an operation of a pump of the CO2 sensing system upon intubation and until a first CO2 signal is obtained by the CO2 sensor, thereby reducing a time required for verification of correct intubation” which deems the claim indefinite. The claim recites an intended functionality, i.e. enhancing or suggesting an enhancement, without claiming any particular operation which would constitute an enhancement. The claim is thus overly broad in design. For example, how is operation of the pump to be altered to provide the intended enhancement?
Claim 31 recites the limitation “the pump” in Ln. 3.  There is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim.  For the purposes of examination the claim will instead be read as dependent on claim 26, which provides the proper antecedent basis.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Claim(s) 18-20, 25-29, and 31 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because the claimed invention is directed to an abstract idea of a mental process without significantly more. The claim(s) recite(s) 1) receiving a carbon dioxide (CO2) concentration in exhaled breath of a subject; 2) determining one or more breath related parameters based on the CO2 concentration; 3) receiving an indication of a type of a tubing system connected; and 4) changing or suggesting a change of an operation mode of the CO2 sensing system based on the indication of the type of the tubing system. This judicial exception is not integrated into a practical application because the only positively recited element of the claim is one or more processors. The courts have held that the mere performing of a mental process with a computer, e.g. the claimed processor(s), fails to extend beyond the judicial exception (see MPEP 2106.04(a)(2)(III). In claim 18 the receiving of sensor data is a mental process, the determining of a parameter from the received data is a mental process, the receiving of type information is a mental process, and the decision to change or suggest a change is a mental process. The CO2 sensor and the CO2 sensing system are merely generic elements in the claim which are conventional in a capnography system and are only recited in regard to the pre-solution activity of data gathering. The tubing system is only incidentally recited in the claim are performs no meaningful function as claimed. The claim(s) does/do not include additional elements that are sufficient to amount to significantly more than the judicial exception because the steps of the claim only pertain to the processing of data and determining of a decision based upon the data. At a minimum the claim only requires suggestion of a change, which fails to meaningfully integrate the judicial exception of the mental process into a practical application.
Each of claims 19 and 20 further only recite the receiving of additional data and at a minimum a further suggestion based upon that additional data. The claims thus fail to extend beyond the judicial exception.
Claim 25 merely requires the communication of a suggestion, which amounts to insignificant post-solution activity. The claim thus fails to extend beyond the judicial exception.
Each of claims 26-29 further recite, at a minimum, a suggestion based upon a particular type of tubing system recited by each respective claim. Again, the mere recitation of a suggestion fails to extend beyond a mental process. It is noted that while a particular type of tubing system is recited by each respective claim that no active use of the tubing system is recited by the claims. The claims thus fail to extend beyond the judicial exception.
Claim 31 at a minimum only requires a suggestion to be made, which fails to extend beyond a mental process. The claim thus fails to extend beyond the judicial exception.
It is noted that claim 30 is not rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because it is found to add significantly more than the judicial exception of a mental process in its active generating of a signal which terminates the usability of the tubing system, in response to determining, based on the usage data, that the tubing system has run obsolete.

Claim(s) 18, 26, and 31 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by Weiss et al. (U.S. Pub. 2016/0073929).
Regarding claim 18, Weiss discloses a method of operating a capnography system (Figs. 1-2; ¶¶0020-0024), comprising: receiving, at one or more processors (Fig. 2 #24; ¶0025; see also ¶0029 – required for sending/receiving of data by gas sampling device 14), a carbon dioxide (CO2) concentration in exhaled breath of a subject (¶0021 – by capnograph) from a CO2 sensor (¶0021 – capnograph measuring EtCO2) of a CO2 sensing system (Fig. 2 #14; ¶0021); determining, by the one or more processors, one or more breath related parameters based on the CO2 concentration (¶0021 – no specific parameter is required by the claim); receiving, at the one or more processors, an indication of a type of a tubing system connected to the CO2 sensing system (¶¶0029-0031 – determined through identification); and changing or suggesting a change, by the one or more processors, of an operation mode of the CO2 sensing system based on the indication of the type of the tubing system (¶¶0029, 0036 – operation begun if appropriate connector).
Regarding claim 26, Weiss discloses the tubing system comprises an intubation tube (¶0016 – endotracheal tube), and wherein changing the operation mode of the CO2 sensing system comprises enhancing or suggesting an enhancement, by the one or more processors, of an operation of a pump of the CO2 sensing system upon intubation and until a first CO2 signal is obtained by the CO2 sensor, thereby reducing a time required for verification of correct intubation (¶¶0029, 0036 – operation begun if appropriate connector). The claim is written in such a broad manner than merely operating the CO2 sensing system upon intubation may be read as satisfying the claimed operation of enhancing operation to reduce a time required for verification of correct intubation because it would be expected to take longer to verify correct intubation without use of the CO2 sensing system than with use of the CO2 sensing system.
Regarding claim 31, Weiss discloses changing or suggesting the change of the operation mode of the CO2 sensing system comprises changing or suggesting the change in an operation of the pump based on the indication of the type of the tubing system (¶¶0029, 0036 – operation begun if appropriate connector). Triggering of operation may be read as a change in an operation of the pump.
