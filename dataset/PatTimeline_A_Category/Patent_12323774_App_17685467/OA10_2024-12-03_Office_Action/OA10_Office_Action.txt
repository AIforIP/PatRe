Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 13, 18, 19, 28, and 33-35 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Claims 13, 18, 19, 28, 33, and 34 contain the trademark/trade name XboxTM.  Where a trademark or trade name is used in a claim as a limitation to identify or describe a particular material or product, the claim does not comply with the requirements of 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph.  See Ex parte Simpson, 218 USPQ 1020 (Bd. App. 1982).  The claim scope is uncertain since the trademark or trade name cannot be used properly to identify any particular material or product.  A trademark or trade name is used to identify a source of goods, and not the goods themselves.  Thus, a trademark or trade name does not identify or describe the goods associated with the trademark or trade name.  In the present case, the trademark/trade name is used to identify/describe a particular game console and, accordingly, the identification/description is indefinite.
Remaining claims 35 depends from claim 34 rejected herein above and are additionally rejected based on the incorporation of issues noted herein above with respect to 34.

Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed October 3rd, 2024 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
In the above dated response, the applicant presents that the use of the trademark “XboxTM” wherein the same is utilized to identify a source of goods and not the goods themselves as described by MPEP 2173.05(u) alternatively described as an adjective modifying the generic term “game console” should be found acceptable under 35 USC §112.  The applicant additionally cites the district court’s decision in PolyVision Corp. v. Smart Technol. Inc. (501 F. Supp.2d 1042 (W.D. Mich. 2007) in support of their position.
MPEP 2173.05(u) notes that the presence of a trademark or trade name in a claim is not, per se, improper under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, second paragraph and the use of a trademark/tradename references a source of goods, and is not the name of the goods themselves, it does not explicitly describe what use in a claim is proper.
In PolyVision Corp. v. Smart Technol. Inc. (501 F. Supp.2d 1042 (W.D. Mich. 2007) the court found that the use of the trade name of WindowsTM, as an adjective to specify “operating system environment” as insufficient to define “any material difference” from any other families of operating system.  While the court ultimately found the use as acceptably definite, it did so by interpreting the trademark/tradename reference in view of the specific product “Microsoft Windows Version 3.0” as set forth in the specification.
The court’s conclusion that the use of tradename is definite based the assertion that the same references a specific product both contradicts the guidance of MPEP 2173.05(u) noting that the use of a trademark/tradename references a source of goods, and is not the name of the goods themselves and is dismissive that the interpretation of the same as an adjective to modify the generic term results in a material difference between the generic reference (i.e. game system) and any other generic reference.  Further complicating the issue, while it is noted that the specification while referencing “Microsoft XboxTM”and “XboxTM”” these refences fairly describe both the original game console product as well as the larger brand trademark/tradename of products.  Absent a clear delineation between the product and source of goods in the applicant’s specification it would not be possible to interpret the claimed refence as a specific product even if the court’s standard were to be utilized.
Accordingly, after further consideration and based on the reasoning presented herein above, the rejection of claims is respectfully maintained.
