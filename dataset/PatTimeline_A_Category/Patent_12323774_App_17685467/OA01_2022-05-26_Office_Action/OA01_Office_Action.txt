Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 103(a) which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
(a) A patent may not be obtained though the invention is not identically disclosed or described as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the subject matter sought to be patented and the prior art are such that the subject matter as a whole would have been obvious at the time the invention was made to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which said subject matter pertains. Patentability shall not be negatived by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claims 8-21, and 23-36 are rejected under pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 103(a) as being unpatentable over REISS et al (US 20110130203) in view Sampat (US  2010050383).
Claim 8: The combination of REISS & Sampat teaches a headset, the headset comprising:
a pair of speakers configured to output sound according to one or more of a game signal, a chat signal, a microphone signal and an external audio signal (REISS Abstract; Figure 1 & Sampat Abstract; Figure 2A);
a microphone configured to generate the microphone signal according to a voice of a user (REISS Abstract; Paragraphs [0038], [0044], [0058]);
an RF circuit (REISS Abstract; Paragraphs [0038], [0094] & Sampat Figure 2, Paragraphs [0005], [0022], [0025], [0038]); and
a signal processor configured to selectively manage the communication of one or more of the game signal, the chat signal, the microphone signal and the external audio signal (REISS Abstract; Paragraphs [0009], [0012], [0054] & Sampat Figure 2, Paragraphs [0022], [0025], [0031], [0076]).
The prior art of REISS teaches the headset including the microphone, RF circuit and signal processor as cited herein above.  While the prior art of REISS is arguably silent regarding the headset including a pair of speakers, Sampat teaches this was a known feature in an analogous headset (Sampat Abstract; Figure 2A).  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art at the time of invention to have utilized a headset with a pair of speakers as taught by Sampat in the invention of REISS in order to provide the predictable and expected result of enabling the presentation of stereo audio to user as taught by Sampat (Sampat Paragraphs [0023], [0041]).

Claim 22: The combination of REISS; Sampat & Wingate teaches the headset of claim 8, wherein the headset comprises a switch configured to select the external audio signal (REISS Paragraph [0079]; Figure 3A & Wingate Figure 2; Element 204).
The combination of REISS; & Sampat teaches the invention including various user inputs/switches for selecting the audio signal (REISS Paragraph [0079]; Figure 3A).  While the combination of REISS; & Sampat is silent regarding the placement of a switch in/on the headset, in an analogous invention Wingate teaches that this feature was known at the time of invention (Wingate Figure 2; Element 204).  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art at the time of invention to have utilized the switch arrangement of Wingate on the headset of REISS & Sampat in order to provide the predictable and expected result of placing an additional control switch at a position readily locatable by a player/user wearing the headset.
Claim 37: The combination of REISS; Sampat & Wingate teaches the method of claim 23, wherein the method comprises selecting the external audio signal via a switch of the headset (REISS Paragraph [0079]; Figure 3A & Wingate Figure 2; Element 204).
