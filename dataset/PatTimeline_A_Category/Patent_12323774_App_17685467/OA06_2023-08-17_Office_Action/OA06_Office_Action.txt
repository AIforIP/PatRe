Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 103(a) which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
(a) A patent may not be obtained though the invention is not identically disclosed or described as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the subject matter sought to be patented and the prior art are such that the subject matter as a whole would have been obvious at the time the invention was made to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which said subject matter pertains. Patentability shall not be negatived by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claims 8-15, 17-30, and 32-37 are rejected under pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 103(a) as being unpatentable over Alden (US 2004/0198436) in view of McLoone et al (US  8,064,972) in further view of Applicant Admitted Prior Art.

Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed July 17th, 2023 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
In the above dated response, the applicant presents that the prior presented rejection under the combination of Alden, McLoone, & Applicant Admitted Prior Art does not identify a signal processor configured to selectively manage (or selectively managing via signal processor) the combination of a game signal, chat signal, the microphone signal, and an external audio signal as recited in claims 8 and 23 (page 6 and 7).
Responsive to the preceding, the prior art of Alden teaches the use of a signal processor (Mixer 66) configured to selectively manage  the combination including a microphone signal (Microphone 29) and an external audio signal (Audio device 16).  While Alden does not explicitly teach the use of gaming environments and voice chat as additional audio sources these features are taught in the analogous invention of McLoone(-game chat- McLoone Col 1:31-51) and Applicant admitted prior art (-game audio/sounds, the player microphone, as well as game chat- Applicant’s specification Pages 1-2).  The rejection presents that it would have been a matter of obviousness under section 103 to combination the known management of different audio signals with the plurality different audio signals recited by the claimed invention to provide a signal processor configured to selectively manage (or selectively managing via signal processor) the combination of a game signal, chat signal, the microphone signal, and an external audio signal as recited in claims 8 and 23.
The applicant’s arguments, as best understood, propose that the signal processor/mixer is not disclosed in a singular reference with the four types of audio signals managed by the same and as such is not suitable to manage the four different types of audio signals.  In response to applicant's arguments against the references individually, one cannot show non-obviousness by attacking references individually where the rejections are based on combinations of references.  See In re Keller, 642 F.2d 413, 208 USPQ 871 (CCPA 1981); In re Merck & Co., 800 F.2d 1091, 231 USPQ 375 (Fed. Cir. 1986).
Continuing on page 7 of the applicant’s remarks, the applicant proposes that Xbox game sound signal would have been separately adjustable via a TV/monitor volume control and therefore as such the applicant admitted prior art would not make the Xbox control of the game sound and additional audio signal implicit to the described arrangement but was instead a new feature introduced in only recently.
Regarding the preceding the, the applicant proposed limitations of Xbox functionality are not at this point mere allegation not supported by evidence of record.  These statements further do not appear to concern the applicant admitted art statement concerning, “On an Xbox game console, the online chat signal is fed to the headset via a cable between the Xbox controller and a so-called communicator headset that is simply a microphone connected to a speaker cup that sits over one ear. Some stereo gaming headsets provide a method of connecting this cable to the headset such that the sound from the online chat is combined with the game sound, allowing the player to hear both at the same time.” (Applicant’s specification page 2), that does not reference the use of the tv/monitor in the audio presentation.
Not withstanding the preceding however, it is additionally unclear how such would impact the combination of references as provided wherein the signal processor element has already additionally been taught by the mixer element of Alden.
Insomuch as the applicant’s argument may be understood as directed at the proposal that the  claimed “signal processor” would be limited to a particular hardware architecture, it is respectfully noted that the applicant’s specification does not present support such an interpretation.
In view of the preceding the rejection of claims is respectfully maintained as presented and clarified herein above.
