Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
In claims 1 and 19-20, applicant amended “… computing a time series of concatenated CI (TSCCI) based on a concatenation of the TSCI and the time series of DCI (TSDCI), each concatenated CI (CCI) being computed by concatenating a respective CI and a respective DCI corresponding to the CI…”  Based on disclosure of paragraphs 00198 and 00255 of filed specification, wording of said amendment appears to be redundant, which is indefinite.  Also, said citation of filed specification supports a concatenation of a basic feature of a respective CI and another basic feature of a corresponding denoised CI, wherein basic feature can be any related feature.  For examination, the broadest reasonable interpretation is taken based on filed specification.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim(s) 1-20 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Liu et al. (“Tracking Vital Signs During Sleep Leveraging Off-the-shelf WiFi”) in view of Xu et al. (“Attention-based Walking Gait and Direction Recognition in Wi-Fi Networks”) and Suyambukesan et al. (US2006/0077306).
To claim 1, Liu teach a system for wireless vital sign monitoring, comprising:
a transmitter configured to transmit a wireless signal through a wireless channel of a venue (Figs. 2, 10);
a receiver configured to receive the wireless signal through the wireless channel (Figs. 2, 10), wherein the received wireless signal differs from the transmitted wireless signal due to the wireless channel that is impacted by a periodic motion of a vital sign of an object in the venue (pages 270-273, sections 4-6); and
a processor (Fig. 2) configured for:
obtaining a time series of channel information (CI) of the wireless channel based on the received wireless signal, computing a two dimensional (2D) decomposition of the time series of CI (TSCI), enhancing the 2D decomposition, and monitoring the periodic motion of the vital sign based on the enhanced 2D decomposition (pages 269-272, sections 3.3-5, Figs. 1, 3, 5-8).
But, Liu do not expressly disclose computing a time series of concatenated CI (TSCCI) based on a concatenation of the TSCI and the time series of DCI (TSDCI), each concatenated CI (CCI) being computed by concatenating a respective CI and a respective DCI corresponding to the CI, computing a one dimensional (1D) characteristic function of CCI of the TSCCI in a sliding time window, computing a two dimensional (2D) decomposition of the TSCCI based on the 1D characteristic function in the sliding time window, enhancing the 2D decomposition based on a non-separable vertical highpass horizontal lowpass filtering.
Xu teach a system for human gait recognition in WiFi networks, comprising modules for: obtaining a time series of channel information (CI) of the wireless channel based on the received wireless signal (page 5, IV-B-1, removing paths with long delays from an obtained time series of CSI measurements, and outputting a time series of concatenated CSI), computing a time series of denoised CI (DCI) by denoising the time series of CI (TSCI) (pages 5-6, IV-B-2, denoising the time series of concatenated CSI), computing a time series of concatenated CI (TSCCI) based on a concatenation of the TSCI and the time series of DCI (TSDCI) (pages 5-6, CSI denoising), each concatenated CI (CCI) being computed by concatenating a respective CI and a respective DCI corresponding to the CI (pages 5-6, IV-B, refined time series of CSI is based on denoised concatenated CSI), computing a one dimensional (1D) characteristic function of CCI of the TSCCI in a sliding time window, computing a two dimensional (2D) decomposition of the TSCCI based on the 1D characteristic function in the sliding time window (pages 6-8, IV-C; page 9, V-B; walking profile splicing with sliding window segmentation), which would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to incorporate into the system of Liu, in order to further process collected signal characteristics by design preference.
Suyambukesan teach a system for frequency content motion detection by decomposing a raw field data into frequency data, which comprising an operation of concurrently applying a vertical low pass filter and a horizontal high pass filter for enhancing the appearance of motion (Fig. 4, paragraph 0028).
Therefore, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to incorporate teaching of Suyambukesan into the system of Liu and Xu, in order to further 2D decomposition with enhancement.
