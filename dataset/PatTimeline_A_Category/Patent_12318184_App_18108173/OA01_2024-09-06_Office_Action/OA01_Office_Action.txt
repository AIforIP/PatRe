Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Claims 1-7, 10, 12, & 14 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because the claimed invention is directed to an abstract idea without significantly more. Claim 1 is directed to a process and Claim 14 is directed to an apparatus, both of which fall into statutory categories; however, the claims recite the following abstract ideas: “determining, based on the first image data, one or more parameters of a second scanning process to be performed on the patient.”
With regards to Claims 1, the cited limitations, under their broadest reasonable interpretation, cover performance in the mind. Nothing recited precludes the cited steps from being practically performed in the mind, or with the assistance of basic physical aids, see MPEP § 2106.04(a)(2)(III)( B). In particular, the act of “determining, based on the first image data, one or more parameters of a second scanning process to be performed on the patient” could be performed manually by selecting any imaging parameter based on visually inspecting an image and making a professional judgement based on practitioner expertise which is routinely performed a clinical setting, e.g. selecting a region of interest, selecting a pulse sequence, or selecting pulse sequence parameters based on a desired diagnosis
These judicial exceptions are not integrated into a practical application as established in MPEP § 2106.04(d). While the cited functions are implied to be associated with a computer processor, it should be appreciated that generically recited computer elements do not add a meaningful limitation to the abstract idea because they merely generically link the use of the judicial exception to a particular technological environment and not more. More specifically, the abstract idea is not specific to magnetic resonance imaging and, thus, does not link the corresponding process steps to a particular technological environment. Furthermore, neither the instant specification nor the claim provides an ordered combination of elements to improve the functioning of a computer or improves any other technology in the field because they amount using computers merely as a tool (see MPEP § 2106.05(a)(I)).
Furthermore, the claims do not include additional elements that are sufficient to amount to significantly more than the abstract ideas recited as laid out in MPEP § 2106.05. In particular, the acts of “scanning, in a first scanning process, the patient to obtain first image data indicative of at least the organ structure of the patient” and “scanning the patient in the second scanning process to obtain second image data indicative of at least the organ structure of the patient” amount to insignificant extra-solution data gathering (see MPEP § 2106.05(g)). The act of “wherein the first scanning process comprises a first quality of imaging scan, the second scanning process comprises a second quality of imaging scan, and the first quality of imaging scan is higher than the second quality of imaging scan” merely specifies the nature of the data which is exploited when executing the abstract ideas (see MPEP § 2106.05(g)(3)).
It should be appreciated that the same logic pattern and rationale are applied to Claim 14 as applied to Claim 1.
In consideration of each of the relevant factors and the claim elements both individually and in combination, claims 1-10, 12, & 14-15 are directed to abstract ideas without sufficient integration into a practical application and without significantly more. The dependent claims do not sufficiently link the subject matter to a practical application or recite element(s) which constitute significantly more than the abstract ideas identified. More specifically, the depending claims are directed toward additional limitations which either:
encompass abstract ideas consistent with those identified above, for example:
With regards to Claim 3, “determining, based on a property of the second magnetic resonance imaging scanner, one or more parameters of the second scanning process” amounts to performance of the mind because a clinician routinely sets an imaging region based on the dimensions of the scanner and/or gradient/RF coil dimensions;
With regards to Claim 5, “determining a region of interest in the first image data; and determining, based on the determined region of interest, the one or more parameters of the second scanning process” amounts to performance of the mind because a clinician routinely sets corresponding regions of interest between corresponding imaging sessions;
With regards to Claim 6, “wherein determining the region of interest comprises performing a segmentation process on the first image data” amounts to performance of the mind because a clinician routinely segments data when manually selecting a region of interest and, further, a clinician can manually segment organs or anatomies of interest using a computer a tool which routinely performed in a clinical setting;
With regards to Claim 7, “wherein determining the region of interest comprises identifying at least one of a landmark in the first image data and a sub-compartment in the first image data” amounts to performance of the mind because a clinician can manually select landmarks using a computer a tool which routinely performed in a clinical setting; and
With regards to Claim 10, “comparing the first image data to the localizer data to determine a consistency metric between the first image data and the localizer data” amounts to performance of the mind because a clinician may visually inspect images and determine a difference between the two based on professional judgement which is routinely performed a clinical setting.
fail to add significantly more than the abstract idea, for example:
With regards to Claim 2, “wherein the first scanning process is performed using a first magnetic resonance imaging scanner, and a second magnetic resonance imaging process is performed using a second magnetic resonance imaging scanner different to the first magnetic resonance imaging scanner” merely specifies the nature of the data which is exploited when executing the abstract ideas (see MPEP § 2106.05(g)(3));
With regards to Claim 4, “wherein the first quality of imaging scan comprises any one or more of a greater spatial resolution than the second quality of imaging scan, a greater signal-to-noise ratio than the second quality of imaging scan, a greater imaging volume than the second quality of imaging scan, a lower slice thickness to be resolved than the second quality of imaging scan, and high-sample recording of nuclear magnetic resonances compared to the second quality of imaging scan” merely specifies the nature of the data which is exploited when executing the abstract ideas (see MPEP § 2106.05(g)(3));
With regards to Claim 12, “reconstructing the second image data based on any one or more of the first image data and a dataset comprising data relating to previously scanned patients” invokes the use of computers or other machinery merely as a tool to perform an existing process (see MPEP § 2106.05(f)(2)).
It follows that the cited claims are directed to abstract ideas without sufficient integration into a practical application and without significantly more, and thus fails to meet the requirements of 35 U.S.C. 101.

A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 11 & 15 are objected to as being dependent upon a rejected base claim, but would be allowable if rewritten in independent form including all of the limitations of the base claim and any intervening claims.
The following is a statement of reasons for the indication of allowable subject matter:
With regards to Claim 1110, the cited prior art of Mazurkewitz and Leng fail to teach of repeating a first scanning process based on a consistency metric of a localizer scan and a first scanning process {e.g. baseline scan}. Chang et al. (US PGPUB 20190369181; hereinafter Chang) teaches of repeating a localizer and calibration scan when the ROI does not coincide with the coil sensitivity map (see Chang FIG. 3), but does not teach of repeating a high-quality baseline or first scanning process. Chang only distinguishes between the localizer scan and calibration scan in the calibration scan is a full mode scan {i.e. all of the coils}.
With regards to Claim 1514, while the cited prior art of Mazurkewitz and Leng teaches of baseline scan geometry and survey scan geometry, they do not teach of the updated scan geometry being based on the baseline scan geometry, it is only based on the mapping of the internal structure 126 in the baseline medical image 152 to the three-dimensional survey image 160.
Chang is also silent to said limitations.
