Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
In the event the determination of the status of the application as subject to AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 (or as subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103) is incorrect, any correction of the statutory basis (i.e., changing from AIA  to pre-AIA ) for the rejection will not be considered a new ground of rejection if the prior art relied upon, and the rationale supporting the rejection, would be the same under either status.
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(e) the invention was described in (1) an application for patent, published under section 122(b), by another filed in the United States before the invention by the applicant for patent or (2) a patent granted on an application for patent by another filed in the United States before the invention by the applicant for patent, except that an international application filed under the treaty defined in section 351(a) shall have the effects for purposes of this subsection of an application filed in the United States only if the international application designated the United States and was published under Article 21(2) of such treaty in the English language.
Claim(s) 1-8 is/are rejected under pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102(e) as being anticipated by US 7,690,378 B1 to Turcott.
As to claim 1, Turcott discloses an air delivery system for clinical management and/or prediction of a patient's respiratory exacerbations, comprising:
a controllable flow generator operable to generate a supply of pressurized breathable gas to be provided to the patient for treatment (see col 1, ln 37-47, col 2, ln 34-38, and col 15, ln 35-46 – CPAP is understood as being a common treatment, which is expressly taught as being evaluated for its effectiveness);
a pulse oximeter configured to generate a signal indicative of the patient's relative breathing effort, breathing frequency, and/or breathing phase (see col 11, ln 35-67); and
a controller configured to determine the patient's relative breathing effort, breathing frequency, and/or breathing phase based on the signal generated by the pulse oximeter (see col 11, ln 35-67).
As to claim 2, Turcott further discloses wherein the patient's breathing phase includes inspiratory and/or expiratory timing (see Fig 11C).
As to claim 3, Turcott further discloses wherein the controller is configured to derive a representation of the patient's respiratory efficiency from inherent arterial oxygenation information provided by the pulse oximeter and the patient's relative breathing effort (see Fig 10, element 1004 and col 14).
As to claim 4, Turcott further discloses wherein the controller is further configured to discriminate between a central apnea and a mixed apnea (see col 4, ln 17-19 – Examiner notes that mixed apneas have at least some obstructive sleep apnea characteristics, allowing a device that distinguishes between CSA and OSA to also be able to distinguish between CSA and mixed apnea; see also col 16, ln 20-60).
As to claim 5, Turcott further discloses wherein the controller is configured to discriminate between a central apnea and a mixed apnea based on whether breathing effort precedes oxygen desaturation (col 16, ln 20-60).
As to claim 6, Turcott further discloses wherein the controller is configured to discriminate between a central apnea and a mixed apnea based on a pattern recognition and/or classifier techniques (see col 16, ln 20-60).
As to claim 7, Turcott further discloses wherein the pulse oximeter is a finger pulse oximeter (col 6, ln 28-36).
As to claim 8, Turcott further discloses wherein the pulse oximeter is configured to be located at the patient's ear (col 6, ln 28-36).
