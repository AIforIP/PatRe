Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
Claims 17 and 31 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Regarding claim 17, the limitations “the application readily processes” and “the application does not readily process” render the claim indefinite, as it is unclear if the application can or cannot process such data or if the application can still process the data, but it is either expecting the data or not. For examination purposes, the limitation “the application readily processes” shall be interpreted as the application is expecting (the data), and the limitation “the application does not readily process” shall be interpreted as the application is not expecting (the data).
Regarding claim 31, the limitations “the application readily processes” and “the application does not readily process” render the claim indefinite, as it is unclear if the application can or cannot process such data or if the application can still process the data, but it is either expecting the data or not. For examination purposes, the limitation “the application readily processes” shall be interpreted as the application is expecting (the data), and the limitation “the application does not readily process” shall be interpreted as the application is not expecting (the data).

Response to Arguments
Applicant’s arguments with respect to claim 16, 27 and 35 have been considered but are unpersuasive.
Applicant has argued that Griesdorf does not disclose or suggest a second apparatus comprising an application that processes pseudo sensor data. Specifically Applicant has argued that the wireless communication device 102C of Griesdorf only processes wireless signals and detects motion in a spaced accessed by the wireless signals. Further, Applicant has argued that 1) detecting motion is not an application and 2) the wireless communication device does not process pseudo sensor data.
Examiner disagrees with these arguments. First, one of ordinary skill in the art would understand that under BRI, an application is simply a software or program run on a processor to perform a specific task. As such, the wireless communication device 102C processing signals would read upon an example of an application. Second, Examiner has not used Griesdorf to teach the processing of the pseudo sensor data, and has instead used Gillian to teach such limitations. In response to applicant's arguments against the references individually, one cannot show nonobviousness by attacking references individually where the rejections are based on combinations of references.  See In re Keller, 642 F.2d 413, 208 USPQ 871 (CCPA 1981); In re Merck & Co., 800 F.2d 1091, 231 USPQ 375 (Fed. Cir. 1986). As a result, independent claims 16, 27, and 35 remain rejected.
Applicant’s arguments with respect to claim 34 have been considered but are moot because the new ground of rejection does not rely on any reference applied in the prior rejection of record for any teaching or matter specifically challenged in the argument. Gillian has not been used to teach the limitation, instead Gillian, Griesdorf, and Yuen has been used to teach such limitations. As a result, claim 34 remains rejected.
Applicant’s remaining arguments (3 and 4) have been considered but are unpersuasive. In response to applicant's arguments against the references individually, one cannot show nonobviousness by attacking references individually where the rejections are based on combinations of references.  See In re Keller, 642 F.2d 413, 208 USPQ 871 (CCPA 1981); In re Merck & Co., 800 F.2d 1091, 231 USPQ 375 (Fed. Cir. 1986). As a result, independent claims 16, 27, and 35 remain rejected.
The remaining dependent claims are rejected for substantially the same reasons as above.
