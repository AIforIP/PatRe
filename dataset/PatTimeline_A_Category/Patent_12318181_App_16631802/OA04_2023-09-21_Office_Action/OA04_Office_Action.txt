(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 31-33 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.

Claims that are not discussed above but are cited to be rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) are also rejected because they inherit the indefiniteness of the claims they respectively depend upon.

Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed 06/22/2023 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
Applicant has argued against the rejections for claims 16, 27, and 35, stating that Gillian does not suggest “wherein the pseudo sensor data is compatible with an application programming interface of the application, and the radio frequency based data is not compatible with the application programming interface of the application.” and cites Gillian [0063], stating that the reformatted radar data may be compatible, and does not describe whether the modified data is compatible with the application or not; and [0075], stating that Gillian actually describes opposite of what is claimed by applicant and that the sensor fusion engine provides or exposes the radar data to sensor based applications 108.
Examiner disagrees with these statements. As stated in [0096] and [0093], the sensor based application is expecting data that is a radar feature, not raw radar data. This means that even if it received the raw radar data, which it may be capable of doing, it would not function as intended. Examiner’s interpretation of incompatible has been based on Applicant’s specification, which states that the “RF based data may not be readily processed by health and fitness tracker applications having application programming interfaces”. This interpretation means that the applications may be able to receive the raw radar data, but would have a more difficult time processing such data as opposed to the intended types of data (such as pseudo sensor data). In addition, the definition of compatible is to exist or occur together without conflict (see Oxford’s definition of compatible). As a result, not compatible would mean that the two things would not be able to exist or occur together without conflict, not necessarily that they cannot be used together at all. As such, one of ordinary skill in the art would understand that while the sensor application of Gillian may be able to receive the raw RF data, there would be conflict as it is not the intended type of data to be used and is not readily processed by the sensor application. Further, what is described in [0075] is a more general application of the device, but in the cited [0096], the sensor based application is expecting the augmented radar feature data. For these reasons above, claims 16, 27, and 35 remain rejected.
The remaining dependent claims remain rejected for substantially the same reasons as above.
