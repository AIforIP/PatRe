Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
Claims 17, 25 and 26 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(a) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), first paragraph, as failing to comply with the written description requirement. The claim(s) contains subject matter which was not described in the specification in such a way as to reasonably convey to one skilled in the relevant art that the inventor or a joint inventor, or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the inventor(s), at the time the application was filed, had possession of the claimed invention.
Regarding claim 17, nowhere in the specification does it detail where the pseudo sensor data is compatible with an application programming interface of the application, and the radio frequency based data is not compatible with the application programming interface of the application. [0024] and [0025] do detail how the RF based data may not be readily processed by the applications, and how the pseudo sensor data can be readily processed by the application, but nowhere does the specification detail the data as being either compatible or incompatible.
Claims that are not discussed above but are cited to be rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(a) are also rejected because they inherit the written description requirement of the claims they respectively depend upon.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 22 and 34 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Regarding claim 22, it is unclear from the language how the applicant is listing the performing limitation. For example, it is unclear if the claim limitations intention is to have the two limitations for performing, (“perform at least one of: docking ,using the first apparatus, the second apparatus, or charging ,using the first apparatus, the second apparatus,”) and then the third limitation “wherein the first apparatus comprising the radio frequency based tracker provides the pseudo sensor data wherein the first apparatus comprising the radio frequency based tracker provides the pseudo sensor data during the time interval when the first apparatus is docking the second apparatus or when the first apparatus is charging the second apparatus”, or if the entire limitation only comprises the two options for performing, such as: perform at least one of: docking the second apparatus using the first apparatus (first option), or charging, using the first apparatus, the second apparatus wherein the first apparatus comprising the radio frequency based tracker provides the pseudo sensor data during the time interval when the first apparatus is docking the second apparatus or when the first apparatus is charging the second apparatus (second option).
For examination purposes the first interpretation shall be used.
Regarding claim 34, in line 6, it is unclear what the alternative to the limitation “or to track the user” is, as there is no indication that there is a choice. For examination purposes, the limitation will be considered as choosing either to “receive with the apparatus comprising the radio frequency based tracker from the another apparatus comprising the application with a wireless signal, an explicit indication that the another apparatus comprising the application is not providing the native sensor data,” and “to track the user”.
In addition, in line 10 it is unclear what the alternative to the limitation “or to track the user” is, as there is no indication that there is a choice. For examination purposes, the limitation will be considered as choosing either to “provide, with the apparatus comprising the radio frequency based tracker, the pseudo sensor data in response to receiving, from the another apparatus comprising the application with the wireless signal, the explicit indication that the another apparatus comprising the application is not providing the native sensor data” or “to track the user”.
