Claim Interpretation
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(f):
(f) Element in Claim for a Combination. – An element in a claim for a combination may be expressed as a means or step for performing a specified function without the recital of structure, material, or acts in support thereof, and such claim shall be construed to cover the corresponding structure, material, or acts described in the specification and equivalents thereof.
The following is a quotation of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph:
An element in a claim for a combination may be expressed as a means or step for performing a specified function without the recital of structure, material, or acts in support thereof, and such claim shall be construed to cover the corresponding structure, material, or acts described in the specification and equivalents thereof.
The claims in this application are given their broadest reasonable interpretation using the plain meaning of the claim language in light of the specification as it would be understood by one of ordinary skill in the art.  The broadest reasonable interpretation of a claim element (also commonly referred to as a claim limitation) is limited by the description in the specification when 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, is invoked.
As explained in MPEP § 2181, subsection I, claim limitations that meet the following three-prong test will be interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph:
(A)	the claim limitation uses the term “means” or “step” or a term used as a substitute for “means” that is a generic placeholder (also called a nonce term or a non-structural term having no specific structural meaning) for performing the claimed function;
(B)	the term “means” or “step” or the generic placeholder is modified by functional language, typically, but not always linked by the transition word “for” (e.g., “means for”) or another linking word or phrase, such as “configured to” or “so that”; and
(C)	the term “means” or “step” or the generic placeholder is not modified by sufficient structure, material, or acts for performing the claimed function.
Use of the word “means” (or “step”) in a claim with functional language creates a rebuttable presumption that the claim limitation is to be treated in accordance with 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph. The presumption that the claim limitation is interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, is rebutted when the claim limitation recites sufficient structure, material, or acts to entirely perform the recited function.
Absence of the word “means” (or “step”) in a claim creates a rebuttable presumption that the claim limitation is not to be treated in accordance with 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph. The presumption that the claim limitation is not interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, is rebutted when the claim limitation recites function without reciting sufficient structure, material or acts to entirely perform the recited function.
Claim limitations in this application that use the word “means” (or “step”) are being interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, except as otherwise indicated in an Office action. Conversely, claim limitations in this application that do not use the word “means” (or “step”) are not being interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, except as otherwise indicated in an Office action.

Because this/these claim limitation(s) is/are being interpreted under 35 U.S.C. 112(f) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, sixth paragraph, it/they is/are being interpreted to cover the corresponding structure described in the specification as performing the claimed function, and equivalents thereof.

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1-10 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Regarding claim 1, lines 2-3 indicate the first and second sticking surfaces are part of the adhesive component. It is confusing in lines 6-9 to indicate these surfaces are part of the sticking component. The way the first and second release component limitations are worded makes it unclear if they are positively part of the claimed sticking component, or rather, if they are only functionally recited limitations which are capable of being provided on the claimed sticking component. For the purpose of examination, it appears Applicant intended the first and second release components to be positively recited as part of the claimed sticking component.
Regarding claim 6, while a core might be interpreted to implicitly have sides on which adhesive layers would be disposed, the sides are not previously recited. Also, a core might be interpreted to have additional sides besides the opposite sides on which adhesive would be disposed. Accordingly there may be some confusion as to which sides are meant by “each side”. The examiner suggests --on opposite sides of the core-- to avoid any potential issue of insufficient antecedent basis and to correct the above noted minor issue of clarity.
Regarding claim 7, it is not clear what structure is required by a half-cut. The specification does not clarify this issue.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claims 1, 3 and 6 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by Gannon (US 2019/0046033 A1).
Regarding claim 1, Gannon teaches a sticking component comprising a sheet-shaped adhesive component 410 having first and second sticking surfaces with adhesiveness opposed to each other, the first sticking surface (defined by adhesive layer 511) constructed to be removably adhered to a bottom surface of a stick-on biometric device; a first release component 510 removably adhered to the first sticking surface of the sticking component; and a second release component 514 removably adhered to the second sticking surface (defined by adhesive layer 513) of the sticking component (Figure 12; paragraphs 61-64), wherein at least one of the first release component or the second release component has an outline that is visible, and that, when the sticking component is to be adhered to the stick-on biometric device, at least partially overlaps an outline of the bottom surface of the stick-on biometric device in a plan view of the sticking component (Figure 13; paragraph 61). It is noted that any release liner material has an outline that is visible, i.e. can be detected visually.
Regarding claim 3, this limitation is indicated by Figures 3, 5-8, 10 and 13.
Regarding claim 6, Gannon teaches these additional limitations (Figure 12; paragraphs 61 and 63-64).
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claims 2, 4-5 and 7 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Gannon as applied to claims 1, 3 and 6 above, and further in view of Colby (US 9580626 B2).
Regarding claim 2, Gannon teaches a central carrier of the sticking component may be printed with a geometric shape or alignment target which facilitates alignment of the sticking component when it is adhered to the device (paragraph 63), but is not limited to this particular alignment structure. Colby is also directed to carefully aligning an adhesive component with a device to which it is adhered. Colby provides the adhesive component with respective release layers on each side and teaches the release layers may be transparent and colored, translucent, have a visible border, or otherwise be visible to enable precise alignment of the adhesive component with the device (column 2, lines 38-67; column 5, lines 20-33). It would have been obvious to one having ordinary skill in the art at the time the application was filed to provide this limitation in Gannon’s sticking component because one having ordinary skill in the art would have been motivated to allow for alignment in a known suitable manner, as suggested by the teachings of Colby.
Regarding claim 4, the first release component has an outline that is visible, as noted above, and that, when the sticking component is to be adhered to the stick-on biometric device, at least partially overlaps the outline of the bottom surface of the stick-on biometric device in the plan view as seen in Figures 12-13 of Gannon. Gannon teaches the second release component may have an outline that is at least partially larger than an outline of the adhesive component to facilitate removal of the second release component (paragraph 61). As to the second release component being made of a colorless transparent material, if the first release component is provided with the above noted colored transparent material to facilitate alignment, naturally providing the second release component as a colorless transparent material would ensure it does not interfere with proper alignment. Clearly, if the second release component is opaque or otherwise interferes with alignment, the desired alignment would not be possible. In particular, Colby specifically indicates it may be desirable to see through such release component (column 5, lines 20-43), which naturally suggests a transparent colorless material. It would have been obvious to one having ordinary skill in the art at the time the application was filed to form Gannon’s second release component from a colorless transparent material because one having ordinary skill in the art would have been motivated to facilitate alignment for the reasons provided above in view of the above noted teachings of Colby.
Regarding claim 5, while not recited by Gannon, Colby suggests this additional limitation to facilitate repositioning of the sticking component if there is any misalignment (column 4, lines 37-47; column 7, lines 13-56; Figures 1-8). It would have been obvious to one having ordinary skill in the art at the time the application was filed to provide this additional limitation in Gannon because one having ordinary skill in the art would have been motivated to facilitate repositioning as suggested by the teachings of Colby.
Regarding claim 7, Colby clearly suggests perforations, a cut completely through the liner or a cut partially through the liner or a weakened line to provide the desired cut line to facilitate repositioning (column 4, lines 37-47). Clearly, the perforation alternative is suggested by Colby. As to a half-cut, this appears to be satisfied by cut a completely through the second release component because it doesn’t go through the adhesive, or it is suggested by a cut partially through the second release component. As to a fold, such is considered to be satisfied or suggested by a weakened line as taught by Colby because a fold provides such a structurally weakened line. It would have been obvious to one having ordinary skill in the art at the time the application was filed to this additional limitation in Gannon’s sticking component because one having ordinary skill in the art would have been motivated to provide a suitable cut line structure to facilitate repositioning, as suggested by the above noted teachings of Colby.
Claims 8-9 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Gannon in view of Colby as applied to claims 2, 4-5 and 7 above, and further in view of Park (US 2018/0243141 A1).
Regarding claims 8-9, Gannon teaches that the adhesive component can be removed (paragraph 61), but does not recite the claimed non-adhesive film-shaped release strip sandwiched partially between the first sticking surface and the first release component. However, such is known in related art to facilitate application of an adhesive surface to a substrate, to facilitate separation of a release layer, and/or to prevent contamination of the adhesive surface. See Park (Figures 1-2; paragraphs 2, 5 and 10). Park also teaches the release strip can suitably be transparent or opaque (paragraph 61). It would have been obvious to one having ordinary skill in the art at the time the application was filed to provide Gannon with these further limitations because one having ordinary skill in the art would have been motivated to achieve any of the above noted advantages in accordance with the teachings of Park.
