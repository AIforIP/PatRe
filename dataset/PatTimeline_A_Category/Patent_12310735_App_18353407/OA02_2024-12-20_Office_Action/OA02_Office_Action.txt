Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
The text of those sections of Title 35, U.S. Code not included in this action can be found in a prior Office action.
Claims 1, 3, 11, and 13 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by Baker et al. US Publication 2008/0139953 (hereinafter Baker).
Regarding claim 1, Baker discloses an electrocardiography monitor, comprising: a housing comprising rounded edges along top and bottom surfaces of the housing (elements 201 and 203 combined, see Figure 2, for the rounded top/bottom, see Figure 1B at the cross section where the top and bottom curve slightly); a microcontroller secured by the housing (microcontroller 512 as per Figure 5, within 202, which is housed in 201/203, see Figures 1A-2), an electrographic front end circuit to sense electrocardiographic signals (circuits 506-508 as well as 516, which work together as a traditional front end circuit, see Figure 5); where the microcontroller is interfaced to the electrocardiographic front end circuit to sample the electrocardiographic signals (512, as per [0076] which includes part of the front end circuit with an ADC that aids in sampling); and electrical contacts (302) that protrude from the bottom surface of the housing (bottom surface 203 as per Figure 3) to connect to a battery to power the microprocessor (some of the contacts 302 connect to the contacts that feed to the batteries as per Figure 4). Baker additionally details that the battery within 107 is between the microprocessor within 102 and the skin (see annotated figure below). Figure 2 additionally shows that the relative thickness of the contact 205 that the battery is set upon is not as thick as the housing base and retainer (203 and 104). Further, given the broadness of the limitation utilizing relative positioning, the human body includes skin on their neck, which when lying down would place the battery between it and the microprocessor even if there were no thickness at all to the above mentioned elements and the two claimed components were evenly placed upon the substrate 101.

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The text of those sections of Title 35, U.S. Code not included in this action can be found in a prior Office action.
Claims 2 and 12 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Baker in view of Cross et al. US Publication 2008/0288026 (hereinafter Cross).
Regarding claim 2 and 12, Baker is silent on the seal. Cross teaches an ECG monitoring patch that includes a seal coupling surrounding the electrical contacts (0051] at element 90, see also Figure 5). It would have been obvious to the skilled artisan before the effective filing date to utilize the seal as taught by Cross with the device of Baker in order to protect the contacts from moisture.
Claims 5-6 and 15-16 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Baker in view of Hugh et al. US Publication 2011/0021937 (hereinafter Hugh).
Regarding claims 5-6 and 15-16, Baker is silent on the actigraphy sensor and associated circuits. Hugh teaches an ECG monitoring device that includes circuitry for an actigraphy sensor (Figure 12B, circuitry “M”, [0052][0064]), wherein the actigraphy sensor generates interrupt signals to the microcontroller based on a position of the housing ([0052][0064]). It would have been obvious to the skilled artisan before the effective filing date to utilize the actigraphy sensor and circuits as taught by Hugh with the device of Baker in order to aid in determining any anomalies in the ECG signals.
Claims 7 and 17 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Baker in view of McGusty et al. US Publication 2011/0237924 (hereinafter McGusty).
Regarding claims 7 and 17, Baker is silent on the material choice. McGusty teaches an ECG monitoring patch that includes a housing (110), wherein the housing comprises polycarbonate, ABS, or an alloy of polycarbonate and ABS ([0100]). It would have been obvious to the skilled artisan before the effective filing date to utilize the material choice as taught by McGusty with the device of Baker as ABS is both light weight and durable enough to protect the circuits ([0100], McGusty).
Claims 8 and 18 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Baker in view of Wang et al. US Publication 2011/0160601 (hereinafter Wang).
Regarding claims 8 and 18, Baker is silent on the use of a flash memory. Wang teaches a flash memory configured to store the electrocardiographic signals ([0074][0075]). It would have been obvious to the skilled artisan before the effective filing date to utilize the flash memory as taught by Wang with the device of Baker in order to store the obtained ECG signals while also allowing transfer of the signals by removal of the flash memory.
Claims 9-10 and 19-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Baker in view of Kovacs US Publication 2007/0208232 (hereinafter Kovacs).
Regarding claims 9-10 and 19-20, Baker is silent on the expansion port. Kovacs teaches an ECG monitoring device that includes an expansion port via which an external device interfaces to the microcontroller (Figure 8 at 46, and [0040] which details off chip sensors can be added to the system and connected to the controller), wherein the external device comprises a physiological sensor ([0040]-[0046] details various sensors including accelerometers, load sensors, pulse oximetry and temperature sensors). It would have been obvious to the skilled artisan before the effective filing date to utilize the additional port/sensors as taught by Kovacs with the device of Baker in order to allow for a more robust monitoring system.
Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed 11/21/2024 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive. The previous 112 rejection and Drawing objection have been withdrawn due to the Applicant’s amendments to the claims.
The phrasing regarding the “between” language does not appear to overcome the prior art of record. The skin of the patient’s location is largely undefined, and using that as one of the pieces to detail where the battery is located still allows for Baker to utilized (as per the annotated figure above). If this is the route the Applicant wishes to amend towards, a lot more structural detail would be required. Further, if the device were to be rotated, given that the electrodes are not mentioned as being configured or adapted to contact skin of the patient, the orientation would allow for another separate interpretation could be taken even if the battery and microprocessor were side by side with the same general dimensions.
