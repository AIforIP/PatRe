Remarks
Applicant’s arguments, filed (02/08/2024), with respect to pending claims 21-23, 25-38 and 40 have been fully considered and they are persuasive.
Claim Analysis — 35 USC § 101
The new 2019 Revised Patent Subject Matter Eligibility Guidance published in the Federal register (Vol. 84 No.4, Jan 7, 2019 pp 50-57) has been applied and the claims are deemed as being patent eligible. In particular, in the prong 1 analysis claims 21, 32 and 40 contains an abstract idea. Further even if they were interpreted to have an abstract idea they claim the practical application of improving functionality of the glucose sensor device under prong 2. Thus claims 21, 32 and 40 are deemed patent eligible under 35 USC 101.
Claims 22, 23, 25-31 and 33-38 are dependent claims of claim 21 and 32 and are also patent eligible under 35 USC 101.
Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 21-23, 25-38 and 40 are allowed.
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
Regarding Claim 21, Gottlieb et al., (US Pub.20100030045 A1),[hereinafter Gottlieb’045] disclose a system comprising:
a glucose sensor device for determining a concentration of glucose in a body of a user (Fig. 4A, para [0032], para [0093]), the sensor device comprising:
a first electrochemical peroxide-based glucose sensor ([0190]; anodic polarization measures hydrogen peroxide);
a first electrochemical oxygen-based sensor ([0190]); and
a second electrochemical oxygen-based sensor ([0190]);
a processor (para [00126]); and
a memory storing instructions (para [0092], where a memory for storing a plurality of measurements) thereon which, when executed by the processor, cause the sensor device to:
receive a peroxide-based output signal from the first electrochemical peroxide-based glucose sensor, the peroxide-based output signal (Fig. 4a; sensor reading (graph glucose detection), para [0019]) being indicative of a level of glucose in the body of the user (para [0190]);
receive a first oxygen-based output signal from the first electrochemical oxygen-based sensor and a second oxygen-based output signal from the second electrochemical oxygen-based sensor(para [0190] and [0019]);
calculate a single oxygen-based signal based on the first and second oxygen-based output signals, wherein the single oxygen-based signal is indicative of the level of glucose in the body of the user ([0190]).
Gottlieb et al., (US Pub.20110319734),[hereinafter Gottlieb’734] disclose a sensor system used for sensing glucose (Abstract) where signal data from different electrodes is weighted and computationally fused to determine an analyte concentration (para [0010] and [0037]).
According to Gottlieb et al., (US Pub.20110319734), the advantage of fusing sensor signals is that it allows for assessment of the reliability of the glucose sensor system (see para [0037]).
The closest prior arts either singularity or in combination, fail to anticipate or render obvious that step of define:
“calculate, based on a membrane resistance of each sensor of the sensor device, a first weight for the peroxide-based output signal and a second weight for the single oxygen-based signal, wherein the fused sensor glucose value is based on fusing the peroxide-based output signal and the single oxygen-based signal using the first weight and the second weight, respectively”.
Claims 22-23 and 25-31 are allowed due to their dependency on claim 21.
