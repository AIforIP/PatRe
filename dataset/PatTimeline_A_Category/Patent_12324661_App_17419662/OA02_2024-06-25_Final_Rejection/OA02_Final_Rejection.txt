Prior Art Rejections
Applicant's arguments with respect to the rejections under 35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
Applicant argues that Mack is silent with respect to “transforming…to obtain a plurality of respiratory signals corresponding to each of the plurality of sensors.” However, Mack is not relied upon for this feature. As stated in the non-final office action, force data from each of the plurality of sensors are processed (i.e., transformed) to determine a variety of data, such as cardiac and respiratory signals, as explained in Young ¶ [0042-0043] (see non-final office action p. 9). Specifically, Young ¶ [0042] states that the “load or other sensors herein utilize macro signals and micro signals and process those signals to determine a variety of data” (emphasis added). Young ¶ [0043] explains that the “variety of data” includes respiratory signals, and that “[respiration] is also detected by the sensors.” The plural sensors indicates that respiratory signals are detected by each sensor, i.e., Young discloses obtaining a plurality of respiratory signals corresponding to each of the plurality of sensors. Additionally, obtaining the plurality of respiratory signals from the raw force data would require processing (i.e., “transforming”). Thus, Young discloses transforming force data to obtain a plurality of respiratory signals corresponding to each of the plurality of sensors.
While Young additionally discloses time-domain and/or frequency domain filtering the force data, this feature does not appear to be explicitly supported by the provisional application (see non-final office action p. 17). Mack is relied upon only for teaching the “transforming the force data [via] time-domain or frequency domain filtering [of] the force data” feature, which is taught in ¶ [0033, 0063] and Fig. 7, see non-final office action p. 17. Mack further teaches in ¶ [0063] and Fig. 7 that the frequency domain filtering is used to separate respiratory and cardiac output signals. Young ¶ [0043] indicates that respiratory and cardiac signals are analyzed separately. In light of these teachings, one of ordinary skill in the art would understand that it would be advantageous to include the frequency domain filtering taught by Mack to allow the system to better separate the respiratory and cardiac output signals.

Claims 9 and 25 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Young in view of Mack, further in view of US Patent Application Publication US-2020/0146910-A1 to Demirli et al. (hereinafter “Demirli”).
Regarding claim 9, Young in view of Mack teaches the system of claim 1. Although Young discloses identifying the change in the force data greater than a predefined threshold of force, this feature does not appear to be supported by the provisional application. Further, Young does not explicitly disclose a predefined duration threshold.
However, Demirli teaches a sensor integrated into an article that is able to detect the presence of a user (change in the load on the article) based on a change in pressure data (which is force data since pressure is force applied over a given area) greater than a specified (predefined) threshold; see [0053]. Demirli further teaches that the presence of a user can be determined when the presence persists longer than a predefined (e.g.,  10 minutes) threshold period of time (duration); see [0078].
Demirli is considered to be analogous to the claimed invention because they are both within the same field of non-invasive monitoring of physiological signals using sensors attached to articles on which a user is positioned. Therefore, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have further modified Young to incorporate the teachings of Demirli by using a predefined force threshold and a predefined duration threshold to determine the change in force data indicative of the change in the load on the article. Doing so would improve the system by allowing it to determine whether a person or object above a certain weight is on the article (see Demirli [0053]) and would provide the ability to use the duration threshold to determine that a user is in bed for the evening (see Demirli [0078]).
Regarding claim 25, see above, in regards to claims 9 and 17.
Claims 10 and 26 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Young in view Mack, further in view of US Patent Application Publication US-2014/0371635-A1 to Shinar et al. (hereinafter “Shinar”).
Regarding claim 10, Young in view of Mack teaches the system of claim 1 and the determination of the plurality of physiological sources on the article ([0034] states that the system can differentiate between two or more bodies (plurality of physiological sources) on the bed (article)). Young does not explicitly disclose that the determination is based on identification of alterations in the physiological signals transformed from the force data.
However, in [0110], Shinar teaches a system that determines when one person is out of bed (determination of the plurality of physiological sources on an article) by determining that the detected signals are characteristic of a second person (identification of alterations in physiological signals).
Shinar is considered to be analogous to the claimed invention because they are both within the same field of non-invasive monitoring of physiological signals using sensors attached to articles on which a user is positioned. Therefore, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have further modified Young by incorporating the teachings of Shinar by using the physiological signals characteristic of a given user to identify when another user has left the article. Doing so would improve the system's ability to detect which user is on or off of the bed, as noted by Shinar in [0110].
