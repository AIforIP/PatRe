Notice of Pre-AIA  or AIA  Status
The present application, filed on or after March 16, 2013, is being examined under the first inventor to file provisions of the AIA .
Election/Restrictions
1.	Claims 1 and 41-42 are withdrawn from further consideration pursuant to 37 CFR 1.142(b), as being drawn to a nonelected subject matter there being no allowable generic or linking claim. Applicant timely traversed the restriction (election) requirement in the reply filed on 06/21/2024
Applicant's election with traverse of claims 47-64 in the reply filed on 06/21/2024 is acknowledged.  The traversal is on the ground(s) that the art of DeBusschere does not disclose a physical, structural reference element. This is not found persuasive because the independent claims do not require the reference element to be physical or have a desired structure. The claims are written broadly, with the only shared limitation being “a reference element”.
The requirement is still deemed proper and is therefore made FINAL.

The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
2.	Claims  47-64 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
In regards to claim 47, Claim 47, “the patient” lacks antecedent basis as it has yet to be claimed. It is disclosed further down in the claim as “a patient”; however, this should be amended to “the patient”.
In further regards to claim 47, it is unclear what exactly the reference element is. The Applicant argues that it is a physical, structural element; however, the specification notes that it can be an image having measurement lines (such as a ruler generated by a display) that is used to compare/measure height or other relevant data (Par. 0087-0088). The reference element is not shown in the drawings either. Further clarification is needed by the applicant. For examination purposes, the Examiner is interpreting this as something that can be used to measure/compare the relevant data to.
In regards to claim 50, “a patient” has already been claimed. It is unclear if this is the same patient as previously claimed or if this is a new patient. Further clarification is needed.
In regards to claim 59, “a neck of a patient” has already been claimed. It is unclear if this is the same neck/patient as previously claimed or if this is a new neck/patient. Further clarification is needed.
Claims 48-49, 51-58, and 60-64 are rejected by virtue of their dependency.

A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
(a)(2) the claimed invention was described in a patent issued under section 151, or in an application for patent published or deemed published under section 122(b), in which the patent or application, as the case may be, names another inventor and was effectively filed before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
3.	Claim(s) 47-48, 51-52, 57-58, and 62-64 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by DeBusschere (US 20170296119 A1)
In regards to claim 47, DeBusschere discloses an apparatus (Abstract), comprising:
a positioning element configured to fit with an anatomic location on a torso of the patient (Par. 0024 teaches an abdominal pressure cuff [108] located on the torso of the patient);
a reference element having a known size (Fig 1 and Par. 0032 teach there being a line [118] that measurement data is compared to/measured by; see also U.S.C. 112b rejection above);
an imaging assembly coupled to and spaced from the positioning element (Par. 0042 teaches an imaging camera), the imaging assembly configured to capture imaging data of at least a portion of a neck of a patient and a portion of the reference element (Par. 0045 discloses the neck can be imaged and recorded); and
a processor operatively coupled to the imaging assembly (Par. 0093 discloses processor means [710]), the processor configured to determine a jugular venous pressure (JVP) or height information associated with the neck of the patient based on the imaging data and the known size of the reference element (Par. 0051 discloses using the imaging/recordings of the neck to determine the JVP).
In regards to claim 48, DeBusschere discloses the apparatus of claim 47, wherein the positioning element includes the reference element (Fig 1 shows the connection of the cuff and the reference [112]).
In regards to claim 51, DeBusschere discloses the apparatus of claim 47, wherein the positioning element, the reference element, and the imaging assembly are disposed on a sensing device, and the processor is disposed within a base, the base including a surface including a set of structures configured to receive a portion of the sensing device and to stably support the sensing device on the surface of the base (Fig 1 and Par. 18 discloses the sensing device itself that can include the processor and the sensing information).
In regards to claim 52, DeBusschere discloses the apparatus of claim 47, wherein the imaging assembly is coupled to the positioning element such that, when the positioning element is positioned within the anatomic location, the imaging assembly can be positioned to capture the imaging data of the at least the portion of the neck of the patient and the portion of the reference element (Par. 0045 discloses the neck can be imaged and recorded).
In regards to claim 57, DeBusschere discloses the apparatus of claim 47, wherein the processor is configured to determine the JVP or height information associated with the neck of the patient by: determining, based on the portion of the reference element captured in the image data, a conversion between pixels of the imaging data and a physical measure of distance (Par. 0050 discloses taking in imaging data and using measuring markers/overlays for determining JVP)
In regards to claim 58, DeBusschere discloses the apparatus of claim 47, wherein the processor is configured to determine the JVP or height information associated with the neck of the patient by: determining, based on the portion of the reference element captured in the image data, a conversion between pixels of the imaging data and a physical measure of distance; determining, from the imaging data, a highest point of a pulsation of an internal jugular vein (IJV) of the patient; and determining, based on at least the conversion between the pixels of the imaging data and the physical measure of distance, a vertical height between a sternal angle and the highest point of the pulsation of the IJV of the patient (Par. 0050-0052 teach measuring JVP peaks using a measurement marker/overlay).
In regards to claim 62, DeBusschere discloses the apparatus of claim 47, further comprising one or more sensors including at least one of: an electrocardiogram sensor, a photoplethysmography sensor, an electrodermal activity sensor, or a temperature sensor (Par. 0039 teaches using positioning sensors [208]).
In regards to claim 63 and 64, DeBusschere discloses the apparatus of claim 62, wherein the one or more sensors are disposed on a body coupled to the imaging assembly and the positioning element such that, when the patient grips the body for positioning of the positioning element relative to the anatomic location, a portion of a hand of the patient is in contact with the one or more sensors to facilitate measurement by the one or more sensors and the one or more sensors are in contact with a skin of the patient to facilitate measurement by the one or more sensors (Par. 0021 discloses the sensors are in the pressure cuff and can come in contact with the skin or any portion of the body coming in contact wit the cuff)
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
4.	Claim(s) 49, 50, 59, and 61 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over DeBusschere in view of Rogers (US 20210113099 A1)
In regards to claim 49, DeBusschere discloses the apparatus of claim 47, except for wherein the positioning element includes a spherical portion that is configured to fit within a suprasternal notch of the patient.
However, in the same field of endeavor, Rogers discloses a medical sensing device (Abstract) that can take relevant measurements and images of the of the user (Par. 0110) wherein the device is places on the suprasternal notch (Par. 0106 and Fig 2) in order to allow for accelerometric measurement of both the chest and neck.
Therefore, it would have been obvious to one having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have taken the teachings of DeBusschere and modified them by having the device sit within a suprasternal notch of the patient, as taught and suggested by Rogers, in order to allow for accelerometric measurement of both the chest and neck.
In regards to claim 50, DeBusschere discloses the apparatus of claim 47, except for wherein the positioning element is a first positioning element, the apparatus further comprising a second positioning element configured to rest against a portion of a chest of a patient.
However, in the same field of endeavor, Rogers discloses a medical sensing device (Abstract) that can take relevant measurements and images of the of the user (Par. 0110) wherein the positioning elements have first and second portions that can be on the neck and the chest (Par. 0062) in order to allow for more robust measurement and assessment.
Therefore, it would have been obvious to one having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have taken the teachings of DeBusschere and modified them by having the positioning device be on the neck and the chest, as taught and suggested by Rogers, in order to allow for more robust measurement and assessment.
In regards to claim 59, DeBusschere discloses the apparatus of claim 58, except for further comprising at least one of an accelerometer or a gyroscope configured to measure an orientation of a neck of a patient relative to a horizontal plane, wherein the determination of the vertical height is further based on an angle of inclination of the neck of the patient as determined based on the measured orientation of the neck.
However, in the same field of endeavor, Rogers discloses a medical sensing device (Abstract) that can take relevant measurements and images of the of the user (Par. 0110) wherein the device comprises an accelerometer (Par. 0135) in order to allow the physician to reconstruct movement/orientations or body positions of the user.
Therefore, it would have been obvious to one having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have taken the teachings of DeBusschere and modified them by having the system comprise an accelerometer, as taught and suggested by Rogers, in order to allow the physician to reconstruct movement/orientations or body positions of the user.
In regards to claim 61, DeBusschere discloses the apparatus of claim 47, except for further comprising an actuator configured to be actuated by the patient to cause the processor to activate the imaging assembly to capture the imaging data.
However, in the same field of endeavor, Rogers discloses a medical sensing device (Abstract) that can take relevant measurements and images of the of the user (Par. 0110) wherein the device comprises an actuator (Par. 0019) in order to allow for control of the sensors and their feedback. Therefore, it would have been obvious to one having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have taken the teachings of DeBusschere and modified them by having the system comprise an actuator, as taught and suggested by Rogers, in order to allow for control of the sensors and their feedback.
5.	Claim(s) 53-56 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over DeBusschere in view of Tzvieli (US 20200221956 A1)
In regards to claim 53, DeBusschere discloses the apparatus of claim 47, wherein the imaging assembly includes a light source configured to emit light toward the neck of the patient and a camera configured to capture NIR light reflected by the neck of the patient (Par. 0043 discloses the device can comprise light emitting devices that help with image capturing).
DeBusschere does not disclose wherein it is NIR light emitted and NIR camera used. However, in the same field of endeavor, Tzvieli discloses a method for monitoring for heart failure (Abstract) wherein NIR light is emitted and measured by the camera (Par. 0050-0052 and 0081) in order to measure for blood flow changes due to pulse waves.
Therefore, it would have been obvious to one having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have taken the teachings of DeBusschere and modified them by having the system emit NIR light and measure it with a camera, as taught and suggested by Tzvieli, in order to measure for blood flow changes due to pulse waves.
In regards to claim 54 and 56, DeBusschere discloses the apparatus of claim 47, except for wherein the imaging assembly includes first and second cameras, each of the first and second cameras configured to capture light in different/sane wavelength ranges.
However, in the same field of endeavor, Tzvieli discloses a method for monitoring for heart failure (Abstract) wherein there are two cameras for measuring light in different or same wavelengths (Par. 0080-0081) in order to calculate an extent of congestive heart failure (CHF) that a user is suffering from by measuring signs of CHF that include changes to facial blood flow, respiration, and/or level of activity.
Therefore, it would have been obvious to one having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have taken the teachings of DeBusschere and modified them by having the system comprise two cameras for measuring light wavelengths, as taught and suggested by Tzvieli, in order to calculate an extent of congestive heart failure (CHF) that a user is suffering from by measuring signs of CHF that include changes to facial blood flow, respiration, and/or level of activity.
In regards to claim 55, the combined teachings of DeBusschere and Tzvieli disclose the apparatus of claim 54, wherein the first camera is configured to capture optical light, and the second camera is configured to capture NIR light (Par. 0080-0081 of Tzvieli).
6.	Claim(s) 60 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over DeBusschere in view of Wagle (US 20170027457 A1)
In regards to claim 60, DeBusschere discloses the apparatus of claim 47, except for wherein the processor is configured to determine the JVP of the patient by: determining, based on a static image of the portion of the neck of the patient and a portion of the apparatus, an angle of a longitudinal axis of the neck relative to an angle of the apparatus; determining, from the imaging data, a highest point of pulsation of an internal jugular vein (IJV) of the patient; and determining, based on the angle of the longitudinal axis of the neck and highest point of pulsation of the IJV, a vertical height between a sternal angle and the highest point of the pulsation of the IJV of the patient.
However, in the same field of endeavor, Wagle discloses a system for monitoring central venous pressure to prevent heart failure (Abstract) wherein the device determines the JVP of the patient by monitoring the highest pulse of the IJV using the patients angle (Par. 0031) since internal jugular valves may affect manual measurement and/or observations of pulse height and cause inaccuracies in determining venous pressure.
Therefore, it would have been obvious to one having ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have taken the teachings of DeBusschere and modified them by having the system meaure JVP through the monitoring of IJV, as taught and suggested by Wagle, since internal jugular valves may affect manual measurement and/or observations of pulse height and cause inaccuracies in determining venous pressure.
