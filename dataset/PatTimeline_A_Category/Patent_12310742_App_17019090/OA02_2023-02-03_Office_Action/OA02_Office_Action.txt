Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
In the event the determination of the status of the application as subject to AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 (or as subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103) is incorrect, any correction of the statutory basis for the rejection will not be considered a new ground of rejection if the prior art relied upon, and the rationale supporting the rejection, would be the same under either status.
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claims 1-3, 7, and 10 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by Niederkrom et al. ‘277 (US Pub No. 2014/0366277).
Regarding claim 1, Niederkrom et al. ‘277 teaches an air-permeable bedding system ([0006]), comprising:
a microclimate fabric layer (Fig. 3 vapor permeable material 120 and [0025]; “…cover sheet 100 also comprises a vapor permeable material 120…”); and
an air-permeable capacitive sensor layer (Fig. 3 pressure-sensing mat 125 and [0025]) below the microclimate fabric layer, the air-permeable capacitive sensor layer comprises two air-permeable substrates (Fig. 3 cover sheet 100, support mattress 160 and [0025]-[0026], [0035]), each carrying a plurality of electrically conductive pathways that are impermeable to air (Fig. 3 conductors 126 and [0029]-[0031]; One of ordinary skill would understand the electrical conductors are made of a type of metal, which is known to be impermeable to air.), wherein the two air-permeable substrates securely position the electrically conductive pathways to define a sensor grid (Fig. 3 grid of electrical conductors 126 and [0030]) and position the electrically conductive pathways that are impermeable to air to be spaced apart to define a plurality of air pathways for the air-permeable capacitive sensor layer (Fig. 3 thickness 117 and [0030]; The spaces between the conductors 126 are air permeable. The thickness 117 of pressure-sensing mat 125 can be reduced as a result of the weight of a person supported by support mattress 160 and cover sheet 100. When the thickness 117 is reduced, one of ordinary skill would understand that air is pushed out as the pressure-sensing mat 125 is compress, indicating that there is air that passed through the spaces between the conductors 126.).
Regarding claim 2, Niederkrom et al. ‘277 teaches wherein the air-permeable capacitive sensor layer further comprises an air-permeable dielectric layer positioned between the two air-permeable substrates (Fig. 3 compressible dielectric 127 and [0029]-[0030]).
Regarding claim 3, Niederkrom et al. ‘277 teaches wherein the air-permeable substrates are formed form meshes or foam ([0023], [0026]).
Regarding claim 7, Niederkrom et al. ‘277 teaches a foam comfort layer between the microclimate fabric layer and the air-permeable capacitive sensor layer (Fig. 3 spacer material 110 and [0026]).
Regarding claim 10, Niederkrom et al. ‘277 teaches an inlet configured to be connected to an active air source that increases airflow within the air-permeable bedding system (Fig. 3 air mover 140 and [0027], [0032]).
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim 4 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Niederkrom et al. ‘277 in view of Federighi et al. ‘144 (US Pub No. 2005/0173144).
Regarding claim 4, Niederkrom et al. ‘277 teaches wherein at least one of the electrically conductive pathways carried by one of the air-permeable substrates is encapsulated (Fig. 3 protective outer layer 128 and [0031]).
Niderkrom et al. ‘277 teaches all of the elements of the current invention as mentioned above except for wherein at least one of the electrically conductive pathways carried by one of the air-permeable substrates is encapsulated by an thermoplastic.
Federighi et al. ‘144 teaches twisted metallic conductors that are formed by a bundled array of insulated metallic conductors of thermoplastic material sheathed in a protective banding and by a metallic shielding. This would aid in a decrease in attenuation and an improvement concerning the problem of cross talk in digital data transmission (Abstract).
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have modified the at least one of the electrically conductive pathways carried by one of the air-permeable substrates of Niederkrom et al. ‘277 to be encapsulated by an thermoplastic as Federighi et al. ‘144 teaches that this would aid in a decrease in attenuation and an improvement concerning the problem of cross talk in digital data transmission.
Claims 5 and 6 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Niederkrom et al. ‘277 in view of Federighi et al. ‘144 further in view of Corzani et al. ‘101 (US Pub No. 2003/0121101).
Regarding claim 5, Niederkrom et al. ‘277 in view of Federighi et al. ‘144, as applied to claim 4, teaches all of the elements of the current invention as mentioned above except for wherein the thermoplastic is bonded to the air-permeable substrate.
Corzani et al. ‘101 teaches a covering sheet for bedding articles that comprises at least a first moisture vapour permeable layer and a second liquid impermeable but moisture vapour permeably film layer of thermoplastic composition bonded onto said first layer ([0030]) which would provide increased comfort to the user (Abstract).
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have modified the thermoplastic of Niederkrom et al. ‘277 in view of Federighi et al. ‘144, as applied to claim 4, to include being bonded to the air-permeable substrate as Corzani et al. ‘101 teaches that this will aid increasing the comfort to the user.
Regarding claim 6, Niederkrom et al. ‘277 in view of Federighi et al. ‘144 further in view of Corzani et al. ‘101, as applied to claim 5, teaches all of the elements of the current invention as mentioned above except for wherein the thermoplastic is bonded to the air-permeable substrate by heat lamination.
Corzani et al. ‘101 teaches that the thermoplastic composition is applied in a continuous layer or film by means of known means, e.g. preferably by hot melt coating ([0035]).
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have modified the thermoplastic of Niederkrom et al. ‘277 in view of Federighi et al. ‘144 further in view of Corzani et al. ‘101, as applied to claim 5, to include being bonded to the air-permeable substrate by heat lamination as Corzani et al. ‘101 teaches that this is a known mean of applying a thermoplastic composition to a bedding article.
Claims 8 and 9 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Niederkrom et al. ‘277 in view of Mc Kenna et al. ‘850 (US Pub No. 2010/0022850).
Regarding claim 8, Niederkrom et al. ‘277 teaches all of the elements of the current invention as mentioned above except for a piezoelectric sensor below the microclimate fabric layer.
Mc Kenna et al. ‘850 teaches a transducer array 102, which is below cover 121, with one or more transducers, such as resistive, inductive, capacitive, acceleration, temperature, Polyvinylidene Fluoride (PVDF), piezoelectric, and/or other transducers. The transducer array 102 is configured to sense deflection indicative of information about the subject, such as physiologic information like one or more physiologic parameters and/or states. Examples of such information include, but are not limited to, heart rate, respiration rate, respiration intensity, temperature, position, activity level, pressure points, blood flow, time on and off (use of) the transducer array 102, weight and wetness/enuresis. The transducer array 102 generates a signal indicative of the sensed information (Fig. 1 and [0016]).
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have modified the air-permeable bedding system of Niederkrom et al. ‘277 to include a piezoelectric sensor below the microclimate fabric layer as Mc Kenna et al. ‘850 teaches that this will aid in sensing deflection indicative of information about the subject, such as physiologic information like one or more physiologic parameters and/or states.
Regarding claim 9, Niederkrom et al. ‘277 in view of Mc Kenna et al. ‘850, as applied to claim 8, teaches all of the elements of the current invention as mentioned above except for a transmitter configured to transmit signals of the capacitive sensor layer for determining a respiration rate and to transmit signals of the piezoelectric sensor for determining a heart rate.
Mc Kenna et al. ‘850 teaches a transducer array 102, which is below cover 121, with one or more transducers, such as resistive, inductive, capacitive, acceleration, temperature, Polyvinylidene Fluoride (PVDF), piezoelectric, and/or other transducers. The transducer array 102 is configured to sense deflection indicative of information about the subject, such as physiologic information like one or more physiologic parameters and/or states. Examples of such information include, but are not limited to, heart rate, respiration rate, respiration intensity, temperature, position, activity level, pressure points, blood flow, time on and off (use of) the transducer array 102, weight and wetness/enuresis. The transducer array 102 generates a signal indicative of the sensed information (Fig. 1 and [0016], [0022]). An audible alarm could be set to complement the flashing status light to alert the user of the sudden/sever change in physiological data or trends in data which indicate a change in physiological status ([0025]).
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to have modified the air-permeable bedding system of Niederkrom et al. ‘277 in view of Mc Kenna et al. ‘850, as applied to claim 8, to include a transmitter configured to transmit signals of the capacitive sensor layer for determining a respiration rate and to transmit signals of the piezoelectric sensor for determining a heart rate as Mc Kenna et al. ‘850 teaches that this will aid in alerting the user of a sudden change in physiological status.
