Reasons for Allowance
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
The closest prior art US Patent Application 2008/0214901 to Gehman discloses an electronic device (10, Figure 2) with a housing (12) that contains a physiologic data collection circuit ([0024]). A skin adhering section (the edges of the patch; Figure 2) extend from the housing. An electrode (14) is positioned on a bottom surface of the skin adhering section (Figure 2), and each electrode is connected to the circuit (via 13). The skin adhering section having an adhesive strip (20) over a top, and extending horizontally beyond the boundary of the skin adhering section. The electrode elements (14) can be conductive adhesives for adhering to skin ( [0031]). If the adhesive electrode elements (14) are the electrodes, then Gehman fails to further disclose a first adhesive layer on the bottom surface of the skin adhering section. If the adhesive electrode elements (14) are the first adhesive layer, then the electrode (13) is not on a bottom surface of the respective wing. There is insufficient teaching, suggestion, or motivation to replace the conductive adhesive electrode with a separate conductive electrode and adhesive (both on the bottom surface of the skin adhering section).
Similar designs were known in the art. Libbus (US 2010/0056881) discloses a device with a center housing (160) a skin adhering section (Figs. 1H, 1G, etc.) with electrodes (112A) and a first adhesive (116A) on the bottom surface. An adhesive strip (164 on cover 162) extends over the top of the skin adhering section. However, the adhesive strip does not extend horizontally beyond a boundary of the adhesive layer (see Figs. 11-1J), and there is insufficient teaching, suggestion, or motivation to modify the adhesive strip. Gehman teaches that the top adhesive layer is useful for securing the device to the user, but the device of Libbus is already secured with the first adhesive.
Oakley (US 2004/0077954), Durand (2009/0048556) and Axelgaard (US 6,038,464) also teach many of the recited features. The adhesive strip of Durand functions as an adhesion guard and protects the first adhesive while extending horizontally beyond the first adhesive. However, the adhesive strip is not positioned over the top of the skin adhering section, and there is insufficient teaching, suggestion, or motivation to move it from the bottom surface of the device. Similarly, the adhesive strip of Axelgaard also functions as an adhesive guard and is over the first adhesive. However, this adhesive strip does not extend horizontally outward beyond the boundary of the first adhesive. Moreover, there is insufficient motivation to modify the adhesive strip of Axelgaard to extend beyond the adhesive layer because such a modification would negate the advantages of the design (particularly controlling the strength of adhesion to the skin).
