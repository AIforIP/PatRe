Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 14 and 18-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
In claim 14, the phrase “the tubular core” lacks proper antecedent basis.
In claim 18, the language “electrically coupling each of the first electrode and the second electrode to the electronic component to a hub coupled to a core” (see last three lines) appears to have been intended to read --electrically coupling each of the first electrode and the second electrode to a hub coupled to a core-- (this will be the interpretation used for purposes of applying prior art). However, it is unclear whether this is indeed the intended claim language.
In claim 20, it is unclear what the difference is between “disposing a printed circuit board (PCB) assembly onto the core, the PCB assembly including a substrate” and “disposing the PCB substrate onto the core”. The first disposing step already disposes the PCB substrate onto the core. It is therefore unclear how the PCB substrate, which is already on the core, can further be disposed onto the core.

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim(s) 1-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Imran over Basu (US2017/0224237A1).
Regarding claims 1-4, 8, 10-1, 16, 18 and 19, as an alternative interpretation, the hub is the shaft 52, instead of 52 with 78. In Fig. 5, it can be seen conductors 69 are part of the flex strip 71, whereas within the lumen of the shaft 52 the conductors 69 are separate, as can be seen in Fig. 4. See col. 3, lns. 22-30 and 53-58. Note that Imran describes Fig. 4 as being a cross-sectional view taken along line 4–4 of Fig. 3. The numbers used in Fig. 4 are, therefore, incorrect, as the shaft should be numbered “52”, not “22”, and the lumen be numbered “56”, not “27”.  From the overall disclosure, it is understood Imran is actually referring to shaft 52 when discussing Fig. 4. Since within shaft 52 the conductors 69 are no longer part of the flex strip 71, one of ordinary skill in the art expects these conductors 69 to actually be wires, attached to ends of traces 69 part of the flex strip 71. However, Imran does not provide any details to confirm this assumption.
Basu discloses (refer to Figs. 1 and 4-7) a mapping catheter, similar to catheter 51 of Imran. The catheter includes spines (42, corresponding to the arms 62 of Imran), each spine including a core (44) having an elongate shape to which is attached a printed circuit board (PCB) assembly (80) onto the core, the PCB assembly including a PCB substrate (81, having layers 81, 81b, 81c; see [0050]) and an electronic component (electrodes 85; see [0050]) disposed on the PCB substrate. Traces (e.g., 87b, 87c in Fig. 7) are formed within the substrate, along with soldering pads (88, Fig. 7), such that wires (30, Figs. 4 and 7) of hub (46) are electrically coupled to the electronic component (see Figs. 4 and 7; see [0047] and [0051]).
In view of the teachings of Basu, one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention would have found it obvious to implement conductors 69 within the shaft 52 of Imran as separate wires, to provide pads at the proximal end of conductors 69 of flex strip 71 received in shaft 52, the wires 69 in the shaft being soldered to the pads on the flex strip. Implementing the transition of the conductors from the flex strip to the wires within the shaft 52 in this manner would have had predictable results and would have been obvious, as a use of a known technique to establish an electrical connection to electrodes on a flexible PCB of a mapping catheter.
Regarding claims 6, 7, 9, 14, 15 and 20, Imran discloses the claimed invention, except for the limitations crossed out above, pertaining to the PCB being ring-shaped and the electrode extending circumferentially about the core. Basu teaches forming the flexible PCB with lateral portions 83, such that electrodes 85 form ring electrodes when the flexible PCB is pre-formed into a ring shape in certain areas, by being wrapped around the core 44. See Fig. 6 and [0057]. The PCB substrate becomes a multi-part ring, as it is wrapped around the core, as shown in Fig. 6.
In view of the teachings of Basu, one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention would have found it obvious to modify the flex strip 71 of Imran to have lateral portions (corresponding to 83 in Basu) at electrode locations, and the electrode being elongated as taught by Basu, such that when the flexible substrate is wrapped into a ring shape at the electrode locations, the electrodes form ring electrodes. One of ordinary skill in the art would have been motivated to do so because ring electrodes are more likely to always make contact with the intended tissue, compared to flat electrodes. Additionally, since such an electrode shape is known in a conventional mapping catheter (i.e., the same type of device as taught by Imran), modifying the flexible substrate and electrode shape to implement such conventional ring electrodes would have had predictable results.
Regarding claims 5 and 14, Imran discloses the claimed invention, except for the limitations crossed out above, pertaining to the core being tubular. Basu teaches a support member 43, which can be made from shape-memory alloy (see [0048]), similar to core 72 of Imran (see col. 4, lns. 8-29). Whereas Imran teaches attaching the flex strip 71 to core 72, Basu teaches first disposing a non-conductive tubular core covering 44 extending along each spine 42 (see [0047]). In view of the teachings of Basu, one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention would have found it obvious to modify the arms 62 of Imran, to include a tubular non-conductive core over the shape-memory element 72, the flex strip 71 being attached to the tubular core. One of ordinary skill in the art would have found it obvious to do so and would have had predictable results, because it constitutes modifying a known device in the same manner as another known device, in the same field of art. Additionally, such a non-conductive tubular core would provide additional insulation between the electrodes and conductors of strip 71 and the shape-memory alloy core 72, avoiding possible short-circuits.
Regarding claim 17, Imran does not disclose the electronic component being a sensor. Basu teaches providing, within lumen 48 of stem 46 (corresponding to shaft 52 of Imran), an electromagnetic position sensor 36 (Fig. 4) to which is connected a sensor cable 34. Basu teaches one or more electromagnetic sensors may also be housed in distal portions of the spines 42 (see [0070]), i.e., disposed on the PCB substrate, whether mechanically and electrically, or just mechanically.
In view of the teachings of Basu, one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention would have found it obvious to modify the mapping catheter 51 of Imran, to include an electromagnetic position sensor at a distal portion of an arm 61, coupled to the flex circuit 71 and electrically connected to a sensor cable in the shaft 52, to provide the added functionality of electromagnetic position sensing as taught by Basu.
