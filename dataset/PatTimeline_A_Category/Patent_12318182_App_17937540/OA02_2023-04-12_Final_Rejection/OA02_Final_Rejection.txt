Specification
Applicant is reminded of the proper language and format for an abstract of the disclosure.
The abstract should be in narrative form and generally limited to a single paragraph on a separate sheet within the range of 50 to 150 words in length. The abstract should describe the disclosure sufficiently to assist readers in deciding whether there is a need for consulting the full patent text for details.
The language should be clear and concise and should not repeat information given in the title. It should avoid using phrases which can be implied, such as, “The disclosure concerns,” “The disclosure defined by this invention,” “The disclosure describes,” etc.  In addition, the form and legal phraseology often used in patent claims, such as “means” and “said,” should be avoided. Accordingly, the abstract is objected to due to its use of the legal phraseology “or the like.”
Claim Interpretation
Please note that for the purposes of this examination, it is understood that claims 1 and 17 introduce the “at least one component” as the component which is “position adjustable relative to a wall of a housing.” Therefore, when a claim which depends from one of these claims further specifies that “the at least one component comprises” a particular component (e.g. “the at least one transmit antenna” of claim 2, “the at least one receive antenna” of claim 4, etc.), the particular component further specified by that dependent claim is also understood to be “the at least one component” which is “position adjustable relative to a wall of a housing,” respectively. Accordingly, as provided in detail below, these claims have, in part, been rejected under 35 USC § 103 to account for this interpretation.

The Applicant asserts on pages 9 to 10 of the Remarks:
… claims 1 and 17, Applicant submits that Leath and Lucyszyn are not properly combinable in the manner proposed. In particular, Lucyszyn is not analogous prior art to the claimed invention. A reference is analogous art to the claimed invention if: (1) the reference is from the same field of endeavor as the claimed invention (even if it addresses a different problem); or (2) the reference is reasonably pertinent to the problem faced by the inventor ( even if it is not in the same field of endeavor as the claimed invention). MPEP 2141.0l(a).
Lucyszyn is directed to an RF MEMS antenna for a generic transmit antenna application where the transmit antenna(s) physically move under some form of mechanical actuation mechanism. See page 1988, left column, Introduction, paragraph 3. There is no disclosure in Lucyszyn that the RF MEMS antenna can be used in a noninvasive analyte sensor. The claimed invention is directed to a non-invasive analyte sensor. Accordingly, Lucyszyn is not from the same field of endeavor as the claimed invention.
In addition, Lucyszyn is not reasonably pertinent to the problem faced by the inventor. The inventor was concerned with designing a non-invasive analyte sensor with or more adjustable transit and/or receive components whose physical position can be adjusted to permit adjustment of the sensing performance of the sensor. See paragraphs [0004] and [0037].
In Lucyszyn, although the RF MEMS antenna is adjustable, there is no disclosure that the RF MEMS antenna is suitable for use in a non-invasive analyte sensor, or that adjustment of the position of the RF MEMS antenna is for adjusting performance of such a sensor.
Since (1) Lucyszyn is not from the same field of endeavor as the claimed invention, and (2) Lucyszyn is not reasonably pertinent to the problem faced by the inventor, Lucyszyn is not analogous prior art that can be relied on under 35 U.S.C. 103. Accordingly, Lucyszyn is not analogous prior art, and there is no suggestion or motivation to modify Leath with Lucyszyn.
For at least these reasons, claims 1 and 17 are patentable over Leath Lucyszyn. Claims 2-8 and 18-29 depend from claims 1 and 17, respectively, and are patentable therewith for that reason alone and need not be separately distinguished at this time. Applicant does not concede the rejections to the dependent claims.
In response, Examiner respectfully disagrees with Applicant’s assertion that, “Leath and Lucyszyn are not properly combinable in the manner proposed.” In supporting this assertion Applicant reasons that, “Lucyszyn is not analogous prior art to the claimed invention” because, “Lucyszyn is not from the same field of endeavor as the claimed invention” and, “Lucyszyn is not reasonably pertinent to the problem faced by the inventor.” However, this reasoning is not considered persuasive in supporting the conclusion that “Leath and Lucyszyn are not properly combinable in the manner proposed.”
Specifically, regarding the suggestion that “Lucyszyn is not from the same field of endeavor as the claimed invention,” Applicant also provides that, “Lucyszyn is directed to an RF MEMS antenna for a generic transmit antenna application where the transmit antenna(s) physically move under some form of mechanical actuation mechanism … There is no disclosure in Lucyszyn that the RF MEMS antenna can be used in a noninvasive analyte sensor. The claimed invention is directed to a non-invasive analyte sensor.” However, in looking to the “explanations of the invention’s subject matter in the patent application, including the embodiments, function, and structure of the claimed invention” this reasoning overly narrowly construes the field of the endeavor of the claimed invention (See MPEP 2141.01(a)(I) (quoting Airbus S.A.S. v. Firepass Corp., 941 F.3d 1374, 1380, 2019 USPQ2d 430083 (Fed. Cir. 2019))). This is because the majority of the discussion provided within the patent application relates to the details of the, “adjustment of the position(s) of one or more transmit components and/or the position(s) of one or more receive components” (Specification Paragraph [0003]). Similarly, as acknowledged by the Applicant, the Lucyszyn reference relates to the, “review and survey of RF MEMS antennas … [which can employ] some forms of mechanical actuation for movements of the radiation elements” (See Lucyszyn Abstract). Furthermore, both the patent application and the Lucyszyn reference relate to wherein these components operate within the radio frequency range. As such, the suggested differences in the field of endeavor between the patent application and the Lucyszyn reference merely pertain to the what these components are operating in relation to rather than to differences between the components themselves. In light of these similarities it is reasonable to conclude that the components discussed within the Lucyszyn reference could have been substituted into the patent application without meaningfully impacting the operation of what the patent application otherwise provides. Accordingly, a person having ordinary skill in the art would have appreciated that the significant overlap in the scopes of these endeavors would have as a matter of common sense and the reality of the circumstances, regarded the patent application and the Lucyszyn reference as being within the same field of endeavor and therefore as analogous forms of art.
Regardless, the Lucyszyn reference is analogous prior art because it is reasonably pertinent to the problem faced by the inventor. This is because the problem which is understood to be addressed by the patent application is, as provided throughout the patent application and as explicitly stated by the Applicant is, “designing a non-invasive analyte sensor with or more adjustable transit and/or receive components whose physical position can be adjusted to permit adjustment of the sensing performance of the sensor.” Therefore, prior art which regards “adjustment of the sensing performance of the sensor,” being achieved by a “more adjustable transit and/or receive components whose physical position can be adjusted” would have been relevant to a person having ordinary skill in the art when looking for a solution. The Lucyszyn reference, is such a piece of prior art. This is because the Abstract of the Lucyszyn reference acknowledges that, “RF MEMS technology can offer enhanced performance over the conventional solid-state devices. … [while, specifically,] generic antennas … employ[] some forms of mechanical actuation for movements of the radiation elements.” Accordingly, a person having ordinary skill in the art would have appreciated that the Lucyszyn reference was reasonably pertinent to the problem faced and would have commended itself to an inventor’s attention in considering their problem and is therefore an analogous prior art reference. For at least these reasons Applicant’s assertions are not considered persuasive.
