Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1-8 are allowed.
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
Oshima et al. (US PGPUB 20210267678) teaches of biomagnetic imaging of with a plurality of magnetic sensors 11 in an array 10 arranged on the chest (i.e. MCG, see Oshima FIG. 1 & ¶ [0042]). Even though Oshima et al. does not teach of an external maker, Oshima et al. teaches of an internal maker 24 having a single coil to track a catheter 20 (see Oshima et al. ¶ [0089]) but does not teach of utilizing a plurality of coils.  Furthermore, Oshima et al. is silent to MR imaging and a corresponding MR compatible marker.
Misaka et al. (US PGPUB 20200100695) teaches of an imaging scanner 100 with combined MR imaging 7 and MEG unit 3. Misaka et al. also teaches of using marker coils M1-M3 detected by the magnetic sensors 1 of the MEG unit 3 in order to determine positional information for generating a transformation matrix between the MRI and MEG coordinate systems (see Misaka et al. ¶ [0045]). While Misaka et al. discloses that markers M1, M2, M3 have a coil for generating a magnetic field, Misaka et al. does not teach of said coils being 3-axis coils. Furthermore, while Misaka et al. teaches of seals S1-S3 for camera registration (see Misaka et al. ¶ [0049]), Misaka is silent to contrast markers imaged by MR imaging.
Kudo et al. (US PGPUB 20200305747) teaches of optical co-registration using marker coils, but not 3-axis marker coils.
Messaritaki et al. (“Assessment and elimination of the effects of head movement on MEG resting-state measures of oscillatory brain activity,” (1 October 2017), Neuroimage, 159:302–324) teaches of mitigation of head movement in MEG imaging (see Messaritaki et al. Abstract). Messaritaki et al. also teaches of MEG-MRI co-registration in which 3 fiducial markers with coils were relied upon in which the fiducial are imageable in both MRI & MEG (see Mesaritaki et al. § 2.2). However, the Messaritaki et al. fiducial coils are not characterized as 3-axis coils and there is no suggestion in Messaritaki et al. that the coils are 3-axis coils.
Similar, to Messaritaki et al., Pfeiffer et al. (see IDS of 13 June 2023) teaches of using co-registration of MRI and MEG data based on MR compatible fiducials and head position indicator (HPI) coils (see Pfeiffer et al. § 2.2 & 2.3.4). While the Pfeiffer et al. coils were arranged in sets of triplets, Pfeiffer et al. does not teach or suggest that the triplets are arranged at different coordinate axes. Pfeiffer et al. only teaches that the coils are energized at different times for accurate localization (see Pfeiffer et al. § 2.2). Accordingly, Pfeiffer et al. does not teach of 3-axis coils.
Hill et al. (“Multi-channel whole-head OPM-MEG: Helmet design and a comparison with a conventional system,” (1 October 2020), NeuroImage Volume 219) teaches OPM-MEG co-registration with MRI imaging using HPI coils which are not described as 3-axis coils (see Hill et al. § 2.4.2).
McGowin (“HEAD MOTION EVALUATION AND CORRECTION IN MAGNETOENCEPHALOGRAPHY,” (May 2015), PhD. Dissertation Physics, Wake Forest University,) teaches of MEG localization using fiducial localization coil (see McGowin FIG. 5), but McGowin does not teach of said coils being 3-axis coils.
In sum, the instant specification defines a 3-axis coil as “a coil adapted to emit magnetic fluxes along three mutually perpendicular axes.” The evaluated prior art, cited or otherwise, does not teach of a 3-axis coil, merely a single HPI coil.
