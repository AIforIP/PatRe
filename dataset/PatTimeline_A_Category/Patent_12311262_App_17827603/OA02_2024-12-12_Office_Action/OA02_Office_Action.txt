Claim Objections
Claims 1, 8, & 15 are objected to because of the following informalities: the claims recite “…responsive to the one or more predictive indicators that the user will be unable to complete the task in the game context…” which appears to be missing a word in the phrase, at least based upon the current set of claim amendments, such as to recite “…responsive to the one or more predictive indicators identifying that the user will be unable to complete the task in the game context…”.  Appropriate correction is required.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Claim 1 recites the limitation “…the hand-held controller operated by the user during the game context…”.  There is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim. While the pre-amble recites “a hand-held controller”, there appears to be no recitations in the claim that provide the necessary antecedent basis being “the hand-held controller operated by the user during the game context”.
Claim 8 recites “…the hand-held controller operated by the user during the game context…”.  There is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim. Unlike independent claim 1, the preamble of claim 8 does not appear to introduce “a hand-held controller”, nor is “a hand-held controller” recited thereafter, thus above-identified limitation lacks antecedent basis for the reasons discussed above with respect to claim 1 regarding “operated by the user” and for failing to previously recite “a hand-held controller”.
Claim 10 recites “the profile model” which lacks antecedent basis.
Claim 15 recites “…the hand-held controller operated by the user during the game context…”.  There is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim. Unlike independent claim 1, the preamble of claim 15 does not appear to introduce “a hand-held controller”, nor is “a hand-held controller” recited thereafter, thus above-identified limitation lacks antecedent basis for the reasons discussed above with respect to claim 1 regarding “operated by the user” and for failing to previously recite “a hand-held controller”.
Claim 15 also recites “provide assistance input via a computer network”, while also reciting “a server coupled to the client device via a computer network” in an earlier recitation, thus it appears unclear if these computer networks are the same or different computer networks.
Claim 15 also recites, in the last clause of the claim, “wherein the client device is configured to receive the assistance input from the server via the computer network” which appears unclear if the limitation was inadvertently overlooked to be removed, as the amended language introduces the hand-held controller (client device?) being provided the assistance input via a computer network. Applicant is requested to review the entirety of the wherein clause with respect to the amended language to remedy the clarity issue.
Claim 20 recites “wherein the client device is a hand-held controller or a head-mounted display”, where claim 20 being a combination of the limitations recited in claim 15 and 20, it appears unclear if the claim 20 recitation of “a hand-held controller” is the same or different hand-held controller to that of claim 15.  Similarly, based on the above discussion regarding the last limitation of claim 15, it appears Applicant would benefit from reviewing claim 20.
