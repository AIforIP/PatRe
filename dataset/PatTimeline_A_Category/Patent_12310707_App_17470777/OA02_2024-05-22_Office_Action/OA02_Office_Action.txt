On pg 10, last paragraph to pg 11, first paragraph, Applicant asserts prior art of record Decant does not anticipate an apparatus comprising an open center to allow substantially unimpeded blood flow therethrough. Examiner respectfully disagrees. Decant teaches a plurality of openings around a center of a vena cava filter (best shown in Fig 1) which are designed to substantially unimpeded blood flow by removing thrombus that may impede blood flow in a vessel [0007]. So, to say Decant teaches exactly the opposite of openings in a center for unimpeded flow is the opposite of what Decant actually teaches.
On pg 11, paragraph 3, Applicant asserts Decant does not anticipate the invention because it teaches a filter monitoring device, not a vessel monitoring device. Examiner respectfully disagrees. The term “vessel monitoring device” is a term in the preamble used to recite purpose (MPEP 2111.02, II.). Although terms used to recite purpose in the preamble can be used to provide context to distinguish the claimed invention from the prior art, such distinction does not exist where the prior art comprises substantially similar structures for performing the purpose or intended use. In this instance, it appears Applicant’s vessel monitoring device comprises several sensing structures for monitoring the vessel, particularly an expandable and collapsible variable inductance coil configured to move with the vessel in response to expansion of the vessel. Decant comprises a similar structure comprising a plurality of adjacent wire strands (10, 12, 14, 16, 18, and 20) connected in a circle by electric conductive bridges 26 (Fig 1A) configured to move with a vessel wall by being configured to be “flexible or semi-flexible wiring” [0053, 0055 - 0056].
On pg 11, last paragraph – pg 12, paragraph 2 of their Remarks, Applicant asserts that the ring formed by wire strands (10, 12, 14, 16, 18, and 20) connected by electric conductive bridges 26 cannot be considered a “coil” under the plain meaning of the term as defined by thefreedictionary.com because it is not a series of connected spirals or concentric rings formed by gathering or winding. Examiner respectfully disagrees. The “coil” limitation was mapped to (10, 12, 14, 16, 18, and 20), which are shown as a series of spirals (having a hook at each distal end 8) connected by electrical conductive bridges 26 in Fig 1A.
On pg 12, paragraph 3 of their Remarks, Applicant asserts that the legs of Decant do not form a coil with an open center because they all converge at the head of the device. Examiner respectfully disagrees. The “coil” was mapped not only to the legs, but also to connective bridges 26 which work together with the legs to measure a strain acting thereon [0055 -0056] in a similar manner to Applicant’s claimed expandable and collapsible variable inductance coil.
On pg 12, paragraph 4 of their Remarks, Applicant asserts the legs of Decant are not configurable to move with the vessel wall in response to expansion and collapse of the vessel. Examiner respectfully disagrees. Examiner’s note: “configured to move with the vessel wall in response to expansion and collapse of the vessel” is interpreted as intended use, wherein if the apparatus is configurable for performing the claimed intended use, then it meets the limitations of the claim. In this instance, the prior art is configurable for performing the intended use because the legs connected to form a conductive circuit are designed to flex in response to an applied strain by being flexible [0053] in a similar manner the claimed inductance coil is configured to expand and contract to the applied force applied by a patient’s vessel wall.
