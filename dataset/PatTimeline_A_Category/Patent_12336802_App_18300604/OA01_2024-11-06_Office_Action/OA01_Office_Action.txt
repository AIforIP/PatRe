Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1-13 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Regarding claim 1, the phrase “magnetization preparation radio frequency (RF) pulse series” does not possess a well-established meaning in the art. It is suggested to supplement the claim language to indicate that the magnetization preparation radio frequency (RF) pulse series inverts a longitudinal magnetization while encoding T2 contrast in a subsequent TI recovery.
Regarding claim 1, it is unclear whether the inter-pulse duration is varied (such as the apparatus configured to vary the inter-pulse duration) or whether it is merely intended to define that the inter-pulse duration determines a degree of T2 weighting (implying that a change of the inter-pulse duration also changes the T2 weighting). Therefore, the claim is considered indefinite. Claims 2-13 are rejected for depending on claim 1.
Regarding claim 1, the claim discloses an “inter-pulse duration” of the T2-prepared inversion pulse series. The specification teaches that the inter-pulse duration is the time between the first and last (tip up and tip down) pulses. The claim language only states an “inter-pulse” duration which could mean a time between any of the pulses of the  T2-prepared inversion pulse series. Therefore, it is suggested to amend the claim language to correspond to the specification. Claims 2-13 are rejected for depending on claim 1.
Regarding claim 3, the claim discloses “the at least one MRI parameter”, which lacks antecedent basis. It is unclear if “the at least one MRI parameter” is the same or different from “the at least one qMRI parameter”. Claims 4-6 are rejected for depending on claim 3.
Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1-13 would be allowable if rewritten or amended to overcome the rejection(s) under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), 2nd paragraph, set forth in this Office action.
The following is a statement of reasons for the indication of allowable subject matter:
Regarding claim 1, the closest prior at is considered Nezafat (US 2023/0194641). Nezafat teaches quantitative parameter mapping using a T2 prep sequence [See Fig. 4]. However, Nezafat is at least silent in teaching “performing, by the MRI system, N scans C_1, . . . ,C_N, wherein each scan C_j, with j=1, . . . ,N, includes: performing at least one magnetization preparation radio frequency (RF) pulse series, each followed by M_j readout blocks, wherein two successive said magnetization preparation RF pulse series are separated by a repetition time interval TR_j, wherein each said magnetization preparation RF pulse series is a T2-prepared inversion pulse series containing multiple pulses and characterized by an echo time, being an inter-pulse duration, TE.sub.p_j, wherein the inter-pulse duration is configured for varying in order to obtain different T2 weightings; acquiring, by the MRI system and during each readout block, an MRI recovery signal generated by at least a part of the biological object, wherein for each said readout block R_i,j, an MRI signal S_i,j is acquired by the MRI system at a different inversion time TI_i,j, with i=1, . . . ,M_j, and M_j ≥ 2; reconstructing, for and from each said MRI signal S_i,j, an image l_i,j of the part; creating a voxel-wise signal by concatenating intensity values obtained for a same voxel for reconstructed images; and fitting a physical model to concatenated intensity values to obtain at least one qMRI map of the at least one qMRI parameter.”
See Fig. 6 of Nezafat which shows the input to the neural network, which is four T1 weighted images with corresponding inversion times. However, the pixel values of the four T1 weighted images do not seem to be concatenated. Further, the T2 weighted sequence in Fig. 4 does not teach different inversion times and multiple readouts after each T2 preparation sequence. Therefore, the claims overcome the most relevant prior art.
