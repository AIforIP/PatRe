Reasons for Allowance
Claims 21-36 are allowed.
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
The closest cited prior art reference of record to the instant invention is Ive (US 2024/0012601). Ive discloses a wearable head-mounted display for facilitating enhanced interaction where the wearer’s eyes is displayed on an outward-facing display. However, the head-mounted display of Ive does not disclose at least a video display having a variable transparency, an adjustable brightness to illuminate the eyes of the wearer, and adjusting the transparency or brightness so that an observer can see the eyes of the wearer through the display.
Saur (US 2018/0308451) describes a wearable LCD display with “LCD shutter technology” that can change the opacity of the LCD display. However, Saur the LCD display of Saur is inward-facing and Saur does not contemplate an outward-facing LCD display allowing an outside observer to see the wearer’s eyes.
Samec (US 2016/0270656) was relied upon for its disclosure of illuminating the eyes of the user. However, Samec illuminates the eyes of the user by directing a beam into the eye of the user. Samec does not contemplate illuminating the eyes of the user so that an outside observer can see the wearer’s eyes.
None of the cited prior art references of record, alone or in combination, anticipate or render obvious at least the limitations of “an outward facing video display comprising a transparency that is variable; and a light having an adjustable brightness to illuminate eyes of a wearer, wherein adjusting at least one of the transparency and the brightness allows an observer to see the eyes of the wearer through the outward facing video display” of independent claim 21, or the limitations of “an outward facing video display positioned within the eyeglass frames; and a controller coupled to the outward facing video display to control a display of media content on the video display, wherein a level of the media content is selectively adjustable such that the display of media content and eyes of a user are each selectively viewable by a patient when the therapeutic training device is worn by the user, wherein the outward facing video display is see-through from the side of the user.” of independent claim 27.
The Examiner notes that the term “level of media content” is explicitly defined by the instant specification publication paragraph [0041] as follows: “As used herein, the term “level of media content” is understood to describe a physical characteristic of media content which may be adjusted to increase or decrease a perceivable amount of the displayed media content on a therapeutic training device disclosed herein, including but not limited to an opacity, a brightness, or a transparency of the displayed media content.”
Therefore, no statutory rejections regarding subject-matter eligibility or prior art exist, and claims 21-36 are allowable.
