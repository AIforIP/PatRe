Reasons for Allowance
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
Page 13, lines 14-17 of the Applicant’s specification discloses, “…The system 1 comprises one or more adhesive monitoring devices for skin application, preferably monitoring patches…”. Accordingly, the adhesive monitoring device of the claim has been interpreted as comprising/corresponding to the recited “monitoring patch” and as such, includes all structural and functional features ascribed thereto.
With consideration to the disclosure of page 13, lines 14-17, the Examiner has determined that the prior art of record fails to teach, suggest or to render obvious:
The system and method of independent claims 1 and 14,
characterized by:
A system for monitoring condition and movements of at least one user, comprising:
- at least one adhesive monitoring device for skin application or at least one monitoring patch   applicable to the epidermis of a user to be monitored, the at least one monitoring patch comprising  an adhesive substrate for skin use in which are embedded or to which are bound at least:
- a sensor configured to detect at least one body temperature of the user,
- a memory module configured to store data, and
- a transceiver module configured to absorb supplied electrical energy from an   electromagnetic radiation received by an antenna, and
-a plurality of detecting devices each detecting device being located in correspondence with at   least an access gate to a sub-area of a plurality of sub- areas, the plurality of sub-areas defining a   monitoring area,
wherein each detecting device is configured to:
- detect presence of the at least one monitoring patch within a predetermined distance from the corresponding gate;
- activate a communication channel with the at least one detected monitoring patch, and   wherein the at least one monitoring patch is configured to:
- transmit a user’s body temperature data,  wherein  each detecting device is configured to:
- transmit to the at least one detected monitoring patch an identification code of the at least one   detecting device and a corresponding timestamp, and the at least one monitoring patch is configured to:
- store in the memory module the identification code and the corresponding timestamp received from each detecting device with which the communication channel has been established.

Conclusion
The prior art made of record and not relied upon is considered pertinent to applicant's disclosure.
FORSTER (US 2017/0091498)  teaches a monitoring patch in paragraph [0023], teaching, “The medical device can be a sensor, including a wearable sensor. The medical device can be any type of medical patch, including a monitoring patch”; also see [0057].  Paragraph [0030] teaches, “The power source can additionally be based on an electromagnetic energy harvesting mechanism “, thus teaching that the patch   absorbs supplied energy. Paragraph [0033] teaches, “The sensing patch can be attached to a patient through the use of a pressure sensitive adhesive, an activatable adhesive, or other fastening means such as a string or hook-and-loop fasteners (also known as VELCRO)” thus teaching  patch attachment using an adhesive. Paragraph [0024] teaches, “The medical patch can use one or more sensors. The sensing device can be included in a wound care bandage as well.” And paragraph [0025] teaches, “Physiological sensors can be a part of the medical device. The connection provides for either a time resolved or context based assessment of data from the various sensing elements.” Thus teaching that the patch includes sensors. Paragraph [0044] teaches, “The RFID device may include and/or store various types of data. For instance, the RFID device may include and/or store data such as when the medical device was first made and when it should be used by to ensure that it cannot be used unless it is safe to do so. The RFID device may contain manufacturing information about the medical device. The RFID device may have data stored in it relating to sensor values, either taken at the time of reading or relating to the history of the device; this data may be processed to provide a good/bad indication either in the RFID device or by the host system.” Thus teaching a memory for data storage. Paragraph [0052] teaches, “By reading a RFID device supplied with the monitoring patch, the smart phone can be paired with the patch to receive and transmit data”, thus teaching a transceiver. Paragraph [0026] teaches a physiological sensing device; while [0027] teaches monitoring physiological parameters including body temperature, thus teaching temperature measurement.
ERAMIAN (US 2016/0188840)   teaches a wearable tag in [0054], see tag 210A.  [0057] teaches opening a communication channel within a predefined distance, in teaching, “when device 210c is in proximity to wireless beacon 222, device 210c either receives signal 262c or emits signal 260c and opens a communication channel with wireless beacon 222.”
PORTER (US 2022/0101992)  teaches that an adhesive patch may provide temperature measurements. Paragraph [0011]  teaches, “In an embodiment, a sensor patch can be configured to sense, to determine, and to provide a clinical temperature. The sensor patch is adhered to skin so as to operate without dermal penetration and is capable of being held in place by an adhesive.” And teaches an operational distance for communication with the sensor patch in [0199] which discloses, “Operational distance between the smart device 440 and the sensor patch 901 can be, for example, in a range of approximately 30 cm or less, or 20 cm or less, or 1 cm or less.” [0382] teaches that the patch transmits an identification signal in disclosing,
“The data is always encoded (and may be encrypted) to include an identifier unique to the individual patient (“subject”) wearing the patch so that the system can monitor consecutive temperature…”).
STOFFER (US 2016/0307437) Paragraph [0048] teaches a wearable tag (16) in communication with a door control unit (12), said tag sending an identification signal to the door unit (12).
AYERS (US 2023/0245770) teaches a receiver which transmits an identification signal to tag. Paragraph [0068] teaches that the location or receiver ID may be transmitted to the tag 12, which then communicates the receiver ID and the tag ID to the controller 28 for processing.
ITO (US 2020/022226) teaches in [0036] a tag which transmits temperature data and identification data of a medicine container to which it is attached.
