Applicant’s arguments, filed 05/14/2021, have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
Rejection of claims 1 and 13 under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) – Zhang et al. (US Patent No. 8,855,755)
Applicant argues that Zhang does not teach or suggest the elements of claims 1 and 13. Applicant claims Zhang does not disclose or suggest
for each of the plurality of ventricular depolarizations, identify[ing] a maximum slope point, a minimum slope point, and an interval from the maximum slope point to the minimum slope point (see Page 2, lines 9-13 of the 05/14/2021 response), and
for each of the plurality of ventricular depolarizations…an interval from the maximum slope point to the minimum slope point” (see Page 2, lines 21 -25 of the 05/14/2021 response) as recited in claim 1.
Examiner respectfully disagrees. Zhang teaches it is known to identify a maximum slope point, a minimum slope point, and an interval from the maximum slope point to the minimum slope point for each of the plurality of ventricular depolarizations (e.g. Col. 11, lines 7-14: the processor is analyzing 9 intervals from the 10 most recent intervals, which means that each of the intervals are analyzed; Col. 13, lines 61-63: slope is used for morphology analysis; Fig. 10: morphology analysis is conducted for multiple cardiac events when sudden rate onset is detected; Col. 19, lines 22-67 – Col. 20, lines 1-5: performing morphology analysis for multiple cardiac intervals). Zhang further teaches it is known to identify an interval from the maximum slope point to the minimum slope point for each of the plurality of ventricular depolarizations (e.g. Col. 15, lines 46-63: the intervals between each beat are identified). Due to the reasons discussed above, the rejection of claims 1 and 13 under 35 U.S.C. 102 is maintained.
