Response to Arguments
The declarations of Dr. Ronald Berger and Shantanu Sarkar under 37 CFR 1.132 filed 02/24/2023 is insufficient to overcome the rejection of claims 1-31 under 35 U.S.C. 101 as set forth in the last Office action because:
The declarations of both Dr. Berger and inventor Shantanu Sarkar explain how the invention of claim 1 is an improvement to current PVC detection technology. In his declaration, Mr. Sarkar explains how the claimed invention is an improvement over current technology by allowing long term cardiac monitoring using an insertable cardiac monitor and using interval detection and beat-to-beat- morphological detection such as slope points, while overcoming limitations inherent in insertable cardiac monitoring devices, such as battery life. Further, the invention was developed to result in both high specificity (e.g. detecting non-PVCs as non-PVC) and high sensitivity (e.g. detecting PVCs as PVCs). Dr. Berger, in his declaration, explains how the claimed invention represents an improvement in PVC detection by allowing long term monitoring of PVC through the insertable cardiac monitor that can assist physicians in the diagnosis of PVCs through the claimed analysis.
However, the rejection of the claims under 35 U.S.C. 101 is being maintained. As recited, the claims require electrodes, processing circuitry, and communication circuitry. The processing circuitry is configured to identify ventricular depolarizations, identify maximum and minimum slope points and an interval from the maximum to the minimum slope point, determine that the intervals satisfy slope criteria and determine PVC based on the intervals meeting the slope criteria, and determine a PVC detection quantification. The communication circuitry is configured to transmit the PVC quantification information to a user computing device. As claimed, the steps performed by the processing circuitry are considered to be data analysis steps, and the steps performed by the communication circuitry are considered to be data output steps. Therefore, the claims are directed towards an abstract idea. Under its broadest reasonable interpretation, the limitation of identifying PVCs is a process that can be performed in the mind but for the recitation of electrodes, circuitry, and the device being an insertable cardiac device. These additional elements do not integrate the abstract idea into a practical application because they are recited at a high level of generality (i.e. as generic electrodes performing the generic function of data gathering (e.g. sensing cardiac electrogram), and as generic processing and communication circuitry to perform the generic function of identifying slope and correlation, determining if the slope and correlation meet certain criteria, and transmitting the data to a user) such that they amount to no more than mere instructions to apply the exception using generic computer components. Further, it is known to use insertable cardiac devices for cardiac monitoring, as explained in the office action below. Therefore, the rejection of the claims under 35 U.S.C. 101 is being maintained.
Applicant's arguments filed 02/24/2023 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
Rejection of claims 1-31 under 35 U.S.C. 101
Claims 13-22 and 28 have been cancelled, and new claims 32-40 have been added. Independent claims 1, 23, 32, and 37 recite a medical system and insertable cardiac monitor for identifying premature ventricular contraction (PVC) based on slope intervals and correlation values between a current ventricular depolarization, a preceding adjacent ventricular depolarization, and a subsequent adjacent ventricular depolarization meeting predefined slope or correlation criteria; determining a PVC detection quantification; and communication circuitry that transmits PVC detection quantification data to a user computing device or a network. Applicant argues (see Remarks, pages 13-33) that claim 1 does not recite an abstract idea and that claim 1 integrates the judicial exception into a practical application of detecting PVC, and therefore constitutes significantly more than the judicial exception. Examiner respectfully disagrees.
As recited, the claims are directed to an abstract idea without significantly more. The claims recite a system for identifying premature ventricular contraction (PVC) based on slope intervals and correlation values between a current ventricular depolarization, a preceding adjacent ventricular depolarization, and a subsequent adjacent ventricular depolarization meeting predefined slope or correlation criteria; determining a PVC detection quantification; and communication circuitry that transmits PVC detection quantification data to a user computing device. The limitation of identifying premature ventricular contraction, as drafted, is a process that, under its broadest reasonable interpretation, covers performance of the limitation in the mind, but for the recitation of generic sensors (e.g. electrodes), processing and communication circuitry, and the device being an insertable cardiac monitor with a housing. For example, identifying PVC can include identifying ventricular depolarizations, a maximum and minimum slope point, an interval from the maximum slope point to the minimum slope point (e.g. Fig. 6 of the instant specification depicts a graph containing a cardiac electrogram including PVC depolarizations. Based on Fig. 6, it would be feasible for a user to visually determine and locate maximum and minimum slope points and an interval from the maximum and minimum slope point), and identify PVC if the slope intervals satisfy predetermined slope criteria. Determining a PVC detection quantification can include a healthcare professional quantifying the amount of PVC, and communicating it to the patient or another user. The claims only recite the additional elements of electrodes, processing circuitry, communication circuitry, and the device being an insertable cardiac monitor with a housing. The additional elements are recited at a high level of generality (i.e. as generic electrodes performing the generic function of data gathering (e.g. sensing cardiac electrogram)), and as generic processing and communication circuitry to perform the generic function of identifying slope and correlation, determining if then slope and correlation meet certain criteria, and transmitting the data to a user) such that they amount to no more than mere instructions to apply the exception using generic computer components. The limitation of an insertable cardiac medical device with a housing having a length greater than the width and depth with electrodes positioned at the outer surface is known in the prior art, as explained below. Furthermore, the system provides no means of improving diagnosis or drug dosage or changing any stimulation or therapy parameters (as described in Applicant’s Specification in par. [0050]). See MPEP 2106.05(b) and (f). Accordingly, the additional elements do not integrate the abstract idea into a practical application, and are not patent eligible. Therefore, the rejection of claims 1-12, 23-27, and 29-40 under 35 U.S.C. 101 is maintained.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Claims 1-12, 23-27, and 29-40 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because the claimed invention is directed to an abstract idea without significantly more. Claims 1, 23, 32 and 37 recite a system and insertable cardiac monitor for identifying premature ventricular contraction (PVC) based on slope intervals and correlation values between a current ventricular depolarization, a preceding adjacent ventricular depolarization, and a subsequent adjacent ventricular depolarization meeting predefined slope or correlation criteria; determining a PVC detection quantification; and communication circuitry that transmits PVC detection quantification data to a user computing device or server. To determine whether a claim satisfies the criteria for subject matter eligibility, the claim is evaluated according to a stepwise process as described in MPEP 2106(III) and 2106.03-2106.05.  The instant claims are evaluated according to such analysis.
Step 1: Is the claim to a process, machine, manufacture or composition of matter?
Claims 1, 23, 32, and 37 are directed towards a system and insertable cardiac monitor, and thus meet the requirements for step 1.
Step 2A (Prong 1): Does the claim recite an abstract idea, law of nature, or natural phenomenon?
Claims 1, 23, 32, and 37 recite a system for identifying premature ventricular contraction (PVC) based on slope intervals and correlation values between a current ventricular depolarization, a preceding adjacent ventricular depolarization, and a subsequent adjacent ventricular depolarization meeting predefined slope or correlation criteria, and determining a PVC detection quantification based on the determination that the ventricular depolarizations are PVC, and communication circuitry that transmits PVC detection quantification data to a user computing device. If a claim limitation, under its broadest reasonable interpretation, covers performance of the limitation in the mind but for the recitation of generic computer components, then it falls within the “Mental Processes” grouping of abstract ideas. Therefore, claims 1, 23, 32, and 37 recite an abstract idea of a mental process.
Claims 1, 23, 32, and 37 recite the abstract idea of a mental process. The limitation of a system and insertable cardiac monitor for identifying premature ventricular contraction, as drafted in claims 1, 23, 32, and 37, under its broadest reasonable interpretation, covers performance of the steps the system performs in the mind, but for the recitation of generic sensors (i.e. electrodes), processing and communication circuitry, and a server and network. For example, identifying PVC can include identifying ventricular depolarizations, a maximum and minimum slope point, an interval from the maximum slope point to the minimum slope point (e.g. Fig. 6 of the instant specification depicts a graph containing a cardiac electrogram including PVC depolarizations. Based on Fig. 6, it would be feasible for a user to visually determine and locate maximum and minimum slope points and an interval from the maximum and minimum slope point), and identifying PVC if the slope intervals satisfy predetermined slope criteria. Determining a PVC detection quantification can include a healthcare professional quantifying the amount of PVC, and communicating it to the patient or another user. Other than reciting electrodes processing and communication circuitry, a server and network, and the device being an insertable cardiac monitor with a housing, nothing in the elements of the claims precludes the step from practically being performed in the mind.
Step 2A (Prong 2): Does the claim recite additional elements that integrate the judicial exception into a practical application?
Claims 1, 23, 32, and 37 recite the additional elements of a “plurality of electrodes”, “processing circuitry”, and “communication circuitry”, claims 32 and 37 recite an “insertable cardiac monitor comprising: a housing configured for subcutaneous implantation within a patient, the housing having a length, a width, and a depth, wherein the length is greater than each of the width and the depth”, and a “network”, and claim 37 recites a “server”. However, these elements are recited at a high level of generality (i.e. as generic electrodes performing the generic function of data gathering (e.g. sensing cardiac electrogram), and as generic processing and communication circuitry and a server and network to perform the function of identifying slope and correlation, determining if the slope and correlation meet certain criteria, and transmitting the data to a user) such that they amount to no more than mere instructions to apply the exception using generic computer components.
Further, the limitation of an insertable cardiac medical device with a housing having a length greater than the width and depth with electrodes positioned at the outer surface is known, as disclosed by Hou et al., US Patent Application Publication 2014/0107723 (e.g. Fig. 3A: implantable device that has a length greater than the width and depth with electrodes 318 on the outside); Bornzin et al., US Patent Application Publication 2015/0335894 (e.g. Par. [0035]: electrodes may be located on the housing of the implantable device; Fig. 10: implantable device that has a length greater than the width and depth); Bornzin et al., US Patent 9,427,594 (e.g. Fig. 5: implantable device that has a length greater than the width and depth with electrode 520 attached to the housing; Col. 14, lines 37-45); Sarkar, US Patent Application Publication 2016/0310031 (e.g. Par. [0024]: “insertable cardiac monitor 300 is defined by a length L, a width W and thickness or depth D and is in the form of an elongated rectangular prism wherein the length L is much larger than the width W, which in turn is larger than the depth D”; Fig. 2: implantable device that has a length greater than the width and depth with electrodes 304 and 306 attached to the housing).
Furthermore, the system provides no means of improving diagnosis or drug dosage or changing any stimulation or therapy parameters (as described in Applicant’s Specification in par. [0050]). See MPEP 2106.05(b) and (f).
Accordingly, the additional elements do not integrate the abstract idea into a practical application.
Step 2B: Does the claim recite additional elements that amount to significantly more than the judicial exception?
The additional elements when considered individually and in combination are not enough to qualify as significantly more than the abstract idea. As discussed above with respect to integration of the abstract idea into a practical application, the additional elements of using electrodes and processing and communication circuitry to perform the steps of identifying cardiac electrogram and determining PVC amounts to no more than mere instructions to apply the exception using generic computer components.  Mere instructions to apply an exception using a generic computer component cannot provide an inventive concept. Therefore, the claims are not patent eligible.
While there are no prior art rejections for claims 1-12, 23-27, and 29-40, they are not indicated as allowable due to the rejection under 35 U.S.C. 101.
