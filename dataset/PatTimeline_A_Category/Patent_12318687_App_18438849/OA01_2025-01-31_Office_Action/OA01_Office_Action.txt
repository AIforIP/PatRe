Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 21-39 are allowed.
With respect to claim 21, claim 21 is allowed since Sato (US 2005/0117761) does not teach “determin[ing] a virtualization, of one or both of the first type of audio signal and the second type of audio signal, according to a head related transfer function; [and] generat[ing] a stereo signal by combining the first type of audio signal and the second type of audio signal according to the desired balance, the desired volume, and the virtualization”. The following serves to distinguish the teachings of Sato from the instant invention.
Sato teaches a game system that allows a user to set the volume of an in-game speech signal from another player (¶¶[0078]-[0113]). For example, during game play, a user may set the volume of the volume of the voice of a player B (¶¶[0078]-[0100]). Thereafter, the voice of player B – with its volume set according to the user’s input – is synthesized with an in-game sound, and the synthesized signal is provided to the user (¶¶[0078]-[0113]). Thus, Sato does teach “[a]n audio device comprising: a plurality of input controls; mixing circuitry; a left speaker; and a right speaker; wherein the mixing circuitry is configured to: determine a desired balance, between a first type of audio signal and a second type of audio signal, according to the plurality of input controls; determine a desired volume, of the first type of audio signal and the second type of audio signal, according to the plurality of input controls . . . output a left channel of the stereo signal to the left speaker; and output a right channel of the stereo signal to the right speaker”; however, Sato does not teach “determin[ing] a virtualization, of one or both of the first type of audio signal and the second type of audio signal, according to a head related transfer function; [and] generat[ing] a stereo signal by combining the first type of audio signal and the second type of audio signal according to the desired balance, the desired volume, and the virtualization”. While Sato does make mention of processing a sound signal with transfer functions to add sound localization (¶¶[0038]-[0044]), Sato is silent to the determination of a virtualization based on a head related transfer function. Munoz et al. (US 2021/0006921), hereinafter referred to as Munoz, attempts to cure the deficiencies of Sato by teach the application of a head related transfer function for binaural rendering (¶[0087]); however, Munoz does not teach the determination of a virtualization and combining a first audio signal and a second audio signal according to the virtualization.
Claim 21 is therefore allowed. Claim 31 is allowed for similar reasons as claim 21. Claims 22-30 and 32-39 are allowed for their dependencies on claims 21 and 31.
