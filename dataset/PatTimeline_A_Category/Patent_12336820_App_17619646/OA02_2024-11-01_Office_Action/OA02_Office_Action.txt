Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Claims 1-4 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because
the claimed invention is directed to an abstract idea without significantly more. The claim(s) recite(s) a method of assessing an impact of a test perfume on the well-bing of human subjects. This judicial exception is not integrated into a practical application because claims X-Y recite an abstract idea of
a) assessing an impact of a test perfume on well-being by having a human subject assess their well-being by scoring a plurality of attributes, a mental process, where each attribute has a weight, which includes formulas and math
b) calculating a base well-being score in the absence of a test perfume by multiplying the score of each attribute by weight to form a product and summing these products, includes formulas and math;
c) repeating the above steps a) and b) in the presence of test perfume to determine a well-being score with the test perfume, which includes mental process, formulas, and math; and
d) determining the impact of the test perfume by subtracting the base well-being score from the test perfume score, which includes formulas and math.
Further, the abstract idea has not been integrated into a particular practical application because after the impact of the test perfume there is no action taken and, therefore, there is no practical application.  The use of the test-perfume is used only to gather data, which constitutes mere data gathering and data gathering to be used in the abstract idea is insignificant-post solution activity and not a particular practical application (see MPEP 2106.05(g)).
The claim(s) does/do not include additional elements that are sufficient to amount to significantly more than the judicial exception because the only recitation outside of the abstract ideas is the use of a test perfume.  Using a perfume is well-understood, routine and conventional.  Therefore, the claims do not amount to significantly more.
With regards to claim 2, the claim only adds the weighted factors for determining well-being.  This claim still involves an abstract idea and formulas/math.
With regards to claim 3, the claim adds selecting a perfume composition that has a positive impact.  This includes the abstract idea of determining what is a positive impact as well as formulas/math.
With regards to claim 4, the claims adds creating a new perfume having such an impact on the wellbeing.  This includes the abstract idea of determining what is a positive impact as well as formulas/math.
With regards to claim 5, the claims add the odor attributes to be used for the test perfume.  This includes the abstract idea of determining what is a positive impact as well as formulas/math.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claim 5 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Regarding claim 5, the phrase preferably renders the claim indefinite because it is unclear whether the limitations following the phrase are part of the claimed invention.  See MPEP § 2173.05(d).
A broad range or limitation together with a narrow range or limitation that falls within the broad range or limitation (in the same claim) may be considered indefinite if the resulting claim does not clearly set forth the metes and bounds of the patent protection desired. See MPEP § 2173.05(c). In the present instance, claim 5 recites the broad recitation that the odor attributes being a list of scent characteristics, and the claim also recites that the list is preferably a more narrow list of characteristics. which is the narrower statement of the range/limitation. The claim(s) are considered indefinite because there is a question or doubt as to whether the feature introduced by such narrower language is (a) merely exemplary of the remainder of the claim, and therefore not required, or (b) a required feature of the claims.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claims 1-5 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by Prigge (WO 2018/206297).
With regards to claims 1 and 2, Prigge teaches a novel fragrance composition for enhancing the mood of a human subject (abstract) that includes selecting the emotion you felt while smelling a scent wherein more then one emotion can be selected and indicating the intensity with which you experienced the emotion on a chart (abstract and figure 1).  The emotions include, for example stress (page 2).
With regards to claims 3 and 4, Prigge teaches the fragrance ingredients to be selected based on the chart.
With regards to claim 5, Prigge teaches the fragrance compositions to include, for example, orange oil (page 13).
