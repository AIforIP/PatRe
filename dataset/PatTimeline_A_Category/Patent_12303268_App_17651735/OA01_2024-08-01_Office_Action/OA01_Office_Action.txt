Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101
Claims 1-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because the claimed invention is directed to non-statutory subject matter.  The claim(s) does/do not fall within at least one of the four categories of patent eligible subject matter because the invention as claimed recites a series of mental steps (claims 11-16) and the use of a generic computer with a machine learning model (claims 1-10 )  or generic media (claims 17 to 20) to store the mental processes of gathering data and performing a mental process of applying data to a mental  process of adjusting an output (opinion)  based upon the learning process of the human mind, that is mimicking a neural network of the human mind e.g., a naturally occurring process to refine the opinion of the data input.
The claimed invention is directed to a judicial  process without significantly more. The claim(s) recite(s) mental steps of gathering data using a naturally occurring process ( the human mind e.g., neural network) to evaluate and refine the data to update an opinion of the observer. This judicial exception is not integrated into a practical application because as implied in the July 2024  update concerning eligibility under 101 for machine learning models, the use of a “black box”  machine learning model with in particular novel details to distinguish eligibility is not enough.  The claim(s) does/do not include additional elements that are sufficient to amount to significantly more than the judicial exception because the claims recite a generic training algorithm relying upon convention (user) feedback input to the evaluation device.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim(s) 1-20 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Alcaide et al. US 2022/0187912. Alcaide makes use of a machine learning network for evaluating patient data and particularly for evaluating mental fatigue. Applicant recites a method of the training of a machine learning model using the conventional steps of gathering body senor data, receiving user input concerning of their mental state along with body sensor data that is indicative of mental fatigue, and providing a machine learning for use in treating the condition. It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art to assemble to the mental fatigue training model of Alcaide using conventional  data gathering  and patient feedback steps involved in conventional machine learning processes. The examiner invites Applicant  to demonstrate any other method to accomplish the result desired by Alcaide other that using the conventional Ai training asserted by the examiner.
