Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
In the event the determination of the status of the application as subject to AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 (or as subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103) is incorrect, any correction of the statutory basis (i.e., changing from AIA  to pre-AIA ) for the rejection will not be considered a new ground of rejection if the prior art relied upon, and the rationale supporting the rejection, would be the same under either status.
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
(a)(2) the claimed invention was described in a patent issued under section 151, or in an application for patent published or deemed published under section 122(b), in which the patent or application, as the case may be, names another inventor and was effectively filed before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claims 21, 29-31, 33, and 34 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by Diamantopoulos (U.S. Pub. No. 2005/0148903 A1).
Regarding claim 21, Diamantopoulos discloses a medical device (Abstract; Figs. 2-2a), comprising: a shaft 14 and an assembly coupled to a distal end of the shaft, the assembly including a plurality of arms 11 circumferentially spaced apart from one another about the distal end and a sensor 10/12 coupled to each of the plurality of arms; wherein the plurality of arms are configured to move relative to the distal end between a first position (Fig. 2a) and a second position (Fig. 2); wherein, in the first position, the plurality of arms are in an undeployed configuration such that each of the plurality of arms lie substantially flush relative to the distal end, and in the second position, the plurality of arms are released to a deployed configuration such that each of the plurality of arms extends radially outwards relative to the distal end ([0113]-[0118]; [0121]-[0125]).
Regarding claim 29, Diamantopoulos discloses that each of the plurality of arms includes a proximal side and a distal side, and each of the plurality of arms has a preset shape with a concave curvature along the distal side and a convex curvature along the proximal side (Fig. 3; [0044]-[0045]; [0133]).
Regarding claim 30, Diamantopoulos discloses that the plurality of arms are biased towards at least one of the undeployed configuration and the deployed configuration ([0127]).
Regarding claim 31, Diamantopoulos discloses that the plurality of arms are configured to passively move between the first position and the second position in response to the shaft moving relative to a bodily lumen and one or more surrounding tissue surfaces of the bodily lumen contacting the plurality of arms ([0127]-[0129]).
Regarding claim 33, Diamantopoulos discloses an actuator configured to move the plurality of arms between the first position and the second position in response to activating the actuator ([0136]).
Regarding claim 34, Diamantopoulos discloses that the plurality of arms are configured to stabilize the shaft within a bodily lumen by engaging surrounding tissue surfaces of the bodily lumen and centralizing a field of view within the bodily lumen when in the deployed configuration ([0041]; [0045]; [0129]).
Claims 21-23, 36, and 40 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2) as being anticipated by Sartor et al. (U.S. Pub. No. 2017/0280970 A1; hereinafter known as “Sartor”).
Regarding claim 21, Sartor discloses a medical device (Abstract; Figs. 2-4), comprising: a shaft 302c and an assembly coupled to a distal end of the shaft, the assembly including a plurality of arms 304a/b circumferentially spaced apart from one another about the distal end and a sensor 306/308/310/312 coupled to each of the plurality of arms; wherein the plurality of arms are configured to move relative to the distal end between a first position and a second position, wherein, in the first position, the plurality of arms are in an undeployed configuration such that each of the plurality of arms lie substantially flush relative to the distal end, and in the second position, the plurality of arms are released to a deployed configuration such that each of the plurality of arms extends radially outwards relative to the distal end ([0055]-[0058]; while the arms are not on the exterior surface of the distal end as in the present application, this is not required by the present claim language; the arms of Sartor are spaced from each other about an interior circumference of the distal end and lie flush in the first position).
Regarding claim 22, Sartor discloses that in the first position, the sensor on each of the plurality of arms faces radially inwards towards the distal end and is in an inoperable state for emitting light and detecting a light intensity resulting from the emitted light ([0055]-[0058]; the inner surfaces 304a1/b1 face each other in the first position).
Regarding claim 23, Sartor discloses that in the second position, the sensor on each of the plurality of arms faces radially outwards from the distal end and is in an operable state for emitting light and detecting the light intensity resulting from the emitted light (Fig. 3; [0056]).
Regarding claim 36, Sartor discloses a medical device (Abstract; Figs. 2-4), comprising a shaft having a distal end 302c for deployment within a bodily lumen and an assembly coupled to the distal end and including two or more arms 304a/b each having a sensor 306/308/310/312, the two or more arms being configured to move relative to the distal end between a first position and a second position; wherein, the two or more arms are positioned substantially flush with the distal end in an undeployed configuration when in the first position, such that the sensor on each of the two or more arms is inoperable for emitting light and detecting light intensity; wherein, the two or more arms are positioned distally relative to the distal end in a deployed configuration when in the second position, such that the sensor on each of the two or more arms is operable to emit light and detect a light intensity from within the bodily lumen ([0055]-[0058]).
Regarding claim 40, Sartor discloses a medical device (Abstract; Figs. 2-4), comprising: a shaft and an assembly coupled to a distal end 302c of the shaft, and including a plurality of arms 304a/b that are flexibly movable relative to the distal end; wherein, in a first position, the assembly is undeployed with the plurality of arms positioned laterally inwards towards the distal end such that a sensor 306/308/310/312 on each of the plurality of arms faces inwards towards the distal end; and wherein, in a second position, the assembly is deployed with the plurality of arms positioned laterally outwards from the distal end such that the sensor on each of the plurality of arms faces outwards away from the distal end ([0055]-[0058]).
Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 24-28, 32, 35, and 37-39 are objected to as being dependent upon a rejected base claim, but would be allowable if rewritten in independent form including all of the limitations of the base claim and any intervening claims.
The following is a statement of reasons for the indication of allowable subject matter: regarding claims 24-28, 37, and 38, none of the prior art of record teaches or reasonably suggests that such an assembly includes a cuff having a proximal end being configured to slidably attach over the distal end of the shaft to couple the assembly to the shaft, in combination with the other recited components.  Regarding claim 32, none of the prior art of record teaches or reasonably suggests that such arms move from the undeployed configuration to the deployed configuration in response to the shaft moving distally relative to the bodily lumen, and from the deployed configuration to the undeployed configuration in response to the shaft moving proximally relative to the bodily lumen, in combination with the other recited components.  The arms of the cited references are either manually moved or move due to being biased.  Regarding claims 35 and 39, none of the prior art of record teaches or reasonably suggests such a controller configured to calculate separate perfusion indices corresponding to a light intensity measured by the detector over time as reflected off of bodily tissue from light originating at the emitter of the same sensor, in combination with the other recited components.
