Response to Arguments
Applicant’s arguments in combination with amendments, see Remarks and Claims, filed 01/08/2024, with respect to the rejection(s) of claim(s) 1-15 under 35 USC 103 have been fully considered.
On page 7, the applicant argues that the Tang reference does not disclose “acquiring physiological characteristics related to one or both eyes of the examinee”. On page 8, the applicant argues that Rao discloses a deep brain stimulation device that is implanted in the patient []. This argument is fully considered and is persuasive. Therefore, the rejection has been withdrawn.  However, upon further consideration, a new ground(s) of rejection is made in view of the following:
Claims 1, 3-9 and 11-15 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over US Pat Pub No 20180046773 granted to Tang et al (hereinafter “Tang” – previously presented) in view of US Pat Pub No. 20080243005 granted to Jung et al. (hereinafter “Jung”).
Claim(s) 10 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Tang as applied by Jung to claims 1-9 and 11-15 above, and further in view of US Pat Pub No. 20190113973 granted to Coleman et al. (hereinafter “Coleman” – previously presented).
Beginning on the bottom of page 7, the applicant argues that Tang can never teach or suggest the limitation of “the diagnosis module configured to receive the analysis report for assisting the examiner for diagnosis and send a diagnosis notification to the user module, wherein the diagnosis notification is returned to the intelligent calculation module, and the intelligent calculation module is trained by the diagnosis notification based on the examination items.” This argument is fully considered and is not persuasive. Tang discloses having a prediction module of the analysis engine that is utilized to help the patient and doctor to estimate the health condition of the patient (para 0053) and wherein the analysis engine further includes a learning module that will update the prediction module over time (para 0057, etc.). Therefore, Tang discloses, suggests and renders obvious the limitation recited above. See detail below under 103 rejection.
It is noted that the limitations that are amended to be included in the current independent claim, from dependent claims 2, 5, and 15, were not previously presented together and a new ground of rejection that is necessitated by applicant’s amendment of the claims.
Claim Objections
Claim 1 is objected to because of the following informalities: Claim 1 recites “the intelligent calculation module is trained by the diagnosis notification based on the examination items”. This limitation should be changed to clarify that the training has been performed prior to diagnosis and/or the system allows for further training. Appropriate correction is required.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 5 and 15 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Claim 5 recites the limitation “physiological characteristics” (plural) in line 2; claim 5 depends from claims 1 and 3 which recite “physiological characteristic” (singular). It is unclear whether the “characteristics” of claim 5 include the one of claim 1 or not.
Claim 15 recites the limitation “wherein the intelligent calculation module is trained by the diagnosis notification based on at least one of the response message and the physiological characteristic signal.” Claim 15 depends from claim 1 which requires the “intelligent calculation module is trained by the diagnosis notification based on the examination items”. Therefore, it is unclear and indefinite whether the training of claim 15 replaces the training of claim 1 or is configured to further train the intelligent calculation module based on the response message and the physiological characteristics signal.

A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.

Claims 1, 3-9 and 11-15 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over US Pat Pub No 20180046773 granted to Tang et al (hereinafter “Tang” – previously presented) in view of US Pat Pub No. 20080243005 granted to Jung et al. (hereinafter “Jung”).
Regarding claim 1, Tang discloses a neurological disorders decision support system (para 0032, “medical system 100”, para 0099 “disease is related to neurology”) for assisting an examiner to diagnose an examinee (para 0048 “diagnosis to the user”), the neurological disorders decision support system comprising: a user module comprising an indication unit and an interface unit (para 0032, “interaction interface includes an output interface and input device”), the indication unit connected to the interface unit (para 0032) and configured to issue an inquiry to the examinee according to a first indication signal (para 0039 “information displayed on the interaction interface 140”, “asks for symptoms”), and the interface unit configured for the examinee returning a response message according to the inquiry (para 0039 “enters his/her input on the interaction interface”) and acquiring a physiological characteristic related to the examinee to generate at least one of a physiological characteristic signal (para 0039 “receiving the initial symptom Sini according to user's manipulation”); a screening module connected to the user module (para 0032 “analysis engine”), the screening module configured toit is noted that the claim as recited only requires either the limitation that come prior to after the “or”.), and the screening module is configured to output the response message and the physiological characteristic signal (fig. 2), an intelligent calculation module connected to the screening module (para 0035 “the analysis engine 120”), the intelligent calculation modulediagnosis notification to the user module (para 0053 “prediction module 124”), wherein the diagnosis notification is returned to the intelligent calculation module, and the intelligent calculation module is trained by the diagnosis notification based on the examination items (para 0007, 0055, 0060 updating the “learning module”).
Tang discloses analyzing the user’s symptoms but fails to explicitly disclose physiological characteristic related to one or both eyes; and wherein the neurological examination application program provides a plurality of examination items, and selects one or more examination items from the plurality of examination items based on the response message.
Jung, from a similar field of endeavor teaches a similar system wherein obtaining user-health data; selecting at least one user-health test function (i.e., examination items) at least partly based on the user-health data (para 0031, 0032, etc.). One or more visual field test functions are performed including one or more eye movement test functions, one or more pupil movement test functions, etc. in performing functions of cranial nerve test (para 0069, 0085-0088). It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to modify the disclosure of Tang with the teachings of Jung to provide the predictable result performing cranial nerve test.
Regarding claim 3, Tang as modified by Jung renders obvious the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 1, Tang discloses wherein the interface unit is an input element and a detection element, the input element providing at least one of an input basis data and a medical history data of the examinee, and the detection element passively or actively detects the examinee to obtain at least one of the physiological characteristic and a consciousness state of the examinee (para 0033 “the interaction interface 140 includes an input device (e.g., a touch panel, a keyboard, a microphone, a scanner or a flash memory reader) for user to type text commands, give voice commands or to upload some related data (e.g., images, medical records, or personal examination reports)”).
Regarding claim 4, Tang as modified by Jung renders obvious the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 3, wherein the detection element detects a spontaneous physiological response of the examinee or the detection element detects a physiological response generated by inducing the examinee to generate the physiological characteristic signal and confirm the consciousness state (para 0039 “interaction interface receives initial symptoms”; it is noted that the limitations that follow “or” are considered not as optional).
Regarding claim 5, Tang as modified by Jung renders obvious the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 3, wherein the detection element is configured to detect the physiological characteristics of the examinee with nystagmus related to one or both eyes of the examinee (Jung, para 0125 eye movement test, 0137-0154 “nystagmus”).
Regarding claim 6, Tang as modified by Jung renders obvious the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 3, wherein the detection element is configured to detect the physiological characteristics of at least one of a cornea, an iris, a pupil, a sclera, a conjunctiva, a retina, a choroid, a periocular skin area of the eye and a head tilt angle of the examinee (Jung, para 0125 “eye movement test, pupil movement, etc.”).
Regarding claim 7, Tang as modified by Jung renders obvious the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 1, wherein the physiological characteristic signal comes from at least one of an eyeball image, an eye image, an eyeball blood vessel flow rate, an eyeball fluid volume, a brain wave, an electromyography, a heart rate, a skin water content, a periocular skin blood vessel flow rate, a body impedance, a hearing and a body temperature (Jung, para 0125 “eye movement test, pupil movement, etc.”, para 0133 “user-health data 116 may be obtained through a camera in place as a user monitoring device 182 that can monitor the eye movements of the user during interaction with the application 104.”).
Regarding claim 8, Tang as modified by Jung renders obvious the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 1, wherein the examination items are at least one of a consciousness assessment, a coma index, an Alert Vocal Pain Unresponsive (AVPU) method, a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), a Dizziness Handicap Inventory (DHI), a light reflex test, an eye movement, a facial information collection, a facial nerve assessment, a corneal reflex, a blink reflex, a head eye reflex, a Cincinnati Prehospital Stroke Scale and a U.S. National Institute of Health Stroke Scale (Tang, para 0168-0171 discussing diagnosing dizziness, seizures, etc. which are considered to be a level of consciousness states).
Regarding claim 9, Tang as modified by Jung renders obvious the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 8, wherein the examination items are combined into an examination group and configured for being chosen by the neurological examination application program (Tang, fig. 5; Jung, para 0032, figs 1 or 2).
Regarding claim 11, Tang as modified by Jung renders obvious the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 1, further comprising an identity verification unit connected to the screening module for verifying the identity of the examinee (Tang para 0034, “smart phone”, it is noted that mobile devices, cell phones, include an unlock function which allows access to the device/app upon verifying the identity of the owner. Therefore, the identity verification unit is inherently included; Jung, para 0207 “identification application”).
Regarding claim 12, Tang as modified by Jung renders obvious the neurological disorders decision support system in claim 1, wherein the algorithm is at least one of a locking algorithm, an adaptive algorithm, a machine learning algorithm and a deep learning algorithm (Tang, para 0035, fig. 4).
Regarding claim 13, Tang as modified by Jung renders obvious the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 12, wherein the algorithm calculates a dynamic eyeball image (it is noted that neither the claim nor specification provides any details on what “calculates a dynamic eyeball image” is and how it is calculated. As best understood, this limitation only requires analyzing the image of the eye), and correspondingly obtains at least one of a nystagmus waveform, a gain value (gain), a phase, a peak velocity, an accuracy, a duration, a phase velocity, a latency, an overshoot, an undershoot and a total harmonic distortion from the dynamic eyeball image, and then captures a feature value (Jung, beginning on para 0134 teaches performing eye movement test function and eye tracking data from a video, pupil movement, para 0137 tracking quick/slow phase,, also see paras 0135-0154, etc.), and the feature value is calculated by the machine learning algorithm to determine a disease type so that the disease type is displayed in the analysis report (Tang, para 0035 “the analysis engine 120” para 0090 “generate a result prediction”).
Regarding claim 14, Tang as modified by Jung renders obvious the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 1, wherein the analysis report includes a raw data and an evaluation content (Tang, para 0040 “generate a result prediction”),
wherein the raw data is at least one of the response message and the physiological characteristic signal, that has not been calculated by the algorithm, and the evaluation content is an indicator, a data and a graphic generated after the algorithm calculates at least one of the response message, the physiological characteristic signal and the examination items (Tang, para 0040 “generate a result prediction [] according to the prediction model MDL, the initial symptom Sini and the responses Sans”, para 0043-0051).
Regarding claim 15, Tang as modified by Jung renders obvious the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 1 wherein the intelligent calculation module is trained by the diagnosis notification based on at least one of the response message and the physiological characteristic signal (Tang, para 0054-0056 “training data”).
Claim(s) 10 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Tang as applied by Jung to claims 1-9 and 11-15 above, and further in view of US Pat Pub No. 20190113973 granted to Coleman et al. (hereinafter “Coleman” – previously presented).
Regarding claim 10, Tang as modified by Jung renders obvious the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 1, Tang discloses further comprising a transmission unit connected to at least one of the screening module and the user module (para 0039, “transmits”, para 0033-0034 “smart phone”), but fails to explicitly disclose the transmission unit executes an encryption procedure to encrypt at least one of the response message and the physiological characteristic signal to form the encrypted response message and the physiological characteristic signal.
Coleman teaches a similar system and method for collecting, analyzing and sharing bio-signal by providing a client device such as a smartphone, cell phone, etc. (para 0050) and a software as a service platform through a network connection (para 0050). Coleman shows that it is known to provide bi-directional encryption and decryption using known encryption techniques (paras 0014, 0051, Fig. 20). This would provide securing data and limiting access to private data (para 0428). Therefore, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to modify the disclosure of Tang as modified by Jung with the teachings of Coleman to provide encrypting data prior to transmission to provide the predictable result of increasing privacy.
