Notice of Pre-AIA  or AIA  Status
The present application, filed on or after March 16, 2013, is being examined under the first inventor to file provisions of the AIA .
Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed 05/25/2023 have been fully considered. Beginning on page 6, the applicant argues that the reference of Rao et al. (20190110754) does not anticipate the claimed invention because Rao is directed towards a system having an implantable medical device. This argument is fully considered but is not convincing. The claims, as directed do not require any limitations regarding the user module, interaction unit, interface unit, screening module, etc. Under its broadest reasonable interpretation, the user module can be any module associated with a user, whether it is implanted or external that is capable of performing the functions required by such devices. The applicant argues that Rao fails to disclose an indication unit. This argument is fully considered and is convincing. Therefore, the rejection has been withdrawn.  However, upon further consideration, a new ground(s) of rejection is made in view of the following: Claims 1-9 and 11-15 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over US Pat Pub No 20180046773 granted to Tang et al in view of US Pat Pub No. 20190110754 granted to Rao et al. Claim(s) 10 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Tang et al. as modified by Rao et al. as applied to claims 1-9 and 11-15 above, and further in view of US Pat Pub No. 20190113973 granted to Coleman et al.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
In the event the determination of the status of the application as subject to AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 (or as subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103) is incorrect, any correction of the statutory basis for the rejection will not be considered a new ground of rejection if the prior art relied upon, and the rationale supporting the rejection, would be the same under either status.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
The text of those sections of Title 35, U.S. Code not included in this action can be found in a prior Office action.
The factual inquiries for establishing a background for determining obviousness under 35 U.S.C. 103 are summarized as follows:
1. Determining the scope and contents of the prior art.
2. Ascertaining the differences between the prior art and the claims at issue.
3. Resolving the level of ordinary skill in the pertinent art.
4. Considering objective evidence present in the application indicating obviousness or nonobviousness.

Claims 1-9 and 11-15 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over US Pat Pub No 20180046773 granted to Tang et al (hereinafter “Tang”) in view of US Pat Pub No. 20190110754 granted to Rao et al. (hereinafter “Rao” – previously presented).
Regarding claim 1, Tang discloses a neurological disorders decision support system (para 0032, “medical system 100”, para 0099 “disease is related to neurology”) for assisting an examiner to diagnose an examinee (para 0048 “diagnosis to the user”), the neurological disorders decision support system comprising: a user module comprising an indication unit and an interface unit (para 0032, “interaction interface includes an output interface and input device”), the indication unit connected to the interface unit (para 0032), the indication unit configured to issue an inquiry to the examinee according to a first indication signal (para 0039 “information displayed on the interaction interface 140”, “asks for symptoms”) and the interface unit configured for the examinee returning a response message according to the inquiry (para 0039 “enters his/her input on the interaction interface”) and acquiring a physiological characteristic related to the examinee to generate at least one of a physiological characteristic signal (para 0039 “receiving the initial symptom Sini according to user's manipulation”); a screening module connected to the user module (para 0032 “analysis engine”), the screening module generating the first indication signal and outputting the first indication signal to the indication unit (para 0032), and the screening module executing a neurological examination application program and generating a second indication signal according to the response message to the indication unit (para 0040 “inquiries Sqry”, figs 2, 8-9, 13), the second indication signal configured to indicate to the examinee to perform a corresponding designated action to obtain the physiological characteristic signal from the interface unit, or the interface unit retrieves one of the physiological characteristic signal and the response message from the examinee (para 0040 “receiving responses Sans”, figs 2, 8-9, 13), the screening module outputting the response message and the physiological characteristic signal (fig. 2); and an intelligent calculation module connected to the screening module (para 0035 “the analysis engine 120 includes a learning module 122 and a prediction module 124”), the intelligent calculation module executing an algorithm to calculate at least one of the response message, the physiological characteristic signal and the examination items to generate an analysis report to the examiner (para 0090 “generate a result prediction”).
Tang discloses analyzing the user’s symptoms but fails to explicitly disclose wherein the neurological examination application program provides a plurality of examination items, and the neurological examination application program selects one or more examination items from the examination items based on the response message.
Rao, from a similar field of endeavor, teaches a system and method for diagnosing and monitoring neurological disorders wherein the system will include a collection of tests the patient will be asked to perform during which time sensor data will be recorded. These tests will be designed to elicit specific diagnostic information (para 0076, paras 0088-0092 discussing various tests; para 0079 performing additional tests; fig 2). It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to modify the disclosure of Tang with the teachings of Rao to provide the predictable result of eliciting specific diagnostic information for a specific test.
Regarding claim 2, Tang as modified by Rao (hereinafter “modified Tang”) renders the neurological disorders decision support system in claim 1 obvious as recited hereinabove, Tang discloses further comprising a diagnosis module connected to the intelligent calculation module (para 0035 “the analysis engine 120 includes a learning module 122 and a prediction module 124”) and the user module (para 0039 “information displayed on the interaction interface 140”, fig. 2), wherein the diagnosis module retrieves the analysis report for assisting the examiner for diagnosis, and sends a diagnosis notification to the user module (para 0009 “result prediction is displayed on the interaction interface”, 0053).
Regarding claim 3, modified Tang renders the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 1 obvious as recited hereinabove, Tang discloses wherein the interface unit is an input element and a detection element, the input element providing at least one of an input basis data and a medical history data of the  examinee, and the detection element passively or actively detects the examinee to obtain at least one of the physiological characteristic and a consciousness state of the examinee (para 0033 “the interaction interface 140 includes an input device (e.g., a touch panel, a keyboard, a microphone, a scanner or a flash memory reader) for user to type text commands, give voice commands or to upload some related data (e.g., images, medical records, or personal examination reports)”).
Regarding claim 4, modified Tang renders the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 3 obvious as recited hereinabove, Tang discloses wherein the detection element detects a spontaneous physiological response of the examinee or the detection element detects a physiological response generated by inducing the examinee to generate the physiological characteristic signal and confirm the consciousness state (para 0039 “interaction interface receives initial symptoms”; it is noted that the limitations that follow “or” are considered not as optional).
Regarding claim 5, modified Tang renders the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 3 obvious as recited hereinabove, Tang discloses wherein Tang discloses monitoring the redness of eyes, Rao teaches wherein the detection element is configured to detect the physiological characteristics of the examinee with nystagmus related to one or both eyes of the examinee (paras 0011, 0148, 0169 “eye movements” in diagnosing patients for stroke, movement disorder, seizure and dizziness).

Claim(s) 10 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over modified Tang as applied to claims 1-9 and 11-15 above, and further in view of US Pat Pub No. 20190113973 granted to Coleman et al. (hereinafter “Coleman”).
Regarding claim 10, modified Tang renders the neurological disorders decision support system of claim 1 obvious as recited hereinabove, Tang discloses further comprising a transmission unit connected to at least one of the screening module and the user module (para 0039, “transmits”, para 0033-0034 “smart phone”), but fails to explicitly disclose the transmission unit executes an encryption procedure to encrypt at least one of the response message and the physiological characteristic signal to form the encrypted response message and the physiological characteristic signal.
Coleman teaches a similar system and method for collecting, analyzing and sharing bio-signal by providing a client device such as a smartphone, cell phone, etc. (para 0050) and a software as a service platform through a network connection (para 0050). Coleman shows that it is known to provide bi-directional encryption and decryption using known encryption techniques (paras 0014, 0051, Fig. 20). This would provide securing data and limiting access to private data (para 0428). Therefore, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to modify the disclosure of modified Tang with the teachings of Coleman to provide encrypting data prior to transmission to provide the predictable result of increasing privacy.
