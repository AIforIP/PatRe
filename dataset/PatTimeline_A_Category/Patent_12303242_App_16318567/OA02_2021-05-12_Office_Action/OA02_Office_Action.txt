Response to Arguments
Applicant’s arguments with respect to the independent claims have been considered but are moot because the new ground of rejection does not rely on any reference applied in the prior rejection of record for any teaching or matter specifically challenged in the argument.
Applicant's arguments filed February 1, 2021 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
While Applicant asserts that Asada teaches that the PPG/ABP relationship is complicated by oscillations such as rhythmic variations, Asada does not specifically teach against using such rhythmic variations.  A simplification of a complex relationship does not remove the motivation to utilize the advantages of the complex relationship.  Although the PPG/ABP blood pressure may be complicated by microcirculatory oscillations, a higher complexity does not preclude advantages that would arise from using an AC portion of the PPG signal.  For the sake of advancing prosecution, Examiner has presented further evidence which teaches the amended limitation of an AC component.
As noted in the rejection of claim 1, additionally or alternatively, Asada may still read upon extracting information from an AC component, since Asada first extracts an original waveform having AC components and then applies an averaging to process information therefrom; an average of an AC component is still extracted information from an AC component.
Furthermore, Asada’s removal of rhythmic oscillations may not read upon removal of an AC component, which Applicant contends that Asada is performing.  It is commonly-known in the art that the term “AC component,” when referring to a pulse plethysmograph signal, typically refers to the waveform containing typical indications of systole, diastole, and dicrotic notches (what first comes to mind when viewing a PPG waveform), where it appears that Asada’s processing is to remove motion noise.
