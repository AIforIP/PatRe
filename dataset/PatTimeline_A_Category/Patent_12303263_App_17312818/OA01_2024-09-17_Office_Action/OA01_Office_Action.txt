Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Section 33(a) of the America Invents Act reads as follows:
Notwithstanding any other provision of law, no patent may issue on a claim directed to or encompassing a human organism.
Claim 29 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 and section 33(a) of the America Invents Act as being directed to or encompassing a human organism.  See also Animals - Patentability, 1077 Off. Gaz. Pat. Office 24 (April 21, 1987) (indicating that human organisms are excluded from the scope of patentable subject matter under 35 U.S.C. 101).  The claim recites “the wearable apparatus is worn to a detected site” which improperly defines the structure in positive relation with a portion of a living organism. The claim should indicate “the wearable apparatus is configured to be worn on a detected site” to avoid the recitation in positive relation to the being.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1 – 3, 7 – 12, 14 – 17, 21, and 24 – 29 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention. With regard to claim 1, it is unclear from the phrase “in multiple predetermined wavelengths” (lines 10 – 11) whether Applicant intends to refer to the phrase set forth in line 2 or if a different grouping of plural wavelengths is intended. With regard to claim 2, it is unclear what relationship Applicant intends when referring to ‘the photosensitive area’ is in “one-to-one relationship” with ‘the light intensity value’.  With regard to claim 24, the amendment to the claim removed reference to all parent claims and thus, although it appears that Applicant intends to set forth a dependent claim, there is no claim that it currently depends therefrom. As such, the metes and bounds of claim 24 and those that depend therefrom cannot be readily determined.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claim(s) 1 – 3, 7 - 9, 12, 14, 24 – 27 (as best understood in view of the rejection under 35 USC 112(b)), 28, and 29 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by Xu et al. (USPGPub 2016/0091496 – cited by Applicant). Examiner notes that the search reports/foreign patent opinions provided on the various IDSs filed in this application generally relied upon this document or the corresponding Chinese priority document. For ease of discussion, the US Patent Publication version is referred to for the rejection herein. Xu et al. disclose (see description, claims 1-24, paragraphs [0049] to [0322], and figures 1-25) a multi-position diffusion spectrum data processing, modeling, prediction method, and processing device. The method comprises using probe light to irradiate a measured medium containing a specific component; obtaining first spectral data of the measured medium at a first radial position and second spectral data at a second radial position (Figures 1; 9a-d); performing differential processing on the first spectral data and the second spectral data; determining a radial position at which a diffuse reflection and/or diffuse transmission light intensity has the smallest absolute value change rate with a concentration change of the specific component as a reference position (or alternately, this would also be a position of light intensity target value); determining a position closer to a probe light source than the reference position as the first radial position, and determining a position further away from the probe light source than the reference position as the second radial position; and obtaining third spectral data at the reference position (Figures 11, 12). An optical fiber probe (Figures 10a-e) comprises a first optical fiber bundle for guiding incident infrared measurement light. An exit end face of the optical fiber probe is located at approximately the center of a detection end face of the optical fiber probe. At the detection end face, an end face of optical fibers in a second optical fiber bundle and an end face of optical fibers in a third optical fiber bundle are at different distances from the exit end face of the first fiber bundle. The second optical fiber bundle and/or the third optical fiber bundle comprise/comprises a plurality of optical fibers, and end faces of the optical fibers are arranged around the exit end face of the first optical fiber bundle into a substantially circular or annular shape centered on the exit end face of the first optical fiber bundle. According to characteristics of the measured medium and/or the specific component therein, probe light having one or a plurality of wavelengths is selected. The change in light intensity (absolute and/or relative) can be measured and used as spectral data. Xu et al. disclose an embodiment for analysis of glucose concentration (paragraphs [0077] – [0083]; Figure 13), in particular. The corresponding measurement apparatus (Figure 22 and the description thereof) has source, detector, and processing elements for obtaining the concentration information of interest.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim(s) 10-11, 15-17, and 21 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Xu et al. as applied to claims 9 and 14 above. As discussed, Xu et al. teach a method in which a light intensity reference value is determined. However, regarding claims 10 and 11, they do not particularly teach consideration of wavelength, optical parameter, or skin structure aspects as part of the determining method. However, these were well known parameters which affect optical measurements from a subject, and without a showing of criticality or unexpected results it would have been within the skill level of the art before the effective filing date to have modified Xu et al. to include consideration of known parameters in a known manner. Regarding claims 15 – 17 and 21, as noted above, Xu et al. generally disclose an arrangement in which light is transmitted to and from a measurement site with a fiber optic probe. Alternate measurement arrangements were well known before the effective filing date of the claimed invention, including to position emitter and detector elements in proximity to the subject during the measurement, by incorporation of the light source and photodetector elements into the probe itself with transmission of electrical signals through a cable from the monitor to the probe, rather than using optical fibers and to use removable sleeve portions for probes which contact a living subject, for providing protection from contamination for intended reusable portions of the system. As such, it would have been within the skill level of the art to have modified Xu et al. to use an alternate known probe embodiment with emitter and photodetector elements in the probe itself, since it has generally been held to be obvious to use know alternate equivalent embodiments in a device.
