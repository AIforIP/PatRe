Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(2) the claimed invention was described in a patent issued under section 151, or in an application for patent published or deemed published under section 122(b), in which the patent or application, as the case may be, names another inventor and was effectively filed before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claims 1, 3, 6-7, and 10 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2) as being anticipated by US Patent Application Publication No. 2021/0244377 to Amoh et al. (EFD at least 08/29/2019 and hereinafter referred to as “Amoh”).

With respect to claim 3, Amoh discloses the monitor pad according to claim 1, wherein the body information is selected from the group consisting of a person's heartbeat signal and a breathing vibration signal (e.g., paragraph [0017]: breathing sounds are detected by microphones 108, 110, 112, 114 when a user rests his/her head on pillow 304).
As to claim 6, Amoh discloses the monitor pad according to claim 1, wherein the transmission circuit includes a connector (e.g., paragraph [0016] and Fig. 2, unnumbered link between microphones 108, 110, 114, 112 and processor 102 and short range digital radio 134), wherein the microphone is connected to the connector (via couplings to the processor 102 and digital radio 134), and the connector is adapted to connect with the processing device to send the body information to the processing device (e.g., Fig. 2 and paragraph [0016]: digital radio transceiver 134 uploads breathing features sensed by microphones and sends the body information/breathing features to an external processing device 204 via a connector or unnumbered link between sleep monitor 202 and smartphone 204).
With respect to claim 7, Amoh discloses the monitor pad according to claim 1, further comprising: a battery (e.g., Fig. 1, battery 120 and paragraph [0014]), wherein the transmission circuit includes a wireless transmission device (e.g., Fig. 1, short range digital radio 134 and paragraph [0014]), wherein, the microphone and the wireless transmission device are connected to the battery to receive power (e.g., Fig. 1 and paragraph [0014]: microphones and digital radio are connect to battery 120 and thus, receive power from the battery), and the microphone is connected to the wireless transmission device to transmit the body information to the processing device via the wireless transmission device (e.g., Fig. 2 and paragraph [0016]: wireless transmission device/digital radio transceiver 134 uploads breathing features sensed by microphones and sends the body information/breathing features to an external processing device 204 via a connector or unnumbered link between sleep monitor 202 and smartphone 204).
As to claim 10, Amoh discloses a body status monitoring system, including: the monitor pad according to claim 1 (see above); an external processing device (e.g., Fig. 2, smartphone or body area network hub 204); and a network server (e.g., Fig. 2, 210), wherein the monitor pad is adapted to detect body information of a person and send the detected body information to an external processing device, and the external processing device is adapted to forward the body information to the network server to analyze the body information (e.g., paragraph [0016]: a physician may use a smartphone etc. through internet 208 and server 210 to access database 214 to view logs of the uploaded respiratory features to better diagnose breathing and sleep-related issues with a patient).
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claims 2 and 5 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Amoh as applied to claim 1 above, and further in view of US Patent Application Publication No. 2014/0101848 to Murphy.
With respect to claim 2, Amoh discloses the monitor pad according to claim 1, but does not expressly disclose that the monitor pad is foldable, and the length of the monitor pad is less than or equal to 420 mm and the width of the monitor pad is less than or equal to 297 mm. However, Murphy teaches a sleeping pad system that is foldable with spaced apart panels and/or with a pocket for receiving a pillow that is foldable (see paragraph [0032] and Figs. 5A-5C of Murphy). Thus, it was known to fold pads as taught by Murphy in order to lessen the volume of the system for storage or travel (see paragraphs [0002] and [0005] of Murphy).
Amoh in view of Murphy differs from the claimed invention in that the combination does not expressly teach that the length of the monitor pad is less than or equal to 420 mm and the width of the monitor pad is less than or equal to 297 mm. It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art at the time the invention was made to make the monitor pad of Amoh in view of Murphy so that it has the dimensions as claimed, in order to make such a monitor pad for an infant or doll, since it has been held by the courts that, where the only difference between the prior art and the claims was a recitation of relative dimensions of the claimed device, and a device having the claimed relative dimensions would not perform differently than the prior art device, the claimed device was not patentably distinct from the prior art device.  In Gardner v. TEC Systems, Inc., 725 F.2d 1338, 220 USPQ 777 (Fed. Cir. 1984), cert. denied, 469 U.S. 830, 225 USPQ 232 (1984).
As to claim 5, Amoh discloses the monitor pad according to claim 1, but does not expressly disclose a packing line and a packing locking structure, such that when the monitor pad is packed up, the packing line is adapted to tighten the monitor pad towards a packing position, and the packing locking structure is adapted to lock the packing line at a tightening position. However, Murphy teaches in a related art: packing a foldable mat, a packing line or cords 72, 74 and a packing locking structure/barrel locks 76, 78 (e.g., paragraph [0038] and Figs. 1-4 of Murphy). Murphy further teaches that when the monitor pad is packed up or folded, the packing line 72, 74 tightens the monitor pad towards a packing position and the adjustable barrel locks keep the packing line in the tightening position (e.g., paragraph [0038] of Murphy). Accordingly, one of ordinary skill in the monitor pad art would have looked to the foldable pad art of Murphy to secure the foldable monitoring pad of Amoh in view of Murphy with a packing line/cords and a packing locking structure as taught by Murphy, and because the combination would have yielded predictable results.
Claim 4 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Amoh as applied to claim 1 above, and further in view of US Patent Application Publication No. 2016/0294040 to Blair and/or US Patent Application Publication No. 2003/0095263 to Varshneya et al. (hereinafter referred to as “Varshneya”).
Amoh discloses the monitor pad according to claim 1, but does not expressly disclose a non-slip structure is provided on one side of the pad cover, the non-slip structure includes at least one non-slip strip, and the non-slip strip comprises silicone or rubber. However, Blair, in a related art: mat based antenna and electronic system for a patient support surface during medical procedures, teaches that the mat based antenna and electronic system 18 may include a bottom cover sheet 42 in the form of a non-slip fabric to retain the mat based antenna and electronic system 18 in place on the patient support surface 12 (e.g., paragraph [0073] of Blair).
Amoh in view of Blair teaches that the cover/pillow case of Amoh may have a non-slip cover sheet in order to retain the pillow in place on the patient support surface/bed as taught by Blair; but does not expressly teach that the non-slip strip comprises silicone or rubber. However, Varshneya, in a related art: fiber optic vital sign monitor or use in medical facilities, teaches that a sensor pad substrate has a casing/encapsulation with a pillow cover-like configuration and a foam rubber sheet added to the underside of the substrate before encapsulation in order to allow the sensor pad substrate to conform to the contour of the human body, optimize mechanical displacement for respiration detection and achieve good coupling to detect heart signals (e.g., paragraph [0115] of Varshneya). That is, Varshneya teaches that a sensor pad like electronic pad 302 of Amoh should have a foam rubber sheet added to its underside in order for sensor pad to conform to the human body and to prevent displacement of the substrate so good coupling is achieved to receive the vital signals as taught by Varshneya before the sensor is encapsulated in it pillow-like cover. Accordingly, one of ordinary skill in the art would have recognized the benefits of a foam rubber sheet to a sensor pad in order to maintain good coupling to receive vital signs as taught by Varshneya. Thus, one of ordinary skill in the art would have modified the non-slip structure of Amoh in view of Blair to comprise rubber in view of the teachings of Varshneya and because the combination would have yielded predictable results.
Claims 8-9 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Amoh as applied to claim 1 above, and further in view of US Patent Application Publication No. 2008/0001735 to Tran and US Patent Application Publication No. 2023/0238827 to Borelli.
With respect to claim 8, Amoh discloses the monitor pad according to claim 7, but does not expressly disclose a power receiving coil and a rectifier circuit. However, Amoh does disclose that the battery is a rechargeable battery (e.g., paragraph [0014]: battery 120 can be recharged by a charge monitor). Tran teaches, in a related art: monitoring system using one or more wireless nodes forming a wireless network, a system having a power receiving antenna and a rectifier circuit where the power receiving antenna is adapted to receive to an external electromagnetic signal and convert the electromagnetic signal into direct current for supply to the rechargeable battery to charge the battery (e.g., paragraph [0195] of Tran: collected RF energy is converted into usable DC power using a rectifier in order to recharge a battery). Thus, one of ordinary skill in the art would have recognized the benefits of a power receiving antenna and a rectifier in view of the teachings of Tran. Tran differs from the claimed invention in that it uses a power receiving antenna and rectifier and not a power receiving coil and rectifier. However, Borelli teaches that a self-powered sensor unit may comprise at least one rectifier 15 connected to at least one receiving coil 14 to generate a first DC voltage and a DC to DC power converter to a predefined second DC voltage, which is supplied to  storage and to power-consuming components of the sensor unit (e.g., paragraphs [0013] and [0058] of Borelli).  Consequently, one of ordinary skill in the art would have modified the monitor pad of Amoh so that its battery charger has a power receiving antenna (or coil in view of the teachings of Borelli) and a rectifier in order to recharge battery 120 since it was a known engineering technique used to recharge a battery in a monitoring network system as taught by Tran, and because the combination would have yielded predictable results.
As to claim 9, Amoh in view of Tran and Borelli teaches the monitor pad according to claim 1, further comprising a power receiving coil and a rectifier circuit, wherein the transmission circuit includes a wireless transmission device, wherein, the power receiving coil is adapted to receive an external electromagnetic signal (see above discussion w/r/t claim 8), and that the rectifier circuit is adapted to convert the external electromagnetic signal into a direct current for provision to the microphone and the wireless transmission device (the rectifier and power receiving coil recharge battery 120 which provides power for microphones and the digital radio/wireless transmission device as discussed above), but does not expressly teach that at least a part of the power receiving coil serves as an antenna of the wireless transmission device. However, Borelli teaches that power source 13 is rectified and converted to a predefined voltage level by the electronic circuitry so that it can power the sensor device (microphones of Amoh) and the transmitter (digital radio 134 of Amoh) (e.g., paragraph [0058]). Accordingly, one of ordinary skill in the art would have recognized the benefits of a coil in view of the teachings of Borelli that the power source (coil and rectifier) can be used to power both microphones of the sensor device and the transmitter as taught by Borelli. Consequently, one of ordinary skill in the art would have modified Amoh in view of Tran to use at least part of the power receiving coil as an antenna of the wireless transmission device in view of the teachings of Borelli that the energy can be conditioned so that it can be used for both purposes, and because the combination would have yielded predictable results.
