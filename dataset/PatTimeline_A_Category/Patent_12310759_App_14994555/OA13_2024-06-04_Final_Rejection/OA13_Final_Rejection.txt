Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112(a) - Withdrawn and Newly Applied
The following is a quotation of the first paragraph of 35 U.S.C. 112(a):
(a) IN GENERAL.—The specification shall contain a written description of the invention, and of the manner and process of making and using it, in such full, clear, concise, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to which it pertains, or with which it is most nearly connected, to make and use the same, and shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor or joint inventor of carrying out the invention.
The following is a quotation of the first paragraph of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112:
The specification shall contain a written description of the invention, and of the manner and process of making and using it, in such full, clear, concise, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to which it pertains, or with which it is most nearly connected, to make and use the same, and shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor of carrying out his invention.
Claims 1-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(a) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), first paragraph, as failing to comply with the written description requirement. The claim(s) contains subject matter which was not described in the specification in such a way as to reasonably convey to one skilled in the relevant art that the inventor or a joint inventor, or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the inventor(s), at the time the application was filed, had possession of the claimed invention.
Regarding claims 1, 9, and 17, the claims recite the limitation “determining a blood flow demand of the portion of the patient’s bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomical model, based on … amount of visceral vasculature and collateral pathway(s) supplying blood to the portion of patient’s bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomic model”. However, the specification and the drawings filed on 01/13/2016 do not disclose determining a blood flow demand based on an amount of visceral vasculature and collateral pathway(s) supplying blood to the portion of patient’s bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomic model. The determination of “blood flow demand” is described in para. [035] of the specification filed on 01/13/2016. However, para. [035] does not disclose or describe that an amount of visceral vasculature and collateral pathway(s) supplying blood to the portion of patient’s bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomic model is used to determine the blood flow demand. Claims 2-8, 10-16, and 18-20 are rejected due to their dependence from claims 1, 9, and 17.
Regarding claims 1, 9, and 17, the claims recite the limitation “determining a blood flow demand of the portion of the patient’s bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomical model, based on a determined mass of the portion of the patient's bowel, amount of visceral vasculature and collateral pathway(s) supplying blood to the portion of patient's bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomic model, and the received physiological state of the patient”. That is, “determining a blood flow demand …”, requires three different variables. The three variables being 1) a determined mass of the portion of the patient's bowel, 2) amount of visceral vasculature and collateral pathway(s) supplying blood to the portion of patient's bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomic model, and 3) the received physiological state of the patient. However, the specification and the drawings filed on 01/13/2016 do not disclose all three of the variables being used in conjunction to determine the blood flow demand, as required by the claims. The determination of “blood flow demand” is described in para. [035] of the specification filed on 01/13/2016. Para. [035] discloses “calculating a mass of the modeled bowel region to determine blood flow demand … may further include using the received physiological state to determine blood flow demand …”. However, para. [035] or the entirety of the specification does not describe using the three variables to determine the blood flow demand. Specifically, the specification does not describe using 2) amount of visceral vasculature and collateral pathway(s) supplying blood to the portion of patient's bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomic model, in conjunction with variables 1 and 3 recited above. Claims 2-8, 10-16, and 18-20 are rejected due to their dependence from claims 1, 9, and 17.
Regarding claims 1, 9, and 17, the claims recite the limitations of “determining a blood flow demand of the portion of the patient's bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomical model, based on a determined mass of the portion of the patient's bowel, amount of visceral vasculature and collateral pathway(s) supplying blood to the portion of patient's bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomic model, and the received physiological state of the patient” and “calculating a blood flow characteristic of blood delivered to the portion of the patient's bowel using the determined blood flow demand and a value of microvascular resistance based on the received physiological state”. The claimed steps of determining and calculating are considered computer-implemented functional claim limitations as they are directed to being performed by a processor or an electronic device. However, it is not clear what steps the processor has to perform in order for a processor to perform the claimed steps. The specification does not disclose the computer, processor, or generic computer components and the algorithm (e.g., the necessary steps and/or flowcharts) in regard to a processor performing the claimed functions in sufficient detail such that one of ordinary skill in the art can reasonably conclude the inventor possessed the claimed subject matter at the time of filing. For example, how does the processor determine a blood flow demand based on multiple variables? Are the multiple variables weighted differently? Are there mathematical equations to determine the blood flow demand? Further, how does the processor calculate a blood flow characteristic, such as a blood pressure or a flow rate, using the blood flow demand and resistance value? Again are the values weighted and are there mathematical equations? It is not enough to disclose that one skilled in the art could write a program to achieve the claimed function or the technique is well known, because the specification must explain how the inventor intends to achieve the claimed function to satisfy the written description requirement. See, e.g., Vasudevan Software, Inc. v. MicroStrategy, Inc., 782 F.3d 671, 681-683, 114, USPQ2d 1349, 1356, 1357 (Fed. Cir. 2015).
As the specification does not provide a disclosure of the computer and algorithm in sufficient detail to demonstrate to one of ordinary skill in the art that the inventor possessed the invention, the claims are rejected for lack of written description. For more information regarding the written description requirement, see MPEP 2161, 2162-2163.07(b).
Claims 2-8, 10-16, and 18-20 are rejected due to their dependence from claims 1, 9, and 17.

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101 - Newly Applied
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Claims 1-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because the claimed invention is directed to a judicial exception (i.e., a law of nature, a natural phenomenon, or an abstract idea) without significantly more.
Analysis of independent claims 1, 9, and 17:
Step 1 of the subject matter eligibility test (see MPEP 2106.03).
Claim 1 is directed to a computer-implemented method, which describes one of the four statutory categories of patentable subject matter, i.e., a machine/a process. Claim 9 is directed to a system which describes one of the four statutory categories of patentable subject matter, i.e., a machine. Claim 17 is directed to a non-transitory computer readable medium, which describes one of the four statutory categories of patentable subject matter, i.e., a machine/a process.
Step 2A of the subject matter eligibility test (see MPEP 2106.04).
Prong One: Claims 1, 9, and 17 recites an abstract idea. In particular, the claim recites the following:
determining a blood flow demand of the portion of the patient's bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomical model, based on a determined mass of the portion of the patient's bowel, amount of visceral vasculature and collateral pathway(s) supplying blood to the portion of patient's bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomic model, and the received physiological state of the patient;
calculating a blood flow characteristic of blood delivered to the portion of the patient's bowel using the determined blood flow demand and a value of microvascular resistance based on the received physiological state; and
generating an assessment of blood supply adequacy defining an adequacy of blood supply to the patient's bowel, or (ii) a risk score defining a risk of disease of the patient's bowel, based on a comparison of the calculated blood flow characteristic to a predetermined threshold blood flow characteristic.
These elements recite in claims 1, 9, and 17 are drawn to an abstract idea since they involve a mental process that can be practically performed in the human mind including observation, evaluation, judgment, and opinion and using pen and paper.
“Determining a blood flow demand of the portion of the patient's bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomical model, based on a determined mass of the portion of the patient's bowel, amount of visceral vasculature and collateral pathway(s) supplying blood to the portion of patient's bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomic model, and the received physiological state of the patient” is drawn to an abstract idea since it involves a mental process that can be practically performed in the human mind including observation, evaluation, judgment, and opinion and using pen and paper. For example, a person with ordinary skill in the art could reasonably determine “a blood flow demand” by viewing a portion of the patient’s bowel modeled on a piece of paper and mentally determine that a large bowel will require a higher blood flow demand versus a small bowel will require a lower blood flow demand. A person with ordinary skill in the art could reasonably determine “a blood flow demand” by viewing the patient’s bowel modeled on a piece of paper and mentally determine that a larger amount of visceral vasculature and collateral pathways supplying blood will require a higher blood flow demand versus a fewer amount. A person with ordinary skill in the art could reasonably determine “a blood flow demand” by mentally determining an active state will require a higher blood flow demand than a resting state. A person with ordinary skill in the art could then reasonably weight the three variables to determine “a blood flow demand”. There is nothing to suggest an undue level of complexity in the “determining a blood flow demand” steps currently. That is, there is nothing requiring the determining step to require complex mathematical equations or weighting to make the determining step unpractical for the human mind, with the aid of pen and paper. Instead, as currently claimed, all variables can be determined mentally, with the aid of pen and paper.
“Calculating a blood flow characteristic of blood delivered to the portion of the patient's bowel using the determined blood flow demand and a value of microvascular resistance based on the received physiological state” is drawn to an abstract idea since it involves a mental process that can be practically performed in the human mind including observation, evaluation, judgment, and opinion and using pen and paper. For example, a person of ordinary skill in the art could reasonably use the determined blood flow demand, determined mentally, and a value of a resistance to determine a blood flow characteristic by using a look up table. For example, a person with ordinary skill in the art could reasonably use a determined “high” blood flow demand and a theoretical resistance value of “2”, in conjunction with a basic look up table to determine a blood flow characteristic, such as a characteristic of a blood pressure or flow rate being high or low. There is nothing requiring “calculating a blood flow characteristic” to require complex mathematical equations to make the calculating step unpractical for the human mind. Instead, as currently claimed, all variables and results can be determined mentally, or with the aid of pen and paper, and a look up table.
“Generating an assessment of blood supply adequacy defining an adequacy of blood supply to the patient's bowel, or (ii) a risk score defining a risk of disease of the patient's bowel, based on a comparison of the calculated blood flow characteristic to a predetermined threshold blood flow characteristic” is drawn to an abstract idea since it involves a mental process that can be practically performed in the human mind including observation, evaluation, judgment, and opinion and using pen and paper. For example, a person of ordinary skill in the art could reasonably generate an assessment of blood supply adequacy or a risk score by comparing the mentally determined blood flow characteristic with a second value, and determine if the values are within an acceptable range or ratio. There is nothing to suggest an undue level of complexity in “generating an assessment of blood supply adequacy defining an adequacy of blood supply to the patient's bowel, or (ii) a risk score defining a risk of disease of the patient's bowel, based on a comparison of the calculated blood flow characteristic to a predetermined threshold blood flow characteristic”.

Taylor teaches receiving a three-dimensional model of a patient’s anatomy (Fig. 2, 200, para. [0111], Fig. 24, 620, para. [0220]), and determining blood for information for the patient based on the three-dimensional model (Fig. 2, 400, para. [0114], [0190-0197], Fig. 24).
Patwardhan teaches obtaining a plurality of images of a patients bowel (Fig. 4, 402, para. [0046]), determining different characteristics of the bowel, and determining a blood flow characteristic from the images (Fig. 4, 412, para. [0046]).
However, the prior art made of record does not disclose, teach, or reasonably suggest “determining a blood flow demand of the portion of the patient’s bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomical model, based on a determined mass of the portion of the patient’s bowel, amount of visceral vasculature and collateral pathway(s) supplying blood to the portion of patient’s bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific anatomic model, and the received physiological state of the patient”.
