Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101 - Maintained
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Claims 1-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because the claimed invention is directed to a judicial exception (i.e., a law of nature, a natural phenomenon, or an abstract idea) without significantly more.
Analysis of independent claims 1, 9, and 17:
Step 1 of the subject matter eligibility test (see MPEP 2106.03).
Claim 1 is directed to a computer-implemented method, which describes one of the four statutory categories of patentable subject matter, i.e., a machine/a process. Claim 9 is directed to a system which describes one of the four statutory categories of patentable subject matter, i.e., a machine. Claim 17 is directed to a non-transitory computer readable medium, which describes one of the four statutory categories of patentable subject matter, i.e., a machine/a process.
Step 2A of the subject matter eligibility test (see MPEP 2106.04).
Prong One: Claims 1, 9, and 17 recites an abstract idea. In particular, the claim recites the following:
determining a blood flow demand of the portion of the patient's bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific three dimensional anatomic model, based on a determined mass of the portion of the patient's bowel and the received physiological state of the patient;
performing a fluid simulation of a blood flow in the received patient-specific three dimensional anatomic model;
calculating a blood flow characteristic of blood delivered to the portion of the patient's bowel using the fluid simulation, the determined blood flow demand, and a value of microvascular resistance based on the received physiological state; and
generating an assessment of blood supply adequacy defining an adequacy of blood supply to the patient's bowel, or (ii) a risk score defining a risk of disease of the patient's bowel, based on a comparison of the calculated blood flow characteristic to a predetermined threshold blood flow characteristic.
These elements recite in claims 1, 9, and 17 are drawn to an abstract idea since (1) they involve mathematical concepts in the form of mathematical relationships, mathematical formulas or equations, and/or mathematical calculations; and/or (2) they involve a mental process that can be practically performed in the human mind including observation, evaluation, judgment, and opinion and using pen and paper.
“Determining a blood flow demand of the portion of the patient's bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific three dimensional anatomic model, based on a determined mass of the portion of the patient's bowel and the received physiological state of the patient” is drawn to an abstract idea since it involves a mental process that can be practically performed in the human mind including observation, evaluation, judgment, and opinion and using pen and paper. For example, a person with ordinary skill in the art could reasonably determine “a blood flow demand” by viewing a portion of the patient’s bowel modeled on a piece of paper and mentally determine that a large bowel will require a higher blood flow demand versus a small bowel will require a lower blood flow demand. A person with ordinary skill in the art could reasonably determine “a blood flow demand” by mentally determining an active state will require a higher blood flow demand than a resting state. A person with ordinary skill in the art could then reasonably weight the two variables to determine “a blood flow demand”. There is nothing to suggest an undue level of complexity in the “determining a blood flow demand” steps currently. That is, there is nothing requiring the determining step to require complex mathematical equations or weighting to make the determining step unpractical for the human mind, with the aid of pen and paper. Instead, as currently claimed, all variables can be determined mentally, with the aid of pen and paper.
“Performing a fluid simulation of a blood flow in the received patient-specific three dimensional anatomic model” is drawn to an abstract idea since it involves mathematical concepts in the form of mathematical relationships, mathematical formulas or equations, and/or mathematical calculations, performable within the human mind. For example, performing a fluid simulation on a three dimensional anatomic model is involves the basic mathematical equations of determining fluid flow through a tube including the modeled radius and modeled length of the model, viscosity of the fluid, and the pressure. Further, the fluid simulation can be performed within the human mind, with the aid of pen and paper. There is nothing to suggest an undue level of complexity in the fluid simulation.
“Calculating a blood flow characteristic of blood delivered to the portion of the patient's bowel using the fluid simulation, the determined blood flow demand, and a value of microvascular resistance based on the received physiological state” is drawn to an abstract idea since it involves a mental process that can be practically performed in the human mind including observation, evaluation, judgment, and opinion and using pen and paper. For example, a person of ordinary skill in the art could reasonably use a performed fluid simulation, determined blood flow demand, determined mentally, and a value of a resistance to determine a blood flow characteristic by using a look up table. For example, a person with ordinary skill in the art could reasonably use a determined fluid simulation, a determined “high” blood flow demand and a theoretical resistance value of “2”, in conjunction with a basic look up table to determine a blood flow characteristic, such as a characteristic of a blood pressure or flow rate being high or low. There is nothing requiring “calculating a blood flow characteristic” to require complex mathematical equations to make the calculating step unpractical for the human mind. Instead, as currently claimed, all variables and results can be determined mentally, or with the aid of pen and paper, and a look up table.
“Generating an assessment of blood supply adequacy defining an adequacy of blood supply to the patient's bowel, or (ii) a risk score defining a risk of disease of the patient's bowel, based on a comparison of the calculated blood flow characteristic to a predetermined threshold blood flow characteristic” is drawn to an abstract idea since it involves a mental process that can be practically performed in the human mind including observation, evaluation, judgment, and opinion and using pen and paper. For example, a person of ordinary skill in the art could reasonably generate an assessment of blood supply adequacy or a risk score by comparing the mentally determined blood flow characteristic with a second value, and determine if the values are within an acceptable range or ratio. There is nothing to suggest an undue level of complexity in “generating an assessment of blood supply adequacy defining an adequacy of blood supply to the patient's bowel, or (ii) a risk score defining a risk of disease of the patient's bowel, based on a comparison of the calculated blood flow characteristic to a predetermined threshold blood flow characteristic”.

Claims 1, 9, and 17 do not include additional elements, alone or in combination, that are sufficient to amount to significantly more than the judicial exception (i.e., an inventive concept) for the same reasons as described above. Rather, the collective functions of the claimed invention merely provide conventional computer implementation, i.e., the computer is simply a tool to perform the process.

Applicant's arguments filed 08/29/2024 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
Applicants have argued on pages 16-17 of Remarks, filed 08/29/2024, that amended claims 1, 9, and 17 each recite “performing a fluid simulation of a blood flow in the received patient-specific three dimensional anatomic model” and “calculating a blood flow characteristic of blood delivered to the portion of the patient’s bowl using the fluid simulation …”, and that the “human mind cannot practically perform such simulations or calculate a blood flow characteristic based on the simulations”.
The Examiner respectfully disagrees. As recited above in the rejection, “performing a fluid simulation of a blood flow in the received patient-specific three dimensional anatomic model” is drawn towards an abstract idea since it involves mathematical concepts, including mathematical formulas and equations and/or mathematical calculations. “Performing a fluid simulation” is drawn to mathematical formulas and/or calculations as a fluid simulation can be performed by determining a flow rate of the modeled section using variables like the radius, the length, the viscosity, and the pressure. As recited above, the claims do not recite additional elements that integrate the abstract ideas into a practical application.
Prior Art Rejection
There is currently no prior art rejection. The following is a statement of reasons for the indication of allowable subject matter. Claims 1-20 appear to be allowable over the prior art made of record. The closest prior art made of record includes: Taylor (US 20120041318 A1) (previously cited) and Patwardhan et al. (US 20160106347 A1) (previously cited), hereinafter referred to as Patwardhan.
Taylor teaches receiving a non-invasive patient-specific three dimensional anatomic model of a patient’s anatomy (Fig. 2, 200, para. [0111], Fig. 24, 620, para. [0220]), and determining blood for information for the patient based on the three-dimensional model (Fig. 2, 400, para. [0114], [0190-0197], Fig. 24).
Patwardhan teaches obtaining a plurality of images of a patients bowel (Fig. 4, 402, para. [0046]), determining different characteristics of the bowel, and determining a blood flow characteristic from the images (Fig. 4, 412, para. [0046]).
However, the prior art made of record does not disclose, teach, or reasonably suggest “receiving a non-invasive patient-specific three dimensional anatomic model including a portion of a patient’s bowel” and “determining a blood flow demand of the portion of the patient’s bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific three dimensional anatomic model, based on a determined mass of the portion of the patient’s bowel and the received physiological state of the patient”.
