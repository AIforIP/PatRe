Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101 - Withdrawn
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Response to Arguments
Applicant’s arguments, see pages 12-16 of Remarks, filed 01/06/2025, with respect to claims 1-20 have been fully considered and are persuasive. Applicants’ amendments and arguments are persuasive. Applicants have amended the independent claims to recite “determining adequacy of blood flow to the portion of the patient’s bowel by comparing the determined blood flow characteristic with a threshold blood flow characteristic; and generating and displaying (i) an assessment of blood supply adequacy based on the determined adequacy of blood flow, or (ii) a risk score defining a risk of disease of the patient’s bowel, based on the comparison”, integrating the judicial exceptions into a practical application specific for assessing ischemia and related disorders Further, as a whole, the claims recite additional elements that amount to more than the judicial exception, and are directed towards a specific medical assessment/simulation technique. Further, Applicants’ arguments regarding performing fluid simulation and determining blood flow characteristics, being too complex to be practically performed in the human mind is considered persuasive. The 101 rejection of claims 1-20 has been withdrawn.
Allowable Subject Matter
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
Claims 1-20 are allowable over the prior art made of record. The closest prior art made of record includes: Taylor (US 20120041318 A1) (previously cited), Fonte et al. (US 10456094 B2), hereinafter referred to as Fonte, and Patwardhan et al. (US 20160106347 A1) (previously cited), hereinafter referred to as Patwardhan.
Taylor teaches receiving a non-invasive patient-specific three dimensional anatomic model of a patient’s anatomy (Fig. 2, 200, para. [0111], Fig. 24, 620, para. [0220]), and determining blood for information for the patient based on the three-dimensional model (Fig. 2, 400, para. [0114], [0190-0197], Fig. 24).
Fonte teaches receiving a non-invasive patient-specific three dimensional anatomic model (Fig. 3, col. 6, lines 21-28, col. 7, lines 50-56), evaluating and assessing characteristics of the anatomic model (Fig. 3, col. 11, line 55 - col. 15, line 32), and generating a patient vascular score based on the evaluation (Fig. 3, col. 15, line 45 - col. 16, line 36).
Patwardhan teaches obtaining a plurality of images of a patients bowel (Fig. 4, 402, para. [0046]), determining different characteristics of the bowel, and determining a blood flow characteristic from the images (Fig. 4, 412, para. [0046]).
However, the prior art made of record does not disclose, teach, or reasonably suggest “receiving a non-invasive patient-specific three dimensional anatomic model including a portion of a patient’s bowel”, “determining a blood flow demand of the portion of the patient’s bowel modeled by the non-invasive patient-specific three dimensional anatomic model, based on a determined mass of the portion of the patient’s bowel and the received physiological state of the patient”, “determining adequacy of blood flow to the portion of the patient’s bowel by comparing the determined blood flow characteristic with a threshold blood flow characteristic”, and “generating and displaying (i) an assessment of blood supply adequacy based on the determined adequacy of blood flow, or (ii) a risk score defining a risk of disease of the patient’s bowel, based on the comparison”.
