Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed March 29, 2024 have been fully considered. With respect to the 102 Rejections in the Non-Final Office Action (See Pages 9-11 of Applicant’s Response “35 U.S.C. 102 Rejections: Claims 1-20”), Applicant argues that, as amended, claim 1 does not disclose or suggest the ability to detect mouth breathing, or to distinguish between shallow breathing mouth breathing by way of a machine learning model. Applicant also states that Shouldice is unable to provide biofeedback adapted to mouth breathing. However, the amendments necessitate new grounds of rejection, and these limitations are rejected under Shouldice in view of Tanriover. It would have been obvious to combine these references because both Should and Tanriover are directed to using machine learning to classify breathing activities, and Tanriover discloses determining mouth breathing ([0011]) and shallow breathing ([0069]).
With respect to claim 2, Applicant argues that Shouldice does not disclose or suggest that data of a first modality is used to generate labels used to train a machine learning model to correctly classify data of a second and different modality.
MPEP § 2111 discusses proper claim interpretation, including giving claims their broadest reasonable interpretation in light of the specification during examination. Under broadest reasonable interpretation (BRI), the words of a claim must be given their plain meaning unless such meaning is inconsistent with the specification, and it is improper to import claim limitations from the specification into the claim.
Under BRI, the limitations of claim 2 may be interpreted to be a machine learning model trained with labels for motion data and audio data. Shouldice discloses that “a machine can “learn” the features with some human supervision (a core concept of the field of “machine learning”) where labeled data with the expected outcome is supplied and some human help provided, or in a fully automatic way where some or no labeled data may be supplied” [0162] and that the machine learning model can receive input from “parameters including from the respiratory waveform, activity levels, cardiac waveform, raw audio samples…biomotion data…” Therefore claim 2 is rejected based on its dependency on claim 1, and for the reason above.
Claims 2-9 and 11-18 are rejected based on their dependency on claims 1 and 10 for the reasons above, and for the reasons described in the 102 and 103 Rejections above.
