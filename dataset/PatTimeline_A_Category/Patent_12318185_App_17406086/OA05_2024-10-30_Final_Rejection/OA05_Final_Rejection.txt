Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed September 18, 2024 have been fully considered. With respect to the 102 Rejections of Claims 21 and 22 in the Non-Final Office Action (See Page 13 of Applicant’s Response “35 U.S.C. § 102 Rejections: Claims 21-22”), Applicant argues that claim 21 patentably defines over the cited reference as Shouldice does not disclose or suggest features relating to training using a teaching architecture.
MPEP § 2111 discusses proper claim interpretation, including giving claims their broadest reasonable interpretation in light of the specification during examination. Under broadest reasonable interpretation (BRI), the words of a claim must be given their plain meaning unless such meaning is inconsistent with the specification, and it is improper to import claim limitations from the specification into the claim.
Under BRI, “a teacher model” may be a method used to train, or teach, a machine learning model. [0162] of Shouldice discloses a method of labeling data and learning the expected outcome, which is recited to be a core concept of machine learning, and [0182] recites respiratory patterns being learned by the system over time. Additionally, [0192-0193] of Shouldice describes a deep-belief network (DBN) that learns uses “movement and activity” data and trained with labeled data, along with the concatenated data described in [0195-0198] comprising movement and audio features being used in the DBN to perform classification.
Claim 22 is rejected based on its dependency on claim 21 for the reasons above, and for the reasons described in the 102 Rejection.
