A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
(a)(2) the claimed invention was described in a patent issued under section 151, or in an application for patent published or deemed published under section 122(b), in which the patent or application, as the case may be, names another inventor and was effectively filed before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claims 1-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2) as being anticipated by US 20210150873 A1 (Shouldice et al.).
Regarding claim 1, Shouldice teaches a method of monitoring a user's breathing ([0006] “detecting movement of a subject, for example, while the subject is asleep. Based on such movement detection, including for example breathing movement, the subject's movements, sleep related characteristics, respiratory characteristics, cardiac characteristics, sleep state, etc. may be detected”), the method comprising:
passively monitoring the user's breathing with a device during a predetermined time interval ([0157] “observations throughout for example the night”) generating sensor-generated signals ([0161] “The sensed signal (a signal representing distance verses time (motion) information) is used to calculate various features, such as sleep features. These features can then be used to derive information regarding the user's physiological state”), wherein the sensor-generated signals include at least one of acoustic signals or motion signals ([0140] “the processing device 100 may use motion detection (e.g., Sonar) techniques for detecting respiration. However, in some cases, acoustic analysis, of an audible breath signal at the microphone, may be implemented by the processing device 100 for the detection of respiration”);
identifying one or more breathing modes of the user's breathing by processing data created from the sensor-generated signals using a machine learning model ([0051] “the processing device 100 and its associated methods can be used to detect, for example, the user's breathing and identify sleep stages, sleep states, transitions between states, breathing and/or other respiratory characteristics”… “presence or absence of a person or subject breathing (inspiration, expiration, pause, and derived rate)”; [0155] “a more complex machine learning system using neural networks or artificial intelligence system. The purpose of this processing is to gain further insights from the gathered biometric data”; [0165]); and
responsive to detecting a trigger mode based on the identifying ([0172] “if the subject stops breathing (e.g., an apnea) or exhibits very shallow breathing (e.g., a hypopnea)”), generating an instruction adapted to the trigger mode and conveying the instruction to the user via the device ([0107] “Based on the report, the processing of the system can also produce audible advice as to improving sleep, such as by suggesting a therapy device to assist with sleep”).
Regarding claim 2, Shouldice teaches the method of claim 1, wherein the identifying is based, at least in part, on determining phases of the user's breathing, and wherein the determining comprising:
receiving acoustic signals generated by an acoustic sensor in response to the user's breathing ([0140] “acoustic analysis, of an audible breath signal at the microphone, may be implemented by the processing device 100 for the detection of respiration”; [0165]);
generating acoustic data comprising features extracted from the acoustic signals ([0191-0192] “breathing and cardiac variability features”); and
determining phases of the user's breathing by classifying the acoustic data using additional machine learning model ([0140] “acoustic analysis, of an audible breath signal at the microphone, may be implemented by the processing device 100 for the detection of respiration”), wherein the additional machine learning model is trained based on signal processing of motion signals generated by a motion sensor in response to human breathing motions ([0192] “A DBN is particularly useful to learn from these extracted features. DBNs can be used without supervision, and then later trained with labeled data (that is, data confirmed by a human expert input)”).
Regarding claim 3, Shouldice teaches the method of claim 2, wherein the machine learning model is trained by:
obtaining acoustic training data created based on a series of sensor-generated acoustic signals generated in response to the breathing of one or more subjects ([0177] “supervised training is performed using expert score polysomnography (PSG) overnight datasets from a sleep lab or home PSG, from multiple sites around the world, and scored by at least one scorer, using a specified scoring methodology. Further unsupervised training is performed from datasets gathered with one or more of the selecting sensing methods”); and
annotating the acoustic training data based on the signal processing of the motion signals ([0193] “extracted features that can be passed onto the DBN, can include: apnea type and location, respiratory rate and variability of same over different timescales, respiration, inspiration and expirations times, depth of inspiration and expiration”);
wherein the motion signals are generated in response to the breathing of the one or more subjects and are generated simultaneously with generating the sensor-generated acoustic signals ([0118] “processing device may detect motion with IR sensing and acoustic sensing”).
Regarding claim 4, Shouldice teaches the method of claim 3, wherein the trigger mode corresponds to shallow breathing or mouth breathing by the user ([0172] “exhibits very shallow breathing (e.g., a hypopnea)”).
Regarding claim 5, Shouldice teaches the method of claim 1, comprising:
identifying a breathing context of the user, wherein the breathing context of the user includes at least one of a physiological context, a psychological context, or a social context corresponding to the user ([0193] “extracted features that can be passed onto the DBN, can include: apnea type and location, respiratory rate and variability of same over different timescales, respiration, inspiration and expirations times, depth of inspiration and expiration, cardiac rate and variability of same over different time scales, ballistocardiogram beat shape/morphology movement and activity types such as gross movement, PLM/RLS, signal quality (integrity of measures over time), user information such as age, height, weight, sex, health conditions, occupation etc.”); and
determining based on the breathing context at least one of a nudge to deliver to the user or a time for conveying a nudge to the user ([0017] “the derived physiological movement signal and generate an alarm or a communication to notify the user or a third party”).
Regarding claim 6, Shouldice teaches the method of claim 1, comprising:
capturing with the device a series of sensor-generated acoustic signals during the predetermined time interval ([0165] “acoustic or radio frequency based sensing approach”); and
discarding one or more of the series of sensor-generated acoustic signals that are each identified as an unreliable signal ([0165] “For example, an inspiration may be sharper than an expiration, and faster, and there may be a notch on the waveform if breath is held for a moment. The inspiration and expiration amplitudes, as well as the respiration frequency, may vary. Some extraction methods focus on detecting the peak and trough, then detecting the better quality of the two (say detecting a local peak and discarding the trough)”);
wherein the identifying is based on a comparison of multiple phases of the user's breathing ([0165] “an inspiration may be sharper than an expiration, and faster”).
Regarding claim 7, Shouldice teaches the method of claim 1, comprising:
conveying the instruction to the user via the device to perform a mindful breathing exercise in response to the identifying a trigger mode ([0015] “cause the interactive audio device to detect respiratory motion of a user based on at least a portion of the derived physiological movement signal and generate output cues to motive the user to adjust user breathing”); and
providing auditory or visual biofeedback to the user via the device based on determining phases of the user's breathing as the user performs the mindful breathing exercise ([0243] “processing device 100 can be implemented with daytime applications, such as for reducing stress and blood pressure, by generating meditation output, such as deep breathing exercises, based on the acoustically derived motion signal. For example, respiratory motion signals can be processed/evaluated by the processing device 100 to generate timing cues that can help to provide customized mindfulness breathing exercises (e.g., “belly breathing” at rates below 8 breaths per minute) by controller output via its speaker(s) and/or related appliances (e.g. controlled lights).”; [0247] “increase the user's breathing rate and modulate the user's inspiration/expiration time, optionally with biofeedback from the low frequency ultrasonic biometric detection”).
