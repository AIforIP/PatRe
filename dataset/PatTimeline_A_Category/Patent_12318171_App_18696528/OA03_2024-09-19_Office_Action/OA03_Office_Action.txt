Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 15--25 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Claim 15 recites “each biomarker parameter being indicative of a particular aspect of endothelium functionality” in line 23. The term “a particular” is a relative term which renders the claim indefinite. The term “particular” is not defined by the claim, the specification does not provide a standard for ascertaining the requisite degree, and one of ordinary skill in the art would not be reasonably apprised of the scope of the invention. Clarification requested. Suggested amendment: delete “particular”.
Claims 16-25 are rejected by virtue of their dependence from claim 15.
Claim 24 recites “filtering the measurement data using a digital zero-phase filter to obtain filtered data comprising an Infinite Impulse Response, IIR, Butterworth filter.  It is not clear which subject comprises “an Infinite Impulse Response, IIR, Butterworth filter”, and it is further unclear what is meant by “an Infinite Impulse Response, IIR, Butterworth filter”. Is it referring to a single type of filtering mechanism – IIR Butterworth, or the combination of two different filtering mechanisms—IIR filter and Butterworth filter.  Is there a difference between IIR filters and Butterworth filters and how they operate? Clarification required. Applicant is requested to make appropriate clarifying amendments to the claim and the Specification.
Claim 25 is rejected by virtue of its dependence on claim 24.

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim 24 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Karapantsios ‘947 in view of Thrower (U.S. 20090281449).
Regarding claim 24, Karapantsios ‘947 teaches all of the elements of the claimed invention except “wherein the step of extracting the impedance time series comprises the step of: filtering the measurement data using a digital zero-phase filter to obtain filtered data comprising an Infinite Impulse Response, IIR, Butterworth filter”.
Thrower, in a related field of endeavor, teaches a method for providing a fundic impedance signal of a subject sensed during an initial calibration period using at least one implantable fundic sensor on the tissue of the stomach of an eating detection device (Abstract), wherein the step of extracting the impedance time series comprises the step of: filtering the measurement data using a digital zero-phase filter to obtain filtered data comprising an Infinite Impulse Response, IIR, Butterworth filter. (Paragraphs [0107]-[0109]: a low-pass IIR filter).
Claim 25 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Karapantsios ‘947 in view of Thrower, as applied to claim 24, further in view of Avidor (WO 2010032252), and Romano (WO 2012032553)..
Regarding claim 25, Karapantsios ‘947 teaches wherein the step of extracting the impedance time series further comprises: digitally processing the filtered data (via Fig. 1, personal computer 7) to obtain recovered data and processing the recovered data to determine the impedance time series. (Paragraph [0007], [0010]).
Thrower, in a related field of endeavor, teaches wherein the digital low-pass filter is Chebyshev Type I IIR filter. (Paragraph [0109] algorithm 100 applies a low-pass filter to impedance measurement 102 by using finite and infinite impulse response digital filter design techniques. For example, the resulting filter can take the form of a Chebyshev topology.)
Therefore, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the invention to have modified Karapantsios ‘947 to provide “wherein the digital low-pass filter is Chebyshev Type I IIR filter” of Thrower. Doing so provides a mechanism to separate one band of frequencies from another at higher speeds and with reduced absolute error. (See The Scientist & Engineer's Guide to Digital Signal Processing, Chapter 20, Chebyshev Filters NPL, https://www.analog.com/en/resources/technical-books/scientist_engineers_guide.html).
However, Karapantsios ‘947 as modified does not teach “generating an envelope signal of the filtered data by calculating absolute data of the filtered data; digitally filtering the envelope signal using a digital low-pass filter with a standard cut-off frequency of 100Hz to obtain the recovered data, wherein the digital low-pass filter is Chebyshev Type I IIR filter”.
Avidor, in a related field of endeavor, teaches a method of diagnosis that comprises generating an envelope signal of the filtered data by calculating absolute data of the filtered data (Page 5, lines 17-19 enveloper identifier used to determine absolute component of an input signal).
Romano, in a related field of endeavor, teaches a method and system for measuring and processing blood pressure starting from a detected pressure signal. (Abstract).
Romano teaches digitally filtering the envelope signal using a digital low-pass filter with a standard cut-off frequency of 100Hz to obtain the recovered data. (Page 8, lines 32-33, Page 9, lines 1-4 method is substantially based on a dynamic low-pass filter applied on a detected pressure signal (e.g., from radial, femoral, aorta, or pulmonary artery), wherein the filter, directly working in the time domain, also takes account of the coupling between pressure line and characteristic dynamic impedance; Page 12, lines 16-20 starting from the thus obtained values of dynamic impedance, the method checks whether the pressure signal constitutes an adequate measurement, and where it does not the method selects the cutoff frequency, preferably ranging from 0,5 Hz to 100 Hz, , of the low-pass filter to apply to the detected pressure signal).
Therefore, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the invention to have modified Karapantsios ‘947 as modified to provide the step of “generating an envelope signal of the filtered data by calculating absolute data of the filtered data” of Avidor and “digitally filtering the envelope signal using a digital low-pass filter with a standard cut-off frequency of 100Hz to obtain the recovered data” as taught by Romano. Doing so provides a method that dynamically adapts to the detection variations. (Romano, Page 12, line 20).
Claim 27 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Karapantsios ‘947 in view of Karapantsios (U.S. 3005942) (cited by Applicant).
Regarding claim 27, Karapantsios ‘947 teaches all of the elements of the claimed invention except “wherein the impedance spectroscopy device comprises a first pair of electrodes and a second pair of the electrodes, wherein the second pair of electrodes is placed between the first pair of electrodes”.
Karapantsios ‘942 teaches an impedance spectroscopy technique wherein the impedance spectroscopy device comprises a first pair of electrodes and a second pair of the electrodes, wherein the second pair of electrodes is placed between the first pair of electrodes. (Paragraph [0049] two electrode pairs may be used to study two-phase flow dynamics).
Therefore, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the invention to have modified Karapantsios ‘947 to provide “wherein the second pair of electrodes is placed between the first pair of electrodes” as taught by Karapantsios ‘942 as the position of the electrodes is a matter of design with no specified criticality and further, it does not modify the operation of the device.
