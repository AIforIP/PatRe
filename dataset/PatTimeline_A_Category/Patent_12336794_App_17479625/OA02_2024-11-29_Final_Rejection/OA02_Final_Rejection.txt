Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of the first paragraph of 35 U.S.C. 112(a):
(a) IN GENERAL.—The specification shall contain a written description of the invention, and of the manner and process of making and using it, in such full, clear, concise, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to which it pertains, or with which it is most nearly connected, to make and use the same, and shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor or joint inventor of carrying out the invention.
The following is a quotation of the first paragraph of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112:
The specification shall contain a written description of the invention, and of the manner and process of making and using it, in such full, clear, concise, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to which it pertains, or with which it is most nearly connected, to make and use the same, and shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor of carrying out his invention.
Claim 1 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(a) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), first paragraph, as failing to comply with the written description requirement. The claim(s) contains subject matter which was not described in the specification in such a way as to reasonably convey to one skilled in the relevant art that the inventor or a joint inventor, or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the inventor(s), at the time the application was filed, had possession of the claimed invention. Claims 2-8 are further rejected due to their dependency to claim 1.
Claim 1 recites “an air leakage testing unit to perform, during the blood pressure measurement to the second transition, air leakage testing on the air system” in lines 19-21. However, the disclosure does not provide support for this limitation. Fig. 4 shows that blood pressure is first measured (steps S1-S13) then the air leakage testing is performed (steps S14-S19) rather than the air leakage testing happening during the blood pressure measurement.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1-3 and 8 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention. Claims 4-7 are further rejected due to their dependency to claim 1.
Claims 1-3 and 8 recite “to make displaying” and/or “to make not displaying” throughout the claims. However, it is unclear as to what it means for the notification control to “make displaying.” Clarification is requested.
The term “more inconspicuous” in claim 1 is a relative term which renders the claim indefinite. The term “more inconspicuous” is not defined by the claim, the specification does not provide a standard for ascertaining the requisite degree, and one of ordinary skill in the art would not be reasonably apprised of the scope of the invention. It is unclear what it means for the display mode to be “more inconspicuous” or “inconspicuous.” Clarification is requested.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Section 33(a) of the America Invents Act reads as follows:
Notwithstanding any other provision of law, no patent may issue on a claim directed to or encompassing a human organism.
Claim 5 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 and section 33(a) of the America Invents Act as being directed to or encompassing a human organism.  See also Animals - Patentability, 1077 Off. Gaz. Pat. Office 24 (April 21, 1987) (indicating that human organisms are excluded from the scope of patentable subject matter under 35 U.S.C. 101). Claim 5 recites “the measurement air bag…to compress an upper arm as the to-be-measured part” in lines 9-11, which is claiming a human organism. Claim 1 recites that the “cuff is configured to surround a to-be-measured part” in lines 3-4. To follow the claim language of claim 1, Examiner suggests to amend lines 9-11 of claim 5 to read as “the measurement bag…configured to compress an upper arm as the to-be-measured part.”
Response to Arguments
Applicant’s arguments with respect to the 35 U.S.C. 101 rejection have been fully considered and are persuasive. The Examiner has performed a new 101 analysis on the amended claim 1. According to STEP 1 of the 101 analysis, the claim is directed to a machine, which is one of the statutory categories of invention. In STEP 2A, PRONG ONE, the claim is analyzed to determine whether it is directed to any judicial exception. The steps of the “to perform main control a first transition from a power-off state to a standby state in response to a power-on operation, to make a second transition to the standby state after the blood pressure measurement, and to make a third transition to the power-off state in response to a power-off operation in the standby state; and to perform, during the blood pressure measurement to the second transition, air leakage testing on the air system to obtain an air leakage testing result of the air leakage testing, and to cause the memory to store the air leaking testing result are steps that could be performed in the human mind or by hand. Thus, the claim is drawn to a mental process, which is an abstract idea. It is noted that these steps are performed by the main control unit and the air leakage testing unit. According to 2106.05(f) of the MPEP, merely using a computer as a tool to perform an abstract idea does not integrate the abstract idea into a practical application. In STEP 2A, PRONG TWO, the claim as a whole is analyzed to determine whether the claim recites additional elements that integrate the judicial exception into a practical application. Claim 1 recites “to perform notification control to control display contents on the display based on the air leakage testing result stored in the memory… when the air leakage testing result is the second air leakage level,to make displaying, on the display, the air leakage testing result in a first display mode during the first transition,to make, in the standby state, displaying, on the display, that the blood pressure measurement is enabled without displaying the air leakage testing result or with displayingthe air leakage testing result in a second display mode more inconspicuous than the first display mode,to make not displaying the air leakage testing result during the blood pressure measurement and the second transition, andto make displaying, on the display, the air leakage testing result in a first display mode during the third transition,” which is merely adding insignificant extra-solution activity to the judicial exception (MPEP 2106.05(g)). The display of the air leakage testing result does not provide an improvement to the technological field, the machine does not effect a particular treatment or effect a particular change based on the displayed air leakage testing result, nor does the machine use a particular machine to perform the abstract idea. In STEP 2B, the claim as a whole is analyzed to determine whether any element, or combination of elements, is sufficient to ensure the claim amounts to significantly more than the exception. Besides the abstract idea, the claim recites the additional steps of to perform blood pressure measurement by driving the p ump to feed an air to the cuff through the first fluid pipe to pressurize the cuff or driving the valve to exhaust the hair from the cuff through the second fluid pipe to depressurize the cuff in response to a measurement start operation in the standby state, which is insignificant pre-solution activity of data gathering. The structural elements of the claim 1 were analyzed under the Berkheimer analysis as the structures are additional elements. Although Tsutsumi et al. ‘245 (US Pub No. 2019/0365245) teaches that the claimed cuff, pump, valve, processor, memory, and display are publically known (Fig. 1 and [0030]), there was no reference that teaches that these structures in combination with the air leakage testing unit is WURC. As such, the 101 analysis ends here and no 101 rejection was made.
Applicant’s arguments with respect to the 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) and 35 U.S.C. 103 rejections have been fully considered and are persuasive.  The 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) rejection of claim 1 has been withdrawn. Although it was found that Mori et al. ‘950 teaches the claimed structure, the main control unit and the steps performed by the main control unit, and the notification control unit, the steps performed by the notification control unit, and the air leakage testing unit, it was found that Mori et al. ‘950 does not teach the air leakage testing unit to perform the claimed steps during the blood pressure measurement to the second transition. An updated search and consideration was performed. Although the Examiner has found art that teaches performing an air leakage test in sphygmomanometers (such as Sawanoi et al. ‘221 (US Pub No. 2011/0282221), there was no art that was found to teach performing the air leakage test during the blood pressure measurement to the second transition. As such, the art rejection has been withdrawn.
