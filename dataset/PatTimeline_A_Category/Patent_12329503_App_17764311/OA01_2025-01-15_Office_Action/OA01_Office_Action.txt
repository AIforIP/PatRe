Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Claim 26 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because the claimed invention is directed to non-statutory subject matter.  The claim(s) does/do not fall within at least one of the four categories of patent eligible subject matter because the claim is directed towards “A computer program comprising instructions…” which does not have physical form. Applicant could consider directing the claim to “A non-transitory computer readable medium having a computer program stored thereon comprising instructions…” to place the claimed invention within one of the statutory categories. Examiner notes that the first paragraph of specification page 7 refers to such a structure.
Allowable Subject Matter
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance: Applicant cites several references the concern measurement of photoplethysmography (PPG) signals and analysis of quality thereof. In particular, Commissariat Energie Atomique (EP‘999) teaches a method and system for evaluating the quality of ratio of ratio values of PPG signals (paragraph [0017], [0069]). However EP‘999 only performs measurements at two wavelengths (paragraph [0047]) and analyzes results therefrom (paragraph [0059]) and thus, there is no teaching or suggestion to include measurements of a third wavelength and to extract a sequence of heartbeat instants therefrom which are used when segmenting the first and second PPG signals. Additionally, Baker, Jr (USPGPub 20140031652 – generally concerned with assessing signal quality) and Ghosh et al. (USPN 2018/0325457 – segmenting in relation to concurrently measured ECG signal) disclose inventions that collect PPG signals and analyze quality thereof. But, like EP’999 there is no teaching or suggestion of capturing a third PPG signal for providing an input for segmenting first and second PPG signals.   Further, Albadawi et al. (USPGPub 2016/0360984) teaches a PPG signal quality analysis arrangement that analyzes shape features of the PPG signal (paragraphs [0026] – [0033]) for determining quality parameters (paragraphs [0040] – [0049]). However, the prior art does not teach or suggest a method or system for evaluating quality or ratio of ratios values computed from PPG signals, wherein a sequence of heartbeat instants are extracted from a third PPG signal, and then first and second PPG signals are segmented into a plurality of signal segments using the extracted heartbeat instants, such that a sequence of ratio values may be computed and evaluating a quality index therefrom, in combination with the other claimed steps or elements.
Claims 15 – 25, 27, and 28 are allowed.
