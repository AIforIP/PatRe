Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
Claims 1-32 rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
Regarding claims 1 and 24, as written these claims read “for at least one of the 3D representation(s), determine(ing) a mean curvature of at least a region of a surface of the dorsal portion and/or a mean curvature of at least a region of a surface of the ventral portion” (Claims 1 and 24). The recitation of “and/or” makes it unclear whether 1) just one or the mean curvatures needs to be calculated or 2) both mean curvatures need to be calculated. The examiner recommends clarifying whether the method requires just one or both of the mean curvatures to be calculated. For the sake of examination, the examiner will be interpretating the claim to mean that just one of the mean curvatures is required.
Additionally, just looking at the independent claims it is unclear what the mean curvature calculations are used for. The examiner notes that in claim 3, for example, the method includes “characterizing whether the patient has multiple sclerosis and/or a type of multiple sclerosis in the patient based at least on part on the mean curvature of the region of the surface of the dorsal portion of the first section and/or the mean curvature of the region of the surface of the ventral portion of the first section”. The examiner recommends amending the independent claims such that is it clear what the mean curvature is used for within the context of the method and system to analyze the one or more sections of a central nervous system of a patient.
Regarding claims 2-23 and 25-32, due to their dependence on claims 1 and 24, respectively, these claims are subject to the reasoning provided therein. Furthermore, these claims do not cure the deficiencies of the independent claims.

Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Claims 1-32 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because the claimed invention is directed to a judicial exception in the form of an abstract idea, specifically a mental process, without significantly more.
Regarding claims 1 and 24, the examiner notes that the claims are directed to: 1) a method for analyzing one or more sections of a central nervous system of a patient (Claim 1) and 2) a system for analyzing one or more sections of a central nervous system of a patient (Claim 24). Therefore, the claims fall within the statutory categories of invention.
With reference to Step 2A, Prong One, the claim recites “segmenting each of the 3D representation(s) into ventral and dorsal portions; and for at least one of the 3D representation(s), determining a mean curvature of at least a region of a surface of the dorsal portion and/or a mean curvature of at least a region of a surface of the ventral portion”.
The limitations, under broadest reasonable interpretation, cover performance of the limitation in the mind and/or read on analyzing an image. In this case, “segmenting each of the 3D representation(s) into ventral and dorsal portions” can be achieved by a user viewing a 3D image and selecting/deciding which sections represent the ventral and dorsal portions via a user interface (i.e. touchscreen, cursor, buttons, and the like). Furthermore, determining the mean curvature can be performed in the human mind by performing a calculation of an average (i.e. mean) by: 1) mental math; 2) pen/paper. A claim limitation under its broadest reasonable interpretation covers performance of the limitation in the mind but for the recitation of generic computer components (i.e. a processor), then it falls within the “mental processes” grouping of abstract ideas.
Following step 2A, Prong Two of the two-prong analysis, the claims do not recite any additional elements to integrate the judicial exception into a practical application because the claim as written does not include elements to 1) improve the functioning of a computer (See MPEP 2106.05(a)); 2) effect a particular treatment or prophylaxis (See MPEP 2106.04(d)(2)); 3) use a particular machine (See MPEP 2106.05(b)); 4) use the judicial exceptions in a meaningful way beyond generally linking the use to a particular technological environment (See MPEP 2106.05(h)).
Following step 2B, the claims do not include additional element(s) to amount to significantly more than the judicial exception.

Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1-32 would be allowable if rewritten or amended to overcome the rejection(s) under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) set forth in this Office action.
The following is a statement of reasons for the indication of allowable subject matter:
Regarding claims 1, 3, 24 and 25, the claims recite “A method of analyzing one or more sections of a central nervous system of a patient, the method comprising: […] segmenting each of the 3D representation(s) into ventral and dorsal portions; and for at least one of the 3D representation(s), determining a mean curvature of at least a region of a surface of the dorsal portion and/or a mean curvature of at least a region of a surface of the ventral portion“ (Claim 1); “[…] comprising characterizing whether the patient has multiple sclerosis and/or a type of multiple sclerosis in the patient based at least in part on the mean curvature of the region of the surface of the dorsal portion of the first section and/or the mean curvature of the region of the surface of the ventral portion of the first section” (Claim 3); “A system for analyzing one or more sections of a central nervous system of a patient, the system comprising one or more processors configured to: […] segment each of the 3D representation(s) into ventral and dorsal portions; and for at least one of the 3D representation(s), determine a mean curvature of at least a region of a surface of the dorsal portion and/or a mean curvature of at least a region of a surface of the ventral portion […]” (Claim 24); “[…] the processor(s) are configured to characterize whether the patient has multiple sclerosis and/or a type of multiple sclerosis in the patient based at least in part on an ethnicity of the patient and: the mean curvature of the region of the surface of the dorsal portion of the first section; and/or the mean curvature of the region of the surface of the ventral portion of the first section” (Claim 25).
During the examiner’s search the following prior art references were found which are pertinent to the claimed invention: Butson et al. US 2013/0150922 A1 “Butson” and Hubbard US 2012/0277572 A1 “Hubbard”.
Butson is pertinent to the claimed invention because it discloses “One embodiment provided a computer-implemented method that includes storing a volume of tissue activation (VTA) data structure that is derived from analysis of a plurality of patients. Patient data is received for a given patient, the patient data representing an assessment of a patient condition. The VTA data structure is evaluated relative to the patient data to determine a target VTA for achieving a desired therapeutic effect for the given patient” [Abstract]; “The invention relates generally to systems and methods for determining a target volume of tissue activation (VTA) for stimulation of a patient’s tissue, such as neural tissue” [0038]; “FIG. 1 depicts an example of a system 10 that can be employed to determine a target VTA. The system 10 is shown as including a computer 12 that employs data and program methods to determine a target VTA 14 for a given patient according to an aspect of the invention” [0042]; “The computer 12 includes a processor 16 that executes instructions programmed for performing the methods described herein” [0043]; “As used herein, a VTA represents an anatomical region of tissue, such as a three-dimensional volume of tissue that includes cells of the nervous system. For example, the tissue can be neural tissue in the brain or other part of the central nervous system, such as the spinal cord” [0044]. Therefore, Butson describes a system and method for analyzing one or more sections of a central nervous system of a patient (i.e. brain and other parts of the central nervous system such as the spinal cord), the system comprising one or more processors (i.e. processor 16);
“for each of the section(s), from data that includes one or more 3-dimensional (3D) representations of the section:” (Claims 1 and 24) (See [0044] above. Therefore, the target volume of tissue activation (TVA) represents data that includes one or more 3-dimensional (3D) representations of the section.);
“segment(ing) each of the 3D representation(s) into ventral and dorsal portions” (Claims 1 and 24) (“As an example, a plurality (e.g., two or more, such as five) of the top scoring electrode designs 204 for the target VTA 206 can be subjected to further analysis including scoring. For example, the electrode location and trajectory can be incrementally adjusted (e.g. relative to the geometric center of the target VTA) in the dorsal/ventral, anterior/posterior, and medial/lateral direction) and the resulting design VTAs 216 can be scored according to the sub-optimal electrode placements” [0116]. Therefore, the method involves segmenting each of the 3D representation(s) into ventral and dorsal portions for performing analysis/scoring.).
However, Butson does not teach “for at least one of the 3D representation(s), determine(ing) a mean curvature of at least: a region of a surface of the dorsal portion and/or a mean curvature of at least a region of a surface of the ventral portion, wherein each of the section(s) includes at least a portion of a brainstem of the patient” (Claims 1 and 24); “[…] comprising characterizing whether the patient has multiple sclerosis and/or a type of multiple sclerosis in the patient based at least in part on the mean curvature of the region of the surface of the dorsal portion of the first section and/or the mean curvature of the region of the surface of the ventral portion of the first section” (Claim 3) or “[…] the processor(s) are configured to characterize whether the patient has multiple sclerosis and/or a type of multiple sclerosis in the patient based at least in part on an ethnicity of the patient and: the mean curvature of the region of the surface of the dorsal portion of the first section; and/or the mean curvature of the region of the surface of the ventral portion of the first section” (Claim 25).
Hubbard is pertinent to the claimed invention because it discloses “wherein each of the section(s) includes at least a portion of a brainstem of the patient” (Claims 1 and 24) (“Finally, in step 400 the HDR graph is analyzed to determine if the HDR response is abnormal and vascular drainage insufficiency in the central nervous system can be identified based on the abnormal HDR response. […] Components of the central nervous system that can be diagnoses include the cerebral cortex, sub-cortical white matter, thalamus, basal ganglia, brainstem, and the spinal cord” [0073].).
However, Hubbard does not teach “for at least one of the 3D representation(s), determine(ing) a mean curvature of at least: a region of a surface of the dorsal portion and/or a mean curvature of at least a region of a surface of the ventral portion, wherein each of the section(s) includes at least a portion of a brainstem of the patient” (Claims 1 and 24), “[…] comprising characterizing whether the patient has multiple sclerosis and/or a type of multiple sclerosis in the patient based at least in part on the mean curvature of the region of the surface of the dorsal portion of the first section and/or the mean curvature of the region of the surface of the ventral portion of the first section” (Claim 3) or “[…] the processor(s) are configured to characterize whether the patient has multiple sclerosis and/or a type of multiple sclerosis in the patient based at least in part on an ethnicity of the patient and: the mean curvature of the region of the surface of the dorsal portion of the first section; and/or the mean curvature of the region of the surface of the ventral portion of the first section” (Claim 25).
Therefore, no prior art references have been found to teach the claimed limitations both alone or in combination with the other limitations of the claim(s).
Thus, claims 1-32 would be allowable if rewritten or amended to overcome the rejections under 35 U.S.C. 101 and 35 U.S.C. 112(b) set forth in this office action.
