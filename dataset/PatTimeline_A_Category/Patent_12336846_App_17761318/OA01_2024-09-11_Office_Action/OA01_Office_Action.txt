Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 29, 33 and 39 rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
Regarding claims 29 and 33:
Claim 29 recites the limitation said at least one optical detector in the limitation
“wherein, in electronically processing the output signal of said at least one optical detector, said controller is configured to perform an electronic selection of a reference window of output signal sampling”.  There is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim.
Claim 33 recites the limitation said at least one optical detector in the limitation “and wherein the controller is configured to multiply the output signal of said at least one optical detector with said pump-associated signal so as to make a result signal produced in an output of said processing cyclically zeroed in correspondence of portions of times wherein said pump provides pulses in at least said segment”.  There is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim.
Claim 29 depends on claims 24-25 and claim 33 depends on claim 24. None of these claims recite “at least one optical detector” before this limitation is recited in these claims. Therefore, there is no antecedent basis for these claims. However, claim 24 does recite “detectors placed at different radial directions, or arranged at different angular degrees, with respect to the emission axis of the source to collect a reflected signal”. These detectors, in the context of claim 24 are interpreted as including optical detectors as these detectors in claim 24 are collecting a reflected signal from a source and claim 24 further describes this source as “directing an optical signal”. However, claims 29 and 33 do not refer to optical detectors (i.e. plural), they refer to the broader language of “at least one optical detector” (i.e. one or more) so this raises questions of interpretation. Is applicant referring to an additional optical detector(s) from the detectors first recited in claim 24, a subset of these detectors first recited in claims 24, attempting to broaden detectors to now include one or more detectors, just attempting to refer to the detectors first recited in claim 24 or something else? The broadest reasonable interpretation and the one taken for this examination is that applicant is referring to the detectors first recited in claim 24. Thus, claim 29 is interpreted as if written:“wherein, in electronically processing the output signal of said detectors, said controller is configured to perform an electronic selection of a reference window of output signal sampling”
And claim 33 is interpreted as if written:
“and wherein the controller is configured to multiply the output signal of said detectors with said pump-associated signal so as to make a result signal produced in an output of said processing cyclically zeroed in correspondence of portions of times wherein said pump provides pulses in at least said segment”
Regarding claim 39
Claim 39 recites the following limitation(s):
“wherein the sensor further comprises:
an optic fiber having one end coupled with the source and the other end placed to direct the emitted signal towards the blood along at least said predetermined emission axis;
one or more of (i) a first detector placed at about 180° with respect to the emission axis of the source, (ii) a second detector placed at about 90° with respect to the emission axis of the source, (iii) a third detector placed at about 45° with respect to the emission axis of the source, or (iv) a fourth detector placed at about 0° with respect to the emission axis of the source;”
Essentially, applicant appears to be further limiting the heart beat sensor first recited in independent claim 24 on which claim 39 is dependent on. However, claim 24 already recites the heart beat sensor includes detectors at different angles. For example, claim 24 recites the following:
“wherein said non-invasive heartbeat sensor comprises: at least one source for directing an optical signal towards the blood flowing in the extracorporeal segment, the optical signal being directed along an emission axis, detectors placed at different radial directions, or arranged at different angular degrees, with respect to the emission axis of the source to collect a reflected signal, a scattered signal and a transmitted signal depending on their respective position”
The way claim is written raises questions of interpretation. Is applicant attempting to further limit the detectors of the heartbeat sensor first recited in claim 24 or reciting additional detectors from the those first recited in claim 24? The broadest reasonable interpretation and one taken for this examination is that applicant is further describing the detectors first recited in claim 24 as including one or more of the listed sensors.
Thus, cited limitation(s) of claim 39 is interpreted as if written as the following:
“wherein the sensor further comprises:
an optic fiber having one end coupled with the source and the other end placed to direct the emitted signal towards the blood along at least said predetermined emission axis;
wherein said detectors include one or more of (i) a first detector placed at about 180° with respect to the emission axis of the source, (ii) a second detector placed at about 90° with respect to the emission axis of the source, (iii) a third detector placed at about 45° with respect to the emission axis of the source, or (iv) a fourth detector placed at about 0° with respect to the emission axis of the source;”

a blood pump configured to pump blood [see Fig. 3 element 3 and pg. 8 lines 35-37… “a blood pump 3”];
a blood circuit [see Fig. 3 element 20 and pg. 9 lines 18-20… “the blood flow in the circuit 20”] comprising:
a filtration unit [see Fig. 3 element 6 and pg. 9 lines 4-10… “the dialyser 6. The blood is lead via a post-dialyser tube segment 10 from the blood-side of the dialyser 6 to a venous drip chamber or deaeration chamber 11 and from there back to the subject via the return tube segment 12 and return needle 14.” ] having a primary chamber and a secondary chamber separated by a semi-permeable membrane [see Fig. 3 element 6 which shows a line with arrows showing some level of permeability (i.e. semi-permeable membrane) between two chambers (i.e. primary and secondary chambers)],
a blood withdrawal line [see Fig. 3 element 2 and pg. 9 lines 1-3… “withdrawal tube segment 2”] having a first end connected to an inlet of the primary chamber [see Fig. 3 showing element 2 fluidly connected via other elements to element 6 (thereby reciting a first end connected to an inlet as claimed) ], and
a blood return line [see Fig. 3 element 12 and pg. 9 lines 6-9… “the return tube segment 12”] having a first end connected to an outlet of the primary chamber [see Fig. 3 showing element 12 fluidly connected via other elements to element 6 (thereby reciting a first end connected to an outlet as claimed)],

a non-invasive heartbeat sensor configured and arranged to determine one or both of a heartbeat in the extracorporeal segment and a heart rate in the extracorporeal segment [see Fig. 3 elements 25 and 26 and pg. 45 lines 25-30… “The surveillance device 25 could be configured to collect and store data on the time evolution of the heart rate”],
wherein said non-invasive heartbeat sensor comprises:
a detector configured to receive an optical informative signal [see Fig. 3 element 26 and pg. 9 lines 30-35… “the device 25 may be connected to a pump sensor 26, such as a rotary encoder (e.g. conductive, optical or magnetic) or the like, for indicating the frequency and phase of the blood pump 3”], and
a controller [see Fig. 3 element 23 and pg. 9 lines 16-24… “Further in Fig. 3, a control unit 23 is provided, i.a., to control the blood flow in the circuit 20 by controlling the revolution speed of the blood pump 3. The extracorporeal blood 20 flow circuit 20 and the control unit 23”] configured to:
receive the respective output signals from the detector, at least one of retrieve a heartbeat frequency and detect a heart rate value based on a predetermined number of the output signals, and [see Fig. 3 and pg. 9… “The device 25 may also be connected any additional pressure sensors included in the extracorporeal blood flow circuit 20. As indicated in Fig. 3, the device 30 25 may also be connected to the control unit 23” and pg. 45 lines 25-35… “The surveillance device 25 could be configured to monitor the heart rate of the patient, by identifying heart pulses in the monitoring signal. The surveillance device 25 could be configured to collect and store data on the time evolution of the heart rate, the breathing pattern, etc, e.g. for subsequent trending or statistical analysis.”]
process the output signals based on the informative signal as altered at least in its amplitude, by a flow perturbation of the blood flowing in the segment, wherein said flow perturbation being at least partially generated by flow impulses originated by a beating heart [see pg. 5 lines 24-26… “In one embodiment, the calculating of a fluid flow rate (Q) involves a pulse parameter P from one or more of amplitude, shape, and timing of the temporal signal profile. However, while Olde discloses a detector that is configured to detect optical signals, Olde fails to discloses a source for directing an optical signal or multiple detectors positioned at different radial directions or angular degrees as claimed. Thus Olde fails to fully disclose the heartbeat sensor comprises: “at least one source for directing an optical signal towards the blood flowing in the extracorporeal segment, the optical signal being directed along an emission axis” or “detectors placed at different radial directions, or arranged at different angular degrees, with respect to the emission axis of the source to collect a reflected signal, a scattered signal and a transmitted signal depending on their respective position, and wherein each detector is configured to receive an optical informative signal comprising the signal emitted by said source after passing at least partially the blood flowing in the extracorporeal segment, wherein each detector is configured to emit a respective output signal related to the received optical informative signal”. Furthermore, nothing in the prior art when viewed with Olde obviates these deficiencies. It is important to note that no limitation by itself is renders the claim allowable by itself. Rather, it is the combination of all the claim limitations of claim 24 together that define the invention over the prior art. Therefore, the combination of claimed limitations recited by claim 24 is neither anticipated, nor obviated in view of the prior art.
