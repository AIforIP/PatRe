Authorization for this examiner’s amendment was given in an interview with Roy Gross on January 30, 2025. Examiner noted that the details set forth in claims 2 and 9 should be associated with the “graphical representation” disclosed in the corresponding base claim for better clarity. Further, Examiner noted that the dependencies of claims 5 and 12 should be updated to provide proper antecedent basis for all claim terms. Applicant agreed to the suggested amendments. Further, Examiner noted that the claims of the instant application were generally aligned with those of the parent patent (12,029,529) such that a non-statutory Double Patenting rejection could be made over the patent document. Applicant agreed to file a Terminal Disclaimer to overcome the proposed rejection and to permit the application to be allowed at this time.

The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance: Applicant cites several references directed towards measuring thermal data and analysis thereof. Of particular relevance, Silva et al. (“Hybrid Analysis for Indicating Patients with Breast Cancer using Temperature Time Series” - cited by Applicant) teach a method, corresponding computer program product, and system including: receiving thermal data, wherein the data is sampled over time while the tissue is being thermally disturbed (see Section 1/Introduction discussing introducing thermal stress for dynamic infrared thermography (DIT)); deriving from the thermal data a set of features associated with each of a plurality of locations within the tissue (via time series for each pixel - Section 3.2 - Construction of the temperature time series); deriving sets of features (see Section 3.3 - Clustering of the temperature time series and evaluation of the clusters); and generating an output indicating said tissue features.  Additionally, Leschinsky et al. (USPGPub 2014/0236020) teach a device and method for non-contact thermographic mapping of tissue viability. Musin (USPGPub 2020/0020584) teaches a non-invasive glucose monitoring arrangement that relies upon assessment of thermal effect measurements. However, none of the prior art teaches or suggests, collecting thermal data from a tissue over both a first time period in which heat is applied to the tissue and a second time period during which the tissue passively returns to a base temperature and deriving a set of thermal-distribution related features, including features for both the first time period and the second time period, with respect to each of a plurality of locations within the tissue from the thermal data, in combination with the other claimed steps or elements.
