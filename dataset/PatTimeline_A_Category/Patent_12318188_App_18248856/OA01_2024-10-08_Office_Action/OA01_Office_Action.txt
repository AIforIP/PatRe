Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claims 1-3, 5-7, and 9-15 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) as being anticipated by Fisker et al (US Pub 2012/0092461 -cited by applicant).
Re claims 1, 13: Fisker discloses a portable three-dimensional image measuring device and method [0023, 0244; see the handheld apparatus for providing 3D surface measurement] comprising:
a light source configured to output patterned light which irradiates an object [0035, 0183, 0244; see light source 110 that outputs patterned light];
a camera configured to generate a light field image of the object by receiving reflected light generated by reflecting the patterned light from the object [0244; see camera 180 that receives the reflected light]; and
an optical path control element configured to reflect the patterned light output from the light source so that the object is irradiated with the patterned light, and to transmit the reflected light reflected from the object so that the reflected light reaches the camera [0244; see the beam splitter 140 that transmits light to the object and transmits the reflected light to the camera],
wherein an optical path of the patterned light output from the light source and irradiated onto the object and an optical path of the reflected light reflected from the object and reaching the camera overlap coaxially in a section between the optical path control element and the object (Figure 1; see the optical path portion between the beam splitter 140 and the object 200 which includes a coaxial reflection/transmission beam).
The above citations further provide for a 3D image measuring method including irradiating the object and generating the light field image.
Re claims 2, 14: The device comprising: a communication circuit; and a processor configured to: generate a three-dimensional image of a surface of the object using the light field image of the object acquired through the camera; and transmit the three-dimensional image of the surface of the object to an external electronic device through the communication circuit [0074, 0183-0192; see the 3D scanning where transfer to an external device (i.e. workstation) is anticipated and where a processor is included to analyze at least one image].
Re claims 3, 15: The three-dimensional image of the surface of the object is an image used for matching with a medical image of the object [0090, 0179; see the registration and stitching of images].
Re claim 5: The light source and the camera are disposed in a direction perpendicular to each other based on the optical path control element (Figure 1; see the source 110 and camera 180 that are perpendicular relative to the control element).
Re claim 6: The camera includes: a lens array in which a plurality of micro lenses are arranged; and an image sensor that captures the reflected light passed through the lens array [0244; see the lens system of camera 180 with image sensor 181].
Re claim 7: The light source includes: a pattern unit in which a plurality of patterns are formed; and an LED that irradiates the pattern unit with light [0072, 0244; see the LED and output pattern 130].
Re claim 9: The optical path control element is a transflective mirror [0042, 0060; see the beam splitter that reflects in both directions].
Re claim 10: The light field image is an image in which a plurality of sub-images including color information and direction information of the reflected light are combined [0151-0158; see the color scanning that includes combining and/or weighting the color information of the plural sensor elements].
Re claims 11, 12: The device further comprising: a first housing in which the light source, the camera, and the optical path control element are disposed; and a second housing coupled to the first housing and formed with an opening through which the object is irradiated with the patterned light output from the light source [0244, 0284, Figure 1; see a first housing 100 and second housing (i.e. housing 150), or, alternatively see Figure 12 with first housing 1001 and second housing1002 that are rotatably coupled].
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim 4 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Fisker, as applied to claim 2, in view of Homan et al (US Pub 2020/0281660 -cited by applicant).
Re claim 4: Fisker discloses all features except for a marker installed to be movable from a predetermined location of the portable three-dimensional image measuring device, wherein the processor is configured to transmit information indicating displacement of the marker from the predetermined location to the external electronic device. However, Homan teaches of a marker installed to be movable from a predetermined location of the portable three-dimensional image measuring device, wherein the processor is configured to transmit information indicating displacement of the marker from the predetermined location to the external electronic device (Abstract; see the marker 14 wherein an offset 26 is determined which indicates displacement). It would have been obvious to the skilled artisan to modify Fisker, to use a marker as taught by Homan, improve measurement accuracy via determination of the device position.
Claim 8 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Fisker, as applied to claim 7, in view of Jian et al (CN 110720986 -cited by applicant).
Re claim 8: Fisker discloses all features except that the light output by the LED is infrared light. However, Jian teaches of a surgical navigation system for obtaining a 3D image wherein infrared light 31 is used as a light source [0037; see the source 31]. It would have been obvious to the skilled artisan to modify Fisker, to output infrared light as taught by Jian, as such is a well known type of light source for irradiating an object and such would provide an enhanced view of a target.
