Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1-11, 13-14, 16-22 are allowed.
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance: The specific limitations of (with respect to Claims 1, 13, 14) “receiving a login request for logging into a game sent by a client computing device, wherein the login request carries a login account; determining at least two groups of corresponding network addresses based on the login account; determining whether the client computing device belongs to a lock region based on a lock region configuration corresponding to the game and the at least two groups of corresponding network addresses, wherein the lock region indicates one or more regions in which at least a part of the game is not accessible by client computing devices belonging to the lock region; and in response to determining that the client device does not belong to the lock region, performing login based on the login request”, in combination with the remainder of the respective claims are not anticipated or made obvious over the prior art of record in the Examiner’s opinion.
A game login method is well known in the art. For instance, Vaughan (2014/0045596) in view of O’Hare et al. (2021/0126940) teaches a game login method. However, Vaughan in view of O’Hare is silent on “receiving a login request for logging into a game sent by a client computing device, wherein the login request carries a login account; determining at least two groups of corresponding network addresses based on the login account; determining whether the client computing device belongs to a lock region based on a lock region configuration corresponding to the game and the at least two groups of corresponding network addresses, wherein the lock region indicates one or more regions in which at least a part of the game is not accessible by client computing devices belonging to the lock region; and in response to determining that the client device does not belong to the lock region, performing login based on the login request”.
As a result, in the Examiner’s opinion, the present invention is not anticipated or made obvious over the prior art.
