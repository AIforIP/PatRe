Response to Arguments
Applicant’s arguments dated 27 September 2024 have been fully considered, but they are not persuasive or moot because the new ground of rejection does not rely on any reference applied in the prior rejection of record for any teaching or matter specifically challenged in the argument.
The Examiner has addressed the amended limitations in the updated text of the rejection below.
Applicant argues that “Chen does not teach or suggest sense the generated signal from the at least one textile- based ECG generator electrode via the one or more textile-based ECG receiver electrode as recited by amended claims 11 and 29” (Arguments, p. 7).  The Examiner has addressed the amended limitations in the updated text below.  The Examiner also respectfully notes that the Chahine reference, not Chen, was used to teach or suggest similar limitations in the previous Office Action.
Applicant argues that “Chen does not teach or suggest based on sensing the generated signal... deselect the at least one determined textile-based ECG electrode from the electrode pairing, and select another textile-based ECG electrode pairing, repeat said generate and said sense of the generated signal using the another textile-based ECG electrode pairing as recited by amended claims 11 and 29” (Arguments, pp. 7-8).  The Examiner has addressed the amended limitations in the updated text below.  Regarding the non-amended portions of the limitations, the Examiner respectfully disagrees and directs Applicant at least to Chen’s paragraphs [0044] - [0046] and [0048], which describe an electrode selection algorithm which changes the sensing electrodes if it is determined that the signal quality is low, for example by considering “contact between the skin and the electrodes” (Chen, [0046]).  Therefore, the Examiner respectfully maintains that Chen describes deselecting one electrode and selecting a different electrode based on the sensed signal.

Claim 17 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Chahine in view of Chen, further in view of Kaib et al. (US 2011/0288605 A1).
Regarding claim 17, Chahine in view of Chen suggests the smart garment of claim 11, but neither Chahine nor Chen explicitly disclose wherein the controller determines a change in a desired location of one or more of the plurality of the textile-based ECG electrodes.  However, Kaib also describes a smart garment for detecting physiological data of a user ([0008]), including a controller configured to determine a change in a location of one or more of a plurality of textile-based electrodes ([0091], determining if the electrodes have shifted or moved to a slightly different location).  As Kaib is also directed towards detecting physiological data of a user and is in a similar field of endeavor, it would have been obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art at the time the invention was filed to use Kaib’s feature of determining whether electrodes have changed locations when using the system described by Chahine and Chen, as doing so advantageously allows the resulting system to account for the electrode movements and select a different set of electrodes, if necessary, in order to obtain the highest quality ECG signal.
Claim 24 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Chahine in view of Chen, further in view of Rapin et al. (US 2019/0223739 A1).
Regarding claim 24, Chahine in view of Chen suggests the smart garment of claim 11, but neither Chahine nor Chen explicitly disclose wherein the controller uses physiological data sensed by the plurality of textile-based ECG electrodes to determine a quantity of the user selected from the group consisting of a PR interval, a QT interval, a corrected QT (QTc) interval, a PR axis, and a QRS axis.  However, Rapin also describes analyzing ECG data for monitoring a user’s health ([0002]), including using a controller to determine a PR interval, a QT interval, a corrected QT (QTc) interval, a PR axis, and/or a QRS axis of a user ([0020], [0060]).  As Rapin is also directed towards using ECG data for health analysis and is in a similar field of endeavor, it would have been obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art at the time the invention was filed to use measures similar to those described by Rapin when analyzing the data generated by the textile-based ECG electrodes suggested by Chahine and Chen, as doing so advantageously allows the resulting system to better characterize a patient’s health status and suggest corrective action when necessary.
