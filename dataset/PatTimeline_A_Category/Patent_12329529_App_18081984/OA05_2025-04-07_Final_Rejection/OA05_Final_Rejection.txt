Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 11-30 are allowed.
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance.
The prior art of record does not disclose or suggest a smart garment including a plurality of textile-based ECG electrode pairs and a controller configured to perform all of the recited steps.  In particular, the prior art of record does not disclose or suggest at least one textile-based ECG generator electrode for transmitting a controller-generated signal generated by a controller device through the skin of the user and at least one textile-based ECG receiver electrode for sensing the controller-generated signal through the skin of the user.  Analogous prior art includes Chahine et al. (US 2017/0056644 A1) and Chen et al. (US 2010/0041975 A1), both of which have been discussed at length in previous Office Actions.
Briefly, Chahine describes a smart garment ([0020]) comprising a plurality of textile-based ECG generator and receiver electrodes ([0026], [0028]) and a controller configured to generate and transmit a signal ([0004], [0026]).  Chen describes determining if an electrode pairing is at least partially out of contact with the user’s skin or causes the transmitted signal to be of unacceptable signal quality ([0011], [0041]), further deselecting a particular pair of electrodes and selecting a different pair of electrodes in response to such a determination ([0044] - [0045]).  However, Chahine, Chen, and the other prior art of record use sensing electrodes to sense physiological signals produced by the user’s body, as opposed to the controller-generated signals transmitted by ECG generator electrodes.  Other prior art documents which generally describe electrode pairs configured to generate and sense signals do not perform these steps as part of an overall smart garment comprising textile-based ECG electrodes similar to those recited in the pending claims, nor do they comprise a controller device configured to carry out steps similar to those recited in the pending claims.  The prior art also lacks the requisite suggestion and motivation to incorporate these paired electrodes into a device similar to that described by Chahine or Chen.
Therefore, the inventive features recited in the pending claims are not disclosed by the prior art and are not suggested by an obvious combination of the most analogous prior art elements.
