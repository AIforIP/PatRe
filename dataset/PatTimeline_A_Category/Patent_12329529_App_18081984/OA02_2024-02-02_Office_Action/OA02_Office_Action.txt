Response to Arguments
Applicant’s arguments dated 20 December 2023 have been fully considered, but they are not persuasive or moot because the new ground of rejection does not rely on any reference applied in the prior rejection of record for any teaching or matter specifically challenged in the argument.
Applicant has amended independent claim 11 to recite that the electrodes make contact with the skin of the user and to add features to the controller device.  The Examiner has addressed the amended limitations in the updated text below.  Applicant presents numerous arguments against the Jain reference in light of the claims as amended, in particular the amended feature that the textile-based ECG electrodes are “in contact with skin of the user.”  The Examiner respectfully submits that the Jain reference has not been used to teach or suggest such features in the updated rejection below, but rather the Chahine reference, which more directly addresses the amended limitations of claim 11.   The Examiner notes that, in response to Applicant’s arguments against the references individually, one cannot show nonobviousness by attacking references individually where the rejections are based on combinations of references.  Please see In re Keller, 642 F.2d 413, 208 USPQ 871 (CCPA 1981); In re Merck & Co., 800 F.2d 1091, 231 USPQ 375 (Fed. Cir. 1986).
Regarding claim 11, Applicant argues that Jain does not teach or suggest a plurality of textile-based ECG electrodes, in particular electrodes which are “in contact with skin of the user,” as recited in amended claim 11 (Arguments, pp. 7-8).  The Examiner respectfully submits that the new rejection below relies on Chahine as the primary reference.  Chahine discloses textile-based electrodes which make direct contact with the skin of the user, and Jain discloses textile-based electrodes that may be used for ECG monitoring.  The Examiner respectfully submits that an obvious combination of the teachings of Chahine and Jain would result in the claimed feature of textile-based ECG electrodes which contact the skin of the user, as described in the updated text below.  Therefore, the corresponding arguments have been rendered moot.
Regarding claim 29, Applicant argues that Jain does not teach or suggest a plurality of textile-based ECG electrodes (Arguments, pp. 7-8).  The Examiner respectfully disagrees and directs Applicant to those sections of Jain which describe electrodes that are integrated into wearable fabrics (for example, Jain’s [0035] describes a sensor pad which may be provided in a fabric with which the human body comes in contact).  Jain further describes integrating the ECG electrodes into “clothing,” or into a “wearable device” such as a “jacket, shirt, t-shirt, sweater, bra etc” ([0069]).  The Examiner considers electrodes that are integrated into clothing as being “textile-based” electrodes.  Therefore, the Examiner respectfully maintains that Jain teaches a plurality of textile-based ECG electrodes.  The Examiner notes that Applicant argues that Jain’s electrodes do not make contact with skin of the user, but claim 29, even as amended, does not recite such a feature.  Therefore, Applicant’s arguments regarding the electrodes making contact with skin of the user, in reference to claim 29, are moot.
Applicant argues that Jain does not teach or suggest the feature of a textile-based ECG generator electrode for transmitting a generated signal through a patient’s upper body (Arguments, pp. 8-9).  The Examiner respectfully submits that Chahine describes a textile-based generator electrode for transmitting a generated signal through a patient’s upper body, and Jain describes that the transmitted signals could be ECG signals, as described in the updated text below.  Specifically regarding claim 29, the Examiner notes that claim 29, even as amended, does not recite “at least one textile-based ECG generator electrode for transmitting a generated signal through the patient’s upper body,” as alleged by Applicant.  The Examiner respectfully submits that the corresponding arguments, in reference to claim 29, are moot.
Applicant argues that Jain does not teach or suggest a controller configured to determine that one of the electrodes is at least partially out of contact with the skin of the user and/or causes the generated signal to be of unacceptable signal quality (Arguments, pp. 9-10).  The Examiner respectfully disagrees and directs Applicant to Jain’s paragraph [0087], which describes measuring the signal quality from each sensor.  Using this feature on the textile-based electrodes described by Chahine would result in textile-based electrodes coupled to a controller which measures the signal quality of each sensor, thereby obviating the claimed limitation.
Applicant argues that Jain does not teach or suggest detecting whether one of the electrodes are in acceptable contact with the skin of the user (Arguments, p. 10).  The Examiner respectfully disagrees for at least the reasons described in the previous paragraph.
Applicant argues that one skilled in the art would not be motivated to use Jain’s controller to determine a percentage of contact surface area of the textile-based electrodes with the skin of the user, since Jain’s electrodes are not making contact with the skin of the user (Arguments, p. 11).  The Examiner respectfully submits that, in the updated rejection below, the modification to incorporate a measurement of the percentage contact area is being made to Chahine’s electrodes, not Jain’s electrodes, rendering the corresponding arguments moot.  The Examiner also highlights that the Biel reference was used to teach this feature, not the Jain or Chahine references.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 11 and 29 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.

It is unclear whether this limitation is the same as, or different from, the “at least one ECG receiver electrode” as recited in claim 11, line 9.  As a result, there is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim.
Claim 29 recites the limitation “at least one ECG generator electrode” in line 13.  It is unclear whether this limitation is the same as, or different from, the “at least one ECG generator electrode” as recited in claim 29, lines 6-7.  As a result, there is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim.
Claim 29 recites the limitation “one or more receiver ECG electrodes” in line 14.  It is unclear whether this limitation is the same as, or different from, the “at least one ECG receiver electrode” as recited in claim 29, lines 7-8.  As a result, there is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim.

Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
