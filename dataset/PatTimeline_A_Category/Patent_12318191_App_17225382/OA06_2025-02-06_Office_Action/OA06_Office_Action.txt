Reasons for Allowance
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance: The prior art of record fails to disclose, teach, or fairly suggest the system for monitoring patient movement, as claimed. The prior art that comes closest to teaching the limitations is Nikou et al. ‘335, which teaches a system for monitoring patient movement comprising:
a first tracking device configured to be affixed to a table;
a second tracking device configured to be affixed to an anatomical element, the anatomical element having a pose relative to the table;
a robotic arm positionable in any pose and configured to support an object;
at least one processor; and
at least one memory storing instructions for execution by the at least one processor that, when executed, cause the at least one processor to:
register a patient to a pre-operative image to track a robotic arm relative to the anatomical element,
receive a first dataset, the first dataset containing a first image depicting the first tracking device and the second tracking device,
receive a second dataset, the second dataset containing a second image the first tracking device and the second tracking device,
compare the first relative pose and the second relative pose to determine if a relative pose of the first tracking device and the second tracking device has changed, and
generate a notification when the relative pose of the first tracking device and the second tracking device has changed, and
reregister the patient to the pre-operative image based on the notification indicating that the relative pose of the first tracking device and the second tracking device has changed, and
automatically position the robotic arm relative to the anatomical element.
Nikou et al. ‘335 teaches all of the elements of the current invention as mentioned above except for process each of the first image and the second image using an image processing algorithm to identify the first tracking device and the second tracking device, and determine a first relative pose of the first tracking device relative to the second tracking device from the processed first image and a second relative pose of the first tracking device relative to the second tracking device from the processed second image. Although it was found that Hale ‘657 remedies these deficiencies, neither Nikou et al. ‘335 nor Hale ‘657 teach registering a patient to a pre-operative image to yield a correlation between a coordinate system associated with the patient and a coordinate system associated with a navigation system, wherein the correlation is used to track the robotic arm relative to the anatomical element and wherein reregistering the patient to the pre-operative image yields an updated correlation between the coordinate system associated with the patient and the coordinate system associated with the navigation system, and use the updated correlation to automatically position the robotic arm relative to the anatomical element. Although it was found that Abbasi et al. ‘375 (US Pub No. 2016/0206375) teaches registering a patient to a pre-operative image to yield a correlation between a coordinate system associated with the patient and a coordinate system associated with a navigation system in [0054], neither Nikou et al. ‘335, Hale ‘657, and Abbasi et al. ‘375 do not teach wherein the correlation is used to track the robotic arm relative to the anatomical element and wherein reregistering the patient to the pre-operative image yields an updated correlation between the coordinate system associated with the patient and the coordinate system associated with the navigation system, and use the updated correlation to automatically position the robotic arm relative to the anatomical element. It would require an unreasonable combination of references that would not suffice for a realistic case of obviousness.
Regarding the 35 U.S.C. 101 rejection, the amendments to the independent claims that include reregistering the patient to the pre-operative image to yield an updated correlation between the coordinate system associated with the patient and the coordinate system associated with the navigation system and use the updated correlation to automatically position the robotic arm relative to the anatomical element is seen as a practical application. The robotic arm is being controlled based from the updated correlation, or determination, which is seen as the practical application. As such, the 35 U.S.C. 101 rejection has been withdrawn.
