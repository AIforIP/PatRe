Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1-7 and 9-15 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention. Regarding claim 1, the phrase “the down-sampler output signal” in lines 25 and 40 lacks proper antecedent basis. The Examiner suggests amending the phrase to read “the down-sampled filtered incoming measured EEG signal” in order to properly refer back to the previously recited down-sampled filtered incoming measured EEG signal. The phrase “wherein the epoch signal being the memory output data” in line 51 of claim 1 renders the claim indefinite. Is the epoch signal the memory output data (i.e., wherein the epoch signal is the memory output data), or is an action taken upon the epoch signal that is somehow connected to the memory output data? Clarification is requested. Lines 64-65 of claim 1 recite that “the animal subject being awake or anesthetized or that the animal subject being awake or anesthetized”. Duplication of the phrase “being awake or anesthetized” renders the claim indefinite; both output results of the classifier are the same. Furthermore, while not a rejection, it is noted that the term “being” in both phrases should be amended to read “is”. Regarding claim 2, the wording of the claim renders the claim indefinite (it is unclear how a filter compensates a power-life interference). As best understood, the first filter of the signal pre-processor is configured to remove or reduce a power-line interference of the incoming measured EEG signal. Regarding claim 3, the wording of the claim renders the claim indefinite. What does it mean for a filtering frequency to be “comparable” to an average frequency of a wide EEG frequency region? Furthermore, the phrase “wide electroencephalography frequency region” is indefinite. What is to be considered a “wide” EEG frequency region? The same indefiniteness issue is present in claim 13. Regarding claim 5, the phrase “each electroencephalography channel” lacks proper antecedent basis as there is no previous mention of EEG channels. Further regarding claim 5, the phrase “short-overlapped epochs” renders the claim indefinite in that the term “short” is a relative term that is not defined by the claim, and it is unclear if the short-overlapped epochs of claim 5 are the same overlapping epochs of claim 1 or different overlapping epochs. Regarding claim 7, the phrase “computing a difference between each of the absolute epoch values and the mean of the accumulated absolute epoch values and a square of the difference to obtain a squared deviation” renders the claim indefinite. The phrase “the difference” is singular, but performing the steps recited in claims 1 and 7 results in a plurality of difference values (a difference between each of the absolute epoch values and the mean of the accumulated absolute epoch values is determined). Furthermore, in order to compute an average of all of the squared deviations for all of the absolute epoch values, more than one squared deviation would need to be obtained. The Examiner suggests amending the phrase to read “computing a difference between each of the absolute epoch values and the mean of the accumulated absolute epoch values and a square of each difference to obtain squared deviations” in order to clarify the claim. Further regarding claim 7, it is suggested that the term “the” be added before the phrase “squared deviations” in lines 11 and 13. The same indefiniteness issues and suggestions also apply to claim 15. Regarding claim 9, the phrase “the percentage of the anesthesia of the animal subject” lacks proper antecedent basis. It is suggested that the phrase be amended to read “a percentage of the anesthesia level of the animal subject”. Regarding claim 10, the “mapping the derivative feature” and “accumulating a plurality of the classifier outputs” limitations render the claim indefinite. What are the derivative feature and variance featured mapped to? What is the output of the mapping and how is it determined? The phrase “the mapping output value” further renders the claim indefinite. The phrase lacks proper antecedent basis, and it remains unclear how the mapping output value is determined. The accumulating step is indefinite in that it is unclear how accumulating consecutive classifier outputs would result in generating a prediction output of a likelihood of the animal subject being awake or anesthetized in a successive epoch. The indefiniteness issues within claim 10 have rendered a proper prior art search unable to be performed. Regarding claim 11, it is unclear what the phrase “increasing a prediction probability” is referring to as there is no previous mention of determining a prediction probability of the animal subject being either anesthetized or awake. The phrases “being anesthetized” and “being awake” in claim 11 should be amended to read “is anesthetized” and “is awake”, respectively.
Claims not explicitly rejected above are rejected due to their dependence on a rejected base claim.

Regarding the previous prior art rejection of claim 10, as noted in paragraph 3 above, the amendments to the claim have rendered a prior art search of the claim unable to be performed.
Examiner’s Note
Regarding claim 1, none of the prior art teaches or suggests, either alone or in combination a device comprising an epoch generator, wherein the epoch generator comprises a two-input multiplexer, an address generator, and a memory, wherein the two-input multiplexer is configured to receive a down-sampled filtered incoming measured EEG signal, a feedback of an epoch signal, and a selector input from the address generator, and further wherein the address generator is configured to generate the selector input to the multiplexer to generate a multiplexer output from the down-sampler output signal during each of a plurality of slow count cycles and from the feedback of the epoch signal during each of a plurality of fast count cycles, in combination with the other claimed elements.
Conclusion
The prior art made of record and not relied upon is considered pertinent to applicant’s disclosure. Tabassum et al. (Novel Multirate…) teaches a device for processing EEG signals, wherein the device comprises a signal pre-processor configured to perform a first filtering technique, a second filtering technique, and a down-sampling technique on acquired EEG signals. Tabassum et al. further teaches use of a two-input multiplexer to process the filtered and down-sampled EEG signals.
