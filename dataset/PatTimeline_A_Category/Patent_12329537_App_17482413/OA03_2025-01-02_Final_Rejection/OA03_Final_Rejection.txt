Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1-4, 6, 7 and 9-15 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention. Regarding claim 1, the claim recites that “the epoch generator is configured to generate and output the epoch signal from the memory output”. This limitation renders the claim indefinite. As currently written, the claim requires the epoch generator to generate the epoch signal from the memory output. It is unclear what is meant by this recitation. For this examination, the limitation is being interpreted as “the epoch generator is configured to generate the epoch signal and output the epoch signal from the memory output”. Further regarding claim 1, it is unclear how the predictor accumulates “a plurality of the classifier’s outputs” and predicts the animal subject’s anesthesia condition from “a plurality of determined levels of anesthesia” if there is one output and determined level of anesthesia per epoch signal and only one epoch signal. As currently written, claim 1 extracts a derivative feature and a variance feature “of the received epoch signal”, and provides a classifier output of the epoch signal. As best understood by the specification, while the claim recites performing the extracting steps and the classifying step for an epoch signal, the steps are actually performed for each epoch of the epoch signal. The same indefiniteness is present in claim 10. Claim 10 extracts features “of the epoch signal”, and classifies “the epoch signal”, but also recites “the classifier output is a probability value of the animal subject is under anesthetized state given the current epoch” and “accumulating a plurality of the classifier output values for consecutive classified epochs”. This provides further confusion as to whether the steps are being performed per epoch signal or per epoch of the epoch signal. Clarification is requested. As noted above, according to the specification, the extracting and classifying steps recited in claims 1 and 10 are meant to be performed on each epoch of an epoch signal such that multiple classifications per epoch signal are determined. Regarding claim 3, it is unclear how a bandpass filter would operate within a range of 0-250Hz. It is unclear how a bandpass filter would filter out signals below 0Hz. Regarding claim 9, the phrase “an output value” lacks proper antecedent basis. Clarification is requested as to what the output value of claim 9 is. Regarding claim 10, the phrase “the classifier output value” lacks proper antecedent basis. Regarding claim 11, the wording of the claim makes it difficult to understand what the claim is reciting. For example, it is unclear what is meant by “increasing the prediction probability of likelihood” and “decreasing the prediction probability of likelihood”. Clarification is requested. Regarding claim 12, the wording of the claim renders the claim indefinite (i.e., grammatical errors and verb tense errors). Further regarding claims 11 and 12, the phrase “the prediction probability” lacks proper antecedent basis.
Claims not explicitly rejected above are rejected due to their dependence on a rejected base claim.
