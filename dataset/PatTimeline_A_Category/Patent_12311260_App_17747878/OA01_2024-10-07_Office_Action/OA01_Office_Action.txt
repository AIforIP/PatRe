Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 101
35 U.S.C. 101 reads as follows:
Whoever invents or discovers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter, or any new and useful improvement thereof, may obtain a patent therefor, subject to the conditions and requirements of this title.
Claims 1-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 because the claimed invention is directed to abstract idea without significantly more.
2019 PEG Analysis
Step 1:  Are the claims directed to a statutory category (e.g., a process, machine, etc.)
Claims 1-11 are directed to a process.  Claims 12-20 are directed to an apparatus.
Step 2A (Prong 1): Does the claim recite an abstract idea, law of nature or natural phenomenon?
Yes, the claims recite an abstract idea.  The following specific limitations in the claims under examination recite an abstract idea:
Controlling a virtual object with a terminal (e.g., see claims 1, 12, and 15)
Shifting a target object to an upper right of the terminal screen (e.g., claims 1, 12, and 20)
determining a scene position according to a target offset (e.g., claim 2)
moving a virtual camera lens in a virtual scene (e.g., claims 3, and 9)
obtaining scene positions (e.g., claims 4, and 5)
setting target conditions (e.g., claim 6)
determining a sum of the target offset and the first scene (e.g., claim 8).
The above listed identified limitations fall within at least one of the groupings of abstract ideas enumerated in the 2019 PEG:
Mental Processes: concepts preformed in the human mind (including on observation, evaluation, judgement, opinion).
Certain Methods of Organizing Human Activity: managing personal behavior or relationships or interactions or relationships of interaction between people (including social activities, teaching, and following rules or instructions).
Step 2A (Prong 2): Does the claim recite additional elements that integrate the judicial exception into a practical application?
Overall, the following additional claim limitations appear to merely implement the abstract idea, add insignificant extra-solution activity to the judicial exception, or generally link the judicial exception to a particular environment or field of use, as outlined below:
Displaying objects in a virtual scene (most claims, insignificant extra-solution activity);
Claims 2-11 and 13-19 include additional limitations that are similar those limited above for claims 1, 12, and 20 (e.g., displaying: insignificant extra-solution activity).
Step 2B: Does the claim recite additional elements that amount to significantly more than the judicial exception?
With regard to claims 1-20, the claims as a whole do not amount to significantly more than the exception itself.   The above listed additional claim limitations display in a well-understood, routine, and conventional way.  Further, the computer hardware of claim 12 (e.g., a processor; and memory) are well-understood, routine, and conventional in the art.  Therefore, claims 1-20 are not patent eligible under 101.
Allowable Subject Matter
Claims 1-20 are objected to as being dependent upon a rejected base claim, but would be allowable if Applicant overcome the 101 rejections.  Applicant is invited to contact the Examiner to discuss potential ways to overcome the 101 rejections.
Claim 1 recites a method for displaying a virtual scene.  The prior art fails to expressly disclose displaying the controlled virtual object at a target position of the terminal screen when the controlled virtual object belongs to a first camp, the first camp being a camp located at an upper right of the virtual scene, the target position being shifted to the upper right position relative to a central position of the terminal screen.  While this appears to be simply moving a game object from a center portion of the display to a specific corner (upper right corner) of the display, the Examiner gave patentable weight to all recited features including “belongs to a first camp” and was unable to locate prior art that anticipated or made obvious the recited features.
U.S. Patent Application Publication No. 2024/0198221 to Li and U.S. Patent Application Publication No. 2022/0365634 to Lu each disclose camps associated with a video game, but is silent regarding moving a controlled object from a central position to a first camp position in the upper right corner of the display.
U.S. Patent Application Publication No. 2015/0018062 to Montano discloses a video poker table where chips move from the central portion of the display screen to the upper right corner (e.g., see Fig. 4C, towards player 702), but the chips are not “controlled” virtual objects as claimed.
Claims 12 and 20 distinguish over the known prior art based on the same analysis set forth above for claim 1, which is similar in claim scope.  Dependent claims 2-11 and 13-19 would be allowed if their base claims are allowed.
