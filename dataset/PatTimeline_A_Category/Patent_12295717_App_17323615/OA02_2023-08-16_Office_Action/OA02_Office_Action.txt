Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 102
The following is a quotation of the appropriate paragraphs of 35 U.S.C. 102 that form the basis for the rejections under this section made in this Office action:
A person shall be entitled to a patent unless –
(a)(1) the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on sale or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
(a)(2) the claimed invention was described in a patent issued under section 151, or in an application for patent published or deemed published under section 122(b), in which the patent or application, as the case may be, names another inventor and was effectively filed before the effective filing date of the claimed invention.
Claims 2-7, 9-10 and 13-14 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1)/(a)(2) as being anticipated by Grunwald et al. (hereinafter ‘Grunwald’, U.S. PGPub. No. 2009/0006575).
In regards to claim 2, Grunwald discloses an intravascular device (endovascular device 150 in Figs. 2, 4A-4C), comprising:
a stylet ([0113]: the device 150 can be configured as a stylet) comprising an elongate member (elongate body 105) having a proximal end (proximal end 110), a distal end (distal end 115), an inner lumen extending between the proximal end and the distal end (lumen within the elongate body 105, [0111]),
a hub having an inner lumen for receiving the stylet (a steering element 153, [0130-[0131]);
a guard extending from a distal end of the hub, and having an inner lumen for receiving and stabilizing the stylet (connector 154 on proximal end, [0131]); and
a connector assembly proximal to the stylet, configured for connecting one or more elements of the stylet to a computing device (ECG lead, transducer connected to a processor 140, [0133]).
In regard to claims 3-5, Grunwald discloses the stylet comprising a plurality of sensors disposed on the distal end ([0120]: electrode sensing segment includes ultrasound transducer and electrogram lead).

In regards to claim 14, Grunwald discloses wherein the one or more elements comprise an electrical conductor comprising a conductive wire that connects to an ECG electrode ([0120]: electrogram lead 130 is connected via ECG lead to a processor 140 as best shown in Fig. 4B).
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102 of this title, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains.  Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
The factual inquiries set forth in Graham v. John Deere Co., 383 U.S. 1, 148 USPQ 459 (1966), that are applied for establishing a background for determining obviousness under 35 U.S.C. 103 are summarized as follows:
1. Determining the scope and contents of the prior art.
2. Ascertaining the differences between the prior art and the claims at issue.
3. Resolving the level of ordinary skill in the pertinent art.
4. Considering objective evidence present in the application indicating obviousness or nonobviousness.
Claim 8 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Grunwald as applied to claim 2 above, and further in view of Lichtenstein et al. (hereinafter ‘Lichtenstein’, U.S. PGPub. No. 2015/0173829).
In regards to claim 8, Grunwald discloses the invention substantially as claimed in claim 2 and discussed above. The Examiner notes that Grunwald’s hub is removably engaged with the connector assembly as the wires can be removed/detached during manufacturing and/or after use. Additionally, the hub and the connector assembly are capable of being rotated together by the operator.
However, Grunwald does not disclose the connector assembly further comprising a stabilizing portion that rotates independent of the connector assembly such that the connection to the computing device is stable when the hub and the connector assembly rotates.
Lichtenstein teaches a stabilizing portion (torsion management apparatus 70 in Figs. 1-3) as part of a connector assembly for reducing torsion to cables/wires of an intravascular device/catheter during use ([0024],[0040]-[0041]). Specifically, when an operator rotates the entire intravascular device/catheter in order to position the distal tip within the patient’s body cavity, the stabilizing portion reduces the entanglement of the electrical lines adjacent to a console/computing device ([0023]-[0026]). Given that Grunwald discloses preventing the connector assembly from being interrupted when the steering mechanism rotates the intravascular device/catheter, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date to additionally incorporate a stabilizing portion as part of the connector assembly such that when the operator rotates the entire intravascular device/catheter, the stabilizing portion reduces the torsion and prevents entanglement of the connector assembly adjacent to the computing device to stabilize the wires/cable connection.
Claims 11-12 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Grunwald as applied to claim 2 above.
In regard to claims 11-12, Grunwald further discloses wherein the intravascular device has a length L1 (entire length of 150 in Figs. 1) and the stylet has a length L2 (length extending from the proximal end 110 to the distal end 115 in Fig. 1). Given that the endovascular member is for insertion into a patient’s vasculature, it would have been obvious to modify the length of the entire intravascular device to be about two times L2 or more depending upon the distance between a processor/computing device with the stylet, since it has been held that the provision of adjustability, where needed, involves only routine skill in the art.  In re Stevens, 101 USPQ 284 (CCPA 1954).
