REASONS FOR ALLOWANCE
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
During the search of the prior art, Grunwald et al. (hereinafter ‘Grunwald’, U.S. PGPub. No. 2009/0005675) was found to be closest to the claimed invention.
In regards to independent claims 2 & 23, Grunwald discloses an intravascular device (endovascular device 150 in Figs. 2, 4A-4C), comprising: a stylet ([0113]: the device 150 can be configured as a stylet); an elongate member (elongate body 105); an ECG electrode ([0113]: the endovascular device may integrate several electrodes); an ultrasound transducer/sensor (an ultrasound transducer 120/121 extends from the electrode disposed at the very distal tip of the endovascular member [0061], [0113]-[0114], [0120]); a first electrical conductor comprising a conductive wire that connects to the ECG electrode/the electrode (in the exemplary Figs. 4A-4C, the conductive wire 135 is connected into a lumen within the elongate body to connect to an electrode [0120], [0125]); a second electrical conductor comprising a coaxial cable disposed within the inner lumen of the elongate member and electrically connected to the sensor/ultrasound transducer (Fig. 4C labels the transducer conductor disposed within the lumen of elongate body 105 for connecting to the processing 140; coaxial wire attaches to the doppler ultrasonic/imaging sensor, [0138]; single lumen for supporting sensors and electrodes and its wiring, [0116]); a hub having an inner lumen for receiving the stylet (a steering element 153, [0130-[0131]); a guard (an introducer to provide a combination of stylet and introducer into a user’s vasculature, [0113]); and a connector assembly proximal to the stylet, configured for connecting the first and second electrical conductors of the stylet to a computing device (connector 154 comprising appropriate connections for coaxial wire and conductive wire, [0131]).
Prutchi (U.S. Pat. No. 6,662,055) was relied upon to teach an intravascular lead having a gradually-tapered coiled wire (col. 3, ln. 41-60).
However, Grunwald/Prutchi combination fail to disclose the structural arrangement between the elongate member, the ring electrode and the sensor as claimed. Specifically, Grunwald does not disclose in part, “a ring electrode extending from a distal-most end of the distal end of the elongate member; a sensor disposed at and extending directly from a distal end of the ring electrode and configured to detect and measure a physiological parameter in a patient’s vasculature…” as required in independent claim 2. Similarly, Grunwald does not disclose in part, “an ECG electrode extending from a distal-most end of the distal end of the elongated tube; an ultrasound transducer distal to the ECG electrode…” as required in independent claim 23. At best, Grunwald discloses the ring electrode is disposed at the distal end of the elongate member ([0113]: one electrode can be at the very distal tip of the endovascular member) and therefore fail to extend from a distal-most end of the distal end of the elongate member.
Although distal tip electrode extending from the distal-most end of an elongate member are known in the art (see Wittkampf et al., U.S. Pat. No. 6,134,463), the prior art does not disclose another sensor disposed and extending directly from a distal end of the ring electrode. Wittkampf is only limited to an eye electrode (electrode 34 in Fig. 12) that extends indirectly from the tip electrode (electrode 31 in Fig. 12 which forms an annular shape for the placement of the electrode 34). There would be no motivation of modifying the distal tip electrode assembly of Wittkampf so that the eye electrode extends directly from the ring electrode as doing so would short the two electrodes and would render the two electrodes inoperable for the desired bipolar configuration.
Claims 4, 6-14, 17, 19 and 24-26 are allowed as being dependent upon independent claim 2 or 23.
In regards to claim 21, Grunwald fails to disclose a second encapsulating member forming a second enclosure around the sensor, wherein the second encapsulating member is attached to the ring electrode, wherein the second encapsulating member forms a fluid-tight seal around the sensor and the ring electrode. Providing a balloon or an encapsulating member at a distal end of a catheter is well-known in the art. For example, Swanson (U.S. PGPub. No. 2002/0111548) teaches providing an encapsulating member around an ultrasound transducer (flexible body 36 in Fig. 13B, [0068]); however, there would be no motivation to provide an additional encapsulating member about the ring electrode of Grunwald/Prutchi/Boling combination and form a fluid-tight seal around the ring electrode.
Claim 22 is allowed as being dependent upon independent claim 21.
