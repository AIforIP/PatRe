Reasons for Allowance
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance.
In view of the claim amendments filed on 1/15/2025, the claim rejections under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, are withdrawn.
In view of the terminal disclaimer filed on 1/15/2025, the double patenting rejections based on U.S. Patent No. 11,051,751 are withdrawn.
U.S. Patent Application Publication No. 2015/0094618 (Russell)(previously cited) teaches a method comprising:
providing the turn protocol for the person supported at least temporarily by a support surface (the combined turn and perfusion timers of Russell),
receiving, at a processor, sensor signals from one or more sensors (the sensors of Russell; paragraphs 0007 and 0022-0027 of Russell),
identifying, by the processor, based on the received sensor signals, when the person is in a pressurization position corresponding with the particular body region (the first position detection of Russell; paragraphs 0038-0042 of Russell),
monitoring the pressurization measure over time, including (a) increasing the pressurization measure as a function of a first pressurization period spent on the support surface and in the pressurization position (the operation of the turn timers of Russell; paragraphs 0038-0047, 0057-0059, and 0076-0084 of Russell; FIGS. 2A-2D and 8 of Russell);
comparing the monitored pressurization measure to the at least one pressurization threshold value over time (the comparing of the time in a pressurization position to a predetermined turn time threshold; paragraphs 0007-0008, 0020, 0038-0047, and 0051 of Russell);
outputting turn protocol alerts based on the comparisons (outputting the turn alert based on the comparison of the time in the pressurization position to the predetermined turn time threshold; paragraphs 0007-0008, 0020, 0038-0047, and 0051 of Russell).
Russell discloses the use of a perfusion timer to indicate when sufficient time has passed such that returning to a previous position will not resume the turn counter (paragraphs 0038-0047, 0057-0059, and 0076-0084 of Russell; FIGS. 2A-2D and 8 of Russell).  U.S. Patent Application Publication No. 2010/0268122 (Drennan)(previously cited) discloses an analogous regime in which the pressurization of a body part utilizes a counter that is initiated to generate a cumulative output whose initial value is zero (e.g., time units such as seconds or minutes), begins to increase once a predetermined pressure is applied, but gradually decreases back toward zero time units if the predetermined pressure is removed (paragraph 0013 of Drennan).  For example, Drennan discloses that the decrease in the counter value can occur at a predetermined ratio less than 1:1 relative to actual elapsed time, for example, at a ratio of one counter minute for every four actual minutes that have elapsed after the predetermined pressure has been removed (paragraph 0013 of Drennan).  The system of Drennan operates such that the avoidance of tissue damage can take into consideration not only how long the predetermined pressure persisted, but also the elapsed time following a corrective measure.  Notably, the counter immediately resumes and its value again increases in actual time (1:1 ratio) if the patient moves to a position that resumes the predetermined pressure condition (paragraph 0013 of Drennan).  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to replace the perfusion timer of Russell which indicates when sufficient time has passed such that returning to a previous position will not resume the turn counter with the decreasing timer operation of the pressurizing (turn) timer, as disclosed by Drennan, since it is a simple substitution of one known element for another to obtain predictable results.
WO 2012/114298 (Steiner)(previously cited) teaches the pausing of repositioning timers when it is detected that the person exits the support structure and resuming the timers when it is detected that the person returns to the support structure (pages 2 and 13-14 of Steiner).   It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to pause the timers (and hence the protocol alerts) upon exiting the support structure and resuming the timers (and hence the protocol alerts) when the persons returns to the support structure since the conditions of monitoring have so drastically changed that previous monitoring is not applicable.
U.S. Patent Application Publication No. 2014/0313030 (Ten Kate)(previously cited) teaches that wearable accelerometers are used to determine a bed exit (paragraphs 0020, 0036, 0058, 0061, 0068-0080, 0113, and 0126 of Ten Kate). It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to use the accelerometers of Russell (paragraph 0027 of Russell) to determine a bed exit since it creates less of a burden (paragraph 0058 of Ten Kate).
