Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim(s) 1-5, 7, 10-15, 17, and 20 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Sipe et al. (2023/0395248).
With respect to claims 1 and 20, Sipe et al. teach of an apparatus and method for generating cardiac catheterization data where a plurality of cardiogram data examples are applied to machine learning engine to determine a cardiac condition measure where physiological data samples are analyzed using machine learning classifiers (see abstract).  Sipe et al. teach of a memory or data storage region 1004 communicatively connected to the processor 1018 where the memory contains instructions configuring the processor to perform one or more operations such as storing cardiogram or ECG data [0123].   Sipe et al. therefore teach of receiving the ECG data, training a data predictor using the plurality of ECG data examples [0073, 0074], inputting a plurality of data signals, generating a plurality of catheterization parameters from the ECG data signal and predictor [0073, 0074, 0076, 0101], and a display 432 for displaying the catheterization parameters [0160, 0161]
With respect to claims 2 and 12, Sipe et al. teach of training a recognition classifier with echocardiogram data examples comprising a plurality of data, inputting the cardiogram data into the recognition classifier, and outputting the data signals and catherization data [0073] where the transformations to the ECG data match transformation applied to input data samples in training the various classifiers to ensure a consistent recognition during analysis of incoming samples [0073].  Sipe et al. teach of training the classifier with cardiogram data examples comprising plurality of data correlated to a plurality of parameters that are sorted in a database or a database querying interface or textbook validators of cardiac catherization parameters which support access by the ECG signal data classifier to the data repository access by the machine learning engine to the physiological raw data store [0166].
With respect to claims 3, 4, 13, and 14, Sipe et al. teach of using the cardiogram data examples or the ECG data and/or cardio-vibrational data to a first tier of the machine learning engine including one or more machine learning classifiers  [0005].  Sipe et al. therefore teach of training a classifier with a plurality of training data correlated to the plurality of cardiogram data, inputting the plurality of training data into the comparison function classifier, and outputting, prediction of the plurality of catheterization parameters [0023, 0073-0075] where the catheter data predictor comprises a neural network [0024, 0032, 0076].
With respect to claims 5 and 15, Sipe et al. teach of the neural network comprising deep learning network [0101].
With respect to claims 7 and 17, Sipe et al. teach of cardiogram data being correlated with ECG and Echocardiogram measurements [0070, 0073, 0074].
With respect to claims 10 and 20, Sipe et al. teach of receiving the plurality of cardiogram data by retrieving the data from health records 612j [0101-0104].
Sipe et al. do not teach of all the claim elements in a single embodiment.  It would have therefore been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art to combine the elements from the various embodiments to effectively apply each data sample to the machine learning engine to obtain a set of cardiac condition measures that may then be combined to determine a final cardiac condition measure by removing anomalies in collected data over in determining the cardiac condition measure [0083].
Claim(s) 6, 9, 16, and 19 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Sipe et al. in view of Khosousi et al. (2020/0205745).  Sipe et al. teach of processor being configured to input a plurality of cardiogram data signals by obviating at least right side of patient’s torso or anatomical sites on the left or right side of patient’s torso [0067, 0097] but do not explicitly teach of right heart catheterization parameters.  In a similar field of endeavor Khosousi et al. a system and method to facilitate the configuration and training of neural network with a cardiac signal data to determine the presence/absence of coronary artery disease (abstract).   Khosousi et al. teach of right heart failures where pulmonary arterial hypertension requires a heart catheterization for diagnosis [0013] where evidence of right heart strain on electrocardiography is typically seen and right heart catheterization is needed confirm the diagnosis [0014].  It would have therefore been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art to use the teaching by Khosousi et al. to modify Sipe et al. to reliably confirm diagnosis of hypertension [Khosousi, 0014].
Claim(s) 8 and 18  is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Sipe et al. in view of Neumann (2020/0315527).  Sipe et al. teach of generating a plurality of catheterization parameters from the plurality of cardiogram data signals and the data predictor but do not teach of training a prognosis classifier.  In a similar field of endeavor Neumann teaches of system and method receiving training sets with plurality of data entries and generate a diagnostic output including a prognostic label [0003, 0004].  Neumann teaches of physiological state data [0032] in generating the prognosis classifier or labels [0037, 0038, 0052, 0053].  It would have therefore been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art to use the teaching by Neumann to modify Sipe et al. such that the prognosis classifier or labels would be combined with the cardiac catheterization data from the Sipe reference to output prognoses and/or conclusions regarding likelihood of future diagnoses that were associated with data samples, and/or other medical diagnostic conclusions [Neumann, 0057].
