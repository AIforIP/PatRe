Priority
Applicant’s claim for the benefit of a prior-filed application under 35 U.S.C. 119(e) or under 35 U.S.C. 120, 121, 365(c), or 386(c) is acknowledged. Applicant has not complied with one or more conditions for receiving the benefit of an earlier filing date under 35 U.S.C. 120 as follows:
The later-filed application must be an application for a patent for an invention which is also disclosed in the prior application (the parent or original nonprovisional application or provisional application). The disclosure of the invention in the parent application and in the later-filed application must be sufficient to comply with the requirements of 35 U.S.C. 112(a) or the first paragraph of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, except for the best mode requirement.  See Transco Products, Inc. v. Performance Contracting, Inc., 38 F.3d 551, 32 USPQ2d 1077 (Fed. Cir. 1994)
The present application 18/610,455 is a continuation of Application No. 18/057,497, which is a continuation of Application No. 15/609,680, which is a continuation-in-part of Application No. 15/157,444, which is one of CIP applications chain, all of which are incorporated by reference herein in their entireties for all purposes.
The disclosure of the prior-filed application, Application No. 15/157,444, and all prior-filed applications in the CIPO chain incorporated by reference herein in their entireties for all purposes, fails to provide adequate support or enablement in the manner provided by 35 U.S.C. 112(a) or pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, first paragraph for one or more claims of this application.
The present claim 1 – 20 are directed to a robotic surgery method, more specifically independent claim 1 recites limitations “the computer system configured to record the relative position of the instrument within the targeted anatomical structure as the instrument is traversed in the targeted anatomical structure after removing the at least portion of the targeted anatomical tissue”, and independent claim 12 recites limitations “recording the relative position of the instrument within the targeted anatomical structure, and illustrate in the display a path of the instrument within the targeted anatomical structure relative to an initial representation of the targeted anatomical structure using a computer system and a display,” and “wherein the instrument other than the cutting tool is configured to be navigated after removing the matter with the cutting tool, within the targeted anatomical structure”. However, the prior-filed parent application 15/157,444 and all other prior-filed applications in the CIP chain fail to provide adequate written description support.
Thus, claim 1, 12 and all corresponding dependent claims 2 – 11 and 13 – 20 are not entitled to the benefit of the filing date of the prior application 15/157,444 and all prior-filed application in the CIP chain incorporated by reference herein in their entireties for all purposes.
The effective filing date of claim 1- 20 are set as the filing date 05/31/2017 of prior-filed application 15/609,680.

Reasons for Allowance
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
Regarding independent claim 1, the cited prior art Kostrzewski et al. (US 2016/0151120 A1; published on 06/02/2016) (hereinafter Kostrzewski) and other search results collectively neither teach nor fairly well suggest a method for performing surgery using a robotic system comprising the step of providing a tracking system comprising “a cutting tool configured to remove at least a portion of the targeted anatomical tissue;” and “the computer system configured to record the relative position of the instrument within the targeted anatomical structure as the instrument is traversed in the targeted anatomical structure after removing the at least portion of the target anatomical tissue”, in combination with the other features recited in independent claim 1.
Although Kostrzewski teaches a robotic surgery method to navigate either cutting tool or non-cutting tool, and further teaches the tracking of tool position during therapy procedure, there is no teach that the computer system in Kostrzewski is designed to record the path of non-cutting tool after using the cutting tool to remove tissue in target area.
Thus, neither the cited prior art nor the updated search results fairly well suggest the specific robotic surgery method to perform the tissue removing then measuring procedure with two different tools.
Regarding independent claim 12, the cited prior art Kostrzewski, Palmatier et al. (US 2017/0209286 A1; filed on 01/22/2016) (hereinafter Palmatier) and other search results collectively neither teach nor fairly well suggest a method for determining an extent of matter removed using a robotic system, comprising the steps of: “removing matter from the targeted anatomical structure using a cutting tool; wherein the instrument other than the cutting tool is configured to be navigated after removing the matter with the cutting tool, within the targeted anatomical structure, wherein the instrument is a non-cutting probing tool”, in combination with the other features recited in independent claim 12.
Although Kostrzewski teaches a robotic surgery method to navigate either cutting tool or non-cutting tool, and further teaches the tracking of tool position during therapy procedure, there is no teaching that the computer system in Kostrzewski is designed to record the path of non-cutting tool after using the cutting tool to remove tissue in target area. Palmatier teaches a robotic method to track the position of a tip and shaft of the instrument withing the target area. However, there is no teaching of using cutting tool to remove bone tissue first, and the measurement is not related to removed matter of tissue.
Thus, neither the cited prior arts nor the updated search results fairly well suggest the specific robotic surgery method to perform the tissue removing then measuring procedure with two different tools.
