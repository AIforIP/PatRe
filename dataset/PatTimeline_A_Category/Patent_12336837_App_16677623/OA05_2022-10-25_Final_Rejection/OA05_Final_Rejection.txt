Response to Arguments
35 U.S.C. 103 Rejections:
For claims 21, 23-26, 29, and 37, Applicant argues that there are unexpected results in the presently claimed method and cites the Musa reference for support. Applicant states that Musa teaches that PU was reduced by 87.2% in acute care settings and 100% in test sites and that these results are unexpected. Examiner however respectfully disagrees that these results are unexpected for three reasons. First, the claims are broader in scope than the scenario that is being argued with respect to the unexpected results. Second, the evidence provided cannot be evidence as the Musa reference is not completely commensurate with the claimed invention. Third, the results of improvement in care based on patient data is not unexpected.
With regard to the first and second points made above, the evidence which is being provided does not meet the criteria from the MPEP to be given substantial weight. MPEP 2145 states:
“Consideration of rebuttal evidence and arguments requires Office personnel to weigh the proffered evidence and arguments. Id.; see also In re Alton, 76 F.3d 1168, 1174-75, 37 USPQ2d 1578, 1582-83 (Fed. Cir. 1996). Office personnel should avoid giving no weight to evidence submitted by applicant, except in rare circumstances. However, to be entitled to substantial weight, the applicant should establish a nexus between the rebuttal evidence and the claimed invention, i.e., objective evidence of nonobviousness must be attributable to the claimed invention. The Federal Circuit has acknowledged that applicant bears the burden of establishing nexus,”
According to section 2145 in the MPEP there is a lack of nexus between the evidence provided and the invention such that the evidence is attributable to the claimed invention. Musa is not related to the claimed invention even if it offers similar subject matter. The evidence is not attributable to the claimed invention but rather attributable to an outside source. Factual evidence of unexpected results of the claimed process. MPEP 716.02(d) further supports this as the objective evidence of nonobviousness must be commensurate in scope with the claims which the evidence is offered to support.  The Musa reference is not commensurate in scope with the present claims as the present claims are broader in scope.
With regards to the third point made above, Applicant argues that improvement in patient care using the claimed method is unexpected. Examiner questions how changing care based on patient measurements can be considered unexpected. For example, the Geleijnse reference teaches that a patient’s condition is monitored and care is adjusted to be better tailored to a patient in paragraphs [0002] and [0004]. Examiner believes that Applicant is trying to highlight that the present invention improves these numbers when there is a transition of care across two settings where the settings may provide that variability of uncertainty. However one can argue that the goals for both the claimed invention and the Geleijnse reference (where the goal is improvements in treatment results based on monitoring a patient’s status) are the same meaning that the results should be expected.
Additionally, Applicant argues with respect to claims 27, 31-36, 39-50 and refers to the independent claim as being a reason why these claims should be allowable. Examiner respectfully disagrees for similar reasons explained above for independent claim 21.
