Response to Arguments
35 U.S.C. 103 Rejections:
Applicant argues with respect to the previously-used Berggren references. These arguments have been deemed moot in view of the newly-cited Tonar et al. reference.
Point (a):
Applicant states that the combination of references do not teach every element of the amended claims. Applicant further states that the references do not teach the “calculating” limitation. Examiner however used the Tonar et al. reference as explained above to teach this limitation. Applicant also states that the “making a further plurality of SEM measurements at a pre-determined frequency corresponding to level-K is longer than every 15 minutes” is also not taught. Examiner however respectfully disagrees as the Bennet-Guerrero reference was used to teach this. Applicant states that Bennet-Guerrero at best teaches a frequency of measurements such as every 15 minutes, in order to conserve battery life. Applicant states that there is no specific recitation of over 15 minutes and one of skill in the art would not think otherwise. Examiner respectfully disagrees however as paragraph [0081] provides suggested frequencies on the scales of hours ranging down to 30 minutes. These frequencies are broad, therefore, Bennet-Guerrero’s broad recitation of “any frequencies of measurement that are beneficial or useful” in column 26, lines 16-19 and “any type of beneficial or otherwise useful frequency for measuring the heart rate” in column 80, lines 21-23 meets the presently-claimed 15 minutes or more.
Point (b):
Applicant argues that no one can replace a broadness of how to obtain measurement values with the specificity of determining a delta between sensors in order to obtain predictable results of captured patient data from sensors. Lachenbruch describes using “other values”. Applicant further states that one of skill in the art would not understand the “other values” in Lachenbruch to be values measuring a disease state. Examiner however respectfully disagrees as paragraph [0037] in fact states that there are sensors collecting measurements relating to the physiological aspects of a person. One of ordinary skill in the art would reasonably be able to understand that the “other values” which are being obtained by the sensors could be measurements related to other physiological aspects of the person.
Applicant further states that there is no reasonable expectation of success for combining Breggen with Lachenbruch as there is no rationale for how a biosensor measuring changes in the dielectric properties when an analyte binds would be successful in the method of measuring impedance in DPU. Examiner used a substitution rationale to substitute using a sensor (assumed to be a single sensor which calculates a value) to collect SEM level measurements with the concept of collecting sensor measurements using a delta value between two sensors. Lachenbruch did not specifically state how the sensos here were able to collect this information, which Breggen teaches how sensors are used to collect this type of information. To further this point, Lachenbruch allows for other values, thus it would be acceptable to combine as the base reference furthers that it is adequate to combine the Berggren reference with the Lachenbruch reference. Examiner has noted this in a clearer manner in the updated 35 U.S.C. 103 rejection section below.
Applicant also claims that the disclosure of Bennet-Guerrero of reducing the measurement frequency is not a basis for predicting success of the claimed method steps of amended claim 21 for at least the reason that there is no suggestion or motivation to identify “a pre-determined frequency” of “a further plurality of SEM measurements” that is “corresponding to” an identified level as is required in amended claim 21. Examiner however respectfully disagrees. There is a reasonable expectation of success as Bennet-Guerrero does not specify or limit the frequency to less than 15 minutes only (see: column 26, lines 16-19), and in fact, Bennet-Guerrero states that it’s beneficial to have a further spaced out frequency as explained in column 26, lines 30-36.
Furthermore, Applicant states that Bennet-Guerrero does not provide an suggestion to measure a series of values at a frequency less often than 15 minutes, let alone a frequency for a high risk level. Examiner would like to point out however that Applicant’s argument above is not reflective of the claimed subject matter. Additionally, Applicant further states that the frequency of Bennet-Guerrero is limited and does not include the claimed subject matter of the amended claims. Examiner however disagrees for similar reasons as stated above where the Bennet-Guerrero reference does indeed not limit the frequencies within its specification, and the present invention’s specification has not described why having a frequency higher than 15 minutes if functionally important within the context of the claimed invention. Accordingly, it is being deemed as a number selected within the range of what is acceptable in Bennet-Guerrero.
Lastly, Applicant states that Lachenbruch and Geleijnse teach away from doing something as is required in claim 21 if the delta value does not exceed the threshold, and therefore it would not be rational to teach of doing something in the form of making a further plurality of measurements. Examiner however respectfully disagrees. The statement “do nothing” was a placeholder to indicate that a decision is being made and that if the value of a threshold is not exceeded, the decision is to maintain the current course. In this case, FIG. 2 shows that there are measurements being made and that the course of action does not change if the threshold is not exceeded. Therefore, actions do occur such as the continuous data monitoring aspect.
