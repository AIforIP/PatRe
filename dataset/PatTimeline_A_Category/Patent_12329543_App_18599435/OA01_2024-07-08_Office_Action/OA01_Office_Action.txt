Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
In the event the determination of the status of the application as subject to AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 (or as subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103) is incorrect, any correction of the statutory basis (i.e., changing from AIA  to pre-AIA ) for the rejection will not be considered a new ground of rejection if the prior art relied upon, and the rationale supporting the rejection, would be the same under either status.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claim(s) 1, 6, 10, 11, 16, and 20 is/are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Brockway et al (US20130289424) in view of Taha et al (US6304772).
Regarding claim 1, Brockway discloses an apparatus for generating a quality diagnostic of analog ECG (electrocardiogram) data, wherein the apparatus comprises:
at least a processor (para. [0072], microprocessor); and
a memory communicatively connected to the at least a processor (para. [0072]), wherein the memory contains instructions configuring the at least a processor to:
receive ECG data (201C and 201G in fig. 2);
extract a plurality of ECG parameters from the ECG data (para. [0049], [0078]);
convert the ECG data to one or more digitized ECG signals (203C and 203G in fig. 2, para. [0042], [0048]);
validate the one or more digitized ECG signals (para. [0082], determining the validity of a detected event), wherein validating comprises:
classifying the one or more digitized ECG signals to a plurality of preliminary ECG parameters (para. [0063], Each event is classified as one of valid, invalid, or uncertain based upon said confidence signal); and
generate a quality diagnostic of the ECG data based on validation of the one or more digitized ECG signals (para. [0082], detecting alarm and notification conditions; para. [0111]; para. [0130], communicate a notification that such as event occurred).
Brockway fails to teach determining an accuracy status of the extracted plurality of ECG parameters by comparing the plurality of preliminary ECG parameters to the extracted plurality of ECG parameters.
However Taha teaches classifying one or more digitized ECG signals to a plurality of preliminary ECG parameters (abstract, analyzing the digital ECG data to generate second pacemaker pulse markers); and
determining an accuracy status of the extracted plurality of ECG parameters by comparing the plurality of preliminary ECG parameters to extracted plurality of ECG parameters (abstract, comparing the first pacemaker pulse markers and energy measures to the second pacemaker pulse markers).
Therefore taking the combined teachings of Brockway and Taha as a whole, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art at the time the invention was filed to incorporate the features of Taha into the apparatus of Brockway. The motivation to combine Taha and Brockway would be to provide a highly specific, highly sensitive patient monitoring and diagnostic system for detecting pacemaker pulses in an ECG waveform (col. 3 lines 3-6 of Taha).
Regarding claim 6, the modified invention of Brockway teaches an apparatus wherein determining an accuracy status comprises using a scoring algorithm using a threshold to determine the accuracy status (para. [0062] of Brockway), wherein the accuracy status comprises one of a pass status and a fail status (para. [0063] of Brockway, Each event is classified as one of valid, invalid, or uncertain based upon said confidence signal).
Regarding claim 10, the modified invention of Brockway teaches an apparatus wherein validating the digitized ECG signals comprises receiving ECG verification data from a user comprising a threshold for a scoring algorithm (para. [0110], [0136] of Brockway).
Regarding claim 11, the claim recites similar subject matter as claim 1 and is rejected for the same reasons as stated above.
Regarding claim 16, the claim recites similar subject matter as claim 6 and is rejected for the same reasons as stated above.
Regarding claim 20, the claim recites similar subject matter as claim 10 and is rejected for the same reasons as stated above.

outputting, by the threshold classifier, the threshold.

[0073], determining a predetermined threshold score with a machine learning classifier (block 103), that has been trained with a sample data set; para. The motivation to combine Sullivan, Taha and Brockway would be to reduce the frequency of false detections of arrhythmia (para. [0002] of Sullivan).

Regarding claim 8, the modified invention of Brockway fails to teach an apparatus wherein the quality diagnostic comprises an auditory representation comprising sounds to indicate a pass status or a fail status regarding an overall quality of the analog ECG data.
However Newton teaches auditory representation comprising sounds to indicate a pass status or a fail status regarding an overall quality of the analog ECG data (para. Regarding claim 9, the modified invention of Brockway fails to teach an apparatus wherein generating the quality diagnostic comprises generating a visual representation regarding the quality status of the analog ECG data by:
training a diagnostic classifier with training data correlating analog ECG data to a plurality of flags;
inputting the analog ECG data into the diagnostic classifier; and
outputting, by the diagnostic classifier, one or more flags indicating an issue with the analog ECG data.
However Newton teaches generating a visual representation (para. [0093], change of display colour or other visual alert) regarding the quality status of an analog ECG data (para. [0011]) by:
training a diagnostic classifier with training data correlating analog ECG data to a plurality of flags (para. [0087], The model can be derived by training either a support vector machine algorithm or a neural network architecture with ECG data that is specifically labelled with classes: ‘clean’, ‘no signal’, and ‘noisy signal’);
inputting the analog ECG data into the diagnostic classifier (para. [0085], The FFT can be analysed in the frequency domain to determine quality of the signal based on frequency response within a target expected range, centred around 3 Hz for a clean signal, very high frequency responses are characteristic of noisy signals); and
outputting, by the diagnostic classifier, one or more flags indicating an issue with the analog ECG data (para. [0085], Where the FFT does not display the anticipated characteristics for a clean signal, an alert may be triggered to warn the clinician; para. [0088]).
Therefore taking the combined teachings of Brockway and Taha with Newton as a whole, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art at the time the invention was filed to incorporate the features of Newton into the apparatus of Brockway and Taha.
