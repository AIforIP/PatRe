Acknowledgement of Amendment
The following office action is in response to the applicant’s amendment filed on 11/11/2024. Claims 1-3, 6-8, 10-11, 13, 17-18, 20-22, 25-27, 29, 32, 41 and 54 are pending. Claim 54 is newly added. Claims 1-3, 6-8, 10-11, 13, 17-18, 20-22, 25-27, 29, 32, 41 and 54 are allowed for the reasons stated in the Response to Arguments and Reasons for Allowance sections below.
Response to Arguments
Applicant’s arguments, see Remarks page 9, filed 11/11/2024, with respect to the rejection of claims 2-3, 6, 8, 21-22, 25, 27, 32 and 41 under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) have been fully considered and are persuasive given the applicant’s amendment thereto.
Regarding claims 2, 3, 6, 8, 21, 22, 25, 27, and 41, the office action of 08/09/2024 alleges that “at least a portion” is a relative term which renders these claim indefinite. In response, the applicant has amended claims 2, 3, 6, 8, 21, 22, 25 and 27 have been amended by changing “at least a portion” to “at least one”. Claim 41 has been amended to delete “at least a portion of”. Therefore, the rejection of claims 2, 3, 6, 8, 21, 22, 25, 27 and 41 under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) has been withdrawn.
Regarding claim 32, the office action of 08/09/2024 alleges that “the change” has insufficient antecedent basis. In response, the applicant has amended claim 32 by changing its dependency to claim 20 and adding “further comprising a computer-readable medium storing instructions to generate an image based on a change in the one or more optical responses of the at least one optical sensor”. Therefore, the rejection of claim 32 under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) in the non-final rejection of 08/09/2024 has been withdrawn.
Applicant’s arguments, see Remarks page 9-10, filed 11/11/2024, with respect to the rejection of the claims under 35 U.S.C. 103 have been fully considered and are persuasive.
Regarding claim 1, the claim has been amended to include “at least one optical sensor comprising ultrasound enhancement material; […] the at least one optical sensor configured to produce one or more optical responses upon receiving harmonic and subharmonic ultrasound echoes, corresponding to the transmitted ultrasound signal, from the medium, the at least one optical sensor having a bandwidth greater than that of the at least one transducer”.
The applicant argues that neither Byrne nor Shen teaches or makes obvious that the at least one optical sensor has a bandwidth greater than that of the at least one transducer, and the bandwidth of the at least one optical sensor ranges from at least f/M to Nf, M and N being integers greater than 1, as specified in amended claim 1.
Byrne and Shen may include harmonic echoes, such as “second harmonic” (e.g. 2f) in Byrne or harmonic signals with frequencies of 2fo and 3fo, as cited in the Office action of 08/09/2024 (i.e. page 6). However, the applicant argues that neither Byrne nor Shen teaches or makes obvious subharmonic echoes, whose frequencies are less than the fundamental frequencies, for example f/M, M being an integer. Further, neither Byrne nor Shen teaches or makes obvious that an optical sensor has a bandwidth ranging from at least one f/M to Nf, M and N being integers greater than 1. Such range extending to f/M allows the at least one optical sensor to sense and produce optical responses by receiving subharmonic ultrasound echoes.
In addition, the applicant argues that neither Byrne nor Shen teaches or makes obvious that the at least one sensor comprises ultrasound enhancement material, as specified in amended claim 1.
The examiner respectfully agrees and acknowledges that neither Byrne nor Shen teaches or makes obvious that the at least one optical sensor has a bandwidth greater than that of the at least one transducer, and the bandwidth of the at least one optical sensor ranges from at least f/M to Nf, M and N being integers greater than 1, as specified in amended claim 1. While Byrne and Shen include harmonic echoes (i.e. second harmonic, 2fo, 3fo), neither Byrne nor Shen teaches or makes obvious subharmonic echoes, whose frequencies are less than the fundamental frequencies, for example f/M, M being an integer.
Furthermore, the examiner acknowledges that neither Byrne nor Shen teaches or makes obvious that the at least one sensor comprises ultrasound enhancement material, as specified in amended claim 1.
Therefore, the rejection of claim 1 under 35 U.S.C. 103 has been withdrawn.
Regarding claims 20 and 41, these independent claims include substantially the same subject matter as claim 1. Thus, these claims are subject to the reasoning provided therein. Therefore, the rejection of claims 20 and 41 have been withdrawn.
Regarding claims 2, 3, 6, 8, 21, 22, 25, and 27, due to their dependence on claim 1, either directly or indirectly, these claims are subject to the reasoning provided therein. Therefore, the rejection of 2, 3, 6, 8, 21, 22, 25, and 27 under 35 U.S.C. 103 have been withdrawn.
Regarding newly added claim 54, the examiner respectfully refers the applicant to Reasons for Allowance section below.
REASONS FOR ALLOWANCE
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
The primary reason for allowance of the claims is the amendment to claim 1 to include: “an ultrasound probe, comprising at least one transducer and at least one optical sensor, at least one optical sensor comprising ultrasound enhancement material […] the at least one optical sensor configured to produce one or more optical responses upon receiving harmonic and subharmonic ultrasound echoes, corresponding to the transmitted ultrasound signal, from the medium, the at least one optical sensor having a bandwidth greater than that of the at least one transducer”.
The examiner acknowledges that the prior art references of record, specifically, Byrne and Shen do not teach these limitations. While Byrne and Shen include harmonic echoes (i.e. second harmonic, 2fo, 3fo), neither Byrne nor Shen teaches or makes obvious subharmonic echoes, whose frequencies are less than the fundamental frequencies, for example f/M, M being an integer. Furthermore, Byrne and Shen make no mention of an ultrasound enhancement material.
Additionally, the prior art references of Szabo, Uhlendorf, Rozental, Fan and Sumetsky do not teach the amended claim limitations.
Furthermore, an updated search was conducted in which no prior art references were found to teach the amended limitations both alone or in combination with the other elements of the claim.
Therefore, claim 1 is allowable.
Regarding claims 20 and 41, the examiner notes that these claims include substantially the same subject matter as claim 1. Therefore, these claims are subject to the reasoning provided therein.
Thus claims 20 and 41 are allowable.
Regarding claims 2-3, 6-8, 10-11, 13, 17-18, 21-22, 25-27, 29, 32 and 54, due to their dependence on claims 1 or 20, respectively, either directly or indirectly, these claims are subject to the reasoning provided therein. Therefore, claims 2-3, 6-8, 10-11, 13, 17-18, 21-22, 25-27, 29, 32 and 54 are allowable.
Regarding claim 54, the examiner notes the prior art references of record do not teach “wherein the ultrasound enhancement material comprises polyvinylidene fluoride, parylene or polystyrene”. Furthermore, no prior art references were found to teach the above recited limitation both alone or in combination with the other elements of claim 1.
