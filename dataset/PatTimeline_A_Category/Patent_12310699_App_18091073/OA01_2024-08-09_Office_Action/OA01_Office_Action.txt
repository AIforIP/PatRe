Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
Claims 2-3, 6, 8, 21, 22, 25, 27, 32 and 41 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
Regarding claims 2, 3, 6, 8, 21, 22, 25, and 27 as written the claims read “wherein the at least one optical sensor is configured to produce at least a portion of the one or more optical responses […]”, however the term “at least a portion” is a relative term which renders the claim indefinite. The term “at least a portion” is not defined by the claim and the specification does not provide a standard for ascertaining the requisite degree regarding 1) how much/many of the one or more optical responses need to be produced by the optical sensor or 2) which portion(s) of the optical responses are actually produced. Therefore, one of ordinary skill in the art would not be reasonable apprised of the scope of the invention.
Regarding claim 32, the claims recites the limitation “The method of claim 30, wherein the change indicates a spectral shift […]”. However, there is insufficient antecedent basis for this limitation in the claim, therefore, it is unclear what “change” is being referred to. The examiner notes that claim 30 has been cancelled, therefore, even if the dependency of this claim were changed, claims 20-22, 25-27 do not contain “a change” therefore, it is unclear what this claim is referring to in the context of the method.
Regarding claim 41, as written the claim reads “wherein at least a portion of the received ultrasound echoes has a frequency that is a super-harmonic frequency of the one or more fundamental frequencies, a subharmonic frequency of the one or more fundamental frequencies, an ultra-harmonic frequency of the one or more fundamental frequencies, or a linear combination of the one or more fundamental frequencies”, however the term “at least a portion” is a relative term which renders the claim indefinite. The term “at least a portion” is not defined by the claim and the specification does not provide a standard for ascertaining the requisite degree regarding 1) how much/many of the received ultrasound echoes have to have a frequency that is super-harmonic, sub-harmonic, ultra-harmonic, or a linear combination of the one or more fundamental frequencies. Therefore, one of ordinary skill in the art would not be reasonable apprised of the scope of the invention.
