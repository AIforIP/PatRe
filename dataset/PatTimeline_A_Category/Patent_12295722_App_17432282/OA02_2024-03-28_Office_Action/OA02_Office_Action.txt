Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claims 1-7, 12-17 and 23-27 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Weiss 2013/0317393 in view of Hotelling 2017/0010746. As to claims 1-4, Weiss discloses a pressure sensing mat comprising a first portion including a first conductive layer 220a contacting a first non-conductive layer 240a, wherein the first conductive layer is continuous except for a first plurality of discontinuities defining a first plurality of conductive strips oriented in a first direction, and wherein the first portion includes first and second sides, with each conductive strip of the first plurality of conductive strips formed as columns including first and second columns extending to the first and second sides (Figs. 3A-3B). Weiss further discloses a second portion including a second conductive layer 220b layered to a second non-conductive layer 240b, wherein the second conductive layer is continuous except for a second plurality of discontinuities defining a second plurality of conductive strips oriented in a second direction and an insulative layer 230 disposed between the first portion and the second portion. The first plurality of conductive strips and the second plurality of conductive strips form a conductive strip matrix having a plurality of capacitors 210 wherein each capacitors of the plurality of capacitors are configured to provide a capacitance indicative of a pressure applied at each capacitor of the plurality of capacitors. However, Weiss does not disclose the first column having a first width and the second column having a second width greater than the first width. Hotelling discloses a pressure sensing panel comprising conductive strips 122 that have different widths in order to accommodate the wiring or conductive leads 124 that connect to the controller; see Fig. 7. It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art to have a second column with a width greater than a first column in Weiss in view of Hotelling in order to accommodate the wiring or conductive leads 124 that connect to the controller.
As to claims 5 and 6, these claims are directed to statements of intended use and do not further structurally define the claimed product.
As to claim 7, Fig. 3A of Weiss shows conductive strips 220a and 220b orthogonal to each other.
As to claim 12, Weiss discloses first and second non-conductive layers 4212a, 4214a enclosing the conductive strips 4220a and defining channels between the strips; see Fig. 3D. Hotelling discloses running leads 124 on opposite sides of the conductive strips in Fig. 7.  It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art to position leads on opposite sides of the conductive strips in Weiss in view of Hotelling since this involves combining prior art elements to yield predictable results.
As to claims 13-17, Fig. 3D of Weiss shows the claimed features.
As to claim 23, Fig. 7 of Hotelling shows the conductive layer as having a surface area less than the non-conductive layers. It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art to adjust the surface area of the conductive layers to be less than the non-conductive layers in Weiss in view of Hotelling since this involves combining prior art elements to yield predictable results.
As to claim 24, the channels or portions of the laminate between the conductive strips in Weiss will obviously have a thickness less than the overall thickness of the laminate since they are a part of the overall thickness.
As to claim 25, Weiss discloses this feature in [0077].
As to claim 26, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art to any well-known conductive material such as copper to form the conductive strips in Weiss since it has been held to be within the general skill of a worker in the art to select a known material on the basis of its suitability for the intended use as a matter of obvious design choice.
As to claim 27, it would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art to adjust the surface resistivity of the conductive layer in Weiss to an amount within the claimed range since it has been held that where the general conditions of a claim are disclosed in the prior art, discovering the optimum or workable ranges involves only routine skill in the art.
