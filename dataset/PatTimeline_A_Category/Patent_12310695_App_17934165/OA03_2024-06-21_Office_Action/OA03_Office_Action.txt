Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 16-24 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
The limitation “receiving an indication that the second software was selected from the option in the graphical user interface” is improper. The selection of the option is ‘optional’ and therefore must be properly claimed as such. The claim must be corrected to “when” or “if” the option is selected. The claim is interpreted as such in the rejections that follow.

The following is a quotation of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 103(a) which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
(a) A patent may not be obtained though the invention is not identically disclosed or described as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the subject matter sought to be patented and the prior art are such that the subject matter as a whole would have been obvious at the time the invention was made to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which said subject matter pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claims 16-24 is/are rejected under pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 103(a) as being unpatentable over Chen et al. (US 2012/123222 A1) in view of Zaleski (US Pat. No. 7,945,457 B2).

Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed 05/15/2024 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive: the selection of an option is necessarily ‘optional’. Limitations that result from conditional or contingent limitations in a method are not given patent weight with respect to prior art. See MPEP 2111.04. Accordingly, Applicant’s arguments with regards to the selection of a second software are moot. Furthermore, it would improper to claim the human activity of picking a specific option when the disclosure makes the selection an option.
