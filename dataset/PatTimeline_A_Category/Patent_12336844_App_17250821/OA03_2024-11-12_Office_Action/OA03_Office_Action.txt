Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claims 1, 3, 5-8, and 19 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over Mannheimer et al. (USPN 8,352,004 – cited in previous action), and further in view of Tjin (USPGPUB 2004/0074307 – applicant cited) and evidenced by Bartol et al. (USPGPUB 2014/0058288 – cited in previous action). In regard to claims 1 and 19, Mannheimer discloses a system and a method (Figs. 1-11 and associated descriptions) comprising an optical fibre assembly (emitter 16 and detector 18, Figs. 10 and 11 and associated descriptions; he emitter 16 and detector 18 may also include optical fiber sensing elements/ fiber optics, Col 10 lines 15-30) configured to measure one or more physiological parameters of an individual (pulse oximetry, Figs. 10 and 11 and associated descriptions); and a pressure sensor configured to measure a contact pressure of the optical fibre assembly on the individual (element 12/12C, Fig. 4 and 11 and associated descriptions).
Mannheimer does not specifically disclose the pressure sensor comprises: an optical fibre comprising a transducer fibre Bragg grating and a matrix; wherein the transducer fibre Bragg grating is embedded in a matrix; and the matrix is configured to cause longitudinal strain in the transducer fibre Bragg grating in response to the matrix being subject to a transverse load.
Tjin teaches a pressure sensor (Figs. 2-6b and associated descriptions) comprises an optical fibre (elements 203, Figs. 2-6b and associated descriptions) comprising a transducer fibre Bragg grating (element 204, Figs. 2-6b and associated descriptions) and a matrix (elements 201/202, Figs. 2-6b and associated descriptions) ; wherein the transducer fibre Bragg grating is embedded in a matrix (elements 201/202, Figs. 2-6b and associated descriptions); and the matrix is configured to cause longitudinal strain in the transducer fibre Bragg grating in response to the matrix being subject to a transverse load (Figs. 2-6b and associated descriptions).
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to substitute the pressure sensor (e.g. strain gauge) of the system/ method (Mannheimer) with the FBG pressure sensor as taught by Tjin to yield predictable results, since one of ordinary skill in the art would have recognized that both pressure sensors are alternative equivalent pressure sensors for measuring force/ pressure as evidenced by Bartol (see at least [0031] of Bartol). The rationale would have been the simple substitution of one known, equivalent element for another to obtain predictable results (obvious to substitute elements, devices, etc.), KSR, 550, U.S. at 417.
Mannheimer as modified by Tjin does not specifically disclose the optical fibre assembly is configured to measure the physiological parameter only when contact pressure of the optical fibre assembly measured by the pressure sensor is determined to be within a predefined range.
However, Mannheimer further discloses to control power to the emitter/ detector based on the information obtained from the contact/ pressure sensor (Col 4 lines 1-9), a predefined pressure ranges for optical sensing (Col 5 lines 1-14), and a monitor processes the signal/ information obtained from the contact/ pressure sensor to determine/ characterize the pressure (Col 5 lines 38-54).
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to modify the system and the method to incorporate additional teachings from Mannheimer to measure the physiological parameter only when contact pressure of the optical fibre assembly measured by the pressure sensor is determined to be within a predefined range, since one of ordinary skill in the art would have recognized that controlling power to the emitter/detector and measuring the physiological parameter only when contact pressure is within a predefined range reduces the usage of power and prevents inaccurate measurements. The rationale would have been to obtain more accurate optical measurements and reduce power consumption.
