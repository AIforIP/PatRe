Reasons for Allowance
The following is an examiner’s statement of reasons for allowance:
The prior art of record fails to anticipate or make obvious the invention of claims 1-8 and 14-17, including, inter-alia, a device including a biosensor with first and second electrodes configured for insertion into a subcutaneous layer beneath a patient’s skin and a first battery at least partially electrically decoupled from at least one of the electrodes; an anode material coupled to a portion of a distal inserted end of the first electrode which, in combination with the second electrode, forms a second battery that is configured to activate upon immersion in an electrolytic fluid and send a signal; and a wake-up circuit coupled to the second battery to receive the signal and, in response, electrically couple the first battery to the at least one of the electrodes; and operation of such a device; in combination with all limitations in the claims.
Yee, cited in previous Office Actions, teaches a device with a biosensor that is configured to be “activated” when the biosensor’s electrodes have been inserted into electrolytic fluid of subcutaneous tissue, but Yee does not disclose the activation being detected by completion of a battery circuit where an anode and electrode/cathode are connected via electrolytic fluid.  Zdeblick, cited in previous Office Actions, teaches a sensing device where an anode is connected to a cathode via electrolytic fluid when the device is in the desired sensing location, but does not teach any part of the cathodic element also serving as a sensor after activation.
Keimel (US 2002/0120186) teaches a device with a biosensor including a plurality of electrodes, and a “battery” formed by an anode in contact with one electrode and another electrode that is activated when in contact with electrolytic fluid, but does not disclose the activation including sending a signal to electrically couple the sensor electrodes to a battery for powering sensing.
