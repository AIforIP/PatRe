Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claims 17-18 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over US 20170245776 A1 (Kurtz et al.) in view of US 20130204155 A1 (Brull et al.).
Regarding claim 17, Kurtz teaches the method according to claim 1.
Kurtz does not explicitly teach wherein the temporal segments of the noise signal (SNoise) used during the estimating of the periodic noise waveform (SPeriodic) are detected during a time interval (TNoise) not overlapping with a stimuli signal interval (TStimulus) of the single stimuli (SStimuli) and not overlapping with the duration of the response signal (TResponse).
However,
Brull teaches wherein the temporal segments of the noise signal (SNoise) used during the estimating of the periodic noise waveform (SPeriodic) are detected during a time interval (TNoise) not overlapping with a stimuli signal interval (TStimulus) of the single stimuli (SStimuli) and not overlapping with the duration of the response signal (TResponse) ([0022] “the start of the evoked muscle response is optionally identified by locating the time at which the level of muscle electrical activity exceeds the baseline level of electrical noise in the subject prior to stimulation of the motor nerve or immediately following completion of the evoked response”; [0097] “In one implementation, the noise, nerve response and muscle response may be separated from one another before each is measured.”; [0098] “the fiducial marks 602 as shown in FIG. 6C are noise-only segments”).
It would have been obvious for one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the invention to have modified the method taught by Kurtz to include the segments of the noise signal not overlapping with the stimuli signal interval and the response signal. One would have been motivated to make this modification because the background noise is able to be determined pre-stimulus or immediately post-response, not overlapping with these periods, which allows the neuromuscular activity to be detected by determining how the signal deviates from the baseline, as suggested by Brull [0098].
Regarding claim 18, Kurtz teaches the method according to claim 1.
Kurtz does not explicitly teach wherein the single stimuli signal (SStimuli) comprises a single pulse.
However,
Brull teaches wherein the single stimuli signal (SStimuli) comprises a single pulse ([0076] “In one implementation, the nerve stimulator 108 may deliver a 200 .mu.s or 300 .mu.s square-wave, monophasic, constant electrical pulse. The electrical pulse delivered by the nerve stimulator 108 should be sufficient in strength to elicit nerve responses”; [0091] “a single electrical pulse”).
It would have been obvious for one of ordinary skill in the art before the effective filing date of the invention to have modified the method taught by Kurtz to include a single pulse. One would have been motivated to make this modification because the evoked response may be recorded from a stimulus consisting of multiple electrical pulses or a single pulse, as suggested by Brull [0091, 0109].
Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed December 28, 2023 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive. With respect to the 102 Rejections in the Non-Final Office Action (See Pages 8-14 of Applicant’s Response “CLAIM REJECTIONS – 35 U.S.C. §102”), Applicant argues that Kurtz does not disclose “estimating a periodic noise waveform”. Applicant states that instead of quantifying the noise in a signal and using the quantified noise to remove the noise from a signal, Kurtz averages multiple signals to average out the noise from the signal. Newly added claim 17 clarifies this estimation process, however independent claim 1 recites “estimating a periodic noise waveform”, which encompasses the noise estimation taught by Kurtz [0006] under broadest reasonable interpretation (BRI).
MPEP § 2111 discusses proper claim interpretation, including giving claims their broadest reasonable interpretation in light of the specification during examination. Under BRI, the words of a claim must be given their plain meaning unless such meaning is inconsistent with the specification, and it is improper to import claim limitations from the specification into the claim.
Claims 2-6 and 8-18 are rejected based on their dependency on claim 1 for the reasons above, and for the reasons described in the 102 and 103 Rejections above.
