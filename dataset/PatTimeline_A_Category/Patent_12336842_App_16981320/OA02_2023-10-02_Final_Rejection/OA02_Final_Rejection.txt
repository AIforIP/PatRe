Response to Arguments
Applicant's arguments filed July 25, 2023 have been fully considered but they are not persuasive.
With respect to the 101 Rejections (see Applicant’s Response pages 6-8, “CLAIM REJECTIONS – 35 U.S.C. §102”), the Applicant argues that the claims, as amended, cannot be characterized as abstract because of the limitations “generating the improved response signal (ΣResponse) by subtracting the noise waveform from the response signal (SResponse), storing the improved response signal (ΣResponse) in a memory in the anesthetizing monitoring unit, and presenting the improved response signal (ΣResponse) on a display in the anesthetizing monitoring unit.” Applicant states that these features cannot conceivably be performed in the human mind, and are therefore not abstract.
However, under MPEP 2106.04, a limitation that recites generic computer components can still be considered an abstract idea if the process performed with the computer components can be performed by the human mind, and the computer components are well-understood, routine, and conventional in the art. Additionally, reciting well-understood, routine, and conventional extra-solution activities of an abstract idea, such as displaying, gathering, or storing data does not prevent the limitation from being considered an abstract idea (mental process). Therefore, the claims are rejected under U.S.C. 101 as described in the 101 Rejections above.
With respect to Kurtz (see Applicant’s Response pages 8-10, “CLAIM REJECTIONS – 35 U.S.C. §102”), Applicant argues that the claims, as amended, teach a configuration different from the method and device caught by Kurtz. Applicant argues that Kurtz does not teach or suggest “transmitting a single stimuli signal (SStimuli) using an output port of the anesthetizing monitoring unit, receiving a single evoked electromyography, EMG, response signal (SResponse), having a duration (TResponse), in response to the transmitted stimuli signal (SStimuli) using an input port of the anesthetizing monitoring unit, estimating a periodic noise waveform (SPeriodic), having the duration (TResponse), by using temporal segments of a noise signal (SNoise)” because Kurtz involves sending out a plurality of stimuli and receiving a plurality of responses. While Kurtz does teach embodiments where a plurality of stimuli is used, Kurtz also teaches using a single stimulus ([0002] “a stimulus”; [0006] “a single instance of the stimulus”; [0063] “may include multiple stimulus sources, such as visual and auditory, or may be a single source”). Likewise, Kurtz teaches “a stimulus-response signal” [0002], which can encompass a single evoked response to the stimulus. Therefore, claim 1 is rejected under U.S.C. 102(a)(1) and claims 2-6 and 8-16 are also rejected under U.S.C. 102(a)(1) based on their dependence on claim 1, and for the reasons described in the 102 Rejections above.
