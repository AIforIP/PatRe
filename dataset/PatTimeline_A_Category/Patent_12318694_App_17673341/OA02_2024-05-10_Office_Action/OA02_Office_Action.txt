Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of the first paragraph of 35 U.S.C. 112(a):
(a) IN GENERAL.—The specification shall contain a written description of the invention, and of the manner and process of making and using it, in such full, clear, concise, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to which it pertains, or with which it is most nearly connected, to make and use the same, and shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor or joint inventor of carrying out the invention.
The following is a quotation of the first paragraph of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112:
The specification shall contain a written description of the invention, and of the manner and process of making and using it, in such full, clear, concise, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to which it pertains, or with which it is most nearly connected, to make and use the same, and shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor of carrying out his invention.
Claims 4 and 12 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(a) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), first paragraph, as failing to comply with the written description requirement. The claim(s) contains subject matter which was not described in the specification in such a way as to reasonably convey to one skilled in the relevant art that the inventor or a joint inventor, or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the inventor(s), at the time the application was filed, had possession of the claimed invention. With regard to claims 4 and 12, while Applicant has support for these claims on their own, it does not appear that Applicant has support for these claim limitations in conjunction with the amended claim limitations of claim 1. That is, it appears that in claim 1 that sometimes the remaining time is canceled and a new time is set and then in claims 4 and 12 that sometimes the remaining time is summed. The problem here is claim 1 requires that it is reset based on “a fourth predetermined duration” (which also has issues as discussed below), and as such any object that enters with continuous losing point would read on claim 1 and thus would have to have their time reset, but now claim 4 says it is sometime summed and effectively not canceled. The specification does not have support for the two limitations combined.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1, 4-9, and 12-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
With regard to the independent claims, the claims have “a first predetermined duration,” the next time a duration is mentioned is “a fourth predetermined duration.” What happened to the second and third numbers? Then when the second virtual operation object enters the target area, the continuous hit points is canceled but then “reducing the hit points of the second virtual operation object at a predetermined rate within a second predetermined duration after the second virtual operation object enters the target region.” This is similar to the previous rejection of claim 3, which Applicant has not explained. As best understood with the current claim language, it would appear that an object that is already losing points with X amount of time remaining will have that time reset (canceled and then reapplied) based upon the object re-entering or entering another target region after being in an earlier target region. The claims are rejected on art based on this interpretation. Finally, while a second predetermined duration is put forth at the end of the independent claims, there never is a third predetermined duration which is confusing.
Claims 4 and 12 are additionally rejected for similar reasoning as discussed above in that there is no distinguishing between a fourth predetermined duration and a fifth predetermined duration so anything that could be considered a fifth could be considered a fourth, so according to the independent claims the time would be canceled and then restarted, yet according to the dependent claims the time would be summed. It is unclear how one could understand claim 1 in one way and then switch interpretations when it comes to dependent claim 4. In other words, in claim 1 ANY predetermined duration could be deemed to be a fourth and thus, in accordance with the interpretation stated above, the remaining time would be canceled and then restarted. However, claim 4 now seems to claim that that only happens for some things and at other times it is not canceled but rather is summed, thus either negating the independent claim or somehow changing it in a way that is not understood.
Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
In the event the determination of the status of the application as subject to AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 (or as subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 102 and 103) is incorrect, any correction of the statutory basis (i.e., changing from AIA  to pre-AIA ) for the rejection will not be considered a new ground of rejection if the prior art relied upon, and the rationale supporting the rejection, would be the same under either status.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103 which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
A patent for a claimed invention may not be obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set forth in section 102, if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which the claimed invention pertains. Patentability shall not be negated by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claims 1, 4-9, and 12-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103 as being unpatentable over PUBG Mobile as evidenced by “[PUBG MOBILE] (noted on the IDS filed 3/30/2023 NPL 2; hereinafter Mobile) in view of PlayerUnknown’s Battlegrounds as evidenced by “Molotov Cocktail” (hereinafter “Cocktail”) and Wowpedia “Damage over Time” (hereinafter “Wowpedia”).
The following mapped rejection is applied as best understood in view of the inconsistencies as described above in the 35 USC 112(b) rejections.
With regard to claim 1, Mobile discloses using a Molotov cocktail in the game by throwing it (0:02-0:04), but does not go into detail about the effects thereof, however Cocktail teaches how a Molotov cocktail is used and what happens as a result (Summary). With that in mind, the following rejection applies. The combination of Mobile and Cocktail teaches a virtual prop control method. performed by an electronic device, the method comprising: displaying. in a target game application, a target virtual throw prop controlled by a first virtual operation object (Mobile at 0:02-0:04); controlling, the first virtual operation object to throw the target virtual throw prop, the target throw operation being used for throwing the target virtual throw prop (0:02-0:04), setting a target region in the target game application as a region surrounding a point at which the target virtual throw prop is thrown wherein the target region is set to reduce hit points of at least one target virtual operation object located in the target region for a first predetermined duration (Mobile at 0:02-0:04; 0:50-1:30; 1:50-2:15; 5:20-5:35; Cocktail at Summary, “Molotov Cocktails create an area of fire on the ground around where they impact. Players hit by or passing through this area will be set aflame, doing constant damage over time”), and wherein the first virtual operation object and the at least one target virtual operation object respectively belonging to different virtual operation object sets (Mobile at 0:02-0:04; 0:50-1:30; 1:50-2:15; 5:20-5:35; Cocktail at Summary, “Molotov Cocktails create an area of fire on the ground around where they impact. Players hit by or passing through this area will be set aflame, doing constant damage over time”).
Mobile in the video does not explicitly describe what happens when a character is experiencing damage over time (known as a DoT in the gaming community) then enters the target region. However, Wowpedia teaches, based on a second virtual operation object being set to continuously lose hit points within a fourth predetermined duration before entering the target region, canceling said continuous loss of hit points of the second virtual operation object within the fourth predetermined duration upon the second virtual operation object entering the target region, and reducing the hit points of the second virtual operation object at a predetermined rate within a second predetermined duration after the second virtual operation object enters the target region (last paragraph before example describing how DoTs are refreshed, “if a DoT was refreshed before it had expired, the tick timer was reset”).
With regard to claim 4, the combination of Mobile, Cocktail, Wowpedia teaches wherein the reducing comprises: based on the second virtual operation object being set to continuously lose the hit points within a fifth predetermined duration before entering the target region, continuously reducing the hit points of the second virtual operation object within a target duration after the second virtual operation object enters the target region, wherein the target duration is a sum of the second predetermined duration and a remaining duration and wherein the remaining duration is the remaining duration in the fifth predetermined duration when the second virtual operation object enters the target region (Mobile at 0:50-1:30; 1:50-2:15; 5:20-5:35; Cocktail at Summary; Wowpedia, last paragraph “For example, a 15 second DoT can be extended when it has less than 4.5 seconds remaining at no duration loss (the full normal duration is added to the remaining duration”).
With regard to claim 5, the combination of Mobile, Cocktail, and Wowpedia teaches wherein the reducing comprises: continuing to reduce the hit points of the second virtual operation object, during the second predetermined duration, the second virtual operation object exits the target region (Mobile at 0:50-1:30; 1:50-2:15; 5:20-5:35; Cocktail at Summary).
With regard to claim 6, the combination of Mobile and Cocktail teaches wherein the controlling comprises: setting a state of the target virtual throw prop to a throw ready state, based on a first touch operation performed for a virtual throw button of the target virtual throw prop being detected. and displaying a throw route in the target game application: and controlling the first virtual operation object to throw the target virtual throw prop according to the throw route based on the target virtual throw prop being in the throw ready state and a second touch operation performed for the virtual throw button being detected, the target throw operation comprising the first touch operation and the second touch operation (Mobile at 0:50-1:30; 1:50-2:15; 5:20-5:35; Cocktail at Summary).
With regard to claim 7, the combination of Mobile and Cocktail teaches wherein the displaying the throw route in the target game application comprises: displaying a first parabola from a first position to a second position in the target game application. and using the first parabola as the throw route, the first position being a position of the target virtual throw prop, and the second position being a predicted point of fall of the target virtual throw prop (Mobile at 0:50-1:30; 1:50-2:15; 5:20-5:35).
With regard to claim 8, the combination of Mobile and Cocktail teaches further comprising: displaying a second parabola from a third position to the second position in the target game application. based on the position of the target virtual throw prop being adjusted li-om the first position to the third position, the throw route being changed from the first parabola into the second parabola; or displaying a third parabola from the first position to a fourth position in the target game application. based on the predicted point of fall of the target virtual throw prop being adjusted from the second position to the fourth position, the throw route being changed from the first parabola into the third parabola; or displaying a fourth parabola from a fifth position to a sixth position in the target game application, based on the position of the target virtual throw prop being adjusted from the first position to the fifth position and the predicted point of fall of the target virtual throw prop being adjusted from the second position to the sixth position, the throw route being changed from the first parabola into the fourth parabola (Mobile at 0:50-1:30; 1:50-2:15; 5:20-5:35).
Claims 9 and 12-16 are mirror claims to claims 1, and 4-8 and rejected in like manner.
With regard to claims 17-19 hit points, mana points, and energy value are all synonyms of each other and are disclosed by Mobile and taught by Cocktail (Mobile at 0:50-1:30; 1:50-2:15; 5:20-5:35; wherein you can see the player losing value and then eventually dying based on coming into contact with the Molotov cocktail; Cocktail at Summary).
Claim 20 is a mirror claim to claim 1 and is rejected in like manner.
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art at the time of filing to combine the teachings of Cocktail with that of Mobile since firstly they are different versions of the same game and thus secondly using the attributes known in Cocktail to make them work the same way in Mobile.
It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art at the time of filing to combine the teachings of Wowpedia with the disclosure of Mobile in order to understand what happens to the damage over time if a player were to enter a target region when a DoT already applied to the character, thus using a previously known mechanic applied to PUBG (i.e. the game being played in Mobile).
