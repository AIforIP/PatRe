Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 112
The following is a quotation of the first paragraph of 35 U.S.C. 112(a):
(a) IN GENERAL.—The specification shall contain a written description of the invention, and of the manner and process of making and using it, in such full, clear, concise, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to which it pertains, or with which it is most nearly connected, to make and use the same, and shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor or joint inventor of carrying out the invention.
The following is a quotation of the first paragraph of pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112:
The specification shall contain a written description of the invention, and of the manner and process of making and using it, in such full, clear, concise, and exact terms as to enable any person skilled in the art to which it pertains, or with which it is most nearly connected, to make and use the same, and shall set forth the best mode contemplated by the inventor of carrying out his invention.
Claims 1, 6-9, and 14-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(a) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), first paragraph, as failing to comply with the written description requirement. The claim(s) contains subject matter which was not described in the specification in such a way as to reasonably convey to one skilled in the relevant art that the inventor or a joint inventor, or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the inventor(s), at the time the application was filed, had possession of the claimed invention.
With regard to the independent claims Applicant has amended the claims to state, “based on the second virtual operation object being set to continuously lose the hit points within the second predetermined duration before entering the target region, re-staring continuously reducing the hit points of the second virtual operation object for a target duration after the second virtual operation object exits the target region, wherein the target duration is a sum of the second predetermined duration and a remaining duration, and wherein the remaining duration is the remaining duration in the second predetermined duration when the second virtual operation object enters the target region,” (emphasis added).
First, there is no mention of “re-staring” in the specification at all, and as discussed below, it does not even make sense what this would mean. Second, there is no discussion of “a sum of the second predetermined duration and a remaining duration, wherein the remaining duration is the remaining duration in the second predetermined duration.”
All dependent claims are rejection as depending from a previously rejected claim.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112(b):
(b)  CONCLUSION.—The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor regards as the invention.
The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph:
The specification shall conclude with one or more claims particularly pointing out and distinctly claiming the subject matter which the applicant regards as his invention.
Claims 1, 6-9, and 14-20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112(b) or 35 U.S.C. 112 (pre-AIA ), second paragraph, as being indefinite for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which the inventor or a joint inventor (or for applications subject to pre-AIA  35 U.S.C. 112, the applicant), regards as the invention.
With regard to the independent claims Applicant has amended the claims to state, “based on the second virtual operation object being set to continuously lose the hit points within the second predetermined duration before entering the target region, re-staring continuously reducing the hit points of the second virtual operation object for a target duration after the second virtual operation object exits the target region, wherein the target duration is a sum of the second predetermined duration and a remaining duration, and wherein the remaining duration is the remaining duration in the second predetermined duration when the second virtual operation object enters the target region,” (emphasis added).
First it is unclear what “re-staring” means. Applicant was told about this problem and suggested it might be a typo in the advisory action mailed on 07/19/2024. However, in ALL instances (both claims AND arguments) Applicant has used the word “re-staring” and as such Applicant must be taken at their writing.
Second, it does not make sense that the target duration is a sum of the second predetermined duration and the remaining part of the second predetermined duration? Does that mean we restart the second predetermined duration and then add what was previously remaining? Also how does that now relate to “a third predetermined duration after the second virtual operation object enters the target region?” Would there be two separate clocks running?
All dependent claims are rejected as depending from a previously rejected claim.
Response to Arguments
Rejections not repeated above have been withdrawn based upon Applicant’s amendments.
Applicant was advised in the advisory action mailed on 07/19/2024 to please 1) provide clarity on “re-staring” and 2) provide support for where in the original specification such a concept (whether it be “re-staring” or “re-starting”) could be found in the original specification. Applicant provided no further amendments and no further explanation. As such the above rejection is necessary. There is no prior art rejection applied to the claims because “re-staring” makes no sense in the context of the claims and as such no prior art seems to apply. Applicant is highly encouraged that if they choose to file further amendments to the claims in the future to provide clear and distinct paragraphs as to where support for such limitations can be found in the original specification in order to avoid further written description rejections of the claims, since there is also no mention of the word “re-starting” in the original specification either.
